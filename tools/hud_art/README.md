# The HUD art generator

Draws the new pictures of the match screen's HUD (the frame, the buttons, the pedestals with their pictures, Stop, the egg tray, the lunch box, the Fullscreen button), the new cursors (the pointer, the hands, the edge-scroll arrows, move, target, attack, food and no entry) and the new windows (the clay panel in its green frame, the dialogs' buttons, the options window with its switches and slider thumbs, the quick help, the countdown card, the results screen's lettering, wells and button), and packs them into the sheets that the game reads. What the game does with them, the setting that chooses them and the files' format are in [`docs/VIEW_AND_HUD.md`](../../docs/VIEW_AND_HUD.md#hud-art) and at the top of `include/ants_app/hud_art.hpp`.

```
python3 tools/hud_art/make_hud_art.py            # writes assets/hud_art/sheet_1x.png, sheet_2x.png, sheet_3x.png and hud_art.txt (about a minute)
```

```
python3 tools/hud_art/check_sheets.py            # builds the sheets again in a folder of its own and compares them with assets/hud_art/ (about a minute)
```

`check_sheets.py` says whether the generator makes the sheets that the repository holds, byte for byte (the manifest and the three sheets; the pixels alone when only the compression differs, because a machine without libdeflate writes larger files: `--strict` makes that a failure too). It needs the browser, so CI does not run it: run it after a change to the generator and before the sheets are committed. Its comparison is tested without a browser (`tests/scripts/test_hud_art_check_sheets.py`).

Needs Python 3 with Pillow and numpy, and Google Chrome or Chromium (headless: it renders the SVG pieces of the HUD; the cursors need no browser; `--chrome PROGRAM` or `ANTS_CHROME` names it when it is not in the usual place). Blender is not needed: the two hands of the cursors are committed (below). `--work DIR` (or `ANTS_HUD_ART_WORK`) is the folder of the scratch files, `--out DIR` the folder of the result. Nothing in the generator names a place on a machine: the repository's root is found from the scripts' own place. The drawing is deterministic: the same inputs give the same files.

## What is here

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `make_hud_art.py` | the command: draws the catalog at 1x, 2x and 3x and packs it |
| `sprites.py` | the catalog: every sprite, its place at 960 x 540, its anchor and slice, and the animations of the original that the sprites replace (names and frame times come from `docs/chd_table4_animations.json` and `asset_catalog/`) |
| `pack.py` | the sheets and the manifest (`hud_art.txt`; its format is described at the top of the file); skyline packing, one cell for each different picture, each sprite's edge repeated round it |
| `pieces.py`, `system.py`, `texture.py`, `ants.py`, `lettering.py` | the drawn pieces as SVG: the bars, strips, panel, windows, buttons and card, the one bevel system, the plate's texture tile, the ornament ants, the labels |
| `dialog_kit.py`, `dialog_windows.py`, `dialog_pieces.py`, `dialogs.py` | the windows (below): the look (the panel, wells, buttons, switches, tracks, thumbs, edit fields and lettering as SVG), the geometry of each window, the sprites and the cutting of a window into pieces, and `dialogs.export()`, which renders them and gives the manifest rows and animations |
| `results_art.py` | the results screen's pieces (below, "The results screen"): its lettering, the nine-slice well and the Leave Game buttons |
| `check_sheets.py` | builds the sheets again and compares them with the repository's, byte for byte (above) |
| `fullscreen_button.py` | the Fullscreen button of the HUD's top right corner (six sprites) |
| `relief.py`, `carve.py` | the carved pieces, built as height fields and lit from the top left: the pedestals and their eight pictures, the gold band and the rim flash, the socket, the eggs, the lunch box |
| `cursors.py`, `crisp.py` | the cursors: one sprite for every sprite of the original's sixteen cursor animations, at 1x, 2x and 3x, drawn flat and hard-edged on each scale's own pixel grid (`crisp.py` is the canvas); the sizes, the offsets from the click point and the frame times are read from the catalog below, so a cursor's click point and timing are the original's; the animations are marked `cursor` in the manifest |
| `hands/`, `make_hands.py` | the two hands (pointing and open), shaded renders made in Blender: `hands/<sprite>_<scale>x.png` are committed and read by `make_hud_art.py`; `make_hands.py --blender PROGRAM` (or `ANTS_BLENDER`) renders them again from `hands/glove_fit.py` (optional: only when that script changes; a new render can differ by one level in a few pixels, so the committed sprites are the reference) |
| `shrink.py`, `derive.py`, `png_write.py` | the size of the windows' sprites (below: "Size"): rounding where the picture is busy, the 2x pictures that the game makes from the 3x ones, the sheets' compression |
| `orig_anim.py`, `cat_orig.py` | read the original's animation names, times, places and sprite sizes from the repository's decoded catalog (numbers only: no picture of the original is used) |
| `glyphs.json`, `label_boxes.json` | the outlines of the small symbols, and where each label's letters stand |
| `reference_frame.py` | composes the frame at one shape and scale from the sheets by the manifest's rules alone; `tests/data/hud_art/` holds its output at 16:9 and 20:9 (1x), which `test_hud_art` compares with the real renderer |
| `fonts/` | the typeface of the Chat word (Lora Bold Italic) with its licence; the other face, Libre Baskerville, is in `assets/hud_art/` (below) |

The reference pictures are made again with `python3 tools/hud_art/reference_frame.py 960 540 --out tests/data/hud_art/frame_16x9_1x.png` (and `1200 540`, `frame_20x9_1x.png`). They change only when the art does.

## The windows

Every window is made of sprites, drawn in the window's own numbers (the original's 640 x 480 picture: the options card is 442 x 440 at (18, 20), the quick help is the whole 640 x 480, the dialogs are 320 x 224, the countdown card 480 x 236 with its own top left as (0, 0)); the game puts a window where it wants it.

- **The panel** is `panel_c`, one repeat of opaque clay with its tooth (160 x 120), and eight pieces of its frame on a transparent ground, `panel_tl` .. `panel_br`: corners of 24 x 24, edge pieces 24 deep and as long as one repeat (160 across, 120 down). The frame pieces carry everything the panel has over its clay (the darkening toward the frame, the shadow and lit lip, the ridge with its brushing and glint). They are cut from one frame at the places where the picture repeats (the darkening is within one level of a repeat from 24 pixels in: 20 is not enough at 2x and 3x), the brushing and the glint are made with the same repeats, and `export()` stops if the picture is not periodic. The game tiles `panel_c` over the whole panel from its top left (the last copy cut), then lays the frame pieces on it, edges repeated along their sides and corners last: the clay has no seam at any size.
- **What changes** is one sprite for each state: `dlg_btn_<yes|no|accept|decline|withdraw|return>_<up|hover|down>` (a button is its face plus 3 across and 4 down for its cast shadow, the face's top left at the sprite's), `dlg_btn_fullscreen_<up|down>` (the countdown card's: a face of 94 x 22), `op_on_*` and `op_off_*` (the switches: up, hover, down, chosen, chosen_hover), `op_thumb_<up|hover|held>`. Each is drawn at the place it has in its window, so the brushing falls on it as in the window.
- **What never changes** is drawn as one layer without the panel and without anything that changes or that the game writes, and cut into pieces (`op_part_NN`, `qh_part_NN`): the layer is cut at its widest empty row or column of at least 4 canvas pixels (empty at every scale), each part is cut again, and each is trimmed to what it holds. The quick help also has `qh_pedestal_move` and `qh_pedestal_special`, small copies of two of the HUD's pedestals. The countdown card has `cd_title` (the title's letters), `cd_count_well` (the well of the count), `cd_row_well` (the well of one player's row) and four `cd_dot_N` (the 11 x 11 color squares). The wells are an edit field's bezel and ground (`field()`): the picture starts 5 pixels left of and above the ground and holds the bezel, its cast shadow and the shadow's soft edge, w + 12 by h + 13 for a ground of w x h.
- **Animations** are one for each picture of the original that a piece replaces (`yes1`, `op_conu`, ...), with the places in window numbers, one for each fixed layer (`op_window`, `qh_window`, `cd_card`), and `cd_row`: one player's well, placed so that the animation's origin is the top left of the row's 268 x 21 ground (the game draws it at every row, and puts the color square, the names and the numbers on it).

What the game does with them: it draws a window from the panel pieces and the window's animation, and the buttons, switches and thumbs from their own animations; the pictures of the original that are kept (ants, power-ups, bombs, fire, hills) and everything that the game writes (the edit fields' text, names, digits, sentences) are drawn over them by the game.

## The results screen

The new results screen ([`docs/VIEW_AND_HUD.md`](../../docs/VIEW_AND_HUD.md#the-new-look-of-the-results-screen)) is laid out by the game, in units of whole device pixels, so its pieces have no place of their own: `results_art.py` draws each one at the size it has in a unit and `dialogs.export()` renders them on a sheet of their own (the sprites above keep their pixels). Every one is a sprite named `sc_*`:

- **Lettering**: the titles `sc_victory_26` / `_22`, `sc_defeat_26` / `_22` and `sc_gameover_36` / `_26` (the number is the height of the capital in units), `sc_matchstats_15`, the heading `sc_others`, the labels in the gold of the windows' lit switch (`sc_k_winner`, `sc_k_colony`, `sc_k_play`, `sc_k_mvp`, `sc_k_blood`, `sc_k_raid`, `sc_k_stand`), the second page's group headings `sc_g_damage`, `sc_g_food`, `sc_g_special` with the small words `sc_s_done`, `sc_s_taken`, `sc_s_trips`, `sc_s_points`, `sc_s_bombs`, `sc_s_fires`, `sc_s_raids`, `sc_s_loot` under them. Each is cut to the box of what it shows. Its manifest place (x, y) is where its alignment point and its baseline are in the sprite, counted from its top left (the middle for the playful titles, the start for the rest): the game puts the line by that point.
- **The well**: `sc_well_tl` .. `sc_well_br`, the eight pieces of an edit field's bezel (brushed steel, one flat color along the whole bezel, so an edge that is repeated shows no fall of light): corners of 12 units and edge pieces of 128 (across) or 32 (down), one period of the brushing, which the game repeats along an edge (the last copy cut). The dark ground inside is a fill.
- **Buttons**: `sc_leave_<up|hover|down>` is the button of the corner (a face of 99 x 22 units and its cast shadow, 3 across and 4 down) and `sc_watch_<state>` Watch (58 x 20 and the shadow). A button as wide as the screen asks is its ends and a run: `sc_btn_<l|m|r>_<state>` (a face 40 high; the left and right ends of 12 units, a run of 128 that the game repeats) with the word `sc_leave_word_<state>` ("Leave Game") or `sc_bw_<room|hub>_<state>` ("Back to the room", "Back to Ants & Uncles") on it, and `sc_sm_<l|m|r>_<state>` (a face 22 high: the wide screen's buttons and the link to the other page) with the words `sc_sw_<allstats|back|room|hub>_<state>` ("All stats", "Back to results", "Back to the room", "Back to Ants & Uncles"; the arrows of the first two are drawn with the word).

The sizes are the numbers of `include/ants_app/scorecard_layout.hpp` (the test `test_scorecard_new` reads them from the shipped manifest), and the game draws the art only when every piece is there at those sizes.

## Size

The windows' sprites are most of what the art adds to the web build's data package, so `dialogs.export()` makes them small in three ways (`shrink.py`, `derive.py`, `png_write.py`); none of them changes how a window looks at 1x or 3x, and the 2x look is the 3x one made smaller.

- **Fewer levels where the picture is busy.** `quantize()` rounds red, green, blue and alpha to a multiple of a step (24 for plain lettering, 16 for the brushed fittings, buttons, switches, pedestals and frame), but only at pixels that are 8 (lettering) or 6 (fittings) levels from the average of their four neighbors, and at the pixels next to those: an edge or a stroke of the brush hides the rounding. A flat color, a smooth ramp of any slope, a dither of a level and the 0 and 255 of alpha are never touched, so no soft shadow or bevel gets bands (a rounding at every pixel that is 2 levels off made contours in the shadows: that is why the limit is 6). Lettering is rounded from the color of its ground, so that a pixel of the ground stays exactly as it was and no ring forms round a letter. A frame piece that the game repeats is rounded as if its picture went on round (no seam). The clay tile is rounded to a multiple of 2 everywhere (at most 1 level off). The four color squares of the countdown card are never rounded: the color names the player.
- **No 2x pictures.** The game makes the 2x picture of every window sprite from its 3x one when it loads the 2x sheet (`derive2x` in the manifest, `derive_two_thirds` in `hud_art.cpp`, `derive.py` here: every 3 x 3 pixels give 2 x 2, weights 2, 1, 0 and 0, 1, 2, premultiplied, whole numbers). `pack.py` puts those sprites in the top rows of every sheet, so only those rows of the 3x sheet are decoded. The generator compares each derived picture with the one drawn at 2x and stops above a mean difference of 25 levels: plain lettering is 2 to 3 levels, the clay 0.2, the brushed pieces 10 to 22 (the brush is drawn on each scale's own pixel grid, so the derived streaks are a little softer and not the same streaks). The 1x and 3x sprites are the drawings; the HUD's own sprites are untouched.
- **A second sheet when the first is full.** A texture is at most 4096 device pixels on a side on every graphics card, so a sheet is at most 4096 / 3 = 1365 canvas pixels high at 3x (`pack.LIMIT`; the width is held to 1200). When the sprites do not fit one sheet, `pack.py` fills the first as far as it goes (the derived sprites always stay on it) and puts the rest on a second one: `sheet2_1x.png`, `sheet2_2x.png`, `sheet2_3x.png`, and in the manifest `sheet2size`, `sheet2` and `sprite2` records (the format is at the top of `include/ants_app/hud_art.hpp`). The game keeps a texture for each sheet it draws from; `check_sheets.py` compares the second sheet's files when either folder has them. The test is `tests/scripts/test_hud_art_pack.py` (made-up pictures and a small limit: no browser needed).
- **A smaller file.** `png_write.py` compresses the sheets' deflate stream with libdeflate when it is installed (`brew install libdeflate`, `apt install libdeflate0`): 5% less than zlib's best, the same pixels. Without it the sheets are written by Pillow alone and are about 5% larger.

## Fonts

The labels and the windows' lettering are set in **Libre Baskerville Regular** and the word Chat in **Lora Bold Italic** (`fonts/Lora-BoldItalic.ttf`, licence `fonts/Lora-OFL.txt`), both under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Libre Baskerville is `assets/hud_art/LibreBaskerville-Regular.ttf` with its licence text beside it (`LibreBaskerville-OFL.txt`): the generator reads that one file, and the game reads it too, to draw the dialogs' sentences, which are not known when the art is made (names, colors). Lora is used here, at drawing time, and nowhere else: the Chat word is baked into its sprite, so the game ships no file of it. The licence texts stay with the fonts ([`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`](../../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)).

## Known limits of the art

- The cursors are drawn in three manners: the hands are shaded renders, everything else is flat and hard-edged. The pointer is a little smaller than its box (its tail ends two pixels short of the original's), and the last two frames of attack are a white block in a red ring. Food keeps the original's own colors and makes of its chunk and brackets, redrawn by hand rather than copied; at 1x the chunk and the brackets are pixel art (the other cursors' slanted and round edges are antialiased there), at 2x and 3x both are smooth.

- The lunch box is a front view; the original's is a three-quarter view of a pail.
- The latch has no gold side walls inside its band, the seat in the rise and the swap is shallow, and the turning slab is flat.
- At 1x the carvings are averaged from a larger render: softer than hand-placed pixels. The ornament ants' legs are faint.
- The plate texture's phase jumps where a right- or bottom-anchored piece meets a repeated run at a width that is not 960 + n x 192 or a height that is not 540 + n x 128.
- The frame's brushing and glint repeat every 160 pixels along the top and bottom and every 120 down the sides, so where the last copy of an edge piece is cut (at the corner pieces) the streaks start again: a jump in the brushing of the frame only; the clay is the same at any size.
- A button's, switch's and thumb's contact shadow reaches 2 pixels left of and 1 above its face (and the thumb's one row below its 18): the sprite's box is the face and its cast shadow, so up to 32 levels of that faint shadow's alpha on one row or column are cut.
