# Home and the Room: the front page's two screens

The front page (`web/lobby.html`, served at `/`) is one page with one WebSocket and two screens: **Home**, a top bar over the games, the players and the chat, and the **Room**, the room page that [Play in the browser](PLAY_IN_BROWSER.md#your-room) describes, under a slim bar. `<body data-screen="home">` or `data-screen="room"` says which one shows. The page's client of the game server (`web/front/lobby_net.js`), its model and its session storage are the same for both, so going from one screen to the other is a class and one render: instant, no request, and the seat is never dropped. Both screens are drawn as the approved pictures draw them. Everything new is in `web/home/` (`home.js`, `home.css`, the chat dock's `chat.js`, `chat.css`, the Games list's `games.js`, `games.css` and the players list's `players.js`, `players.css`; the last three load after the first paint), because `web/front/` is at its size cap and the page at its line limit.

## Home

- **The top bar:** the logo, **Quick host**, **Quick join**, **My room** (there only while the page holds a room, made or joined: it is hidden, with no place in the row, out of the tab order and with no name for a screen reader, until then, and goes when the room does; with how many of the colors that are in the match play, "3/4"), **Games** (with how many games are open), and at the right the login chip and the Menu button (the chip is hidden until the chat's hub is there and offers a login: [the login](#the-login) below). Quick host, Quick join and Games are lit by `games.js` when it has loaded (until then they are drawn and disabled), and nothing on the bar is a dead button (My room is there only when it does something: on a phone Games then has the whole row, and held sideways it spans the place of My room). **Nothing on either bar is ever cut off or pushed past the window's edge, at any width from 320 px up** (`tests/scripts/web_home_games_browser.js` checks Home and the Room at every tenth width and at both sides of every limit). The bar's width budget, for the widest state (the counts "4/4" and "50+", the login chip with a long name): from 1280 px it is as drawn (1273 px at most); the chip shows its circle only below 1280 px (1075 px needed); a step below 1077 px shrinks the gaps, the padding and the type (890 px); below 892 px it is one low row (no icons, "Room" for My room and no count on it: 773 px; a phone held sideways, 640 px wide and more, has it too); below 775 px the type shrinks once more (620 px, to 640 px wide); under 640 px it is the phone's three rows (184 px high), for a phone upright and for any window of that width, except that a phone held sideways (landscape, at most 500 px high) 360 to 639 px wide has **two compact rows** (106 px high, so the body and the strip below it keep the rest of the window): Quick host and Quick join with the logo and the Menu on the first, My room (with its count), Games (with its count) and the login chip on the second (347 px needed for the widest state; the step starts at 360 px, and a window narrower than that keeps the three rows). The players list has no button on the bar: it is a column of Home's body in a wide window and the Players view of a phone ([the players list](#the-players-list) below), so none of the limits above moved for it (the real-browser check of the bar runs with the list there too). The name chip is in the budget above.
- **The page's height:** Home is exactly as tall as the window shows (`100dvh`, with `100vh` for old browsers, in `home.css`), less `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` as padding: a phone whose page reaches under the system bar (`viewport-fit=cover`) would otherwise put the bottom of the body, the chat panel and the phone's strip under it. The bar, the body, the chat panel and the strip always lie inside the visible window (`web_home_games_browser.js`, part 1i).
- **My room** shows the room that the page holds (a room that Quick host (Public or Private) or Quick join made or joined, or a seat that the tab takes back in the background): it is not drawn while there is none, and if the focus is on it when the room goes (Leave room), the focus moves to Quick host. Its press only shows the room (the first press of a button with no room made a private room: that is now Quick host's Private room, through the same `openRoom` path). **A plain visit makes no room.** A tab that holds a seat takes it back in the background, so My room stays live.
- **The Menu** (also in the Room's bar): Matches, Live now, Map maker, How it works, the Discord server, then the small links of the footer (Sprites and sounds, Changelog, GitHub, Feedback, Legal), the live numbers and the version. It is at most as tall as the window leaves under the bar and scrolls inside itself (a phone held sideways is about 360 px high). The footer is not drawn on Home. **How it works** is a dialog like the map list: while it is open the page behind it (both bars, the Room, the strips, the footer, the chat dock and the list's toast) takes no key or tap.
- **The body** (`#hmain`) is the area under the bar, a grid: **the Games list** at the left (the same list as the popover below: one code, `games.js` paints the rows, polls `GET /lobbies` and runs Join, Join by code, Quick join; always shown, a panel whose heading and foot stay while the cards scroll inside it), **the players list** in a narrow column at the right (220 to 280 px, 24% of the window; [the players list](#the-players-list)), and **the chat dock** as a panel along the bottom, as wide as the two and the gap between them ([the chat dock](#the-chat-dock)). Each open room is a **card**: the map's small picture, the host, the map's name, the seats as four colored dots with how many are taken, the bots, **Join**, and the **Yours** and **BEST** tags as before (a card is 290 px wide at least, so the cards fill the panel in as many columns as fit: three at 1440 px). With no open room the panel says "No open games right now." and shows that line with "Rooms show up here when their host switches them to Public." and no buttons (the bar right above has Quick host and Quick join), and Join by code under it. Press **Games** in the bar and the focus goes to the list (there is no second copy); its count is the same. A **phone** (a window up to 700 px wide, in either direction) has one of the three at a time and a **strip** along the bottom, **Games**, **Players** and **Chat**, with the number of open games and of people beside the first two: the view shown is the one pressed (Games at first), the Games button of the bar shows the Games view, the strip shows a count of three digits or more as 99+, the login chip shows the Chat view, the strip is inert with the bars during the hand-off to the game, and the Players tab is not there until the list is (a hub that has none). The chat is open only on its own view (`home.js` opens it with the view and folds it with the others, so what comes while it is away counts as unread). A window of 701 px or more shows all three and ignores the strip; a window that changes from one to the other opens or folds the chat for the new one. Where the window is **short** the games keep the room: the chat dock opens folded to its header under 601 px high (a press opens it; it is a panel of 160 to 260 px, 28% of the window high, when open), and the legend, the label of Join by code and the guest's line of the chat are left out under 701 px high. **The Yard has no place in this layout yet**: Home has no area for it, no stand-in and no label that the page reads; the site label of the build (`ANTS_SITE_LABEL`) only goes into the title and the footer.
- **The strips** (the Rejoin button, "Back from the match") stand above both screens; Home is as tall as the window, strips included, so it never scrolls.

## The players list

`players.js` and `players.css` load after the chat dock, once, when the dock is mounted (`home.js` asks for them with the loader of the Games list; a page whose dock never comes has no list), and speak through the dock's socket: the page keeps one socket to the hub, and the list adds no address of its own. Nothing is kept in the browser. It is the hub's list of who has the front page open ([`HUB.md`](HUB.md#presence): the names are the chat's, a guest is counted and never named, nothing is stored); this page only draws it.

- **What it shows.** A header, **Players**, with the count (everybody: the visitor and the guests too, so it is the number that the chat's "here now" says, "500+" over 500; one person alone is no number; the Players tab of a phone's strip says "99+" over 99), then the visitor first as **you** (the gold frame, the first letter in a circle, where the page itself says they are, not where the hub last heard; the visitor's row in the hub's list is the one that the welcome names, `me`, and is left out of the groups, so another person with the same name is not), a divider, and the groups **Moderators**, **Looking**, **Waiting** and **Playing** with their counts, each person by name (a moderator marked **mod**, and told where under the name, as the visitor is), and last **+ N guests, not logged in**. Under a name: nothing for a person who looks; a room that waits by its map and the colors that play, "Treasure . 2 of 4", or "In a room" where the hub says nothing of it (it keeps no details of a private room, nor of the room of an account that may not chat, so the page does not call it private); a match by its map and its players, "Islands . 3 players", or "In a game"; the visitor's own row, which knows its room, says "Private room" or "Private game" for a private one. For a room that the Games list shows (its code) a map's name, an original's or a map of the players', is drawn only if the list shows the same name; for a room that it does not show, an original's name is the page's own table's (one of six) and a map of the players' is not drawn; otherwise the line has the seats and no name, because what a person says about a room is text from a stranger. A dot in the circle has the color of the group. Where a public room waits **and the Games list has that room** (its code) there is a **Join**: the Games list's own Join of that room is pressed (its questions, its notices, its refusals; a list that is a popover, as on the test site, is opened for it; a room that the list does not hold says "That game is not open any more."), and none on the visitor's own room. Past 200 named people the list ends with "and N more". Every name, map and number is checked as text from strangers, as the chat's lines are, and drawn with `textContent`.
- **Where.** The panel is a column of Home's body at the right of the Games list, **220 to 280 px** wide (`clamp(220px, 24vw, 280px)` in `home.css`, only once the hub has sent its list: until then the Games list has the whole body), as high as the Games list and starting on its line; the list inside it scrolls. The panel has no fold button and no folded state: it is always open (the chat's own fold button is the chat's). A window up to 700 px wide shows it as the **Players view** of the phone's strip, between the bar and the strip. There is no sheet, no drawer, no button on the bar and no scrim. The panel is inert with the bars during the hand-off to the game and under a dialog of the page.
- **When there is nothing to show.** The list shows nothing until the hub has sent it (no flash of "just you"), and for a hub that has no list (an older one: its welcome does not say so) nothing at all, and Home is the same as without the two files (a real browser finds nothing but a few pixels at the edges of rounded buttons, which it draws either way). A hub that is not there (the chat says "Chat is offline") is **Players list is off right now**, with a grey dot instead of the count, in the panel; the list comes back by itself with the chat. Nobody else here: "Just you here. Host a game!" and a **+ Host a game** that presses Quick host (off while Quick host is).
- **What the page tells the hub.** Only a visitor who is named says anything: where they are, from the page's own room (no room: looking; a room that has not started: waiting; a match, or a page that is on its way to one: playing), a public room's map and seats (and its code, if the visitor is the host: the Join is on the host's row), a private room's nothing, at most every 3 seconds, and again at once on a new socket. The list is asked for once for each socket. A guest asks and says nothing. The page looks at its room once a second, so the visitor's own row follows a change at once and the hub is told within 4 seconds.
- **What it cannot show.** The game page opens no socket to the hub (it never did: the front page's seat is handed to it on START), and the hub lists a person only while their socket is open, so a person whose tab went to the game is out of the list until they are back on the front page. "Playing" is therefore a person whose page has heard START and is on its way to the match (a hand-off that is slow, or has not finished), or still has the front page open in a room that started; a list that shows everybody in a match needs the game page, or the game server, to say so, and the hub to hold that word past a closed socket or the game page to open one to it: not done here.

## Games, Quick join, Quick host and the public Room

`games.js` and `games.css` come after the first paint (the page appends them to the head; a script that does not come leaves the buttons disabled and the page as it was) and own everything about public rooms. The page's `client`, model and session stay the page's own; the script reads them through the small bridge that `lobby.html` hands to `home.js` (`client()`, `join`, `typed`, `name`, `handing()`, `cancel`, `hold`, with `info`, `make`, `leave`, `how`, `stalled`).

- **Notices.** The notices of this part (the public room, "Your match is running.", "Joined Pebble's room.") are one **toast**: a box over the page, `position: fixed`, in the middle of the window and just above whatever lies at the bottom of the screen: the chat (on a phone's Chat view, just above the chat's field, so the field stays free), the strip of views of a phone, or, on the Room of a window up to 700 px wide, the bar with START! (START! stays free at every size). It is never put into Home's bar at the top. Where there is no room for it between that bar and what lies at the bottom (a phone held sideways, a window under about 400 px high), it covers the top of the chat's lines; on the Room, START! comes first, so there it can cover Copy link, the top of the card or the Room's slim bar until OK or the 6 seconds clear it. It is placed again when the window changes, when the screen changes (Home, Room), when a phone's view changes, when the chat folds or unfolds and when one of the page's strips above the bar comes or goes. It takes no room: the bar, the list, the players and the chat keep their size and place when it comes and goes. One toast at a time: a later notice replaces the one that is there. It has the page's colors (a gold edge for a warning), an **OK** button and, where the notice asks something (**Rejoin**, **Make it public**, **Reload**), that button too. A notice that asks nothing goes by itself after 6 seconds, and waits while the pointer or the focus is on it; one that asks stays until it is answered, replaced or closed. **OK** closes it, and so does Escape while the focus is on the toast (Escape pressed anywhere else, in the chat's field or to close the list or a question, leaves a toast that asks something where it is; the focus goes back to where it was). It comes in with a short fade and slide (none for a reader who asks for no motion), has `role="status"` and `aria-live="polite"`, and its words are set as text. The sentences are the same as before. The page's own strips above the bar (the Rejoin offer, "Back from the match", the page's banner) are not part of this and still stand in the page.
- **The Room's switch.** The host sees **Private** (link only) and **Public** (listed in Open games) in the card, a guest reads "Public room" or "Private room". A press sends one `Visibility` through `LobbyClient.setPublic` (the page's pace applies: a request that is held back sends nothing and says "One moment, then press again."); the switch shows what the last Room message said and never what a press asked. A room that went private after 30 minutes says so to its host with a one-tap **Make it public**; another person's lead says "This room is private again because the host changed." While the Room screen shows, the tab is visible and the host looks at a public room, the page says "public" again every 10 minutes (a refresh that counts as use; never from a hidden tab, from Home or by a guest, and not once the host has touched nothing (no pointer, key or touch) for 20 minutes: the room is then left to the server's 30-minute idle rule, and a touch brings the repeat back). A "One moment, then press again." goes by itself when the moment is over, and a Private that the server did not answer for 10 seconds is not taken for the leader's own choice when the room goes private by itself later. Each button is one line of text, the small word on the baseline of the big one, which the browser centers in the button (a flex row with `align-items: baseline` hangs at the top of a tall button): the middle of the capitals is within 1 px of the button's middle. Under 360 px the whole label scales with the window (`font-size: 4.7vw`, the small word at .765 em, 4 px of side padding), so that "link only" stays inside its button at every width from 300 px.
- **Quick host** flips the open room that you lead to Public and shows the Room ("Your room is public now. It shows in Open games; ..."); with no room it asks first, in a card over Home ("Host a game": **Public room**, which has the focus, **Private room** and **Cancel**; each button 48 px high, Escape and Cancel change nothing and the focus goes back to Quick host): Public makes the room (the page's own machinery) and sends `Visibility(public)` once the Room message says that the room is there, Private makes the same room and never sends it (the room is for the people who have its link, and is not listed); with a room of its own it does not ask this; a public room says "Your room is already public."; as the leader of a private room with people in it it asks first ("Make your room public? Sage is in it."); as a guest it asks first ("Leave Pebble's room and host your own?"), and only then leaves and makes one; while the page is on its way to a game (a match for one too) it does nothing but say "The game is opening. If it does not, reload this page." with a **Reload** (a navigation that is stopped leaves the page here), and so do Join and Join by code; START waiting or a match to go back to (the page's Rejoin offer is up) says "Your match is running." with **Rejoin**, and so do Quick join, Join and Join by code, which open nothing; a full server gives the page's own "The server is full right now" strip.
- **Quick join** finds the best open public room and moves the page into it. One look at the list ([`PUBLIC_ROOMS.md`](PUBLIC_ROOMS.md); the answer of less than a second ago is as good), the page's own room left out by its code, the rest **best first**: the fullest room that still has a free seat (the fewest free seats), then the oldest (the longest listed), and rooms that tie keep the door's order. It tries up to **three** rooms, one after the other, 0 to 300 ms apart (at random), with the page's probe: a second client that only joins, so the old room is let go of only when a new one welcomes the page. A room that is full, started or gone is a reason for the next one; another server version (the page's reload banner) and 15 s of silence end the tries. While it works the card **Finding a game…** stands over Home (four seats, a bar, "Looking through 5 open games for the best one.", and a **Cancel**, mid-card under the bar, that takes no press for 700 ms: a double press is not a Cancel; Cancel and Escape let the second client go, and no seat is left behind). The Welcome of a room shows the card **Joined Pebble's room** (the room as it is now, "Opening the room…", **Go to the room**), and the Room opens by itself 1.5 s later or at the press, with no strip. If nothing is open, or the three that were tried were filled meanwhile, the card says "No open games right now." (or "Games list is off right now") and offers **Host one!** (Quick host's machinery without its Public or Private question, because the card says that the room goes public: it flips the page's own room to public, or makes one) and **Not now**. If other people are in the page's room (a guest always counts: the host is there) it asks first, in the card of the other questions, about the best room (tagged BEST): "Leave your room?" "Sage is in your room. If you go, Sage becomes the host." **Leave and join** / **Stay here**. Like Quick host and Games it is off while a hand-off is stalled (the page's words are its title) and does nothing but say "The game is opening…" on a page that is on its way to a game, and "Your match is running." when a match waits. Every card is described by its first line (`aria-describedby`), and the Joined card, which closes by itself and leaves no strip, is also told by a line that only a screen reader reads ("Joined Pebble's room.", a polite status line in the body). A press on Quick join or Quick host while a Join of the list waits shows the list again, with the line and the Cancel of that Join, and the toast says "Joining Pebble's room… Wait for it, or cancel it in the list." (it goes when the Join ends). In a window under 351 px tall or 261 px wide (a phone on its side, a page zoomed to 200 %) the cards keep their look but use smaller gaps, a smaller shadow ant and, under 261 px, a smaller title and text, so that both buttons of every card stand in the window without a scroll; the other sizes are as drawn.
- **The Games list** is the popover under the button (the mode of a list that is given no dock; Home always docks it in its body, above, so the popover is the same code that the page does not ask for): `GET /lobbies` ([`PUBLIC_ROOMS.md`](PUBLIC_ROOMS.md)) as rows, best first (the map's picture, host, map, the seats as four colored dots and n of the size, the bots, **Join**; the first other row, the room that Quick join takes, is tagged **BEST** when there are two or more other rows; the heading says "5 waiting · updates live" while the looks go well), the page's own room, when it is Public and in the answer (recognized by its code, the same test that Quick join uses to leave it out), as the **first row**, tagged **Yours** (the style of BEST; the same text as the other rows, but no **Join** and nothing to press), counted by the Games button and the heading, never tagged BEST and never taken by Quick join (BEST is the first other row, and only when there are two or more other rows); "No open games right now." with a line that says rooms show up when their host makes them Public (and no buttons: the bar has Quick host and Quick join) only when the list has no row at all (the own room included), "Games list is off right now" when the door answers 404, 503 or something that is not a list, and **Join by code** at the bottom (it replaces the Room card's "Have a code?"). A Join opens a second client that only joins; the page's own room is let go of **only when the new one welcomes the page**, so a refusal (full, started, gone, another version, no answer in 15 s) costs nothing and is said in plain words; a room with other people in it is asked about first. While a Join waits (up to 15 s) the list says "Joining Pebble's room…" with a **Cancel** that lets the second client go (it takes no press for 700 ms: it stands where the Join button stood, and the second click of a double click is not a Cancel); a Welcome that comes to a page that is on its way to a game is let go of at once. A press on the question card or the name card is not "a press elsewhere": the list stays open for the words that tell why a Join did not go.
- **The picture of a row** (a square of 56 px, 52 on a phone and 48 in the card of a question) is the map's: an original's own (`front/preview_<word>.png`), or, for a map of the players, the library's, `/maps/previews/<file name without .lvl, percent-encoded>.webp`, the address that `AntsMapPicker.previewPath` (`web/front/map_picker.js`, the rule of the match pages) makes from the row's checked file name and that `GET /maps` gives as `thumb`, so no list of maps is fetched for it (`tests/scripts/web_home_games_check.js` draws all 534 maps of `Community-Maps/` and checks each address against `previews.json`). A picture is loaded when it is near (lazily). One that does not come, a 404 (a map that the library has no picture of), a failed load or a page without the picker, is taken away and the plain square stays: never a broken image. A map that a player sent and the maintainer approved, and a test map of the Map Maker, have no `.webp`; the game server renders their `.png` for the picker's list, which this row does not ask for, so their square stays plain.
- **Polling:** every 30 s with the popover closed, every 5 s while it is open (the docked list is always open: every 5 s while Home shows) and at once when it opens, +-20 % each, doubled after every failure up to 5 minutes (1 minute while open), never while the tab is hidden or the Room shows, and never two looks less than a second apart (a tab or a screen that comes and goes does not make a look each). The count on the button caps at "50+" and is hidden while the list is off. An answer that says it is over 2 MB is not read; rows are made again only when a room's host, map or seats changed (a press that straddles a look is not lost).
- **Games in progress.** The matches that run now stay in the list as rows under the open rooms (a box with the heading "In progress"), each with a red **Live** mark, the map's square and name, who plays (a dot in each player's color, a ring for a computer player as in the open rooms, and the names as the door writes them: a server names its bots like "Pip (bot, Hard)" or "Kit (bot, idle)", an older one "Bot (Medium)"; all are rings), how long it has run ("2:05 so far") and **Watch**. Watch is a link to `/play.html?live=<id>` ([`REPLAYS.md`](REPLAYS.md#the-match-pages)) and opens in a **new tab**, so the seat in a room that this page holds stays; there is no Join on a row in progress. They come from `GET /live` (the matches of 30 seconds or more, the newest 50, all of them shown, a match of computer players too, as on the Live now page): that door carries no room code (an id holds the map and the second the match began, nothing of the room), and `GET /lobbies` lists a room only while it waits and has a free seat, so a room that fills or starts is no longer a row of the open list and its match is its own row here, once it is 30 seconds old (a match of less than that is not in `/live` yet and shows when it is). No room has to be matched to a match, and a match whose room the list never had is a row like any other. There is no switch: the matches always show, and nothing about them is kept in this browser (a word that an earlier version stored, `ants.home.games.live`, is never read and left as it is). The heading says "3 open, 2 in progress" while the rows are shown (the other words as before when the door is not there); the **Games** button and its count are still the open games only, the ones that can be joined. It is one list: the open rooms first, then the box of matches directly under them. With no open room but matches in progress, there is no "No open games right now." card at all: the box of matches is the first thing under the heading, and the first match shows without a scroll. The empty card (that line, the line under it and the picture, with no buttons: the bar has Quick host and Quick join) shows only when there is literally nothing to list: no open room and no match in progress. With none in progress the box says "No games in progress.". `/live` is looked at every **10 seconds at the least** (10 to 12 s, doubled after each failure up to 5 minutes; a return to Home or a tab that shows again waits out that backoff too), once when the list shows and never faster than 10 s after the last look, only while the list is drawn (a docked list: Home on screen with a visible tab, and on a phone not while the Chat or Players view covers it; a popover: while it is open), never while the tab is hidden or the Room shows, and never after START reaches the page. A server with no live door (404), a failure, a look that does not end or an answer that is no list hides the box and says nothing: no notice, no row; the open rooms go on. The door sends the map as a whole file name ("TREASURE.LVL", "DRY LAND.lvl"), which is checked by the same rule as the map of an open room and shown the same way. A name or a map is set as text and never as markup; the only address made from the answer is the player's, from an id of the server's shape (and encoded).
- **START** puts the list away, ends its toast and stops its looks, the way it takes the bars' presses (the toast of the list is inert with them), and the switch of the Room cannot be pressed. A hand-off that is still here after 15 seconds leaves the page's room frozen (see "START while a guest sits on Home"), so **Quick host**, **Quick join** and **Games** stay off, each with the page's words as its title, the switch says them under itself, and the list asks for nothing and polls nothing until the page is reloaded; a part that loads after START is put in the same state.
- **Untrusted text.** Every field of a row is checked (the nine fields of the answer, their types and ranges; a name is printable ASCII, a map a file name, a code lower case), a row that does not fit is dropped, at most 50 rows are shown, and all of it is drawn with `textContent`: never markup, never a link.

## The Room

The room page as it was, with the logo row replaced by a slim bar: **Home** (the room stays open), the logo, **Leave room** (leaves it and shows Home; the next Quick host makes a new room; when other people are in the room it asks first, in the card of the other questions: "Leave your room?" with **Leave room** and **Stay here**, which has the focus, and for a guest "Leave Pebble's room?"; alone it leaves at once) and the Menu. The old header's buttons live in the Menu and are hidden. Below 340 px the picture of the logo is left out of the bar (the heading keeps its name).

## Addresses

|---|---|
| `/` | Home |
| `/#room` | the Room (My room, or the Home button, pushes it: Back goes to Home, Forward back to the Room) |
| `/?room=CODE` | the Room, behind the name card (a link opens the Room directly) |
| `/#room` after a reload, in a tab that holds a seat | the Room again; in a tab with none, Home |

A reload keeps the screen. The page writes nothing to the history until a button is pressed; the entry that it opened on is marked with its screen the first time a button leaves it, with its address unchanged.

## START while a guest sits on Home

The hidden Room still takes the hand-off: the key of the seat is written for the game page (`ants.rejoin.<room>.<seat>`), the tab goes to the game page, and Home shows **Opening the game…** at once. The hand-off never looks at the screen, and from then on both bars take no press (a Leave room would take the seat out from under the game that is about to take it over). A hand-off that is still here 15 seconds later has failed, was stopped or is only slow: the card goes and Home takes presses again, but this page's room stays as it was (a page on its way takes no message), so the toast says "The game is slow to open. Reload this page to get your seat back." with a **Reload** button (the page that comes up offers **Rejoin it**), and "The game did not open. ..." once a minute has gone since START (a page that is only slow is not told that it failed), and **Leave room** stays off: a Leave would end the START for everybody and leave a game page that is only slow with no seat to take over (a game that comes late takes the seat with the key).

## The chat dock

Home carries the site's global chat in a panel along the bottom of the body, under the games and the players, as wide as they are together: 160 to 260 px high (28% of the window), with its field and Send on one row under the log (who the visitor is, and Log out, are in the chip's dropdown in the bar, not here). The minus button folds it to its header, and it opens folded in a window under 601 px high or 700 px wide or less. On a phone it is the **Chat view** of the strip: open, filling the page between the bar and the strip, with no fold (`home.js` opens it with its view and folds it with the others). It is a part of Home's column, so nothing lies over it and it lies over nothing, and there is no "no room" state: the games take what the chat leaves. `chat.js` and `chat.css` load once, after the first paint, when Home is first shown (a link that opens the Room loads nothing until Home shows), and no other page loads them or opens a hub socket. The socket stays open while the Room shows (and [the players list](#the-players-list) speaks through it: the dock hands it the welcome, the frames of the list and the loss of the link, and writes what the list sends), so the **unread count** on the strip and the header goes on: the lines that came while the dock was folded, on the Room or in a hidden tab, not the visitor's own and not the history.

- **The log** shows `name: text`, the moderators' lines marked "mod", 100 lines at most (`del` takes one away). The name is the one that the author was shown by when it wrote the line (its Discord display name or its username, as it picked: [`HUB.md`](HUB.md#names)); a name is not unique, so a line is the visitor's own (the gold name, and not unread) by the key that the hub sends with it and with the welcome's `you`, and by its name only for a hub that sends no keys. A moderator's copy of a line also carries the author's username (nobody else is sent it); the page keeps it in its model and does not draw it: every line shows the name and the text, with the "mod" mark for a moderator's own lines. All of it is text from strangers: made with `textContent` only, never markup or a link, cut to 32 and 200 characters, the invisible characters that the hub removes taken out here too (a test keeps the two lists in step; a name loses its variation selectors as well), each name and line in a `<bdi>`; a frame that is too big, not JSON or of a kind that is not known is ignored. The log is `role="log"`, polite, and a flood is not read out line by line: two lines in 3 s are, then one summary ("8 more messages"), which goes when the next window of 3 s starts.
- **Who may write** is the hub's welcome (`docs/HUB.md`). Without `you` and with `login:false` the dock is read-only: "Chat is read-only for now", no field and no Send. With `login:true` and no `you` the field is off and says "Log in with Discord to chat", and a Log in button is where Send would be (as drawn). With `you` the field and Send are there, and the dock's login place shows who it is ([the login](#the-login)). A line goes out only on an open, welcomed socket, at the page's own pace (two at once, then one every 2.5 s; the hub's is three and 2 s), and every `err` code has its plain words (a line with `<` or `>` is not sent: "Lines cannot hold < or >"; a text that the hub accepted in the last 31 s is not sent again, as the hub remembers every one; a text that it refused is forgotten here, so it may be sent again: the hub answers in order, so the refused line is the oldest that has no answer yet, whatever was said since; a line is answered by its echo, which the hub composes (NFC) and may take characters out of, so an echo that is the line composed, or the line with characters taken out, answers the oldest line that it is part of; a moderator's command (`/mute ...`) waits in the same list as the lines and has no answer when the hub takes it: the hub says `bad` to a wrong one, so a `bad` is the oldest command's and any other refusal the oldest line's (a wrong command and a line sent together are told apart; when nothing of that kind waits, the oldest send of the other kind; a refusal of a command with another code, with a line that the hub took in flight, is told to that line), and a command is never put back in the field, as Enter would issue it again; a send that nobody answers is forgotten after 8 seconds). "7 here now" says "500+" over the hub's 500.
- **Offline.** A hub that is not there, slow or garbage is "Chat is offline" and "reconnecting…": a try after 1 s, doubling to 60 s, each wait spread from half to one and a half times (never under 1 s or over 60 s), so that pages that lost one hub do not come back in waves; a link must hold 30 s to start again at 1 s; a tab or a network that comes back tries at once. A ping every 25 s, and a hub that does not answer for 30 s is let go. A hidden tab keeps the socket and paints nothing. Nothing is kept in the browser, and nothing the chat does can block Home, the Room or START.

## Emoji and voice

Two small buttons stand between the chat's field and Send: an emoji button and, where the browser can listen, a microphone button. `talk.js` and `talk.css` load after the chat dock, once, when the dock is mounted (`home.js` asks for them with the part loader; a page whose dock never comes has neither button), and mount into the dock's own form. They are hidden with the field when nobody may write. Nothing is kept in the browser, and neither button sends anything: the person presses Send. Where the form is narrow (a phone up to 420 px wide, and windows 701 to 1000 px wide and at least 501 px high, where the account line takes the rest of the row) the two buttons take a row of their own under the field and Send, and there, while the dock is open, it is about 60 to 80 px taller (`:has`) so that the log keeps its room; a low window (a phone held sideways) keeps the buttons beside the field and the dock's own height, and a folded dock is as low as it was.

- **Emoji.** The button opens a picker that lies over the log, right above the form (72 to 180 px high, with a scroll of its own), so it never covers the field or Send (in a wide window it can reach over the bottom of the Games panel until it is closed): about ninety common emoji in five groups (Faces, Hands, Hearts and signs, Ants and nature, Games), written with the page's own code (no library, no address). The picker is a group of buttons: one key is in the tab order and the arrow keys, Home and End move between them; a pick goes in at the caret of the field or in place of the selection and the picker stays open, so several can be added; the button, Escape (which does not fold the chat) or a press outside closes it, and Escape puts the focus back in the field. The hub's text rules ([`HUB.md`](HUB.md#text-rules)) decide what the picker may send, so each one is a single code point that they keep as it is: a joiner is removed by the hub (a family would come out as three people), so there are no joined sequences; the tag characters of a flag of a country's part are removed too; a variation selector is kept but is a second code point. A line is 200 code points (and 800 bytes), while the field's own `maxlength` counts UTF-16 units (400): an emoji outside the basic plane is two units and one code point, so the picker counts code points, adds nothing past 200 ("The message is full.") and 200 of them are exactly the field's 400 units.
- **Voice.** The microphone button uses the browser's own speech recognition (`SpeechRecognition`, or `webkitSpeechRecognition` in Chrome, Edge and Safari). It is not made at all where the browser has none (Firefox) or where the page is not on a secure address. A press asks the browser to listen in the page's language (`navigator.language`): the words go into the field at the caret as they are heard, the interim words replaced by the final ones, with the text around the caret kept, and the person presses Send. A second press stops it (the last words still come in), and so do Escape, typing in the field, a send, a page that is hidden or goes away, and a field that is switched off. The state is said in a polite status line (the first press: "Your browser's speech service hears you. Allow the microphone if it asks.", then "Listening. Press the microphone again to stop. Then press Send.") and the button is a toggle (`aria-pressed`, a red look that does not move for people who ask for less motion). A refused microphone, no speech, no microphone, a service that cannot be reached or any other failure is a short plain line, never a dialog. Words from the speech service are text from outside: they go into the field as a value, the markup characters that the hub refuses are left out, and the line is cut at 200 code points ("The message is full.").
- **Privacy.** The browser may send the audio to its maker's speech service (Chrome, Edge and Safari do); this page receives no audio, records nothing and uploads nothing (the script has no `MediaRecorder` and no `getUserMedia`: the browser asks for the microphone itself), and the site keeps nothing of it. The button's tooltip and label say so ("Voice to text: your browser's speech service hears you"), and so does `web/legal.html`.

## The login

The login is the hub's ([`HUB.md`](HUB.md#login): Discord, the one scope `identify`); the page only shows it, and never sees a token or a cookie (the cookie is `HttpOnly`). It is there only where the welcome says `login:true`, so a hub without a Discord application shows none of it.

- **Logged out** (`login:true`, no `you`): the chip in the bar (the mark of Discord and "Log in"; the mark alone on a phone, held sideways or in a window under 1280 px wide, 46 px) and the dock's button (the field is off, "Log in with Discord to chat"). Either one is a full navigation to `/hub/login?next=home` (`next=room` from the Room): the tab goes to Discord and comes back to the same screen, as drawn. The two ways back are `/` and `/#room`, chosen by the hub from that one word.
- **Logged in** (`you`): the chip is a circle with the first letter and the name (on a phone held upright, up to 720 px wide, the name stands beside the circle in the bar's first row: it takes the room that the logo leaves and is cut with an ellipsis where it does not fit, at 320 px as at 412, and the logo, the chip and the Menu stay one row; the circle alone in a window under 1280 px wide and on a phone held sideways), and the chip is a button with a dropdown (`aria-haspopup`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`): a press opens `#gchat-login-slot`, a box over the page under the chip, to its right edge, no taller than what is left of the window (it scrolls inside itself, so Log out is reachable on a phone held sideways), and puts the focus on Log out; a second press, a press anywhere else, Escape (wherever the focus is; the focus goes back to the chip) and the Room close it, and when it goes under the focus (Log out, Forget me, a name that is picked) the focus goes to the chip. It stands right after the chip in the bar, so the Tab order follows and the bar's inert covers it. It does not show the Chat view of a phone and does not open or shut the dock. In it: "Chatting as <name>", the choice of the name (below), **Log out**, and **Forget me** (a quiet underlined control, not a button, with a sentence of what it does: the first press only asks, "Are you sure? Press again"; the second, after a moment (a double press counts once), sends the POST; a press elsewhere, Escape and the dropdown going away put the question back); the chat panel itself has no account line, so its field and Send have the whole row. Both are a `fetch` POST of this page to the hub (`/hub/logout`, `/hub/forget`: same origin, with its cookie, no redirect, one at a time); after either the dock drops its socket and connects again at once, as a reader. A POST that does not work says "That did not work, try again" and changes nothing. The name is text (cleaned and cut like a line's), and nothing of this is kept in the browser.
- **The two names** (an account that has a Discord display name as well as its username; [`HUB.md`](HUB.md#names)). The chat shows the display name unless the person picks the username. The chip's dropdown then has, above Log out, a sentence that says which one the chat shows ("Chat shows your display name.") and one button that offers the other, with its name ("Show my username: maple.leaf"). It appears only where the hub's welcome says that the account can choose (`pick` is one of the two words and `other` is a name), so a hub that says nothing of it, an account with no display name, a reader and a hub with no login have no such button. A press sends the one message `{"t":"pick","pick":...}` over the socket that is open (nothing else: no request, no address) and changes nothing on the screen by itself: the hub stores the choice and closes the sockets of the account, the dock says "Chat is offline" for a moment and connects again by its usual wait, and the new welcome names the account by the other name; the dropdown stays open, now offering the first. A switch that the hub refuses (too many in a short time) says "That did not work, try again", and the socket stays. Lines that were written keep the name they were written with, and are still the visitor's own, by the key. The words are in `TEXT` (`shows`, `names`, `use`).
- **The round trip and a room.** The tab leaves for Discord and comes back: the session storage that holds the seat of the room (`ants.lobby`) belongs to the tab and survives it, so the page opens, takes the seat back in the background as after any reload, and shows Home (or the Room, for `next=room`; a link `?room=CODE` also works). The server holds the seat of a dropped page for its usual window, so a login that takes longer than that (a first visit that reads Discord's page for a minute) finds the seat gone, like any visitor who was away that long: the page then offers the room's usual Rejoin or makes no room, and Home is as it was. `tests/scripts/web_home_login_browser.js` presses it through in a real browser against a real game server.
