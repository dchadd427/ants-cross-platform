# Home and the Room: the front page, with the room inside it

The front page (`web/lobby.html`, served at `/`) is one page with one WebSocket and one screen, **Home**: a top bar over the games, the players and the chat. The **Room**, the room page that [Play in the browser](PLAY_IN_BROWSER.md#your-room) describes, is inside it: while the page shows its room, the room takes the Games list's place in Home's body, and the bar, the players and the chat box stay. `<body data-screen="home">` or `data-screen="room"` says which of the two the body shows. The page's client of the game server (`web/front/lobby_net.js`), its model and its session storage are the same for both, so going from the games to the room and back is a class and one render: instant, no request, and the seat is never dropped. Everything new is in `web/home/` (`home.js`, `home.css`, the chat dock's `chat.js`, `chat.css`, the Games list's `games.js`, `games.css`, the players list's `players.js`, `players.css`, the room's own `room.css` and the room's tab of the chat box, `roomchat.js`, `roomchat.css`; the parts that are not the bar load after the first paint), because `web/front/` is at its size cap and the page at its line limit.

## Home

- **The top bar:** no logo (the page's name is a heading for a screen reader, drawn nowhere), **Quick host**, **Quick join**, **Match history** (a link to the list of matches, `/matches`; it opens in a new tab while the page holds a room, like the other links to the matches, so that the room's tab stays connected), and at the right the login chip, [the bell](#alerts) and the Menu button (the chip is hidden until the chat's hub is there and offers a login: [the login](HOME_CHAT.md#the-login) below; nothing on the bar moves when it comes: the Menu stands at the right edge from the first frame, with or without the chip, and Quick host and Quick join keep their size). **There is no Games button on the bar**: Home shows the list of open games, as a panel at the left of a wide window and as the Games view of a phone's strip, with the count of open games and games in progress together on it (while the room shows it takes the panel's place: [The Room](#the-room); the bar is the same one, with no second bar for the room), so a desktop user has nothing to open and loses nothing. **There is no button for the room either**: a room is made by Quick host (and Quick join joins one), Quick host shows the room again while the page holds one (and changes nothing), and while the page holds a room there is no Games view at all: the Menu's **Home**, Back and a reload lead to the room, and Leave is the way out. Quick host and Quick join are lit by `games.js` when it has loaded (until then they are drawn and disabled); Match history is a plain link and works from the first frame. **Nothing on either bar is ever cut off or pushed past the window's edge, at any width from 320 px up** (`tests/scripts/web_home_games_browser.js` checks Home and the Room at every tenth width and at both sides of every limit). The bar trims in steps as the window narrows (`home.css`): from 1280 px the chip shows its name, a step below 1077 px shrinks the gaps, the padding and the type, below 892 px it is one low row with no icons (a phone held sideways, 640 px wide and more, has it too), below 775 px the type shrinks once more; a phone held upright (up to 720 px wide, taller than wide) has **two rows of 44 px**: Match history, the login chip and the Menu on the first, Quick host and Quick join on the second (the type is a step smaller, the chip's name is cut with an ellipsis where the row is short, and under 360 px the chip is the badge alone), and **one row** while the page holds a room (Quick host and Quick join are for people with no room: [Tiles](#the-room)); under 640 px any other window (wider than high) has the chip and the Menu at the right of the first row, Quick host and Quick join on the second and Match history on a third; a phone held sideways 360 to 639 px wide has **two compact rows**: Quick host, Quick join, the chip and the Menu on the first and Match history on the second. Every target keeps 44 px. The players list has no button on the bar: it is a column of Home's body in a wide window and the Players view of a phone ([the players list](#the-players-list) below). The name chip is in the budget above.
- **The page's height:** Home is exactly as tall as the window shows, less `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` as padding: a phone whose page reaches under the system bar (`viewport-fit=cover`) would otherwise put the bottom of the body, the chat panel and the phone's strip under it. The bar, the body, the chat panel and the strip always lie inside the visible window (`web_home_games_browser.js`, part 1i). No single unit is trusted, because Home hides what overflows and a window that is shorter than its units say (an installed window on a phone, with its own status bar) would cut the strip off with no way to scroll to it. In `home.css` the old two lines (`100vh`, then `100dvh`) stay as they were, and what is new is inside `@supports`, so that a browser with no `dvh` keeps them (a declaration with `var()` is accepted at read time and would compute to `auto` there): in a browser tab the height is `var(--home-h, 100dvh)`; in an installed window (`display-mode` standalone, fullscreen or minimal-ui) it is the smallest of `100dvh`, the page's percentage chain (`html` and `body` at `100%`), `100svh` and `--home-h`. A tab keeps `100dvh` because there the root's `100%` and `clientHeight` stay at the small window when the address bar hides. `home.js` (`heightGuard`) sets `--home-h` on `<html>` only while Home, measured with no limit, is taller than the smallest of `window.innerHeight`, `visualViewport.height` and, in an installed window only, the root's `clientHeight` by more than 1 px, and takes it away when the box fits again. While the page is pinch-zoomed (visual viewport scale not 1) `innerHeight` and the visual viewport count for nothing: both can follow the pinch. It looks on load, on `resize` of the window and of the visual viewport, on `orientationchange` (and 300 ms later: a turn reports its size late), 350 ms after a field loses the focus, and when Home is shown. It does nothing while a text field has the focus (the keyboard takes its room and the page slides under it, as it did), and a window under two thirds of the box's height is taken for a keyboard or a split window, not for a page that is too tall: no limit. The Menu's box uses the smaller of `100dvh` and `100svh` in an installed window too. The game page and the map list's sheet use the smaller of `100dvh` and `100svh` in an installed window (`web/shell.html`: the picture's width and the replay bar; `web/front/map_picker.css`), and the page's own fullscreen is the box between the four edges, with no unit.
- **The room that the page holds** is a room that Quick host (Public or Invite only) or Quick join made or joined, or a seat that the tab takes back in the background. **A plain visit makes no room.** A tab that holds a seat takes it back in the background, so the page still holds the room when it shows Home. Leaving the room is the **Leave** button of the room's header: one Leave message to the game server, the saved seat forgotten, and the room off the Games list once nobody is in it (when the host leaves a room with other people in it, the server's own rule hands the room on to the one who has been there longest). A room with no other person in it (computer players do not count) is left at once, with no question; with another person in it, or as a guest, the card of the other questions asks first ("Leave your room?", Leave room and Stay here; Stay here, Escape and a press outside the card keep the room). Afterwards a short notice says "You left the room." and the focus goes to Quick host. The button is off while a started game is on its way. `tests/scripts/web_home_games_quickjoin_check.js` (part 8) checks what it does.
- **The Menu**: Home (the first item, marked as the page you are on; while the room shows it brings the Games list back), Matches, Map maker, How it works, the Discord server, **Alerts** (the sheet of [Alerts](#alerts)), then the small links of the footer (Sprites and sounds, What’s new, GitHub, Feedback, Legal), the live numbers and the version. It is at most as tall as the window leaves under the bar and scrolls inside itself (a phone held sideways is about 360 px high). The footer is not drawn on Home. **How it works** is a dialog like the map list: while it is open the page behind it (both bars, the Room, the strips, the footer, the chat dock and the list's toast) takes no key or tap.
- **The body** (`#hmain`) is the area under the bar, a grid: **the Games list** at the left (the same list as the popover below: one code, `games.js` paints the rows, polls `GET /lobbies` and runs Join, Join by code, Quick join; shown unless the room is, a panel whose heading stays while the cards scroll inside it) or, in its place while the page shows its room, **the room** ([The Room](#the-room)), **the players list** in a narrow column at the right (220 to 280 px, 24% of the window; [the players list](#the-players-list)), and **the chat dock** as a panel along the bottom, as wide as the two and the gap between them ([the chat dock](HOME_CHAT.md#the-chat-dock)). Each open room is a **card**: the map's small picture, the host, the map's name, the seats as four colored dots with how many are taken, the bots, **Join**, and the **Yours** and **BEST** tags as before (a card is 290 px wide at least, so the cards fill the panel in as many columns as fit: three at 1440 px). With no open room the panel says "No open games right now." and shows that line with "Rooms show up here when their host switches them to Public." and one button, Host a game (it does what Quick host in the bar does), and Join by code under it. Press **Games** in the bar and the focus goes to the list (there is no second copy); its count is the same. A **phone** (a window up to 700 px wide or up to 500 px high: a phone held upright, and a phone held sideways at any width, 844 x 390 and 932 x 430 too) has one of the three at a time and a **strip** along the bottom (60 px high when the phone is upright: 44 px buttons in 15 px type; 54 px held sideways), **Games** (it says **Room**, with the number of people in the room, while the page shows its room), **Players** and **Chat**, with the number of open games and of people beside the first two: the view shown is the one pressed (Games at first), the Games button of the bar shows the Games view, the strip shows a count of three digits or more as 99+, the login chip shows the Chat view, the strip is inert with the bars during the hand-off to the game, and the Players tab is not there until the list is (a hub that has none). The chat is open only on its own view (`home.js` opens it with the view and folds it with the others, so what comes while it is away counts as unread). A window of 701 px wide and 501 px high or more shows all three and ignores the strip; a window that changes from one to the other opens or folds the chat for the new one. Where the window is **short** the games keep the room: the chat dock opens folded to its header under 601 px high (a press opens it; it is a panel of 160 to 260 px, 28% of the window high, when open), and the legend, the label of Join by code and the guest's line of the chat are left out under 701 px high. **The Yard has no place in this layout yet**: Home has no area for it, no stand-in and no label that the page reads.
- **The strips** (the Rejoin button, "Back from the match") stand above both screens; Home is as tall as the window, strips included, so it never scrolls.

## The players list

`players.js` and `players.css` load after the chat dock, once, when the dock is mounted (`home.js` asks for them with the loader of the Games list; a page whose dock never comes has no list), and speak through the dock's socket: the page keeps one socket to the hub, and the list adds no address of its own. Nothing is kept in the browser. It is the hub's list of who has the front page open ([`HUB.md`](HUB.md#presence): the names are the chat's, a guest is counted and never named, nothing is stored); this page only draws it.

- **What it shows.** A header, **Players**, with the count (everybody: the visitor and the guests too, so it is the number that the chat's "here now" says, "500+" over 500; one person alone is no number; the Players tab of a phone's strip says "99+" over 99), then the visitor first as **you** (the gold frame, the first letter in a circle, where the page itself says they are, not where the hub last heard; the visitor's row in the hub's list is the one that the welcome names, `me`, and is left out of the groups, so another person with the same name is not), a divider, and the groups **Staff** (the administrators, then the moderators), **Looking**, **Waiting** and **Playing** with their counts, each person by name (a moderator's name in yellow and an administrator's in red, administrators first, with the role also in the accessible name; each is told where under the name, as the visitor is, and the visitor's own row follows a live change of the role at once), and last **+ N guests, not logged in**. Under a name: nothing for a person who looks; a room that waits by its map and the colors that play, "Treasure . 2 of 4", or "In a room" where the hub says nothing of it (it keeps no details of a private room, nor of the room of an account that may not chat, so the page does not call it private); a match by its map and its players, "Islands . 3 players", or "In a game"; the visitor's own row, which knows its room, says "Private room" or "Private game" for a private one. For a room that the Games list shows (its code) a map's name, an original's or a map of the players', is drawn only if the list shows the same name; for a room that it does not show, an original's name is the page's own table's (one of six) and a map of the players' is not drawn; otherwise the line has the seats and no name, because what a person says about a room is text from a stranger. A dot in the circle has the color of the group. Where a public room waits **and the Games list has that room** (its code) there is a **Join**: the Games list's own Join of that room is pressed (its questions, its notices, its refusals; a list that is a popover, as on the test site, is opened for it; a room that the list does not hold says "That game is not open any more."), and none on the visitor's own room. Past 200 named people the list ends with "and N more". Every name, map and number is checked as text from strangers, as the chat's lines are, and drawn with `textContent`.
- **Moderation and the person's sheet.** A row opens the person's sheet (a right click, a long press, the menu key): for anybody who is logged in it has Message, Follow, Ignore and Report and, with a room, Invite; for staff the moderation too: [the sheet](HOME_CHAT.md#private-messages-and-follows) has what it does and what the list gives it.
- **Follows.** A visitor who is logged in has two **tabs** under the header: **Everyone** (the list above, with a small gold **star** after the name of each person that they follow) and **Following** (the people that they follow, by what they do now: **Waiting** (a public room with Join, as in the list), **Playing**, **Looking**, then **Not here**, dim, for a person who is not in the list or is away). The tabs say the count of each. Following with nobody in it says "You follow nobody yet" and how to follow; a line at the bottom says that only the visitor sees the list and that nobody is told. A card of a person who is not here opens the same sheet (Message, Following, Ignore, Report); the hub's handle names the person, never a row. The hub's `follows` frame feeds all of it ([Follows](HUB_PEOPLE.md#follows)); a guest has no tabs, and the tab is not kept in the browser.
- **Away.** A person whose last socket closed a short while ago (the hub keeps the row for ten minutes: [`HUB.md`](HUB.md#presence)) is a row with `at` `away`: a name and the word. The page draws it dimmed in a group of its own, **Away**, last (a moderator who is away stays under **Staff**, dimmed, with "Away" under the name, as a moderator who looks is told "Looking"), with no Join and nothing about a room, whatever else the frame holds; a row that is back is Looking again. A page without this code leaves an `away` row out, so it keeps what it last drew for that person until the hub drops the number.
- **Keeping the link.** The page does not make a hidden page prove that the link lives: while the page is hidden it sends no ping, runs no timer loop (the ping timer and the Players list's one-second look stop, and start again when the page is shown) and does not call a missed answer lost (the hub's own PING frames, which the browser answers whatever its timers do, keep the socket); a late answer on a hidden page gets a moment more (5 s). When the page is back (it becomes visible, resumes from a freeze, is shown again from the back-forward cache, or the network returns while it is shown) it asks the hub at once and waits 5 s for the answer, so a socket that died while the page slept is let go quickly; a socket that the browser closed meanwhile is opened again with no backoff wait, once, and the page says again where its visitor is. The first show of a normal load is not a wake. While the socket is open the page holds a shared Web Lock, where the browser has locks, and lets it go when the socket closes; a held lock may make a page not eligible for the browser's back-forward cache (not measured here).
- **Pictures.** A row that carries `pic` shows that picture in its 32 px circle, the visitor's own card too (from the chip's `you`); the list is made again at every change (the whole of it, not only the row that changed), with no flicker: a picture that is already loaded comes from the browser's own copy, with no new request and no frame that shows the letter, and a row that gains, changes or loses a picture is drawn with it or with its letter. The circle keeps its letter and its ring (red for an administrator, yellow for a moderator) and the status dot stays on top; see [the login](HOME_CHAT.md#the-login) for how the picture and its switch work.
- **Where.** The panel is a column of Home's body at the right of the Games list, **220 to 280 px** wide (`clamp(220px, 24vw, 280px)` in `home.css`, only once the hub has sent its list: until then the Games list has the whole body), as high as the Games list and starting on its line; the list inside it scrolls. The panel has no fold button and no folded state: it is always open (the chat's own fold button is the chat's). A phone's window (up to 700 px wide or up to 500 px high) shows it as the **Players view** of the strip, between the bar and the strip. There is no sheet, no drawer, no button on the bar and no scrim. The panel is inert with the bars during the hand-off to the game and under a dialog of the page.
- **When there is nothing to show.** The list shows nothing until the hub has sent it (no flash of "just you"), and for a hub that has no list (an older one: its welcome does not say so) nothing at all, and Home is the same as without the two files (a real browser finds nothing but a few pixels at the edges of rounded buttons, which it draws either way). A hub that is not there (the chat says "Chat is offline") is **Players list is off right now**, with a grey dot instead of the count, in the panel; the list comes back by itself with the chat. Nobody else here: just the visitor's own row, and nothing under it (no note, no button).
- **What the page tells the hub.** Only a visitor who is named says anything: where they are, from the page's own room (no room: looking; a room that has not started: waiting; a match, or a page that is on its way to one: playing), a public room's map and seats (and its code, if the visitor is the host: the Join is on the host's row), a private room only that it is private (`pub`:false), at most every 3 seconds, and again at once on a new socket. The list is asked for once for each socket. A guest asks and says nothing. The page looks at its room once a second, so the visitor's own row follows a change at once and the hub is told within 4 seconds. While the visitor is in a room the page also keeps a small note in this tab (`ants.here` in the tab's session storage: whether the visitor is logged in, a hash that only tells which room the note is for, whether the room is public and, for a public room, its map and seats; never the room's code or a name), so that the game page knows what it may say. The note is gone when the tab has no room. If the hub's row for this account says Playing (another tab is in a match), the visitor's own card says Playing too.
- **Who is playing.** The game page (`web/hub_here.js`) opens a small socket of its own to the same door when its visitor is in a match of a room or in a game against the computer, and never for a recording, a live match, a frame of another page or a test map. It says only `here`: `play` with the note's details (a public room: map and seats; a private room or match: only that it is private; a game against the computer: `solo` and the map, for an original map only), and `wait` when the match is over (the results screen holds a seat in the room's lobby: no seats and no code). A match opened from a shared link has no note: the page reads the public Games list (`GET /lobbies`) once, and a room that is in it is public with the list's own map and seats, one that is not is private. A guest is no row: the note says so and no socket is opened, or the welcome says so and the socket is closed and never tried again; a `bye` stops it too, and a close or an error is tried again with the chat dock's wait (a second at first, longer each time, a minute at the most, with some jitter). The socket reads nothing but `welcome`, `bye` and `err`, draws nothing and logs nothing, and the game never waits for it. The list then shows the person under Playing: "Islands · 3 players" for a public match, "Private game" with a small lock for a private one (and "Private room" while it waits), "Treasure · vs computer" for a game against the computer on an original map and "vs computer" on any other, and "In a game" for an account that may not chat (its details are not kept). A private room or match has no Join and no way in from the list. Where the hub may not have heard yet (the front page's socket closes a moment before the game page's opens), a person can be missing from the list for one send. Nothing is kept past the socket: a closed game tab is out of the list one send later.

## The splitters

`split.js` and `split.css` load after the first paint (the loader of the Games list) and give a **wide, tall** window (701 px wide and 501 px high or more) two drag handles; a phone has the strip of views and no handle, and a part that does not come leaves Home as it was.

- **Where.** One stands in the gap between the Games list and the players list (it shows only while the players list does), one along the top of the chat dock (only while the dock is open). Each is a thin line that is brighter under the pointer, with the keyboard focus and while it is dragged; the target around the line is about 30 px, drawn over the edge of the panels, so the layout takes no room from it. The pointer is a resize cursor, no text is selected while dragging, and in forced colors the line is drawn in the system colors.
- **How.** Mouse, pen or touch (pointer events, the pointer is captured). With the keyboard each handle is a stop of Tab and a `separator` (`aria-orientation`, `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`, `aria-controls`): the arrow keys move it 16 px (Shift 64 px; Left or Up makes the players list or the chat bigger), Home and End go to the least and the most, Enter or a double click puts the size back to the first one.
- **How far.** The Games list and the players list keep 240 px each at least; the chat is 120 px high at least and 70% of the body (the window under the bar) at most. Until a handle is used the sizes are the ones above (`clamp(220px, 24vw, 280px)`; the dock's own height in `chat.css` and `talk.css`).
- **What is kept.** The two sizes are kept in this browser only (`ants.home.split.v1`, in `localStorage`; every read and write is guarded, so a browser that refuses keeps them for the visit) and put on Home as soon as the script comes, before the lists. A smaller window clamps them (the size that was asked for comes back when the window grows again); a window that turns into a phone's width drops them and clears what was kept. They are two properties of Home's div, `--pl-w` and `--gc-h`; the rule for the chat's height has the id of Home in it, so it wins over the dock's own height.
- **The Room** has no handle between it and the players list (the handle there is not drawn while the room shows: the room has its own, narrower list of players); the one along the chat's top is the same.

`tests/scripts/test_web_home_split.py` and `web_home_split_check.js` run it in node (the limits, the keys, a drag, a storage that throws, a window that changes, a phone, the budgets, the loader) and `tests/data/home_split_mutants.json` checks that the checks fail when one thing changes; `tests/scripts/web_home_split_browser.js` (opt-in, Chromium) drags the handles with a real mouse.

## The Room

The room page (`#lobby` in `web/lobby.html`, laid out by `web/home/room.css`) is a panel of Home's body. While the page holds a room (a link was opened, Quick host or Quick join made the room, a reload took the seat back), the panel takes the place of the Games list and the Games list is not drawn; the bar, the players list and the chat box stay where they are. There is no second bar: **Leave** is a red button in the room's own header and the bar's Menu is the only Menu. Leaving the room brings the Games list back; nothing else does while the page holds the room (the Menu's **Home** and Back show the room, see [the addresses](#addresses)). The room is drawn as the approved pictures draw it, with far fewer words and boxes than it had; every function it had is still there.

- **The header** is one row: the room's name (and the game type's tag, "187", beside it), then at the right **Share** (the room's code is on the button, "k7m 2xq"; it copies the link and says "Copied!" for a moment, and on a phone it opens the browser's share sheet), **Rules** (it says who can join: "Public" or "Invite only", and "Fog" after it while fog is on) and **Leave**. There is no field with the link and no line about it. On a phone upright the name and the tag are not drawn: the one row is Share, Rules and Leave ([Tiles](#the-room)); held sideways in a narrow window the header is two rows, the name and Leave, then Share and Rules.
- **The Rules sheet** (`web/front/room_sheet.js`, `room_sheet.css`) is a card in the middle of the page, a sheet from the bottom on a phone, opened by Rules and closed by Done, the cross, Escape or a press on the dark ground; the page behind it takes no press and the focus goes back to Rules. It has one row for each rule and one line of what it does under each: **Who can join** (Invite only | Public: [`HOME_GAMES.md`](HOME_GAMES.md)), **Fog of War** ([`PLAY_MATCH_SETUP.md`](PLAY_MATCH_SETUP.md#fog-of-war)), **Direct connection** ([`P2P_HINTS.md`](P2P_HINTS.md); each player's own switch) and, last, the note that online matches are recorded. A guest reads the first two (the switches show the room's state and take no press) and has the third as their own.
- **The body** is two columns (on a phone upright one column with tiles: below). At the left the four colors as four slim rows, each with the dots (drag a player onto another color; a tap on the dots and then on a color does the same), the ant, the color's name, and the person: their name with **You** and **Host** tags and, for the host, a small **Remove** button on another person; an open color or a computer player is a button with its choice ("Open", "Hard bot"): one press opens a short menu (`web/front/seat_menu.js`): Open, Easy bot, Medium bot, Hard bot, Closed (the three bots are dim while Fog of War is on, and say why when pressed). At the right end of a row stand the Team buttons "1" and "2" when three or four play (a guest reads a "Team 1" tag) and the **Ready** mark ([Ready up](READY_UP.md)). At the right, in one column: the map (its picture, its name with its kind beside it, its minutes, **Change map**), the game type (a field for the host, a read-only line for a guest) and the big button with one line under it: **START!** for the host, **READY** for a guest ([Ready up](READY_UP.md)). The line says what matters most now: who is in (and that open colors stay empty), whose Ready START waits for, who is not back from a match, a refused team, a color that was tapped, or, for a guest, who starts.
- **It fits.** At 1920 x 1080, 1600 x 900, 1366 x 768 and 1280 x 800 the page does not scroll and the room's panel does not either: the room is only as tall as its rows need (about 300 px at 1280 x 800) and the chat box takes the rest of the window. A window that is lower than the room needs (below about 760 px of page height: a laptop with its toolbars) keeps the page still and scrolls the room's panel inside itself, with START pinned at the panel's bottom. The players list is narrower while the room shows (200 to 250 px) and as tall as the room.
- **Targets.** With a mouse the small controls are small, as the pictures draw them (the Team buttons 30 px, a color's choice 32, Share, Rules and Leave 36, Remove 26). ## Dialogs that cover the window

A dialog that dims the page and covers the window is closed by a press on the dimmed page around it, and that press does exactly what the dialog's Cancel (or its Escape) does: the same function, the focus goes back to what opened it, and a choice is never picked or sent. The press has to start and end on the dimmed page: a drag that began in the box, a text selection and a scroll gesture (a touch that the browser takes as a scroll) do not close it, and neither does a click that no press began (a key or a script). The dialogs: the cards of Home and the Room (Host a game, the questions before leaving a room, Join by code's confirms: `web/home/games.js`), How it works and the map list of the Room (`web/lobby.html`, `web/front/map_picker.js`), the settings menu and its Leave game question (`web/game_menu.js`), the game page's Reload question (`web/game_bar.js`), the map maker's dialogs (`web/front/mapmaker_dialog.js`), the test game's Leave question (`web/front/test_game.js`) and the admin page's boxes (`web/admin_page.js`). Left as they are: the name step before a room or a match (it has no Cancel; its way back is a link), the card of a replay that cannot be played (nothing to cancel), the loading screen, and the box that shows a new password once (only its Done button or Escape closes it, so that a stray press cannot lose the one sight of the password). The drop-down of the game type needs no handler: a press anywhere outside it already closes it.

## The screen numbers

The address `/?vp` loads `web/home/vp.js` (only for that address: a plain visit asks for nothing) and shows a small box of plain text over Home with the numbers that the page's height depends on: the display mode, `innerWidth x innerHeight`, `outerWidth x outerHeight`, the root's `clientHeight`, the visual viewport's size, offset and scale, `screen` and its available size, the pixel ratio, the measured heights of `100vh`, `100dvh`, `100svh` and `100lvh` (hidden probe boxes), the four safe-area insets, Home's own top and bottom with the limit if the guard set one, and the Chrome and Android version numbers of the browser's name. It is text only (`textContent`), makes no request of its own, keeps nothing, and a tap puts it away. The installed app has no address bar, so the manifest has a fourth shortcut, **Screen numbers**, which opens `/?vp` (press and hold the app's icon).

## START

The Room takes the hand-off: the key of the seat is written for the game page (`ants.rejoin.<room>.<seat>`), the tab goes to the game page, and the Room's own cards say **Opening the game…**. From then on both bars take no press (a Leave room would take the seat out from under the game that is about to take it over). A hand-off that is still here 15 seconds later has failed, was stopped or is only slow: the bars take presses again, but this page's room stays as it was (a page on its way takes no message), so the toast says "The game is slow to open. Reload this page to get your seat back." with a **Reload** button (the page that comes up offers **Rejoin it**), and "The game did not open. ..." once a minute has gone since START (a page that is only slow is not told that it failed), and **Leave room** stays off: a Leave would end the START for everybody and leave a game page that is only slow with no seat to take over (a game that comes late takes the seat with the key).
