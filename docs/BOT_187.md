# The computer players in 187: the brawl

How a standard bot plays the game type 187 ([GAMEPLAY.md](GAMEPLAY.md#187-a-game-type-of-the-remake): no food and no power-up, the kills are the score, the last side with ants wins). The bots in general are in [BOTS.md](BOTS.md).

**The hill is closed in 187 now** ([GAMEPLAY.md](GAMEPLAY.md#187-a-game-type-of-the-remake), simulation rules number 7, 10 since the fire wall of a map file loads as the original's): no ant enters its hill, so there is nothing to heal in and the brawl **sends no ant home**. The sections and the measurements below were made under the earlier rule (rules number 6, the hill works, an ant at 1 hp walks home) and say so where it matters; they were not made again for the closed hill, and what they say about hurt ants going home or hiding in a hill no longer happens in a match that is played now. A standard bot of any level plays **`BrawlTask`** in 187 instead of every task of [BOTS.md](BOTS.md#the-standard-bot). `StandardBot::think` reads the room's mode from the engine it plays in (`BotView::game_mode()`; every client of a room knows it) and hands the whole look to the brawl. The profile (look interval, reaction time, command budget), the accept rule for an invitation and the rules of fairness are the level's as always; a match of the original's game never reaches the code (AI30.3: the same orders to the tick with the switch on and off; `bot_arena` reports of the original's game are byte for byte those of before). `LevelPlan::brawl187` is the switch, and `bot_arena --tune brawl=0` is the bot before: it plays the food game's tasks in 187 too, which have no food to work on (it strikes back at a blow and hunts what is hurt, and nothing else). There is no power-up in 187, so there is none to take.

## What the engine allows

Measured with the bot's own orders and in hand-made worlds; the times are Hard's (looks 4 ticks apart, orders leave 8 ticks after the look).

- **One blow lands per hit clip**: about every 40 ticks on one victim, however many ants strike it. A worker's blow takes 1 hit point and throws the victim one tile away from the attacker, a Combat Ant's punch takes 2 and throws it four. The victim stays in its hit clip for about 15 ticks after it landed, and an attacker that reaches a victim in its clip is refused ("can't do that") and stops.
- **An attack order names a tile**: the ant that stands there when the order arrives is the target. A victim that was struck lands on another tile, so the next blow needs an order that names the new tile, and an ant that walks has moved on before the attacker arrives: it is not caught by an order.
- **An ant left with one hit point walks home** (under the earlier rule only: in 187 now it stays where it is) at the speed of its hunters ([BOT_TACTICS.md](BOT_TACTICS.md#fights-of-its-own-health-the-kill-and-the-skirmish-contest-batch)) and is caught only when a hunter is beside it as its walk begins, or when its way is blocked. In a lab of one hunter and one victim with two hit points the second blow killed it from two of the eight sides, from different sides at different places of the map: no side works everywhere.
- **Water kills whatever the hit points**: a victim thrown into water drowns, onto a bomb it loses 2, onto a fire wall 1. In 412 deaths of two Hard brawl bots on 45 maps (earlier rule: the hurt healed, so 1 hp deaths were fewer than now), 40 percent were drownings, 42 percent the last hit point of a plain ant, 9 percent Combat punches (maps whose ants are Combat Ants) and 9 percent bombs and fire of the map.
- **222 of the 540 maps of the library (41 percent) join the hills of seats 0 and 1 by no walk** (walls of fire and bombs, water, islands). On most of them nobody can be struck and a match ends at the clock without a kill.

## What the brawl does

At every look:

1. Under the earlier rule an ant with 3 hit points or fewer (`retreat_hp`) goes home to heal (the hill restores all hit points) and fights again with 9 or more. **In 187 now (`BotView::hill_enterable()` is false) nothing of this happens**: no ant is sent home, no `Home` job exists, and a hurt ant is ranked, packed and ordered like any other (the tune keys `bretreat` and `breturn` then do nothing). As a second line of defense the controller's filter refuses any click of the seat onto its own hill when the hill is closed (`BotController::allowed`, counted in `SeatStats::filtered`), so no task, the food game's included, can spend the command budget on an order the engine would refuse.
2. The enemy ants that can be struck (`attackable`: not an ally's, none on a hill tile or in water) and that some ant of the bot can reach by a walk (the walker components of the start; on a level of Swimmers or Fire Ants the question is not asked) are ranked: the ants of the seat that the ring names first ("Who is struck first"), then the wounded (a hit point counts three tiles), then the near. An enemy that walks is only an object once it is within two tiles of an ant.
3. Each victim gets a **pack** of the nearest ready ants, at most 2 (`per_target`; one for a side of 4 living ants or fewer, `solo_ants`); ants without a victim of their own join the nearest fight, 3 on a victim at most (`crowd`). Blows land in parallel on different victims, and the third ant finishes. A pair that was refused twice within 160 ticks ("can't go" with no hit clip of the victim to explain it) rests 300 ticks, so a fenced-in enemy draws a few orders and not a flood.
4. A pack is ordered onto its victim at once when an ant joined, and again (no sooner than 8 ticks later) when the victim was thrown to another tile or an ant of the pack stands idle after its blow; one command names the whole pack. A victim with one hit point that walks (under the earlier rule home, in 187 now wherever its own bot sends it) is ordered onto again only by a pack with an ant within two tiles of it: with a lead it is not caught (AI30.17).
5. When no enemy ant can be struck (every one is on a hill tile) the idle ants march to the door of the nearest enemy hill that a walk reaches, never an ally's, and wait there. When enemy ants are in sight but none can be reached, nobody moves.

## Who is struck first

Two rules decide which enemy ant a brawl bot goes for and how many ants go for it. They were measured with four Hard bots against each other on the six maps that ship, in 187 under the closed hill (`bot_arena --game-mode 187 --replay-check`, seeds 1 to 100 unless said, every match played again from its commands without a bot). A win is the engine's rule: the last side with ants, else the most kills at the clock; a tie counts for nobody.

**Why.** The nearest enemy ant by tiles, a tie to the lowest ant id, picks the same seat for every bot, and a seat that no bot picks first wins. The first attack order of each bot, the same in all 100 seeds: on TINY seat 0 is the first victim of all three other bots, seats 1 and 3 of none; on MEDIUM seat 0 is named by nobody and seat 1 by two; on TREASURE seats 1 and 2 by nobody and seats 0 and 3 by two each. The wins follow the seats that nobody names (the table below).

**The ring.** From the hills alone (`MapInfo`: the walking distance between each two) every bot works out the same ring of the seats, the shortest way round all of them (of two ways that are equally long, the one first in the order of the seat numbers: `BrawlTask::ring_of`). A seat names the next seat of its ring first, and the ants of that seat count 100 tiles nearer (`ring_bonus` 1000 points, 10 to a tile): a first choice that is given up only for ants that cannot be reached (no walk joins them, they are more than 40 tiles away, or they walk off). A seat with no ant (or an ally, or a team that has dropped out) is skipped for the next one of the ring. In a match of three or four seats every seat is named by exactly one other, so none is every bot's first victim, whatever the map and the ids (on all five maps where bots meet, every seat is named once in 100 of 100 matches); with two seats, or one enemy left, the ring changes nothing. A bot reads nothing but the map, the roster and who has ants: all of it is on a person's screen too.

**One ant at a victim with four ants or fewer.** A victim in its hit clip refuses a second attacker, so with few ants the second ant of a pack is wasted. A side with 4 living ants or fewer (`solo_ants`) sends one ant at a victim; the ants left over still join the nearest fight (`crowd`). The number was swept (one tuned seat against three bots of the ring, five maps, 400 matches a seat and map, 2,000 in all; the same seats of four such bots won 476): 2 gave 503, 3 gave 592, 4 gave 638, 5 gave 622 and 6 gave 522. Six sends one ant at each of the six starting ants of MEDIUM, GAUNTLET and TREASURE and loses there (MEDIUM -9, GAUNTLET -31 of 400); 3 gains less on GAUNTLET and TREASURE (+14 and +17 against +30 and +47). Four is kept: TINY and SMALL start with 3 and 4 ants and have it from the start, the others once a side has lost two.

## Tried and left out

*Earlier rule: found with the hill working; the retreat tries and the door camp below depend on it.*

Hard, 90 maps of the design half, seeds 1 and 2, the variant against the bot as it ships; 50 is a tie and one standard error is about 2.2.

- **Walking an ant to the side of a victim from which one blow kills it** (water, a bomb or a fire wall behind the victim) before it strikes: the bot with the walk scored 42.5 against the bot without it, the bot that ships. The victims that drown anyway are the ones the packs hit from the right side by themselves, and the walk takes the ant out of its pack. A bonus for victims with water beside them, without the walk, scored 54.2 and 55.0 against the bot with the walk, where the plain bot scored 57.5.
- **Striking from the tile on the victim's way home** (so that a retreat leads back past the striker), in an earlier form of the code: 28.6.
- **Packs of 1, 3 and 4; crowds of 2, 4 and 5; retreat at 1, 2, 4 and 5 hit points; orders again after 4 and 14 ticks; the victims near the own hill first; victims within 12 or 20 tiles only; the hit points counted at 10 and at 60:** all between 46.4 and 52.2.
- **The tune keys** of the numbers above, for `--tune` and `standard+K=V`: `brawl` (0: the bot before), `bretreat`, `breturn`, `bper`, `bcrowd`, `bhp`, `bregap`, `bring` (the ring bonus in points; 0: no ring) and `bsolo` (the side size that sends one ant at a victim; 0: never). `bring=0,bsolo=0` is the bot as it was before the ring.
- **A door camp** (ants on the ring tiles of the enemy hill kill what comes home wounded): in a lab of one wounded enemy and 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 ants on the ring it was killed in 5, 5, 5, 8, 10 and 0 of 15 tries; not built.

## Limits

- A map that no walk joins is not fought on (see above), and a match on it ends at the clock.
- The 16 maps whose ants are all Bombers could plant bombs; the brawl does not.
- Under the earlier rule a hurt ant walks home alone and is hunted by whoever stands in its way; it does not wait for an escort. In 187 now a hurt ant fights on and dies to the next blow: the bot has no way to save it, and no rule that keeps it back.
- Two allied brawl bots do not help each other beyond not striking each other.
- Not pinned by a test (mutants not made): the rest of a refused pair (300 ticks), the 600-tick limit on an ant's walk to a victim, the march pace (`march_gap_ticks`), the skipped walk analysis on levels of Swimmers and Fire Ants, and a healed ant that rejoins its old pack.
- Every seat names its ring seat first however the map is laid out. On a map that is not square the seats still differ in what is near them (SMALL and GAUNTLET keep a skew), and a ring bot in a room of bots of the earlier kind is struck by all of them. Teams of two allied pairs were not measured by this work (the skew of the seats on non-square maps is likely to show), and Easy and Medium use the same numbers as Hard (their seats are more even too, with more draws on TINY).
