# The computer players in 187: the brawl

How a standard bot plays the game type 187 ([GAMEPLAY.md](GAMEPLAY.md#187-a-game-type-of-the-remake): no food and no power-up, the kills are the score, the last side with ants wins). The bots in general are in [BOTS.md](BOTS.md).

In the game type 187 ([GAMEPLAY.md](GAMEPLAY.md#187-a-game-type-of-the-remake): no food and no power-up, the kills are the score, the last side with ants wins) a standard bot of any level plays **`BrawlTask`** instead of every task above. `StandardBot::think` reads the room's mode from the engine it plays in (`BotView::game_mode()`; every client of a room knows it) and hands the whole look to the brawl; the profile (look interval, reaction time, command budget), the accept rule for an invitation and the rules of fairness are the level's as always, and a match of the original's game never reaches the code (AI30.3: the same orders to the tick with the switch on and off; the state hashes and the golden tables of the original's game are those of before). `LevelPlan::brawl187` is the switch and `bot_arena --tune brawl=0` is the bot before: it plays the food game's tasks in 187 too, with no food to play with (it strikes back at a blow and hunts what is hurt, and does nothing else).

**What the engine allows** (measured with the bot's own orders and with hand-made worlds; the numbers are for Hard, whose looks are 4 ticks apart and whose orders take 8 ticks to leave):

- One blow lands per hit clip: about every 40 ticks on one victim, however many ants strike it. A worker's blow takes 1 hit point and throws the victim one tile away from the attacker; a Combat Ant's punch takes 2 and throws it four. The victim stays in its hit clip about 15 ticks after it lands, and an order that reaches an ant in a clip is refused ("can't do that").
- An attack order names the tile an ant stands on; the occupant of the tile when the order arrives is the target. A victim that is struck lands on another tile, so the next blow needs an order that names the new tile; an ant that walks is not caught by an order at all, it has moved on before the attacker arrives.
- An ant left with one hit point walks home at the speed of its hunters (see "Fights of its own") and is caught only when a hunter is beside it at the moment its walk begins, or when its way is blocked. In a lab of one hunter and one victim with two hit points the second blow killed it from two of the eight sides, and from different sides at different places on the map; no side works everywhere.
- A victim thrown into water drowns whatever its hit points, onto a bomb it loses 2, onto a fire wall 1. In a sample of two Hard brawl bots on 45 maps (236 deaths) 46 percent of the deaths were drownings, about a fifth bombs and fire of the map, a quarter the last blow on a wounded ant and the rest Combat punches.
- 222 of the 540 maps of the library (41 percent) join the hills of seats 0 and 1 by no walk (walls of fire and bombs, water, islands): nobody can be struck there, a match of two bots ends at the clock with no kill, and a brawl bot has no order to give.

**What the brawl does.** At every look:

1. An ant with 3 hit points or fewer (`retreat_hp`) goes home to heal (the hill restores all hit points) and fights again with 9 or more.
2. The enemy ants that can be struck (`attackable`, no ally's, none on a hill tile or in water) that some ant of the bot can reach by a walk (the walker components of the start; on a level of Swimmers or Fire Ants the question is not asked) are ranked: the wounded first (a hit point counts three tiles), then the near. An enemy that walks is not an object until it is within two tiles of an ant.
3. Each victim gets a **pack** of the nearest ready ants, at most 2 (`per_target`); ants that find no victim of their own join the nearest fight, 3 on a victim at most (`crowd`): blows land in parallel on different victims, and the third ant finishes. Nobody is sent to a victim that it was refused twice within 160 ticks ("can't go" with no clip of the victim to explain it): the pair rests 300 ticks, so a fenced enemy draws a few orders and not a flood.
4. A pack is ordered onto its victim at once when an ant joined, and again (no sooner than 8 ticks later) when the victim was thrown to another tile or an ant of the pack stands idle after its blow; the command names the whole pack. A victim with one hit point that walks home is followed only by ants within two tiles of it.
5. When no enemy ant can be struck (all are in their hills) the idle ants march to the door of the nearest enemy hill that a walk reaches (never an ally's) and wait there; a hill that no walk reaches is no goal, and when enemy ants are in sight but none can be reached nobody moves.

- **A door camp** (ants on the ring tiles of the enemy hill kill what comes home wounded): a lab of one wounded enemy and 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 ants on the ring killed it in 5, 5, 5, 8, 10 and 0 of 15 tries; not built.

**Limits.** A map that no walk joins is not fought on (see above). The 16 maps whose ants are all Bombers could plant bombs, and the brawl does not. A hurt ant that walks home alone is hunted by whoever stands in its way; it does not wait for an escort. The ants of two allied brawl bots do not help each other beyond not striking each other. The opening is the same on every map: all ants go for the nearest enemy ant.
