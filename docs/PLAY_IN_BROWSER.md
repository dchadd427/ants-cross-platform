# Play in the browser

The game runs in a modern web browser, as WebAssembly, with nothing to install: **[Play Ants Online at beta.playants.org](https://beta.playants.org)**. This page says what that site does. To build and serve it yourself, see [`BUILD_AND_RUN.md`](BUILD_AND_RUN.md). The game is developed and tested in a Chromium-based browser (the engine of Chrome and Edge); Firefox and Safari have not been tried. A phone can play it too: [`TOUCH.md`](TOUCH.md).

## The front page: a room that is ready when the page opens

**[beta.playants.org](https://beta.playants.org)** is the front page, the lobby (`web/lobby.html`, served at `/`). It used to be a separate Play online page; that page's address, `/four.html`, now redirects here with its query. The page is built like the game's own menus: the "ants!" logo, the black boxes and teal buttons of the setup screen and the original's help sheets. The pictures are in `web/front/` (the four ants are one sheet, `ants.png`, made from the game's assets by `tools/front_page_art/`). **START!** is not a picture but a real button in the same teal, drawn by the browser so that it stays sharp at any size; the font is Libre Franklin, under the SIL OFL. The page is built from the approved lobby cards 1 to 18.

There is nothing to set up first. Opening the page makes a **room** on the game server and seats you in the first colour, as its host, under a name the page picked (or the one this browser used last); the link is there to send. Nothing is played until the host presses **START!**. From top to bottom the page shows:

- A line of numbers in the footer, for example "3 matches being played · 7 players online · 1,284 games played (21 today)". "Today" means the last 24 hours. It counts what the game server knows: the page reads its `/stats`, else `/busy` for the live part, else it shows no line ([`SERVER.md`](SERVER.md#busy-and-stats)). It looks when the page opens and every 30 seconds while the page is visible.
- A strip with the button **Rejoin it**, only while there is a match to go back to ([below](#rejoin-your-match-code)), and a strip for the page's notices ([below](#the-notices)).
- **Your room**, with the **room code** and the **link**; under them the four colours; beside them the map and **START!**.
- **How it works**, a button in the footer that opens the lobby's rules in a few lines beside the original's two help sheets. The footer also has the one link to **Watch matches** (the matches that are being played and the earlier ones; the front page's header has it as a button too), the sprites and sounds, the changelog, GitHub and feedback; the version and build; and the choice of **Screen**, the game page's four shapes, Classic 4:3, 16:10, 16:9 (the default) and 21:9, which the games and the game for one use. The shape nearest to the computer's screen has a small "fills your screen" tag under its button (only where there is a mouse, the rule of the game page); a shape picked here or in a game is remembered under the same key (`ants.aspect.v2`) and shown pressed on the other page, and `?aspect=` in the address wins.

### Your room

- **The link and the code.** The link is `https://<site>/?room=k7m2xq`; the code is shown in two groups, `k7m 2xq` ([below](#the-rooms-code-and-its-create-block)). **Copy link** copies the link, **Share** (only where the browser has it) opens the browser's share sheet. Players who open the link take the next free colour and show up in the room at once. The page remembers the room for this tab (session storage `ants.lobby`), so a reload takes your seat back. A new tab has a room of its own; a tab that was duplicated holds the same seat, and when the copy takes it the first says "This room was opened in another window, so it is closed here." with a button, **Use this window**, that takes the room back.
- **Have a code?** opens a field: type or paste the code of a match that somebody else made, as it is shown (`k7m 2xq`: blanks and capitals do not matter; a code is up to 32 letters, digits, `-` and `_`) and press **Join**. Your name goes with it, and you get the first free colour. A code that no room has is answered "There is no room with that code. Check it, or ask the host to send the link again." (the page never makes a room for a typed code: its Hello joins and carries no create block).
- **The four colours** lie as the four hills lie on the Small and Treasure maps: **Black** top left, **Green** top right, **Red** bottom left, **Blue** bottom right (one column, in the same order, on a phone). A colour's number is its seat (Green 0, Red 1, Blue 2, Black 3), so a new room fills Green, Red, Blue, then Black. Each colour shows its ant, its name and what it is: a **person** (tagged **Host** and, on your own colour, **You**), **Open** ("Takes the next player who joins."), a computer player (**Easy**, **Medium** or **Hard**: "Computer player") or **Nobody** ("Stays out of the match.").
- **What the host can do.** A colour that no person holds has a drop-down (**Open**, **Easy**, **Medium**, **Hard**, **Nobody**) that sets it. Drag a player onto another colour to move them (a free colour is taken, a taken colour swaps places, and the team goes with the player); on a phone, tap a player's dots and then the colour. The small **Remove** button beside another player's name asks "Remove Priya from the room?" with **Remove** and **Keep** (the focus is on Keep, Esc is Keep), and opens the colour again; the removed player gets a room of their own with a note that says why, and can come back with the link. The host's own colour, a computer player and an open colour have no Remove, and neither does a player's screen. A player cannot change anything but their own name (the **pencil** beside it).
- **Teams**, with three or four players: every colour that plays has a **Team 1** and a **Team 2** button. Two colours on the same team start the match allied; with four players the other two colours are the other team (their button shows it), with three the third colour plays alone, and with no two colours on one team it is free for all. A line under the colours says which ("Free for all. For a team, put two colours on the same team." or "Teams: Green + Red against Blue + Black."). A team is two colours at most: pressing the button of a full team changes nothing and says "Team 1 has two colours already. Press one of them to take it off first." A player's screen shows the teams but cannot change them.
- **The map**: the six maps of the original, each with a picture and the map-info line of the setup screen, with arrows and a list. **Treasure** is the map until the host chooses another. The host's choice goes to the room and to the other players' screens.
- **The line under START!** says who plays and what an open colour does, for example "You, Sam and a Medium bot play on Treasure. Blue is open and stays empty unless a player joins first." With nobody else it says "A game for one" and what the open colours do ("A game for one. Red, Blue and Black are open and stay empty unless a player joins first."), or, with every colour out, "A game for one: only your colony is on the map." A player's screen has "Waiting for <host>" on the button and "<host> starts the match when everybody is in."
- **When the host leaves**, the player who has been in the room longest becomes the host and everybody reads "Juniper left. Sam is the host now." (the new host: "Juniper left, so you are the host now. The map, the colours and START are yours."). Teams that needed the one who left clear. Computer players never become the host. When the last player leaves, the room closes about a minute later, so a reload does not lose it; a link to it then says "There is no room with that code. Check it, or ask the host to send the link again." on the name card, which offers **Start a room of my own instead** (a link never makes a room of its code). Only a page that had a seat in the room and finds it lost later (a server restart) makes the room again under the same code ("That room is gone, because everybody left, so this one is yours now, with the same map.").

### The room's code and its create block

A room code is **only a name**: six random letters and numbers of `abcdefghjkmnpqrstuvwxyz23456789` (no `i`, `l`, `o`, `0` or `1`, which are easily mistaken), made by the page. The screens show it in two groups of three, `k7m 2xq` (the lobby's header, **Rejoin it**, the name step of a shared link, the field of **Have a code?**); a link, a field's value and the game's arguments keep the plain `k7m2xq`. A typed or pasted code ignores blanks and capital letters, and a link with capitals leads to the same room. Any code of 1 to 32 letters, digits, `-` and `_` is valid: a code that was made some other way, or by an earlier page (`demo-small-2p-x7k2`), joins like any other and names nothing but itself.

What a room is made of can travel in its **create block**, which the game sends in its first Hello: the map, the seats, the teams and whether a full room waits for its leader's START. The server reads the block of the first Hello of a code only (a room that exists keeps its own choices), and a Hello for a code that has no room and brings no block is answered "There is no such room on this server." The lobby needs none of these: it speaks protocol 16 (`web/front/lobby_net.js`), whose Hello carries the map for the room it makes (a plain visit, and a page that had a seat and lost its room, so that the room is made again) and carries nothing for a link or a typed code, which only join. Nothing on the front page writes the block's parameters any more (they belong to the addresses of the earlier pages, and to a game page that somebody opened with them); they are those of `web/shell.html`, which gives the game one whitelist each and only with a valid `join`. In an address they are written `&roommap=<key>`, `&roomseats=<n>`, `&roomteams=A%2BB`, `&roomleaderstart=1` and `&platform=<word>`, after the room's plain code:

| Parameter | Value | The game's argument |
|---|---|---|
| `roommap` | `tiny`, `small`, `medium`, `gauntlet`, `treasure` or `islands`, in any case; none: the server's own map | `--room-map <key>` |
| `roomseats` | `2`, `3` or `4` | `--room-seats <n>` |
| `roomteams` | two different seats 0 to 3 as `A+B`, written `0%2B1` in a link; none: free for all | `--room-teams <A+B>` |
| `roomleaderstart` | `1`: a full room waits for its leader's START instead of starting by itself | `--room-leader-start` |
| `platform` | `windows`, `macos`, `linux`, `android`, `ios` or `other`, with or without `browser-` in front, in any case: what the room shows beside the player (cosmetic). The pages make none and do not look at the browser: only an address's word counts | `--platform <word>` |

### START!

- The host presses **START!**. The match does not begin at once, because each player's browser has to leave the lobby and open the game: the server keeps the request and starts the match when everybody is in a game. Meanwhile the lobby is frozen (nothing can be dragged or pressed), START! says "Getting ready…", and each player's colour shows "Opening the game…" and then "In the game". A link opened now gets a room of its own: nobody joins while the games open.
- Each page goes to the game page of its seat: `/?join=/ws&room=<code>&seat=<n>&name=<name>&aspect=<shape>`, with the key of the seat written to this browser first (`ants.rejoin.<room>.<seat>`, [below](#rejoin-your-match-code)), so the address has no key. The game seats the computer players of the plan, leaves the **Nobody** colours empty and starts. The game's loading screen, quick help and the "Get ready" dialog come first, as in any match.
- A game that does not come in within 90 seconds ends the wait: everybody is back in the room, as they left it. The host reads "<name>'s game did not come in time. Everybody is back in the room, as you left it. Press START to try again." (the first part is the server's own sentence); every other player reads "The match did not start, so everybody is back in the room. <host> can press START to try again." The host can then **Remove** the player whose game never opened.
- **A game for one**: with nobody else in the room and no computer player (every other colour **Open** or **Nobody**), START! takes this tab to a game on this computer (`/play.html?map=<key>&name=<name>&aspect=<shape>`: no server, no room, no bots, no teams, and only your colony on the map). The page leaves the room first.
- **After a match** the game's **Leave Game** takes each player to the front page, where a new room with a new code is ready. The room of a match is not kept (the server's lobby does not return to the lobby by itself: "Protocol 16" in [`NETWORK_PORT.md`](NETWORK_PORT.md)), so the strip "Welcome back. Your match on Treasure is over…" that picture 15 draws is in `TEXT` but no path shows it yet.
- The server's own rules apply as ever: a match is made for 2 to 4 seats, **the first player in the room can start early with START once at least 2 players are in** (a computer player counts; the lobby's host can start alone against computer players), a running match takes nobody new (only a player who comes back to their own seat), and no computer player plays with Fog of War ("Bots cannot play with Fog of War.", the server's words). The game's own setup screen is the original's host screen with the START button ([`GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`](GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md) section 5.50); the lobby makes its press for the host, and in the waiting room **T** (or a click in the chat box) opens a line to say something to everybody.

### The notices

A strip at the top of the page says in one line what the page changed for you or what did not work: green when the page changed something (a room of your own after a link to a room that was full, running or closed to you; the new host), gold when something did not work (the server is full, the connection is lost, a newer version of Ants is out, the server's own sentence for a map that could not load or a start that failed). While the connection is lost the room goes grey and read-only, START! says "Reconnecting…", and the colour is kept for a minute; the page rejoins by itself with its key. If the page cannot reach the server at all, a gold strip says "Cannot reach the game server. Check your connection; trying again…". The sentences are `TEXT` in `web/front/lobby_rules.js`; the ones that start with the server's own words repeat what the server said.

### The old addresses

The front page is the only way to play: the page has no test mode, no frames and no windows of its own. The addresses of the earlier pages (`/?map=<key>&players=<n>`, `/?room=<code>&roommap=<key>`, `&play=here`, and the test page that the "More ways to play" button used to open) are the front page like any other now: a plain address makes a room of its own, and `/?room=<code>` is the link to somebody's room, whatever else follows it. A code of an earlier page (`demo-small-2p-x7k2`) is a link to a lobby room too, and the page asks your name. `/play.html?map=<key>&bots=<levels>` still plays a game on this computer, with no room ([below](#the-game-page-and-its-addresses)).

### Your name

The lobby has no field for it at the top: your room is ready when the page opens, under the name this browser used last (`ants.name`) or, for a first visit, one the page picked ("Maple", "Clover", "Pebble"...). The **pencil** beside your own name changes it: Enter or leaving the field asks the room to rename you, and the new name goes to the whole room and is remembered in this browser. The "Bot (" rule is the desktop start menu's, in the same words: a name that starts with "Bot (", whatever its blanks and capitals, is refused with the reason. A name has at most 32 printable ASCII characters; this page refuses one that has more, or another character, and says why (the desktop menu drops such characters and cuts the name), and it accepts an empty field (the page picks a name). A bad name changes nothing and says why under the field. The room only takes a new name while it is an open lobby room: not while START waits.

- **A link that somebody sends you asks for your name first, every time**, except on a reload of a page whose seat this browser holds (the reload takes the seat back and asks nobody). A card over the page, titled "Join the room k7m 2xq", shows your remembered name with a **Join** button (or press Enter), the hint "The name the other players see.", the recording notice and a button **Start a room of my own instead**, for a link that was opened by mistake. The page behind it shows no names and no map ("Joining a room"); the lobby does not connect before you have chosen. The same card comes from **Have a code?**. The game page's own `?join=...&room=...` link asks the same, in the game page's own card; the address that START leads to does not, because it carries the name and this browser holds the key of its seat.
- A visitor who opens the plain page is not asked: the room is made at once.
- The line about recorded online matches (its words, where the pages show it and what an operator who runs the server with other settings changes) is described in [`SERVER.md`](SERVER.md#replays), "The notice on the pages".

### Rejoin your match (CODE)

While this browser holds a fresh key of a seat on this site's game server, a strip stands at the top of the front page, above its header, with the line "Your match in room CODE is still running: go back to your seat." and one button, **Rejoin it**. Without such a key nothing of it is shown.

- The game page keeps a key for every seat that it plays, for three hours, and lets go of it when the match ends, the seat is dropped or the player leaves.
- The button takes this tab to the game page of that seat (the newest, when there are several). The game page finds the key itself: it is in no address, text or log. The match goes on where it was. The line shows the code as a screen does (`k7m 2xq`); the address that the button opens has the plain code, the seat and no create block, so a match that has ended is not made again by it.
- The exceptions: the others voted to go on without you (they may after 30 seconds), or the pauses of the match reached their cap, 30 minutes (10 in a demo room). Then the game says so. The rules of a held seat are in [`MULTIPLAYER.md`](MULTIPLAYER.md), "Lag, silence and drop-outs", and [`NETWORK_PORT.md`](NETWORK_PORT.md#the-behaviour-that-is-decided) (the vote and the cap); the options are in [`SERVER.md`](SERVER.md#reconnect).

### The game page and its addresses

**Menu**, the game page's header button (and its footer link), goes back to the front page in the same tab; so does the small "ants!" logo at the left of the header. Both ask first ("Leave the game and go back to the menu?") while a match runs or a room is joined, and Yes drops your seat at once, where a closed tab or a reload holds it. The game's own **Leave Game** (on the results and on the setup screen) goes to the front page too, with the room left at once, and so does the quit dialog's Yes while more than one opponent is left (with one left it ends the match first, and the results have the Leave Game button). Nothing opens a new tab by default: only the links to the sprites, the changelog, GitHub and the feedback do.

nginx tells the two pages apart by the query: at `/`, `?join=<something>` opens the game page and anything else is the front page, so the addresses of a game that was shared (`/?join=...&room=...`) keep working.

| `/watch.html` | the list of the matches to watch again ([`REPLAYS.md`](REPLAYS.md#watching-a-replay)); the footer of the front page, the game page and the list has a **Watch matches** link to it, and the header of the front page and of the game page a button |

## Starts at once, and the sound follows your first click or key

- There is no start button. The game runs as soon as its data has downloaded (a loading card shows the download and goes away by itself); the original's own loading screen and quick help follow as always.
- Browsers let a page make sound only after you have done something on it, so the music and the sound effects begin with your first click, key press or touch on the page. The page keeps trying until the browser lets the sound run (Safari is the strictest; what each browser needs is under "Sound and the autoplay rules" below).
- The page downloads the game's data (14.7 MB) itself and tries again, up to three times, when a download fails (a browser's cache can refuse a download). If every try fails, if a file of the game (`index.js`, `index.wasm`) cannot be loaded, if the game never starts, or if the download is a web page instead of the data (a captive portal), a card with a **Reload** button replaces the loading screen.

**Sound and the autoplay rules.** A browser lets a page make sound only after the person has done something on it, so the game's Web Audio context (SDL's, `Module.SDL2.audioContext`, opened when the game starts) begins suspended and the game's sound and music begin the moment it is resumed; the game itself does not wait for it, and there is no start overlay any more (the page used to hide the game behind a card that said "Click anywhere to play!" until a click). SDL's own Emscripten audio already resumes the context at the first `keydown`, `mousedown` and `touchstart` (`autoResumeAudioContext`: once each, on the document and the canvas); the old page's own tries did nothing (it looked for a global `SDL2`, which does not exist, and made a second context that played nothing). `web/shell.html` now keeps trying until the context really runs (no `once`: a press that grants nothing, such as the Esc key or a shortcut of the browser, or a press before the context exists, would use the attempt up), on `pointerdown`, `mousedown`, `click`, `touchend` and `keydown`, and takes its listeners off as soon as the context runs; a context that stops later is resumed by the next press (`statechange`, `visibilitychange`). What each browser needs: *Chrome and Edge*: a context created before any user activation starts suspended; `resume()` works after a user activation (`mousedown`, `pointerdown` of a mouse, `pointerup` / `touchend` of a finger or a pen, `keydown` of a key that is not Esc or a shortcut of the browser), and the activation is sticky: after the first press anywhere on the page a context created later starts running. *Firefox*: the same rule (a suspended context, `resume()` in an input event: click, keydown, touchend; the person may also allow audio for the site, then the context runs at once). *Safari* (macOS and iOS): the strictest: `resume()` must be called inside the handler of a gesture (`click`, `touchend`, `keydown`), it is not sticky, `touchstart` does not count on iOS, a context that a phone call or the lock screen interrupted (`interrupted`) needs a new press, and the ringer switch silences Web Audio on an iPhone. The built-in browser lets pages play at once (its context was `running` before any press), so the check there exercised the page's logic with a real context that was suspended by hand: a trusted click resumed it, a second suspension was resumed by the next click, with the context running the listeners were gone (no `resume()` call), and a press while no context existed raised nothing and the next press resumed it. The keyboard focus: the canvas takes it when the game is ready, and again at the first press inside the game.

## A network match keeps playing in a hidden or minimised tab

- A browser stops drawing a page that you cannot see and slows its timers. Every message of the game server still wakes the game, which plays its turns without drawing anything and without sound or music. So the match goes on at the normal speed, nobody is told that you lag (`<your name> is lagging`), and you find it up to date when you come back (the music of a match that begins or ends meanwhile follows then). A single game (no network) simply waits while the page is hidden, as the desktop game does when minimised.
- A server that stops answering is noticed after 10 seconds of real time, a hidden page included (the page's timer is a Worker, which the browser does not slow): a seat hidden for minutes noticed a paused server at +10 s, as a shown one did.
- **Limits**: a computer that sleeps, a phone that locks its screen or leaves the browser, and a tab that the browser freezes stop the page (a frozen tab loses its connection when it wakes up). Since v0.6.0 the server holds such a seat and pauses the match; the game reconnects with the seat's key, or **Rejoin your match** takes the seat back. A seat whose page stays connected but runs no turns is still called lagging after 3 seconds and dropped after 30 ([`MULTIPLAYER.md`](MULTIPLAYER.md), "Lag, silence and drop-outs").
- No automated test covers this code, which exists only in the web build. The manual check and the opt-in script `tests/scripts/test_web_hidden.sh` are in [`NETWORK_PORT.md`](NETWORK_PORT.md#the-browser-as-a-client-hidden-tabs-and-sound-v0096), "The browser as a client: hidden tabs, and sound".

## Your own orders show at once in a network match

- The game can run a second engine next to the match's own (the confirmed one, which the host's turns, the server's referee and every hash keep exactly as they were). An order is run on it in the same call and the screen shows it, a few ticks ahead, so the ants answer a click as they do in a game of one machine instead of one round trip, one seal and one jitter buffer later. The corner's `delay` shows what the click feels.
- **It is off by default**: other players' ants still hop about a dozen pixels, a tile at most, when their orders arrive. `?prediction=on` on the address of a network game (a `?join=...` link; the front page does not pass it on) turns it on, as the game's `--prediction on`; a game on one computer never predicts. Once on, it switches itself off for a while (ten seconds, twice as long each time) when its work takes too long on a slow device, and it is off in a hidden tab, in a pause and while the "Get ready" dialog is up.
- More: [`MULTIPLAYER.md`](MULTIPLAYER.md), "Prediction of your own orders"; the design: [`NETWORK_PORT.md`](NETWORK_PORT.md#prediction-of-ones-own-orders-predictionhpp-cue_routerhpp-netgame-applicationview_sim).

## A 16:9 picture that fills the page

- **The picture**: the game draws a fixed 960 x 540 canvas (`?aspect=16:9` on the page's address; this is the default), shown in a game box that is 16:9 and as large as fits: its width is `min(100%, 1280 px, (window height - the page's header and bar) x 16 / 9)`, so the whole picture is on the screen without scrolling.
- **Exact pixels**: the box takes the screen's own resolution (CSS size x `devicePixelRatio`, in whole steps of the shape's smallest whole numbers, 16 x 9 device pixels for 16:9, 8 x 5 for 16:10, 7 x 3 for 21:9 and 4 x 3 for the classic picture), so the canvas has exactly the picture's shape and there is no black bar inside the box. The picture and the pointer stay right when the window is resized, the page is zoomed or fullscreen is toggled. On a display of about 100 Hz or more the frame rate is held to 60 a second.
- **Four shapes, one click away**: the selector under the game, left to right, Classic 4:3 (the original's 640 x 480 picture), 16:10 (960 x 600, laptops and 16:10 monitors), 16:9 (960 x 540, the default) and 21:9 (1260 x 540, ultrawide monitors). A press changes the picture **at once, in the running game**: no reload, no "Leave the match?" question, no new connection (a joined match keeps its seat and its room), and the simulation is not touched. The browser remembers the choice and the address gets `?aspect=` (without a new entry in the history), so a reload or a link has it. A small tag "fills your screen" stands under the shape nearest to the computer's screen, on computers with a mouse only. `?aspect=4:3`, `?aspect=16:10`, `?aspect=16:9` or `?aspect=21:9` on the address chooses a shape the same way.
- **Which shape**: only those four are accepted, anything else is ignored, and the address beats the remembered choice. Every device starts in 16:9, a phone held upright included (a portrait phone gets a tip to turn it sideways for a bigger picture). The choice is remembered under the browser's key `ants.aspect.v2`, which only the selectors write. The front page's own selector has the same four shapes and reads and writes the same key.
- **The pages in 16:10 and 21:9**: the setup screen, the room, the loading screen, the quick help and the results are the 960 x 540 page centred in the canvas with black around it; the match fills the whole canvas and shows more map when the picture is wider or taller ([`VIEW_AND_HUD.md`](VIEW_AND_HUD.md)).
- **Fullscreen** (the header's button) shows the picture as large as the screen allows, with bars on a screen of another shape; a browser with no Fullscreen API for an element (Safari on an iPhone) gets the page's own fullscreen with a button to leave it. The bars count as the picture's edge: a pointer over a bar scrolls the map like the edge, corners included.
- **The mouse in fullscreen**: the page holds the mouse for the game (the browser's pointer lock, taken by the click on the Fullscreen button). The game's cursor cannot leave the picture, so every edge scrolls and a Mac's Dock and menu bar have no screen edge to come up at. - The page gives the game `--aspect` with the shape it shows (the desktop game's own option, which wins over the settings key `aspect`), and a press of the selector calls the game's `ants_set_aspect`.
- What the wider picture shows (the map view, the frame, the HUD) is in [`VIEW_AND_HUD.md`](VIEW_AND_HUD.md#169-by-default-a-wider-view-with-more-of-the-map-desktop-and-web-builds). The setup screen in the browser is the wide one with its map preview ([`CONTROLS.md`](CONTROLS.md#the-169-setup-screen-desktop-and-web-builds-the-960-x-540-picture)).
- The opt-in `tests/scripts/test_web_aspect.sh` and `tests/scripts/test_web_edge.sh` check the picture and the pointer in a real browser (they need a running page and a Chromium-based browser).
- The design and the measurements are in the three items that follow: "The page's picture", "The pointer in fullscreen" and "The margin of a windowed page".

* **The page's picture (widescreen milestone M5, `web/shell.html`, `web/lobby.html`)**: the game page shows a 16:9 picture by default and offers the original's 4:3, 16:10 and 21:9; the page passes the choice to the game as `--aspect 16:9|4:3|16:10|21:9` (the desktop's own option: the game's canvas is 960 x 540, 640 x 480, 960 x 600 or 1260 x 540, `Application::choose_aspect`, `kWebDefaultAspect` is 16:9). **Which shape**: the address `?aspect=16:9`, `?aspect=4:3`, `?aspect=16:10` or `?aspect=21:9` (the first one if repeated; anything else is ignored), else the choice the page's selector ("Classic 4:3 / 16:10 / 16:9 / 21:9" under the game) remembered in `localStorage` ("ants.aspect.v2", written by the two selectors only, the game page's and web/lobby.html's, read in a try / catch: a private window remembers nothing; the first versions of the pages used "ants.aspect", which is no longer read: a browser that had remembered a Classic 4:3 under it starts in 16:9 once, and its player picks the classic picture again if wanted), else 16:9 on every device, a phone held upright included (there is no exception for portrait phones since v0.1.0; the page only shows them a hint to turn the phone sideways for a bigger picture, and a remembered 4:3 still wins there). The lobby's selector has the same four shapes (Classic 4:3, 16:10, 16:9 the default, 21:9; a "fills your screen" tag stands under the one nearest to a computer's screen, with a mouse only): it reads and writes the same key, and its START hands every game `&aspect=` of its choice. **The boxes**: `#game-stage` is the slot (CSS: width `min(100%, 1280 px, (100dvh - --chrome) x 16 / 9)`, `--chrome` = the height of the header, the bar and the paddings, measured by the page; 960 px and 4:3 for the classic picture; the other shapes have rules of their own for 16:10 and 21:9) and the element that goes fullscreen; `#game-container` is the picture's box inside it, sized by the page (`ANTS_PAGE.fitBox`) to a whole number of canvas steps (16 x 9 device pixels, 4 x 3 for the classic picture, 8 x 5 for 16:10, 7 x 3 for 21:9) with the largest that fits, centred. **Why exact pixels**: the game's window system (SDL's Emscripten driver) sets the canvas to `floor(CSS size x devicePixelRatio)` of the canvas's box on every `resize` of the window (measured: a box of 1000 x 562.5 CSS pixels gave a canvas of 1000 x 562, 853.33 x 480 gave 853 x 480, at 1.5x 853.33 x 480 gave 1279 x 720) and takes its first size from the box too; the game then draws its logical canvas into the real one with the largest scale that fits, centred, with black bars where the shapes differ, so a canvas of the wrong shape by a pixel has a black line of its own. With a box of exactly 16 x 9 steps the canvas has the picture's shape exactly and no bar of the game's shows. A CSS size that is exact stays exact (a device ratio with at most six binary places, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.625, 3 ..., makes exact products with a size that is a multiple of 1/64, which is a whole or a half pixel, so that the floor cannot lose a pixel: 1600 / 1.25 = 1280), any other size gets a quarter of a device pixel more (`ANTS_PAGE.cssFor`) which the floor drops and the layout's rounding (1/64 px in Chrome and Safari, 1/60 in Firefox) cannot take away. **The bridge**: SDL listens to the window's `resize` event only (and reads the device ratio at that time: measured, a change of the ratio changes nothing until the next `resize`), and a fullscreen toggle, a header that wraps, or a move to a screen of another density does not always send one (reproduced in the research for the page). The page observes the slot, the header and the bar (`ResizeObserver`), the fullscreen change (`fullscreenchange`, `webkitfullscreenchange`: again after 150 and 600 ms, as browsers settle at different times), the window's `resize` and the device ratio (`matchMedia("(resolution: Xdppx)")`), sizes the box, and sends a `resize` of its own whenever the box's device pixels change. **The pointer**: the browser's mouse position reaches the game through Emscripten's SDL as `trunc( trunc(clientX - trunc(box.left)) x floor(box.width) / box.width )` (library_html5.js stores `clientX - (rect.left | 0)` as an integer; SDL 2.28's `Emscripten_HandleMouseMove` scales by `window->w / client_w`, and `window->w` is the CSS width cut to a whole number), and the same for y. Two terms of it move the reading by up to a pixel: the cut of the box's position (a box at 68.98 read one pixel too high) and, for a box whose CSS size is not a whole number, the scale `floor(width) / width`, a hair under 1, which turns every whole offset t into t - 1 (a box of 720.19 read one pixel low across the whole box). `ANTS_PAGE.snapOffset` says where the box must stand: a box whose size is whole (scale exactly 1) on a whole pixel, any other 1/64 px (the layout's step) short of one, which puts back the pixel that the scale takes away; `snapBox()` shifts the box by that (at most half a pixel, with `left` / `top`) after every layout and on every scroll. Measured with the real mouse (the hover edges of the setup screen's START button, `--pointer`): before, up to 0.94 CSS px off (1280 x 720 at 1x: top and bottom -0.94; 1536 x 864 at 1.25x: +0.94; 1000 x 640 at 1x: left and right -0.94); after, 0.06 (the bisection's own step) at every layout (1280 x 720 at 1x, 1000 x 640 at 1x, 1536 x 864 at 1.25x, 1500 x 844 at 1.5x, 1280 x 720 at 2.625x, 844 x 390 at 3x). The box keeps its size, so the picture is as large as before. **Fullscreen**: the stage goes fullscreen, black, and the box is the largest picture of the chosen shape that fits the screen, centred (bars are the stage's black, not the game's); a browser that has no `requestFullscreen` for an element (Safari on an iPhone) or refuses it gets the page's own fullscreen (`#game-stage.pseudo-fullscreen`: fixed over the window, the safe areas respected, a button to leave it; the page behind does not scroll). **The frame cap**: the game's loop is `requestAnimationFrame`; the page wraps it with `ANTS_PAGE.makeFrameLimiter`, which keeps measuring the display (the MEDIAN of the last 24 gaps between display frames: below about 100 Hz nothing is touched, above it the game draws 60 frames a second by a schedule that keeps its phase), so a 144 Hz display does not draw the game 144 times a second at its full resolution. A median, because the smallest gap would cap a jittery 60 Hz display by mistake (a short gap now and then), the first frames of the game (loading) would decide for ever if only the first ones counted, and a long frame of a hidden page is an outlier; a window that moves to a display of another rate is measured again within a dozen frames. **The data download**: the file packager of `index.js` downloads `index.data` (14.7 MB) with an `XMLHttpRequest` whose `onerror` throws, which nothing catches: when several frames of `web/lobby.html` (the page had frames until v0.12.0) asked for the file at the same moment the browser's cache refused one download (Chromium: `net::ERR_CACHE_WRITE_FAILURE`, reproduced on beta in v0.0.96 and in the built-in browser) and that frame stayed at "Downloading data (0%)..." for ever. `web/shell.html` therefore downloads the file with `fetch` before it loads `index.js` (`Module.getPreloadedPackage(name, size)` is called synchronously by the packager at that moment, with the bytes; if the page has none it falls back to the packager's own download, as a browser without `fetch` does): the first try with the browser's cache (a repeat visit is a 304), up to 3 retries (`ANTS_PAGE.downloadWithRetries`, after 0.4, 1.2 and 3 s) with `cache: 'no-store'` so that a cache entry that cannot be written cannot fail them again, progress as before (the size comes from `Content-Length` when the server sends one, else from the size that the last download left in `localStorage` "ants.datasize"), `index.js` and `index.wasm` asked for at the same time (the cache has them when the game asks; measured, the time to the running game does not change); after the last failure the card says so and has a Reload button (`showDownloadError`, also used for a `NetworkError` that the packager throws itself). **What comes back must be the data** (`ANTS_PAGE.badDownload`): a body whose `Content-Type` is `text/html` (a captive portal answers every address with "200 OK") is refused before it is read, and a body that is not the package's size is refused after it (the exact size, which the Dockerfile writes into the page after the build from `stat` of `index.data`; a build outside the Dockerfile has none and refuses anything under a megabyte); a refusal is a failed try like any other, so the retries and the card take care of it, and the file packager is only handed bytes of exactly the size that it names for exactly `index.data` (`Module.getPreloadedPackage(name, size)`), once. `window.antsDownloadLog` lists the tries. **The program's own files** can fail too, and each puts the card up instead of leaving "Downloading data" on the screen for ever: `index.js` that cannot be loaded (the script's `error`), `index.wasm` that cannot be fetched or compiled (Emscripten leaves that to a promise, `Module.ready`, that nobody listens to: the page's `unhandledrejection` handler, only while the game is not ready and no card is up), and a game that does not start `ANTS_START_TIMEOUT_MS` (60 s) after the data arrived (the watchdog); a game that starts after the card was up takes it away. `index.js` is asked for with a `<link rel="preload">` that the script added after the download takes over, so it is one request with one `?v=` (the Dockerfile's `sed` that adds the build's `?v=` now touches `<script src=index.js>` tags only: it used to add a second `?v=` to the page's own address of the script, and the warm-up request was wasted). **Checks**: `tests/scripts/test_web_aspect.sh` (opt-in, not part of `./run_tests.sh`; needs a running web page and a Chromium-based browser) runs the page's logic (`ANTS_PAGE`: the shape of the address, the box's rules over 64,000 boxes (2,016 areas, 16 device ratios, both shapes, including the layout's rounding), the frame cap at 30 - 360 Hz and over a hidden gap), the page at desktop and phone sizes at device ratios 1 to 3 (the canvas has exactly the picture's shape and fills the box, the box fits the window, no sideways scroll), the address and the selector, resizing, fullscreen (the browser's and the page's own) and the pointer (the game draws its own cursor at the position it reads: two screenshots with the pointer at two places show it there; the box stands where the pointer is exact, and the edges of START found with the real mouse are where the game's arithmetic puts them at six layouts), the selector in a running match (the picture changes at once, nothing is asked, the match goes on), the mouse-wheel zoom (`--wheel`: the wheel, a ctrl + wheel and Safari's pinch are cancelled over the canvas only, the title, the selector and the guide scroll the page as usual, and in a running match the wheel zooms the game to 0.5, 1 and 2, the middle button goes back to 1), the loading faults (`index.js` or `index.wasm` failing, a game that never starts, a web page, a small body, an error status, a cut or a one-byte-short body for the data, the package handed over once, a late promise failure, `index.js` asked for once); the script's own exit statuses are checked first (`--exit-codes`: a page that is not there is a skip, a page that hangs is a failure: a hang or a crash is never a skip); the web build's 16:9 default is pinned by a unit test (`kWebDefaultAspect`, suite 3.12).

* **The pointer in fullscreen (edge-pan, `web/shell.html`, "The pointer in fullscreen: the bars, and the pointer lock")**: a report: on a 16:10 screen the 16:9 picture has bars above and below in fullscreen and the view no longer pans when the pointer goes off the edge of the game (left and right were fine: the picture fills the width). **What was wrong**: the stage, not the canvas, is the fullscreen element, so a pointer over a bar is over the stage: the browser sends the game's canvas nothing (SDL's Emscripten driver listens on the canvas), and the canvas's `mouseleave` becomes SDL_WINDOWEVENT_LEAVE, which sets the game's `pointer_outside_` (no more scrolling: the last position must not keep the map scrolling); measured with the real game in headless Chrome (`ants_probe`): the pointer in the top or bottom bar is gone for the game and the view stays. (SDL 2.28.4 also drops the FIRST leave of a page's life: its window starts with the mouse focus but with `SDL_WINDOW_MOUSE_FOCUS` clear, and `SDL_SendWindowEvent` drops a LEAVE that finds the flag clear; measured as flag 0 and focus 1 right after the game is ready. So the baseline sometimes kept scrolling on the first trip into a bar, and every trip after it stopped.) **The bars**: in fullscreen a pointer over a bar is at the nearest edge pixel of the picture, as in the desktop game, where the window maps the bars to positions outside the picture that `clamp_pointer_event` holds to the edge pixel (the native clamp needed no change; `tests/test_app/test_canvas_layout.cpp` pointer group and `test_app_integration.cpp` 7.8f pin the 16:10 screens 1440 x 900 and 1512 x 982, a real fullscreen window of the dummy driver and the 4:3 picture's pillarbox). The page (1) hands the game a `mousemove` at the clamped pixel for every pointer event over the stage outside the box (`ANTS_PAGE.edgePixel` is the pixel that Emscripten reads, `trunc(clientX - trunc(left))` held to `0 .. floor(size) - 1`: the position `size` would be a pointer that left SDL's window; `ANTS_PAGE.clientFor` is the client position that is read as exactly that pixel; mouse and pen only: a tap's emulated mouse events are not a pointer over a bar), (2) does not pass on the canvas's `mouseleave` towards the stage (a capture listener on the window, so it comes before SDL's whenever that was added) and reports a leave of the canvas when the pointer really goes (the stage's `mouseleave`: another monitor; the page's fullscreen left), and (3) leaves a click, a wheel and a context menu over a bar to the stage (the wheel and the menu are cancelled there). The first pointer that the page hands over is preceded by a leave of the same pixel, which takes SDL's focus away and gives it back (an ENTER sets the flag), so that SDL's flag and focus agree from then on and a leave that the page hands over later is delivered. The browser's arrow is hidden over the fullscreen stage (`cursor: none`: the game draws its own cursor at the edge). The windowed page has its own margin (the next item, "The margin of a windowed page"). **The pointer lock**: on a Mac the Dock and the menu bar come up whenever the pointer touches the screen's edge, even in browser fullscreen, and a page can only prevent that by locking the pointer. The click on the Fullscreen button asks for the lock (`canvas.requestPointerLock()` in the same gesture; once more at `fullscreenchange`; a click on the game asks again where the browser let it go, and where the page has held the lock before that click is only that, the game does not get it) when the browser's own fullscreen is made (not the page's own: an iPhone has neither) and the setting is on. While the pointer is locked the browser sends the canvas relative motion (`movementX / movementY`, the position frozen): the page swallows that (a capture listener on the window stops it before SDL's), keeps the game's own cursor (`ANTS_PAGE.lockStart`: the middle of the picture, or the click's position when a click took the lock; `ANTS_PAGE.moveLocked`: the same CSS pixels as the mouse moved, fractions kept, held to the picture) and hands the game that cursor as ordinary absolute motion, so every edge and corner scrolls from the clamped cursor, the way back from an edge is at once, and a click goes where the cursor is. **Why not SDL's own relative mode** (the page requests the lock itself; SDL's `pointerlockchange` listener sets `has_pointer_lock` for ANY lock, and SDL would then treat all motion as relative): Emscripten's `fillMouseEventData` stores `movementX` as an int32 (a browser that reports fractions would lose them on every event), SDL's relative motion needs the mouse focus and a start position that the page has to supply anyway, and a model in the page is a pure function that `tests/scripts/web_edge_check.js` runs (1000 steps of 0.1 px; the edges, corners and the way back; random motion in six sizes) while SDL's is not ours to test; so the page swallows the browser's `pointerlockchange` (window capture, before SDL's listener on the document) and SDL never hears of the lock. When the lock ends (`exitPointerLock`, Esc in a browser without the Keyboard Lock API, another window with the focus, fullscreen left) the pointer is the normal one at once: nothing is handed to the game (its cursor stays where it was: no jump, no click) and the first report of the free pointer over a bar, where it was when the lock began, is not acted on until it has moved by a pixel. **Esc**: in fullscreen with the setting on the page calls `navigator.keyboard.lock(['Escape'])` (Chromium; it needs a secure context and fullscreen) and `unlock()` when fullscreen ends: Esc then reaches the game and HOLDING it leaves fullscreen (and the lock), as the browser says; elsewhere Esc releases the lock first (Safari leaves fullscreen with it). **The setting**: "Fullscreen mouse: Locked / Free" next to the picture's selector (`.seg button[data-lock]`), remembered in `localStorage` under `ants.pointerlock` (`on` / `off`; only `off` turns the lock off, `ANTS_PAGE.lockSetting`; the control is not shown where there is no `requestPointerLock`); Free also leaves Esc to the browser; the bars' rules are the same in both. **For the tests** the game exports `ants_probe(what)` (read-only: the pointer's x, y, whether it is gone, the view's origin, whether a dialog is open, the zoom). **Checks**: `tests/scripts/test_web_edge.py` and `web_edge_check.js` (the quick tier: the pixel over a bar, `clientFor` against Emscripten's own arithmetic, the locked cursor, the setting's block with a page of fakes, the listeners that must exist), and `tests/scripts/test_web_edge.sh` (opt-in: real headless Chrome with a REAL fullscreen and a REAL pointer lock, whose input gives the page real locked motion): on 1440 x 900 every side and corner of the bars scrolls the right way and the game's pointer is the clamped pixel, a walk from the picture into a bar goes on scrolling, back in the picture it stops, a click, a right click, a wheel and a ctrl + wheel over a bar reach the canvas as nothing, the windowed page is unchanged, fullscreen left with the pointer over a bar and the stage left leave the game's pointer gone, 2560 x 1080 and the classic 4:3 picture have their side bars, the page's own fullscreen does the same and asks for no lock; with the lock: the click on Fullscreen gives both, the cursor starts in the middle and follows the motion exactly (an independent copy of the rule), every edge and corner scrolls, a click opens the Options when the cursor is on its button, the release and the way out of fullscreen leave the cursor alone and unlock Esc, a click takes the lock again, a browser that refuses the lock still has the bars, the setting and its key, a slow mouse at device ratios 1 and 2. (The new checks fail on the previous page: 10 of the 20 bar checks.) **Not tested headless**: the Dock and the menu bar themselves (a real screen), that Esc is the game's key and holding it leaves fullscreen (the browser's own interface does that), the browser's own bubbles, Safari and Firefox (no Keyboard Lock API; Safari wants its gesture inside fullscreen: the click on the game is the second chance), a real mouse's acceleration and fractional movement on a Retina screen, a lost focus. **macOS desktop game**: SDL 2.32.10's window delegate answers the system's fullscreen question with FullScreen | HideDock | HideMenuBar when the window has `SDL_WINDOW_FULLSCREEN_DESKTOP` (read from the installed library's disassembly: `window:willUseFullScreenPresentationOptions:`), so `--fullscreen` and Alt+Enter already keep both away for good; a fullscreen Space that the green button or Cmd+Ctrl+F makes carries no SDL flag when the system asks and gets the system's own proposal (auto-hide: both come up at the edge), so `Application::update_mouse_grab` asks for `FullScreen | HideDock | HideMenuBar` itself while the window is in such a Space (`wants_hidden_dock_and_menu_bar`, `-[NSApplication setPresentationOptions:]` through the runtime, an exception caught) and gives the system's behaviour back when it leaves and at shutdown. Not tried on a real Space here (the Mac's screen was locked): it remains to be tried on a Mac.

* **The margin of a windowed page (`web/shell.html`, "The margin of a windowed page")**: a request: going off the screen with the mouse must not spoil the scrolling (agreed: keep scrolling while the mouse is just past the game's edge, about an inch, stop when it goes farther). The same cause as the bars of the item "The pointer in fullscreen": the browser says that the pointer LEFT the canvas the moment it crosses the edge, and the game takes that for a pointer that went away. A pointer that is outside the box but within `ANTS_EDGE_MARGIN` (96 CSS pixels, one CSS inch; a band around the box with square corners: `ANTS_PAGE.marginPixel`) is handed to the game as a motion at the nearest edge pixel (`edgePixel`), the canvas's leave towards the margin is not passed on (a capture listener on the window; a leave with no `relatedTarget` is the pointer leaving the browser window and is passed on), and the pointer is gone, as it always was, beyond the margin, out of the window (`mouseleave` of the document element), over a control of the page (`ANTS_PAGE.overControl`: `a, button, input, select, textarea, summary, label` and the roles radio and button: hovering the picture's and the mouse's selectors under the game or the links above it never scrolls the map) and when a scroll or a resize of the page moves the box away from a pointer that did not move. Clicks, the wheel and the context menu in the margin are the page's (SDL listens on the canvas; a button release that SDL sees without a press changes nothing in it); the browser's arrow shows there while the game draws its own cursor at the edge. Not for touch, not for a locked pointer, not in fullscreen. SDL 2.28.4 drops the FIRST leave of a page's life (see "What was wrong" in the item "The pointer in fullscreen"), so before this change the first trip from the game onto a button of the page kept the map scrolling once; now the first motion of a mouse over the game is preceded by a leave of its own (`primeFocusOverTheGame`) and every leave is delivered. **Checks**: `tests/scripts/web_edge_check.js` (the quick tier: `marginPixel` on a table and on a sweep against an independent copy of the rule, `overControl`), `tests/scripts/test_web_edge.py` (the listeners and the constant), and `tests/scripts/test_web_edge.sh --only margin` (opt-in, headless Chrome in a 1800 x 1000 window: 1, 40, 90 and 96 px beyond every edge and 90 px beyond every corner scroll, 97 and 120 px do not, the selectors and links never scroll, a click, a right click, a wheel and a menu in the margin are the page's, leaving the window, scrolling the page under the pointer, a tap, the order of Chrome's and Safari's leave events, the first leave of a page's life).

## 32-Channel Spatial Sound & Soundtrack

The game mixes up to 32 sounds in its own mixer, with volume and left / right pan by distance; in the browser the sound goes through SDL's Web Audio. The sound effects are the 91 sounds of `ants.chd`: the units' voice cues, the explosions and the rest. The soundtrack is the original's four MIDI pieces, `INTRO`, `ANTS2A`, `ANTS2B` and `ANTSFUN3`, played from MP3 renderings of them that the game decodes itself and streams from memory. The sound law and the rest: [`VIEW_AND_HUD.md`](VIEW_AND_HUD.md#sound-and-music), "Sound and music".

## Interactive Asset Catalog

A page that shows every sprite, sound and animation that the game reads from the archive `Original-Ants/ants.chd`, as files you can look at, play and download: **[beta.playants.org/asset_catalog/](https://beta.playants.org/asset_catalog/)**, called "Sprites and sounds" on the site. It is one static page (`asset_catalog/index.html`) with its data (`asset_catalog/catalog_data.js`), the pictures (`asset_catalog/sprites/`) and the sounds (`asset_catalog/sounds/`).

**What it shows.** Three tabs, with a filter box above them that matches an ID or a name (press `/` to reach it, `Esc` to clear it). The page also works on a phone: it scrolls as a document and a table scrolls inside its box.

| Tab | Items | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Sounds** (the tab that opens first) | 91 | WAV sound effects (8-bit PCM). Each row has the sample rate, length and size, a play button, an audio player and a "⬇ WAV" download. The page draws no waveform. |
| **Animations** | 1,344 | Animation sequences on a stage, described below. |
| **Sprites** | 2,794 | 8-bit RGBA PNG files (`sprite_0000.png` and on), converted from the archive's paletted bitmaps. A card for each shows the picture, ID, BMP name, size, file name and the animations that use it, with a one-click "⬇ PNG". A click opens an inspector with zoom levels 1x, 2x, 4x and 8x (it opens at 2x) and "⬇ Download PNG". |

The counts are the archive's own (2,794 sprites, 91 sounds, 1,344 animations); the tests of the asset reader pin them.

**The animation stage.**

- The steps of a sequence are composited from their sprite layers, with the timing of each step and the sounds that a frame triggers (a toggle switches the sound off). The order of the layers is automatic ("Auto (Smart Layering)") or the order of the archive's animation table (Table 4, `chd_table4_animations.json`), reversed or forward.
- Stage background: a checkerboard grid, solid black, neutral gray or game turf green. The picture is centred on its bounding box or on its origin, and a crosshair and the bounds can be shown.
- Zoom: fit to the view, or 25% to 800%. Drag pans, the wheel zooms, a double click fits.
- Playback: play and pause (`Space`), the previous and next frame (arrow keys), a scrubber, and speeds from 0.25x to 2x (1x is 20 frames a second).
- A timeline strip lists the steps, and a table lists the layers of the active step, each with a show / hide box and a PNG download. "⬇ Frame PNG" downloads the whole composite frame.

**Opening it.** Online, at the address above. Locally, open the file in a browser: `open asset_catalog/index.html` on macOS, `xdg-open asset_catalog/index.html` on Linux, `start asset_catalog\index.html` on Windows. The data and the files are relative to the page, so it works from a checkout. Its look is the site's stylesheet, `/front/classic.css` (the same Classic look as the front page, with the logo as a link back to it and a **Play** button in the header); opened as a file it shows plain, without that sheet.

The web image serves it at `/asset_catalog/`; `tests/scripts/test_web_catalog.py` pins what the page must keep (the head's two links, the header, the ids that the script uses, the colour contrast).
