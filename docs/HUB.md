# The chat hub

`ants-hub` is the site's global chat: a small service of its own, next to the game server and never in its thread. Python 3.12 or newer, standard library only (the image is 3.12; `python -m hub` refuses to start on an older one, with a message: on 3.11 a logout or a forget raced a handshake that was in progress, and a socket of the ended login stayed open), about two thousand lines (`hub/`). It speaks JSON over one WebSocket, `wss://<site>/hub/ws`, keeps the last 50 lines in memory and seven days of lines in SQLite for moderation, and enforces every limit below itself. Reading is open to everybody. Writing needs a login with Discord ([Login](#login): the one scope `identify`, the id and the username are all that is kept); a hub that is not given the settings of a Discord application tells every client `login:false` and is read-only for all.

The pages never need the hub: the site, the rooms and the games list work whether or not it runs, and the chat shows "offline" and retries.

## The protocol

Text frames, each a JSON object with a `t`. Version 1 (`v` in the welcome).

| Direction | Message | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| up | `{"t":"say","text":"..."}` | a line of chat (needs a login) |
| up | `{"t":"ping"}` | keep-alive; the answer is `online` |
| down | `{"t":"welcome","v":1,"you":{"name","mod"}\|null,"login":bool,"online":n,"history":[line,...]}` | first message: who you are (`null` for a reader), whether this hub has a login at all, how many sockets are open, the last 50 lines oldest first |
| down | `{"t":"line","id","at","who","text","mod"}` | a new line (`at` is seconds since 1970, `who` the account's name, `mod` true for a moderator) |
| down | `{"t":"del","id":n}` | take line `n` off the screen |
| down | `{"t":"online","n":n}` | the number of open sockets, sent when it changes (at most every 5 seconds) and as the answer to `ping` |
| down | `{"t":"err","code":c}` | the last message was refused; the page owns the words |

Error codes: `login` (a reader tried to write), `rate`, `muted`, `banned`, `long`, `empty`, `young` (a Discord account younger than 7 days, from its id), `bad` (the message cannot be read, the line holds `<` or `>`, or a moderator's command is wrong). A reader or a member gets no other reply to a good `say` than the `line` that everybody gets.

## Limits

All in `hub/limits.py` (`Limits`), all enforced by the hub.

| What | Limit |
|---|---|
| frame from a client | 2 KB of payload; text only, in one piece (a fragment, a binary frame, a frame over 2 KB closes the socket: 1003, 1003, 1009) |
| a chat line | 200 code points after the text rules (NFC) and 800 bytes |
| writing | a burst of 3, then one line every 2 seconds, for an account across all its tabs; and 5 lines a second (a burst of 50) for all accounts together |
| the same text twice | within 30 seconds the second is dropped (`rate`); a dropped line costs no token |
| mistakes | an `err` or an unreadable message is a mistake; 20 within a minute close the socket with 1008. A `rate` that comes from the limit of all accounts together or from a full database is not the client's doing and is not counted |
| frames of any kind | a burst of 10, then 5 a second for a socket; the rest are mistakes. A refusal that is not counted as a mistake (`rate` from the limit of all accounts together, from a full or a locked database) is not free: it costs the socket one frame of this budget more, so a client that keeps asking runs out of it twice as fast, and then it is a mistake per frame |
| sockets | 8 for each client address (an IPv6 address counts as its /64), 500 in all; the ninth gets 429, the 501st 503, before the upgrade |
| keep-alive | the hub pings every 25 seconds; a socket that sends nothing for 60 seconds is closed (1001) |
| handshake | 10 seconds to send the request head, 8 KB at most, 64 connections that have not finished it (the login layer has 5 seconds to answer, and a handshake that waits for it keeps its place among the 64 meanwhile; a late or failing login is a reader) |
| a slow reader | a socket with 128 KB unsent is dropped; a socket that was closed with output still unsent is cut after 5 seconds |
| history | the last 50 lines, sent on connect; 7 days in the database (a newcomer is never shown a line past them), and no new line is kept while the pages of `hub.db` that hold something, plus its log file, are 64 MB (`rate`; the pages that deleted lines free count as room again, though the file does not shrink; a mute, a ban and a deletion are never refused for it) |
| new sockets | nginx lets 5 a second through for each client address (a burst of 10) and 16 open at a time; beyond that is 503 and 429 before the hub is asked |
| the login's pages | `/hub/login` and `/hub/callback`: 10 a minute for each client address (a burst of 10 in the hub, of 6 in nginx), 6 logins that wait for Discord at one time (the 7th is answered at once), 10 seconds for Discord's two calls in all, 10,000 logins started and not finished, 10 logins kept for an account and 100,000 in all (the oldest go); `/hub/me`, `/hub/logout`, `/hub/forget`: a burst of 20, then one every 3 seconds |

## The handshake

nginx (see [the site's nginx](#the-sites-nginx)) passes `GET /hub/ws` for a socket, and the five pages of [the login](#login). The hub checks, in this order, and answers with JSON and a closed connection (`{"error":"..."}`): the method (405), the **Origin**, which must be exactly the site's (`ANTS_HUB_PUBLIC_URL`; 403 for anything else and for none, and a hub that was not told its site lets nobody in), **Sec-Fetch-Site**, which a browser sets and which must be `same-origin` where it is there at all (403 `fetch_site`), no body (400), `Upgrade: websocket` with `Connection: Upgrade` (426), version 13 (426), a valid key (400), the 500 socket total (503) and the 8 sockets of the address (429). The request head is read strictly: CRLF only, no folded lines, no control characters, at most 60 headers, and the headers the hub believes at most once. Extensions and subprotocols are never negotiated. Who the socket is comes last, from the login's cookie of the handshake ([Login](#login)), and holds for the life of the socket.

The client address is the `X-Client-Address` that nginx sets (an IP address; anything else falls back to the connection's own). `GET /hub/healthz` answers `{"ok":true}` for the image's health check; nginx does not route it. The 64 connections that have not finished their handshake are a shared pool: through nginx a connection reaches the hub only with a complete head, but any container on the stack's network can open 64 silent ones and hold the door for 10 seconds at a time, and can set `X-Client-Address` to anything; the network is trusted (the game server's ports are in the same position), so keep no container there that you do not trust.

## Frames

`hub/ws.py` is the whole WebSocket (RFC 6455, the server side of text frames, about 150 lines). A client frame must be masked, have no reserved bit, use a known opcode and the shortest length form; control frames are at most 125 bytes and whole; text must be UTF-8. A broken stream gets a close frame with the RFC's code (1002 broken, 1003 not taken, 1007 not UTF-8, 1009 too big), and the frames that came whole before the break are still handled. A frame over the cap is refused from its header, before its payload is read. The parser never raises (`Parser.feed` returns frames and sets `error`) and keeps at most one header and one frame.

## Text rules

What a visitor types is data (`hub/text.py`). In order: NFC; every blank (space, tab, line end, other space characters, line and paragraph separators) becomes one plain space and runs collapse; control characters, format characters (the bidirectional controls, zero-width characters, joiners, the byte order mark, tag characters), surrogates, private use and unassigned code points are removed, and so are the letters that draw as blanks (the Hangul fillers, the braille blank, the combining grapheme joiner, the Khmer inherent vowels, the musical null notehead); more than 4 combining marks in a row are cut; NFC again. Then: nothing left, or nothing but combining marks (a variation selector or the Khitan filler shows nothing by itself), is `empty`; over the limits is `long`; a `<` or `>` is `bad` (checked on the result, so nothing can hide one). There is no markup, no link detection and no mention: the page shows the text with `textContent` only and puts a `bdi` or an isolating `dir` around names and lines, because right-to-left letters still reorder what is next to them.

Names are what a Discord username is: lower-case letters, digits, `_` and `.`, 2 to 32 characters. The hub refuses an identity with any other name.

## Moderation

Mutes, bans and deletions are kept by account id in `hub.db`, so they survive a restart, a new session and a rename. A banned account can still read.

From a terminal on the machine (no network door; run it as the image's own user, which `docker exec` does by default, so that the database files stay the hub's):

```text
docker exec ants-hub python -m hub.admin list [--lines N]      bans, active mutes, the newest lines with their ids
docker exec ants-hub python -m hub.admin mute ACCOUNT MINUTES  (0 lifts a mute; at most a year)
docker exec ants-hub python -m hub.admin ban ACCOUNT
docker exec ants-hub python -m hub.admin unban ACCOUNT
docker exec ants-hub python -m hub.admin del LINE_ID
```

`ACCOUNT` is an account id (digits, no leading zero), or a name that has said something in the last 7 days (`@name` when the name is all digits). A mute or a ban counts from the account's next line; a deleted line leaves every screen within two seconds (the hub looks every two seconds). Exit status: 0 done, 1 nothing to do, 2 wrong use.

In the chat, the accounts listed in `ANTS_HUB_MODS` (digits, comma separated) may type `/mute name minutes` (1 to 10080, never a moderator) and `/del id`. A name means the account that is in the chat with it; when two accounts are, or when nobody is and no line of that name is kept, the command is refused (`bad`): use the command line with the account id. The command is not a chat line; a wrong one is answered `bad`. For everybody else a line that starts with `/` is text.

## Data

The hub, the login and the command line reach the data only through the interface of `hub/store/base.py`: lines, deletions (a counter that only goes up), purge, mutes, bans, logins, forget and size. A store that cannot do one now (locked, full, out of reach) raises `StoreError`, which is the only error a caller catches. `hub/store/sqlite.py` is the one backend and the default; what only it has (the log file's cut, `secure_delete`) stays on its class. `tests/scripts/test_hub_store_contract.py` runs the same checks against any backend. The rest of this section describes the SQLite one.

`/data/hub.db` (SQLite, WAL, file mode 600; the volume `ants-hub-data`): `lines` (id, time, account id, name, text, a moderator flag, a deletion mark), `mutes`, `bans`, `sessions` (the logins, below). A line is kept 7 days and then deleted for good: the hub does it every hour, 2,000 lines at a time (a long backlog goes over several seconds, not in one wait), the freed pages are zeroed (`secure_delete`) and the log file is cut; when a reader (for example `hub.admin list`) holds the file at that moment, the cut is tried again every 10 seconds, and once at every start. The size guard keeps the database from growing past the 64 MB for lines, so the hub does not fill the volume by itself; when the disk refuses a write, or another process holds the database's write lock for longer than the hub waits (0.3 seconds), the line or the moderator's command is not done and the sender is told: `rate` for a line (not counted as a mistake, the line did not cost a token, and the same text may be tried again at once) and `bad` for a command, the socket stays, and one `error` line (the class of the error, never the text) is logged every 10 seconds at the most; the command line says "the database refused it" in one line. Any other database error closes the socket that caused it (1011). Deletions are numbered by a counter in `meta` that a purge cannot lower, so a mod's `/del` still reaches every screen after old lines were purged. A purge that fails (another process holds the write lock, the disk refuses) is tried again after a minute, and the hub's writes wait at most 0.3 seconds for another process, so the one thread is never held for seconds. A line that a moderator deleted stays, marked, until its 7 days are up. The last 50 lines that are not deleted and not past their time are the history after a restart. A login (`sessions`) holds the SHA-256 of its cookie's value, the account id, the username as it was when the visitor logged in, and the time it ends; the hub deletes the ones that ran out with the lines past their time. Nothing else about a person is stored. **Forget and logout leave nothing in the log file:** a forget cuts the log file (`hub.db-wal`) before it answers, so that the lines, the name and the hashes of the logins are in neither file (the freed pages are zeroed, and when a reader holds the file the cut is tried again every 10 seconds); the end of a login (a logout, a login that replaces the old one of a browser, one that the caps push out) has the cut done within 10 seconds, one cut for any number of them. **The schema is 3** (`PRAGMA user_version`; the `sessions` table came with it). A hub of an earlier version does not start on such a database (`RuntimeError: hub.db is from a newer hub (schema 3)`), so a rollback of the hub to a version before the login needs the volume of before it (or a new volume): the file is never changed back.

## Login

The hub core asks a `Sessions` object (`hub/session.py`) who is on the other end of a handshake: `identify(headers)` returns an `Identity(account_id, name, young, session, expires)` or `None`. An account id is digits (1 to 20: a Discord id), `young` says the account is too new to write. `identify` is a coroutine (a real login looks something up); the hub waits 5 seconds for it. Without the settings of a Discord application the hub has `NoLogin`: nobody is identified, the welcome says `login:false`, and the hub is read-only for everyone. With them it has `DiscordLogin` (`hub/login.py`, `hub/discord.py`); the core and its tests do not change.

**What is asked of Discord, and what is kept.** The one scope `identify` (the visitor's id and username), through the authorization code grant: the visitor is sent to `https://discord.com/oauth2/authorize` with `client_id`, `response_type=code`, `scope=identify`, `redirect_uri=<ANTS_HUB_PUBLIC_URL>/hub/callback`, a `state` and `prompt=none` (a visitor who agreed before does not see the consent page again). The code that comes back is exchanged by the hub, never by the page (`POST /api/v10/oauth2/token`, a form body with the client secret), and the access token is used once, for `GET /api/v10/users/@me`. **Only the id and the username are kept.** The access token and the refresh token are local variables of one function and are gone when it returns; the email, the avatar and everything else of the answer are never looked at. Every answer of Discord is data: https only, the certificate checked, 3 seconds to connect, 6 for a call and 10 for the two, no redirect followed, at most 32 KB read, JSON of the shape that was asked for or no login (a name that is not a name, an id that is not an id, a token that is not a plain word). PKCE is not used: Discord's reference for the authorization code grant mentions it once, in passing (a warning at the implicit grant), and describes no parameter for it; its guide for web logins says a server-side client authenticates with its client secret and that PKCE is for public clients such as native and mobile apps. The login is therefore held by the `state` (below), and the client secret that only the hub has.

**The pages** (nginx passes these five and the socket; `hub/login.py`):

| Page | What it does |
|---|---|
| `GET /hub/login?next=home\|room` | a random 128-bit `state` is kept in the hub (one use, 10 minutes, at most 10,000 at a time) and in a cookie `__Host-ants_oauth` (`Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax`, because Discord's way back is a navigation from another site), and the visitor is sent to Discord. Only a request of the front page's own button (or the address typed: `Sec-Fetch-Site` `same-origin` or `none`, or none at all) starts a login; a link or an image of another site (`cross-site`, `same-site`) gets a small page, and nginx turns it away before it spends the visitor's allowance, as does the hub (a hostile page cannot use up a visitor's tries). `next` is one of two words, and the way back is `/` or `/#room`: never an address that was sent |
| `GET /hub/callback?code&state` | the state of the cookie, of the address and of the hub must be one, the hub forgets it at once (one callback for a state, whatever it ends in), the code is exchanged, a login is kept and the visitor goes back to the front page with the cookie `__Host-ants_hub`. A replayed callback, a code that Discord has seen, a state that ran out, a missing or doubled cookie, a visitor who said no at Discord, a Discord that is down: a small page with a link back (`Login unavailable, try again later` for a Discord that does not answer or answers wrongly), never a login, and the sockets of everybody else are never held up |
| `GET /hub/me` | `{"login":true,"you":{"name","mod"}\|null}`, `no-store`; only for the front page's own request |
| `POST /hub/logout` | ends this login (the row is deleted, the cookie cleared, the sockets that it let in are closed with 1000) |
| `POST /hub/forget` | ends every login of the account and deletes every chat line that it wrote (the screens drop them at once), and wipes the name from its mute and its ban (the screens are told of the newest 100 of the lines, one `del` frame each: a page keeps 100 lines at most, and a forget of 300,000 lines must not be 300,000 frames for every socket on the hub's one thread; the database work for the largest backlog that the limits allow holds the hub for under a second). A ban or a mute stays, by id: **neither a logout nor forgetting lifts one** |

`/hub/me`, `/hub/logout` and `/hub/forget` answer only what the front page itself asked: the `Origin` must be the site's (a POST always has one) **and** `Sec-Fetch-Site` must be `same-origin` (403 otherwise, nothing done); a body is refused (400), a query is 404. The cookie is also `SameSite=Strict`, so a request of another site does not carry it at all.

**The cookie.** `__Host-ants_hub=<32 hex digits>; Path=/; Max-Age=2592000; Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Strict`: 128 random bits, 30 days, of which only the SHA-256 is kept (a copy of the database logs nobody in), a new value for every login (a value that a visitor already had is never taken over, and the old row is deleted), at most 10 for an account. The `__Host-` prefix makes a browser refuse the cookie unless it is `Secure`, has `Path=/` and no `Domain`, and stops a sibling subdomain from setting or replacing it ("cookie tossing"); that is why the path is `/` and not `/hub`, and why nginx narrows what it passes on: a cookie of the site goes to every request of the site, so [nginx](#the-sites-nginx) passes the login's cookie to the five pages and the socket only, passes no other cookie to the hub, and drops the Cookie header at the game server's doors (`/ws`, `/busy`; the admin page's API gets its own cookie and no other). `HttpOnly`: the page never reads it. A cookie that comes twice, or has the wrong shape, is nobody's.

**A socket's identity** is fixed at the handshake from the cookie and holds for its life: it ends with the login (logout and forget close the sockets of the login or of the account; so does the end of the 30 days, within a second), and a socket never changes who it is. The welcome's `you` is `{name: <Discord username>, mod}`; the page reads nothing else. A banned account can log in and read; it cannot write (`banned`), and a mute stands too. An account younger than `ANTS_HUB_MIN_ACCOUNT_DAYS` (7; the age is in the id's first 42 bits, so no call is needed) gets `young` and cannot write.

A visitor's username is untrusted text: it must be a Discord username (`[a-z0-9_.]{2,32}`) or the login is refused with a page that does not show it; it is only ever written into a JSON body, never into a header, a page or the log. A username that could pass for the hub's own voice (`mod`, `admin`, `system`, and a few more: `RESERVED` in `hub/login.py`) is refused with a page of its own that says why, and so are its lookalikes (dots and underscores are ignored, and a digit is read as the letter that it looks like: `admin.`, `adm1n`, `system_`, `a.d.m.i.n`); digits at the end are not read as anything (`system32`, `hub99` and `ants1998` are fine: a lookalike gains nothing, because a moderator is marked by the hub's `mod` flag, by Discord id, never by a name, and real people have such names).

**What the site's operator does once.** (0) **Before the login goes live:** `docker/trusted-proxies.conf` must name the reverse proxy in front of the site (see [Behind a reverse proxy](#behind-a-reverse-proxy-not-ready-for-the-public-until-this-is-set)). Until it does, every visitor has the proxy's address: the first 6 quick `GET /hub/login` (one, and a burst of 5) from one visitor turn every later login of everybody into "Too many tries" for a minute, and the limits of the sockets (8 for each address, 5 new a second) are the whole site's. (1) Create a Discord application in Discord's developer portal (a name, a picture) and read its client id; (2) under OAuth2, add the redirect `https://<the site>/hub/callback`, and a second one for the staging site; (3) put the client id and the client secret in the stack's environment (`ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_CLIENT_ID`, `ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET`), the secret **only** there, never in a file of this repository, a chat or a pull request; (4) optionally the Discord ids of the moderators in `ANTS_HUB_MODS` (Discord's Developer Mode, "Copy User ID") and the privacy and terms links of the application, which can be [`/legal.html`](../web/legal.html). The hub calls `discord.com` over https, so its container needs outbound network access. Then redeploy the stack, log in once on the real site, and look at the hub's log for a `login` line. Real Discord is never asked by the tests; this step is the first time it is. **Try it with a fresh Discord account** that never authorized the application: the hub asks with `prompt=none`, which is meant to show a first-time visitor the consent page, and that is not verified against the real Discord; if Discord answers such a visitor with an error instead, they see "Login cancelled", and the `prompt` parameter (`hub/discord.py`) is what to change.

**The page** (`web/home/chat.js`, [HOME_SCREEN.md](HOME_SCREEN.md#the-chat-dock)): the Log in button shows only when the welcome says `login:true` and has no `you`, and it is a full navigation to `/hub/login?next=home` (the Room's way is `next=room`), so the tab leaves for Discord and comes back to the same screen; the page's own session storage (`ants.lobby`) is untouched, so the seat of a room is taken back as after any reload. Log out and Forget me are `fetch` POSTs of the page itself; after either the page connects again, as a reader.

Environment variables only (a command line shows in the process list).

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ANTS_HUB_PUBLIC_URL` | the site's address, `https://host`: the one Origin a socket may come from, and the base of the login's way back, `<it>/hub/callback` (no default in the program; the production stack file defaults to the beta site) |
| `ANTS_HUB_MODS` | account ids that may moderate in chat: Discord ids, digits only, comma separated |
| `ANTS_HUB_DB` | the database, `/data/hub.db` in the image |

| `ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_CLIENT_ID`, `ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET` | the Discord application of the login (the id: digits; the secret: 8 to 256 printable characters). Both, with the site's address, switch the login on; with none of them the hub is read-only and says `login:false`; with one of them it is the same, and the start line says so. Never printed, never in a file of the repository: the stack files take them from the stack's environment |
| `ANTS_HUB_MIN_ACCOUNT_DAYS` | a Discord account younger than this many days cannot write: 7 by default, 0 for none, 3650 at the most |
| `ANTS_HUB_TEST_LOGIN` | tests only: the header login of the hub's own tests. The hub refuses to start with it unless it listens on a loopback address, the image listens on every interface so it can never run there, no stack file or image sets it, nginx passes no such header, and it cannot be on together with the Discord login |
| `ANTS_HUB_TEST_DISCORD_URL` | tests only: where the fake Discord of the tests listens (`http://127.0.0.1:<port>`). Refused unless that address and the hub's own are loopback addresses; no stack file or image sets it (`tests/scripts/test_hub_stack.py` checks both) |

A wrong value stops the start with a message that names the variable and never its value (exit status 2).

## Logs

One line for each event on standard output: `<time> <event> key=value ...`. A line never holds a secret, a cookie, a chat line, a name, an account id or a whole address: values are cut to plain characters, and a connection is told apart by a tag, a keyed hash of its address that changes with every start. The start line lists the settings that are safe (the port, the origin, the number of moderators, whether the login is on, the age limit), and a `note` line says that sockets are counted for each address that nginx names. The login writes `login`, `logout` and `forget` (a tag, never an account) and `login_failed` with a kind (`rejected`, `config`, `busy`, `down`, `name`, `database`, `bug`) and Discord's status: nothing that Discord or the visitor sent is in a line, not the code, the state, the token, the cookie, the id or the name. nginx writes no access log for `/hub/ws` and the pages of the login, nor for its answers when the hub is not there (its default format would write the address, the referrer and the browser of every handshake, and the address of `/hub/callback` holds the code and the state; nginx logs a request with the settings of the place where it ends), and the error log of `/hub/login` and `/hub/callback` holds the serious errors only (nginx writes the whole request line, query and all, into the error of a request that a missing upstream fails). The container's log is rotated by docker (`json-file`, 3 files of 10 MB). A `crowded` line (sockets and groups, no address) is written every minute while one address group holds more than 6 sockets. `tests/scripts/test_hub_server.py` and `tests/scripts/test_hub_login.py` start the real process with canary values for every secret (and a fake Discord) and read its log.

## The stack and the site's nginx

`ants-hub` is the third service of `docker-compose.stack.yml` and `docker-compose.staging.yml` (own volume, own container name in staging): built from `Dockerfile.hub` (slim Python, a non-root user, bytecode made at build time), `read_only`, `cap_drop: [ALL]`, `no-new-privileges`, `pids_limit: 32`, `mem_limit: 192m`, a tmpfs at `/tmp`, a health check (`python -m hub.health`), no published port. It has no `ANTS_BUILD_ID` argument: an image depends on `hub/` only, so a deploy that changes nothing there builds the same image and the chat is not restarted. `tools/deploy_filter.py` counts `hub/` and `Dockerfile.hub`.

### The site's nginx

`docker/nginx.conf` has the chat's door, `location = /hub/ws`, and five pages of the login (below): the door is a GET without a query, the name `ants-hub:4040` resolved per request through Docker's resolver (so the site starts, and works, without the hub), 5 new sockets a second for each address (a burst of 10; 503 beyond) and 16 open at a time (429 beyond), 120 seconds of read timeout (the hub pings every 25), no buffering, no access log (one exception, below). The hub gets only the headers of the upgrade, `Origin`, the login's cookie (picked out of the whole `Cookie` header by its name and shape, so that no other cookie of the visitor, the admin page's among them, reaches the hub), `Sec-Fetch-Site` and the one `X-Client-Address` that nginx sets from the connection; nothing else of the visitor's request, and no header of that name from a visitor. A hub that is not there is a JSON 503 `{"error":"hub_off"}`, never a page of the site. nginx counts an IPv6 client by its whole address and the hub by its /64, so for a visitor who has a /64 the hub's 8 are the limit that matters.

The pages of [the login](#login) are exact locations of the same kind (the name resolved per request, only the one method, the checks before anything is passed on, no body, `proxy_redirect off`, no access log): `/hub/login` (a GET; gets `Sec-Fetch-Site` and the address), `/hub/callback` (a GET; gets the cookie of the state and the address; 15 seconds of read timeout, for the 10 that the hub gives Discord), `/hub/me`, `/hub/logout` and `/hub/forget` (the login's cookie, `Origin`, `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin` required here as well, no query). The first two have 10 a minute for each address (a burst of 5 more; the hub has the same), the others 20; over the limit is 429, and for the two that the browser navigates to a small page with a link back, as is a hub that is not there (503, "Login unavailable, try again later"); the other three answer with the JSON of the chat's door. The login's cookie is for the path `/`, so a browser sends it to every request of the site, and nginx makes sure that it goes to the hub's pages and nowhere else: the game server's doors that pass the visitor's headers on (`/ws`, `/busy`) drop the `Cookie` header, every other door passes none of the visitor's headers, and the admin page's API gets its own cookie (`ants_admin`) and no other. `tests/scripts/test_nginx_hub_login.py` checks every proxy block of the file for it, and runs the real blocks. **The one exception to "no access log":** a request that nginx itself refuses before it has chosen a place (a `Cookie` header over 8 KB is 400, an address over 8 KB is 414) is written by the site's stock access log with its whole request line, query included. For `/hub/callback` that line can hold a `code` and a `state`. Neither is of any use to a reader of the log: the hub refuses a callback without the visitor's own `HttpOnly` state cookie, the state is good for one use and ten minutes, and a code is good once and only with the application's client secret. It is still worth knowing for anybody who keeps the site's access logs for long. It is not closed in `docker/nginx.conf`: that would mean a conditional `access_log` for the whole server, which changes what the site logs for every other page.

### Behind a reverse proxy: not ready for the public until this is set

The hub counts sockets for each address that nginx puts in `X-Client-Address`, and nginx knows the address of the connection it sees. Behind a reverse proxy that is the proxy's address, for every visitor, until `docker/trusted-proxies.conf` is filled in ([MAP_SUBMISSIONS.md](MAP_SUBMISSIONS.md), "Deploying"). Until then **all visitors are one address**: the hub's 8 sockets for each address are 8 for the whole site (one visitor with 8 tabs locks everybody else out), and nginx's 5 new sockets a second are 5 for the whole site. **The chat must not be switched on in a public page until that setting is filled in.** The staging site sits behind the same kind of proxy ([WORKFLOW.md](WORKFLOW.md#deploy-from-ci-and-the-staging-site)), so its web container needs the file mounted as well. The hub says it at every start (a `note` line) and writes a `crowded` line when one address group holds more than 6 sockets.

Even then an address group is a /64 for IPv6, and a visitor who owns a /48 has 65,536 of them: such a visitor can still open the hub's 500 sockets from many groups (8 each) and keep them with ping answers. The limits that bound it are the 500 in all, nginx's 5 new sockets a second for each address, and the 60 seconds of silence; the answer to a real attack is at the proxy in front, which is outside this service.

### Deploys

A push that changes `hub/`, `Dockerfile.hub`, `docker/nginx.conf` or a stack file counts for the deploy job (`tools/deploy_filter.py`), like any file an image copies: the job waits for a moment with no match running on the game server (`/busy`), then calls the stack's webhook. The webhook rebuilds the stack from the clone, and every image of the page and of the game server carries the build id of the commit (`docker/resolve_build_id.sh`: the commit named by the clone's `.git`), so **a hub-only change also rebuilds and recreates the page and the game server**, after the wait for an idle server (the game server brings its matches back from the restart records; the page is away for a moment). The hub has no build id: its container is recreated only when `hub/` or `Dockerfile.hub` changed, so a deploy for something else does not restart the chat. There is no way to deploy one service of the stack from the webhook; a change that must not touch the game server waits for a deploy that would happen anyway (documents, tests and the other tools are never deployed).

CI builds the image in the Web job, starts it with the stack's flags, waits for `healthy`, runs the command line against its volume and checks that a socket from another site gets 403.

## Tests

`tests/scripts/test_hub_*.py`, `test_nginx_hub.py` and `test_nginx_hub_login.py` (run by `./run_tests.sh --fast` and CI): the text rules and their laws (`test_hub_text.py`); the framing, the request reader and their fuzz (`test_hub_ws.py`: every first byte against a model, random and mutated streams cut at random places); the chat, limits, store, settings and command line without sockets (`test_hub_core.py`); the hub on real sockets, noise on the wire, and the real process with its log read for secrets (`test_hub_server.py`); the stack, the image and the wiring (`test_hub_stack.py`); the nginx block, run for real where nginx is installed (`test_nginx_hub.py`); the Discord login against a FAKE Discord (`tests/scripts/fake_discord.py`, `test_hub_login.py`: the state, the callback and every way for it to fail, Discord answering with a status, nothing, a drip, a flood, wrong shapes, a redirect, the cookie, the socket's identity, young and banned accounts, logout and forget, the log read for canaries, the real process), and its nginx pages, run for real in front of the real hub (`test_nginx_hub_login.py`). `ANTS_HUB_FUZZ_SCALE=10` runs the fuzz ten times longer. The client in `tests/scripts/hub_client.py` and the browser of `tests/scripts/login_flow.py` share no code with the hub. The page's part is in [HOME_SCREEN.md](HOME_SCREEN.md#the-chat-dock). Real Discord is not asked by any test; the first real login is by hand.

`tests/data/hub_mutants.json` breaks the hub on purpose (the bidirectional strip, the rate limit, anonymous writing, the Origin check, the history size, the ban, the mute, a secret in the log, the frame cap, and more): `python3 tools/mutate.py tests/data/hub_mutants.json --keep-going --jobs 1`; every mutant must be caught. `tests/data/hub_login_mutants.json` does the same for the login (a state that is not checked or can be used twice, a cookie without its flags, a token that is kept or logged, a secret in the log, a return address from the query, a logout without the Origin check, young or banned accounts that may write, a session id kept raw, an expired session accepted, a header that is believed, a replayed code, a callback address in nginx's log, ...).
