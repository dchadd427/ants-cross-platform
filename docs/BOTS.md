## What a bot is

A bot is a **virtual client**: a seat in a match whose commands are produced by a program instead of a person. It sits next to the simulation, never inside it. It reads the world through a read-only view, decides, and sends `ants::sim::Command`s through exactly the door a human uses.

## The rule

Project rule 5 ([`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md)) allows bots as virtual clients, in the `ants_ai` library only:

- A bot uses the public command interface of a human: a `Command` (group move, special, attack, stop, hatch, the alliance commands) through the `CommandSink` of a local game or the sequencer of a room. `SimulationEngine::apply_command` validates every command, so a bot has a human's powers and cannot break a rule.
- A bot never changes the simulation, the original screens, the lock-step rules of the network protocol or any golden state hash. A normal game runs no bot code.
- **Off by default, always visible.** A bot exists only when asked for (command line, room specification). A bot seat is shown as a bot: its name is `Bot (Level)`, the room marks the seat as a bot, and a person cannot take a name that looks like a bot's (the lobby renames them: spaces and case do not matter), while a bot's name always carries the marker. (In a local game `--team-name` / `-N` may rename a seat; a room never does.)
- **Fair.** A bot knows nothing that a human of its team could not know, has a command budget per difficulty level (see below) and does not move before the people it plays against can: the simulation does not run while the "Get ready to play!" dialog that opens every match is up (v0.2.0: the match clock waits for it), so no bot looks or orders while it is up, and it opens with one token, not a burst. The machine that runs the bots (a LAN host, the server's referee) starts the match before a remote person's screen does, by the link's delay plus the jitter buffer (see "Fairness in detail"). Fog of War is rarely used; with fog off every player sees all ants, so a bot reads the whole world. **A bot together with fog is refused** until a per-team view exists.
- The only computer behaviour inside the simulation stays the original's own: a Combat Ant that has had no order for 2 seconds punches the first enemy its search finds (within 2 tiles when it arrives at a tile, within 3 when it is idle without a path) and after the fight is given its saved order again (the auto-engage reflex).

## Fairness in detail

**What a bot sees (`BotView`).** Its own ants (type, tile, hit points, state, whether it carries food), every other team's ants (type, tile, **hit points**, whether they carry food, and their state **as the screen draws it**), the numbers of the score boxes (a team's score plus its ally's, never below 0, not the individual scores; 0 for a team that is not in the match or has dropped out, because its box is covered), its own egg count and incubation, the food piles (remaining units, points per unit; a lunchbox is always reported as worth 30 points, because the picture of a lunchbox does not tell what the ant that died carried), the power-ups that lie on the map, the flower droppers with the droplet that is falling (its kind and how long it has fallen: the picture shows both), the map, the clock, and an alliance invitation waiting for it. A bot does **not** see other teams' eggs or incubation, other teams' carried points, or any ant's order. It never reads the news or audio queues (they belong to the screen). (Complete since B2.)

**The state of another team's ant** is read off the clip that is drawn, not off the engine's label. The engine labels an ant "walking" the moment it is ordered, but its sprite keeps the idle clip until the path manager has delivered the path (one path per tick and team: the last of 24 ants waits more than a second) and while it waits behind a blocker; a person sees a standing ant, so the view says idle (the idle label of the ant's type: guard for a Combat Ant, swimming for a swimmer on water). Own ants keep the engine's label. `has_pending_path` answers only for the seat's own ants and `predict_ack` only counts them, so neither says what another team's ants were told; the test AI1.25 compares the views of two engines that differ only in what other teams hide (carried points, eggs, an egg incubating, orders in flight) and requires them to be identical, for every seat.

**Accepted deviations** (a bot sees a little more than a person, and nothing that decides a game; each is listed so that nobody is surprised): **the hit points of every ant, the other teams' too** (the owner's decision on what players know, 2026-10-05: "they see all the ants' health in their view as a number: 10 is full, 1 is 1 hp left, 0 is dead"; the original draws a health bar over a selected enemy ant, one at a time; a bot reads them all, 0 to 10, `sim::AntUnit::MAX_HP`, and an ant it cannot see gives nothing: with Fog of War, which bots are refused anyway, an ant outside the team's view is not in the view), the exact egg stock (the tray draws at most nine eggs), the exact units of a pile (a person counts the bites), and **the owner and the remaining life of fire walls and bridges** on the grid that `BotView::grid()` lends (the screen draws every fire wall and every bridge alike). Decision on the last one: it is accepted, not hidden. A bot that watches continuously would know the same by noting the moment it first saw a wall (a wall burns for a fixed 3,600 ticks) and who lit it is the fire ant standing next to it; hiding it would mean a copy of the grid at every look (144 kilobytes on a 60 x 60 map, 2.6 megabytes on a 256 x 256 one, against a look that costs 1.6 to 3 microseconds now) or a different type for the grid that `MapInfo`, the tests and the bots share. `BotView::grid()` is the one place to change if it ever matters.

**`predict_ack` is the prediction a person's click gets, not a copy of `apply_command`.** It is the engine's own `predict_order_ack` (the lock-step client's immediate feedback), and the differential test AI1.5b holds it against `apply_command` over typed ants, a lake, rocks, bombs, a fire wall, a team that drops out and tiles off the map (about 4,200 random orders from random seats in six worlds: 91 percent agree). It differs in exactly two ways, which the test pins: (1) a special order (bomb, fire wall, bridge) of a Bomber, Fire ant or Swimmer onto a tile where a walking ant stands is predicted 0 (the cursor's rule refuses any occupant) but acknowledged by `apply_command` (the order's rule refuses only an ant that stands still); (2) a goal that the engine's goal adjustment then refuses, such as a water tile with nothing enterable within four tiles, is predicted as acknowledged but gets 0 from `apply_command` (status `Applied`). So 0 is not a promise of a refusal and non-zero is not a promise that the order will be carried out: a bot checks the next view.

**The opening (v0.2.0): a bot does not move while the dialog is up, and starts with one token.** Every match opens with the original's "Get ready to play!" dialog (string 105). It takes every click and every key for at least 5 s: the original's task KWFO closes it 5000 ms after it was made (`docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`, section 21: Ants.exe `0x10254b0`). In the original the match clock and the ants run behind it, so a person loses those seconds; **the remake deviates on purpose, at the owner's request: the simulation does not run while the dialog is up** (a local game counts it in real time, a match of the network starts its first turn 5 s after the match began: network protocol 12), tick 0 runs when it closes, and a person can select and order from the first tick. A bot is a virtual client of the simulation: `BotController::on_tick` is called after every simulation tick and nowhere else, so while the dialog is up no bot looks and no bot orders (the tests assert it in a local game, in a LAN host's room and in a server's room: AI6.9, AI6.10, S3.76, N5.75) and nothing needs to be held back, which is why v0.1.1's hold of 100 ticks is gone. What the opening still gives is the human-like start: a bot looks first on tick 1 + seat (the stagger of the seats is kept, so not every seat looks on the same tick), its first order leaves one reaction time later, and its token bucket starts with ONE token (and refills from the first tick at the level's rate, up to its depth), so its first orders come one by one, as a person's first clicks do, instead of in a burst of up to ten on one tick (the bucket of v0.1.0 started full). History: v0.1.0 started the bucket full and looked on tick 1 + seat; v0.1.1 held the bots for the dialog's 100 ticks while the simulation ran behind it (first look on tick 100 + seat, one token, no refill before tick 101); v0.2.0 has the first look of v0.1.0 and the one token of v0.1.1. **The first orders of a bot come at about 0.5 s into the match at Hard** (a look on tick 1 + seat, a reaction of 6 to 10 ticks: tick 7 to 14), **1.3 s at Medium** (a reaction of 18 to 30 ticks: tick 19 to 34) **and 3.1 s at Easy** (a reaction of 45 to 75 ticks: tick 46 to 79); the match begins for everybody after the 5.0 s of the dialog, which no longer count against it. The opening is the same in every product path: a local game (`--bot`, the desktop start menu), the bot seats of a LAN host, the rooms of a dedicated server and the arena, because `BotController::set_start_hold` has the one constant `ai::kStartHoldTicks` = 1 as its default (the first tick: the first on which a seat looks, releases and refills; a hold of N ticks still works, the tests of the mechanism use 100). Only the tests that measure something else from tick 0 switch it off (`set_start_hold(0)`, `ArenaSpec::start_hold = 0`, each with a comment): with it off the controller is exactly the controller of v0.1.0 (a full bucket). The dialog's own length is another number for another thing, `sim::kMatchStartDialogMs` = 5000 (the HUD's dialog and the hosts' start delay: `net::kMatchStartDelayMs`). There is no wire change for the bots (they run on one machine and their commands travel as data); a match without bots is as every match is. On a dedicated server the referee, which runs the bots, and on a game on the local network the host, whose machine runs them, are ahead of a remote person's screen by the delay of the link plus the buffer of the lock-step runner (measured by the independent review of v0.2.0 with loopback links: 20 and 150 ms on links of 20 and 150 ms one way, 85 and 290 ms on links of 60 and 250 ms with 40 ms of jitter), so a Hard bot's first order can be applied a few ticks before a person's first click can reach the host: the head start that remains is that delay, not seconds.

**How fast a bot acts.** After the opening above, three limits, all in simulation ticks (50 ms): it looks at the world only every `decision_interval` ticks; every command of one look is released after a `reaction_delay` (plus or minus 25 percent, drawn ONCE per look from the seat's own random generator, so the commands of a look leave in the order they were proposed and a later look never overtakes an earlier one); among commands that are due on the same tick the newer one wins for an ant they both name (like the later click of a person); and a token bucket limits commands per second (one token per command, whatever the number of ants). A command that cannot be paid waits and is dropped after a time-to-live because the world has moved on. The one exception is the later click of a timed chain ("Timed clicks" in "Islands"): it leaves at a tick that the sink's own clock fixes, not after a reaction delay, and is paid for all the same (the bucket goes into debt).

**What a bot may command.** Nothing a human could not click: at most 24 ants per command (a chosen conservative cap: the remake's HUD lets a person send up to 32, and the original's limit has not been established), special orders (bomb, defuse, fire, extinguish, bridge) and thief raids name exactly one ant and never a tile that a living ant stands on (the HUD shows the target cursor of a special order only where no ant is under the pointer: the controller refuses it, `Fate::Filtered`), no `Quit`, no `Drop`, never an attack on an ally (a break of the alliance comes first and a bot never does that to attack), no command for another seat's ants.

**The human's zoom is no part of any of this.** The mouse-wheel zoom changes what a person's screen shows, never what the simulation holds: a bot reads the world through `BotView`, which has no camera and no view (a bot has no zoom), and no state hash reads the zoom. Every kind of match has the same zoom levels: a local game against `--bot` seats, a server's room, a LAN game, and a match that the leader's START filled with bots (up to v0.2.0 a match of the network offered no zoom-out). What the zoom-out adds to a person's view, and that with Fog of War on every level draws what the zoom 1 draws: `docs/NETWORK_PORT.md`, "The view's zoom and the network".

**Honesty.** Bots are deterministic given the match seed and the seat (integer arithmetic, a private random generator, no clock), so a match can be reproduced and a recorded match replays from its commands alone, without any bot.

## What the game looks like to a bot (measured)

Numbers measured on the headless engine (release build) with the worker bot of B3 (the scores per map are in "Measurements" below and pinned by a test), see `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` for the rules themselves.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| What counts | The score: food deposited, plus loot stolen, minus loot lost, minus 200 per hatch. Kills, losses and ant counts are shown on the results screen but never ranked. |
| Food | Finite, shared, never respawns (the reachable points are pinned by test AI1.15). Points per map: TINY 4800, SMALL 4000 (3000 reachable on foot), MEDIUM 4900, GAUNTLET 1800 (1500 reachable), ISLANDS 5600 (none reachable on foot), TREASURE 10950 (8850 reachable). |
| Harvesting | One group move onto a pile starts a loop that runs by itself until the pile is empty: bite one unit, walk home, enter (22 ticks), score, walk back. The economy needs well under one command per second. |
| The hill gate | A hill banks about one deposit per 100 ticks (5 s) once three ants queue. Income is therefore capped near 11 deposits a minute and extra workers beyond 4 to 6 add nothing (more can lower it). |
| Hatching | Always an ant of the level's default type (a plain worker on the six shipped maps; see "Typed ants"), costs 200 points and needs 200, 160 ticks of incubation and 11 more (the emerge clip) before the worker takes an order, one egg at a time, eggs per team TINY 3, SMALL 2, MEDIUM 6, GAUNTLET 6, ISLANDS 4, TREASURE 9. A lone worker that hatches once helps a little on TINY (seat 0 gains 190 to 220 points, the mean of the four seats 35) and loses on the other maps (SMALL -6, MEDIUM -10, GAUNTLET -19, TREASURE -7 percent); one that hatches whenever it can loses 9 to 86 percent on four maps and 21 percent on TINY (see "Measurements"). |
| Typed ants | Only power-ups make Bomber, Fire, Thief, Combat and Swimmer ants: an ant walks onto the power-up and keeps the type for life. **The exception is a level that names a default ant type** (block 3 of the file: 99 of the 540 community maps that load; none of the six shipped maps): every ant of every team, the starting ones and every one that hatches, is of that type from the first second (Combat: the punch and the reflex; Bomber: mines; Fire: fire walls; Swimmer: water and bridges; Thief: the look, the voices, but no raids until it takes the power-up). `AntView::type` shows what the ant is (it is what the sprite and the panel show) and `BotView::default_ant_type()` is the level's default, so the worker bot's pool is "the ants of the default type"; the own type field, which a pick-up and a death look at, is not visible to a person and not to a bot (`docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 5.62). |
| Fighting | Every ant has 10 hit points; one blow per order (1 hp, a Combat Ant 2 hp); an ant at 1 hp walks home and heals. Fights are decided by who strikes first. |
| Thieves | A raid takes up to 50 points from a hill and cannot be interrupted at the raid tile; three fire walls (or bombs) on the tiles east of the hill stop it. |
| ISLANDS | No hill can walk to any food. A bomber plants a bomb inland of a shore tile and an ant that walks onto it is thrown over the channel. |
| Contested play | With four players the whole reachable pot is exhausted on every shipped map, so scores are shares of a pot. |

## Architecture

```
SimulationEngine --(const)--> BotView (+ MapInfo, built once) --> Bot::think(view, orders)
                                                   |
                          BotController: schedule, reaction delay, budget
                                                   |
        CommandSink, issuer = the bot's seat --> apply_command / the sequencer of a room
```

`ants_ai` (namespace `ants::ai`) depends only on `ants_sim`; it has no SDL, no sockets, no threads and no clock, so it builds for the web too. Adapters that talk to the network live in the application (`NetBotSink`) and the server.

**What B1 built** (include/ants_ai, src/ants_ai, tests/test_ai): `rng.hpp` (the splitmix64 generator of a seat and its seed), `bot.hpp` (levels and profiles, `BotSpec`, `parse_bot_spec`, `bot_display_name`, `check_setup`, `Priority` / `Intent` / `Orders`, `Bot`, `BotContext`, `make_bot`), `bot_view.hpp` (the minimal view: the clock, the score and alliance state of every team, the ants), `bot_controller.hpp` and `idle_bot.hpp`. `worker` and `standard` are accepted names that run the idle bot until B3 and B4.

**What B2 added**: the complete `bot_view.hpp` (own egg stock and incubator, the food piles, the borrowed grid, `predict_ack`, `has_pending_path`, `map()`), `map_info.hpp` / `map_info.cpp` (hills, walking-cost fields on the engine's own step weights, pile approach and click tile, walker components, power-ups, the trip model), `arena.hpp` / `arena.cpp` (one match with the real engine and the controller: pure, no threads, no clock, no files; the sink latency, recording, the replay without a bot), `tools/bot_arena.cpp` (the command line, the threads, the report; CMake target `bot_arena`, not part of the normal build), and `BotController::map()` / `BotContext::map` (the controller owns one `MapInfo`, built when it is created). Tests: `tests/test_ai` AI1.x and AI4.x, and `bot_arena --selftest` as suite 2.21.

- **`BotView`** is a copy of what the seat can see: the clock, the scores of the boxes, the alliance state, its own egg stock and incubator, its own ants (everything), the other teams' ants (what is on the screen), the food piles. Everything it keeps is a COPY (the engine's `get_world_state()` reference is a cache that is rewritten after any tick or command). Three things are BORROWED from the engine and valid only while the bot thinks: `grid()` (a const reference to the map: terrain, objects, food, power-ups, bombs, fire, bridges), `predict_ack(command, &needed)` (the engine's own prediction of which ant would acknowledge an order: the prediction a person's click gets, which agrees with `apply_command` except in two documented ways, see above) and `has_pending_path(ant)` (an order given a moment ago has not been answered by the path manager yet). A COPY of a view has none of them (an empty grid, helpers that answer "nothing"): the type cannot be used to read the engine later.
- **`MapInfo`** is the analysis of one match, built once from the grid at the start: hill geometry (ramp, hole, queue row, raid tile, waiting spot; the FIRST hill of a team, as `Grid::find_anthill` picks it), one walking-cost field per hill on the engine's own step weights (Dijkstra from EVERY walkable tile of the hill's queue row at once, because an ant on the ramp steps onto any of the three: a rock on the middle one does not cut a hill off), per pile and team the approach cost and the click tile, walker components (which ants can reach which piles: an ant stranded on another island is never sent to a pile of the hill's island), the power-up list (the engine's own cells: power-ups are tile ids 62 .. 66, so a community dictionary that calls them `.` changes nothing, test AI1.22) and the trip model (`trip_ticks`). The rules it takes from the engine (`MapInfo::walkable`, `step_cost`, `can_step_onto`): terrain, the 4 x 4 mound of every hill, solid objects, the queue row of another LIVE team (R5), a bomb of the team itself or of its ally (R7), the path finder's refusal of tile row 90 on a map taller than 90 rows (the original's path finder never generates a tile of row 90: `path_planner.cpp`, `Ants.exe` `FUN_01019a66`, verified with Capstone and the decompilation, see `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 18; a map of 91 rows or more is cut in two there; the shipped maps have at most 60), and for the LAST step onto a pile or a power-up: not water (a step into water costs the engine's 8,000), not inside a mound, not on another live team's queue row. It is a HINT, never a promise: it describes the map as it was at the start, and a bot still checks in the next view that an order did what it meant. **`approach_now`** asks the same question of the map as it is now (a pile's footprint shrinks as it is eaten, and the click tile of the start may no longer be food; a wall, a bridge or a bomb may have changed the way): it takes the engine's dynamic rules as a `WalkContext` (the team's ally, so that allied bombs block, and the dropped teams, whose queue rows are open ground; `BotView::walk_context()` gives both), works for every object of the engine's table, also a lunchbox dropped after the start (`MapInfo::pile()` is null for it), and costs one walkable mask and one Dijkstra per call (27 to 254 microseconds on the shipped maps, 1.9 ms on an open 256 x 256 map, release build): a bot that re-targets several piles in one look makes ONE `field_now()` and asks for each pile with the overload that takes it.
- **`Bot`** has `think(view, orders)` and `on_command(command, fate, tick)`. A bot never sees the engine's answer to a command (a lock-step room does not give one), so it checks the result in the next view.
- **`Orders`** is the only way out of a bot. Its named methods cannot express `Quit` or `Drop` (`push_unchecked` exists for the tests of the controller's filter, which refuses Quit, Drop and None whatever way they come).
- **`BotController`** owns the bots of one game and is called after every simulation tick: it runs each bot when due, applies the reaction delay and the budget, stamps the issuer, drops ants that died, filters forbidden commands and counts everything.
- **Tasks** (`tasks.hpp`, B3): a bot's wishes become tasks that claim ants in an `AntLedger` (an ant belongs to at most one task), emit orders through `Orders`, check progress from the next view and end with success or failure. Everything a task knows is SOFT: an ant that was hit, healed, born or stranded is simply idle and empty-handed and re-enters the pool, a pile that is empty is no candidate, so a bot may be restarted at any moment (tested: a new worker in the middle of a match finishes within 8 percent of one that was never restarted). What a task does remember (which ant was told what, when the order LEFT, which pile did not start) is a cache and a piece of learning. A bot never sees the answer of the simulation to a command, so a task counts the time of an order from the tick it was RELEASED (`Bot::on_command`, `Fate::Sent`), not from the tick it was decided (the reaction delay and the budget hide the order from the bot for a while; a timer from the decision blacklisted every pile before its first order had left in the first prototype).

**What B3 added**: `tasks.hpp` / `tasks.cpp` (`AntLedger`, `TaskContext`, `Task`, `HarvestTask`), `worker_bot.hpp` / `worker_bot.cpp` (`WorkerBot`), `baselines.hpp` / `baselines.cpp` (the procedure of the pinned numbers, shared by the test and by `bot_arena --write-baselines`), the registry (`make_bot`: `idle`, `worker`, and `standard` as an alias of `worker` until B4-1), `tests/test_ai/test_ai_worker.cpp` + `baselines.inc` (suite 2.22). **`HarvestTask`** is the economy: the pool is the own Workers that stand idle with nothing in their hands and no order of the task that is pending or younger than 24 ticks; the candidates are the piles with units left that the hill can walk to and that the ant can walk to as well (walker components, so an ant stranded on another island is never sent; an ant that stands where a big pile used to be, on tiles that the start took for solid, is not stranded either), that did not fail lately and whose trip can still be finished (`trip + 10 + the level's longest reaction delay` ticks must be left on the clock: points carried at 0:00 are lost); piles are ranked by points per tick of the trip (`value * 100000 / trip`, Medium and Hard) or by walking cost (Easy: the nearest first); an ant goes to the first pile with room (at most `max_ants_per_pile` and `trip / 90 + 2` ants per pile, because the hill banks about one deposit per 90 to 110 ticks), else to the best; one group move per pile onto the pile's click tile (of the pile as it is now when it has been eaten into: `approach_now`), **and at most 8 ants newly ordered at one look** (the rest of the pool at the next looks). After that the ants loop by themselves.

- **Packed ants.** The limit of 8 is the engine's doing: the path manager (the original's PATHMGR: one request per 50 ms, four search grids) never recovers from ONE order of 24 workers that stand on adjacent tiles: they block each other's paths (a team-mate that stands idle or paused costs 8000), 15 requests stay pending for the rest of the match, and the seat scores 0 (no bot is needed for it: `test_ai_worker` AI3.18 does it with a plain group move). Groups of 8 sent at once still box each other in for hundreds of ticks; 8 at a look do not. Shipped maps start with at most 8 ants per team, so nothing changes there. A **watchdog** is the safety net: an ant on its way to a pile that walks, or waits for its path, on the same tile for 300 ticks (not one that queues at the pile, not a carrier) gets a Stop and is ordered again from the pool, and the number of ants ordered at one look is halved until a quiet time has passed (a bot that sends all 24 at once is repaired after about 300 ticks; 0 points for the whole match without the watchdog). On the community maps the throttle and the watchdog are within the noise of the totals (this bot against itself without them: +4.7 and -1.7 percent, +0.7 and -0.3 percent without 4cornersn3, a map on which no policy wins); they are there for the jam of packed starts, which the shipped maps do not have.
- **Failure learning, ant first.** An order that came back `Applied` and did nothing is a failure: the ant stands where it stood, empty, 24 ticks after the order LEFT, or it shows the "can't go" reaction within one look of those ticks while it is still within a tile of where it stood (a "can't go" later is a jam on the way, no failure of the pile). Whose fault it is: **the ant's** when another ant of the task walks to the pile, bites at it or carries from it, or when the only ant that failed there is one that failed on another pile lately (an ant that is shut in, or whose nook has a blocked door, fails on every pile it is sent to); the ant is then kept away from that pile for 900 ticks and the pile stays as it was. **The pile's** otherwise, when two ants that fail nowhere else failed on it, or when no ant of the task makes progress anywhere (a seat that gets nowhere cannot tell, and a wall that came up after the analysis of the map is learned this way, in one look): it is blacklisted for 900 ticks for every ant. Measured in a hand-made world with a shut-in ant (one or two ants in a walled nook whose door a stranger blocks, two healthy workers, four piles of 14 units): 900 to 950 points and 22 to 23 piles blacklisted before, 1050 points and at most one pile blacklisted now, which is what the two healthy workers make alone. On the community maps the new attribution against the old one (the pile is blacklisted on the first failure) is within the noise of the totals: +2.3 and -0.3 percent, +1.0 and -0.3 percent without 4cornersn3.
- **The map as it is now.** `MapInfo` is the map of the start, and the bot asks again every 200 ticks (10 s) when it matters: a pile that was shut off at the start (a wall that opens, food that plugs a corridor and is eaten) is asked for through `approach_now`, and an ant that the start analysis took for shut in is sent when the hill's walking field of now reaches it. (In a hand-made world a pile behind a plug of 8 units had 60 units left after 6,000 ticks, 350 points; now it is worked.)
- **A stuck carrier is sent home, by the clock.** After a bite the engine's walk home can end in "Can't go there." and the ant stands with its food for the rest of the match: nothing in the original retries it (the cause is written down in `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`, 5.40: a team-mate that stands paused or idle on the doorway in front of the hill's entrance makes the hill unreachable for the moment of the search, the failed search is answered in the same tick, and the count-0 branch only stops the ant). It happens far from the hill (TINY seat 3: seeds 2, 5 and 8, the ant at its pile 7 tiles from the hill, a fifth of the seat's score) and on the tile in front of the gate (TREASURE seat 0: seeds 1, 2, 3 alone and 1, 2, 5 with four workers, one of six workers for 94 percent of the match, at Medium in v0.0.88; since the walking costs of a hill start from its whole queue row the first piles of that seat are other ones and the jam comes later and more rarely: of seeds 1 to 12 alone only seed 5 at Hard and seed 7 at Easy, with four Medium workers seeds 11 and 12), and a blast or a blow on the way home leaves a carrier standing anywhere. The ants that queue for the gate are idle with food too, so the distance from the hill tells nothing (the first version said "more than 4 tiles"; with 12 or more workers the queue reaches past 4 tiles, and that rescue stirred the queue): **time tells.** A carrier that has stood idle with its food for 900 ticks (the longest legitimate wait for the gate: 772 ticks in 100,000 waits on the five shipped maps with food, 12 seeds, three levels, alone and with four workers; the median is 130 to 136) is sent to the hill entrance by hand, as a person would click it (alone, and not again for 200 ticks); it queues for the gate and delivers. A carrier more than 4 tiles from the mound with nobody queueing within 4 tiles is helped after 40 ticks (nobody queues there: this is the fast path of TINY seat 3). Nobody is helped while 8 or more carriers stand idle at once: with 12 to 24 workers on one hill the queue is deep (waits of 870 / 1,439 / 1,800 / 2,384 ticks for 12 / 16 / 20 / 24 workers), the gate is the bottleneck, one idle carrier more or less costs nothing, and a rescue sends an ant through the crowd (those hills scored up to 10 percent less with rescues). What frees an ant on the waiting tile in the engine: the order to the entrance sets the ant's `home_state` to 1, the path of length 1 completes at once, the arrival queues the ant (priority) and ANTHILLQ dispatches it about 100 ticks later (probed on TREASURE seed 1: delivered 180 ticks after the order). TREASURE seat 0 alone: 2795 to 2980 (Medium), four workers 2265 to 2545 (v0.0.88; now, at Hard seed 5, 2845 without the rescue and 2940 with it, played without the start hold: that jam belongs to the opening of v0.1.0 and AI3.5 keeps it). On the community maps, whole matches with four Medium workers on 492 maps, the rescue is worth 4 to 5 percent: this bot without any rescue scores 5.1 and 3.8 percent less than with it (seeds 1 and 2).

**`WorkerBot`** answers an invitation with one Deny (an invitation waits for ever and an alliance of all live teams ends the match within a fifth of a second; the controller lets an answer through when it answers an invitation that is there), runs the `HarvestTask`, and nothing else: it never hatches (see "Measurements"), fights, raids, uses a power-up or builds.

Bot kinds: `idle` (stands still, for tests), `worker` (harvest only, a fixed yardstick for measuring other bots: frozen now that the standard bot exists), `standard` (the bot of the three levels and four styles, B4-1: "The standard bot"; it was an alias of the worker until B4-1). The bench bots of the tournaments (`aggressor`, `aggressor2`, `rusher`, `saboteur`, `aggr1` .. `aggr9`: bots that raid and harass, in `tools/bench_aggressor.hpp`) are arena-only kinds of `tools/bot_arena.cpp`: they are not in the registry, `make_bot` refuses them, and no game, room or server can seat one.

`SeatStats` counts commands after splitting: `intents` = queued, `released` = handed to the sink, `expired` = dropped after the time to live, `pruned` = commands that lost ants that died (one with no ant left is never released), `filtered` = refused when proposed (the bot is told, `Fate::Filtered`: the harvest blacklists the pile, the raids the team, the gate, the economy's rescue and the aid of a hit carrier the tile for 900 ticks, instead of proposing the same click at the next look), `superseded` = commands that were due together with a newer one and lost every ant to it, `rejected` = released and refused by the sink (0 when the sink is the engine or the sequencer and the bot keeps to what the filter lets through; AI6.7 and AI6.8 check it with a bot that acts, through both of the application's sinks). The controller refuses (counts `filtered`) a command that a person could not click: `Quit`, `Drop`, `None`, an empty list or more than 32 ants, a special order of other than one ant, a group order off the map, an attack on the tile of an ally, an alliance command that names no other seat.

A bot seat is a room slot in state `Bot`: it has no connection, never reports a hash, is never dropped for silence and counts as a player. A person whose name starts with "Bot (" is renamed by the lobby, so a name alone never makes a bot, and a bot is never taken for a person.

## Difficulty levels

The economy is nearly insensitive to reaction time and command rate (a harvesting bot needs a few hundredths of a command per second), so levels are defined by the **tactics they use**; delay and rate are fairness limits that bite when fighting.

| Level | Thinks every | Reaction | Commands per second (burst) | Economy | Tactics (the standard bot, B4-1: "The standard bot") |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 5 s | 3 s | 0.4 (2) | nearest pile first, at most 4 ants per pile | strikes back with one ant, fire walls at its thief hole once an enemy Thief has been seen (the only power-up it takes is a Fire one), counters enemy fire and bombs, races for the contested food with the ants that the gate cannot use, hunts a wounded enemy next to its ants, raids only when far behind; on water maps (ISLANDS, SMALL) takes two Swimmers (a bomb-flight expedition on ISLANDS) and ferries food with them, slowly (one order in 2.5 s) |
| Medium | 1 s | 1.2 s | 1.5 (6) | piles by points per trip | adds the Fire, Bomber and Thief power-ups of its own side, a Combat Ant that harvests and fights, raids on the leader, the race for the contested food, hunts of the wounded within its leash, two defenders, walls when a thief is seen or can come; on water maps takes three Swimmers (the expedition) that ferry food |
| Hard | 0.2 s | 0.4 s | 3.0 (10) | as Medium, and every carrier guided at the gate by hand | adds the power-ups that the flowers of its side let fall, the Fire that lands within its reach and a watcher at the flower of its side ("The flowers"), walls before an enemy plays, theft of an unguarded Thief power-up and a second thief, a second Combat Ant once attacked, raids for 15 points, three defenders, the race for the contested food, hunts of wounded ants and of the leader's carriers, and an aggressive or a raider style (harassment squad, sabotage, strike), and the island play of Medium, quicker (shore kills: not built; alliance invitations: a bot never invites) |

The profile numbers (look, reaction, rate, burst) are fairness limits and did not change in B4-1; the numbers of the plans were decided by the tournaments of B4-1 and are in `tactics.cpp` (`plan_for`); B5 tunes by self-play. The burst is the depth of the bucket, what a bot that has been quiet can send at once; every bot STARTS with one token (the opening, since v0.1.1), so its first orders come one by one.

## The standard bot

`standard` (B4-1, v0.3.0) is the bot that people meet: in a local game (`--bot`, the start menu), in a room's fill and in a room's specification. It is the economy of the worker bot (`HarvestTask`, its numbers unchanged: alone the standard bot scores within 1 percent of the worker on every shipped map at Easy and Medium, see "Measurements") plus the tactics of its level and one of four styles. It is a virtual client like every bot: it reads the world through the `BotView`, sends nothing that a person could not click, knows nothing that a person of its seat could not know, and plays at the limits of its level (look interval, reaction time, command budget: the numbers of B1, never changed by a style or a tactic). The worker stays what it was, the fixed yardstick that the standard bot is measured against. Source: `include/ants_ai/standard_bot.hpp`, `standard_tasks.hpp`, `tactics.hpp` and `src/ants_ai/standard_*.cpp`, `tactics.cpp`.

**What a level does** (the plans are in `tactics.cpp`, `plan_for(level)`; every number is a field of `LevelPlan`):

| | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economy | the nearest pile first, at most 4 ants to a pile | the pile with the most points per trip | as Medium, and every carrier is guided at the hill's gate by hand ("The gate") |
| Answers a blow on an ant of its own | 1 ant (the nearest healthy ant that carries nothing), 8 tiles of leash | 2 ants, 10 tiles | 3 ants, 12 tiles |
| Fire walls on the three tiles in front of its thief hole | when an enemy Thief ant has been seen; lit again after they burned out | when one has been seen or an enemy that plays can reach a Thief power-up; renewed 200 ticks before they burn out | as soon as an enemy plays; renewed 130 ticks early |
| Power-ups | the Fire power-up for the walls, nothing else | the Fire, Bomber and Thief power-ups of its own side in the opening (one ant each, in that order), a Combat Ant once an enemy plays | the same, a second Thief stolen from another side, a second Combat Ant once it is attacked |
| Contested food ("The race for contested food") | the ants that the gate cannot use go to the contested piles first, in the first 1,200 ticks (only with 6 or more ants at the start) | the same | the same |
| Raids with a Thief | never (from tier 2 of "Behind the leader") | on the leading team's hill when its box shows 30 points | when it shows 15 points |
| Hunts a wounded enemy of at most 4 hit points ("Fights of its own") | what stands within 3 tiles of its free ants, a force of 2 | also what is within the leash of its hill and at its piles, a force of 3 | also the carriers of the leader |
| Behind the leader: tiers 1, 2, 3 at a deficit of this percent of what can still be earned (the marks of the catch-up alone; the war tier of "The war batch" raises the tier too, at lower marks: Easy 45, 100, 180, Medium 25, 55, 105, Hard 15, 35, 70) | 100, 200, 325 | 60, 120, 195 | 40, 80, 130 |
| Water maps (ISLANDS, SMALL; "Islands") | two Swimmers (a bomb-flight expedition on ISLANDS), food ferried slowly | three Swimmers, the same | three Swimmers, the same, quicker |
| Styles | all four, only their numbers move | all four | aggressive or raider |

Every level also puts out enemy fire walls with a Fire Ant and defuses enemy bombs with a Bomber (a healthy worker sets one off without one) where they are in the way of its economy.

Everything else is the same at every level: a hit carrier is sent home at once, an enemy that stands on a power-up is never attacked, a power-up tile is never clicked by accident, a special order never names a tile with an ant on it, the last ants stay out of every fight, an invitation to team up is answered by the accept rule ("Alliances"). What the tournaments measured as a loss is OFF in every shipped plan and stays in the code behind a flag ("Aggression").

### Task ranks

Every task of the standard bot claims its ants in one ledger (`AntLedger`: an ant belongs to at most one task), and a task may TAKE an ant from a task of a lower rank (`standard_bot.cpp`; `AI21.1` pins the table):

| Rank | Tasks | Takes from |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | fights: strike back, hunts, the skirmish | the economy, the guard and the ferry only: a fight never takes an ant whose task has rank 3 or more (its own rule) |
| 4 | fire walls, bombs, the strike, the harassment squad | ranks below 4 |
| 3 | power-ups, raids, the sabotage, **the island task, the expedition** | ranks below 3, so not from each other |
| 2 | the guard (off), **the ferry** | the economy |
| 1 | the economy (`HarvestTask`) | free ants |
| none | the gate, the hatch (off), the carrier aid | they claim and never take |

The two batches were joined with this order on purpose. The island tasks sit with the power-up task at 3: a pick-up on its way and a flight do not break each other off, both take free ants and the economy's, and neither takes an ant from a fight, a wall, a bomb, a strike or a squad (a bot that is attacked defends first). Above 3 they would take ants from the defence; below 3 the fights could draw the crew away, and the crew is the only use of the hill's ants on ISLANDS for the first minutes (a team without a Swimmer scores nothing there): `AI21.2` hits the crew with an enemy Combat Ant and finds no ant of it in a fight. The strike and the squad recruit Combat Ants (workers only where a plan asks for them: no shipped plan does), the walls take Fire Ants and the bombs Bombers, so no task of rank 4 takes a plain ant of the crew; the Bomber of a leg is the one ant that the bomb task could take, to defuse an enemy bomb at the hill, and none lies there while the expedition flies. The ferry sits with the guard at 2 because its Swimmers are not plain ants: no fight, hunt, pick-up or raid can use one (their filters take the level's plain ant and the Combat Ant), the economy never claims one, and the island task (3) takes one as a builder (a lab; the level plans have no builder). The contest's tasks never order a special order onto a tile that an ant stands on and neither does the island task (`AI20.8`, `AI15.16`: the controller refuses such an order).

### Strategy

The reasons for the tactics are the way strong players play the original, as the project's owner described it; each point is built, measured, or left out with the numbers that say so (no player is named).

1. **The first moves go to power-ups, not to food: the Fire Ant first, the Bomber second, the Thief, the Combat Ant and the Swimmer equal.** Built: Medium and Hard send one ant to each of the Fire, Bomber and Thief power-ups of their own side at the first look, in that order ("Power-ups"). The Swimmer is left out (it costs 6 to 7 percent: TREASURE, two standard bots against two workers, 2,526 points with it against 2,714 without it); the Combat Ant comes when an enemy plays, and it harvests (alone, a Combat Ant on a guard post cost 8 to 11 percent).
2. **Some players steal a neighbour's power-ups for their first move; one steals the other's Thief ant and raids twice from the start.** Built: Hard steals an unguarded Thief power-up of another side (never the ally's) and raids with two thieves (+46 points of margin against Medium, Medium and Easy: +515 against +469 with one thief); every level defends the own side's Thief power-up (it is taken in the opening) and the thief hole ("Defence").
3. **A steal is worth about 100 points (50 for the thief, 50 off the victim), so the raid goes to the leading score box first.** Built: `RaidTask` takes the leading team that has points to take; a raid for 15 points (Hard) is worth the trip (raid thresholds of 10, 30 and 60 won 96.5, 94.1 and 93.1 percent against Medium, Medium and Easy).
4. **Guiding for eating: a carrier ordered into the hill entrance by hand jumps the queue.** The engine reproduces it and its queue rules are the original's; built for Hard ("The gate"): +4 to +23 percent alone on every shipped map.
5. **Mud humping: a click on the next mud tile in the middle of a step snaps the ant to the tile centre.** The engine reproduces it exactly (it is the original's `GoTo` snap, `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` section 18), the gain is small (a click-perfect person saves 2.7 of 18.5 ticks per mud tile, mud is 0 to 8 percent of the walking of a match), and a bot with a Hard profile cannot hit the window of 3 ticks (reacting to a look loses 61 to 97 ticks per 11 tiles). **Not built at any level**; nothing in the original or the remake differs from what the owner described.
6. **Hatching wins a team fight, and a wipe-out forces a hatch (-200).** The forced hatch is real: when the last ant of a team is removed (the end of its death clip, about 42 ticks after the lethal blow) the engine hatches an egg for `min(200, score)`, and a team without an egg is out (`docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`, `CheckNoAnts`). Built and left off: hatching for the economy never pays (one hatch: TINY +28, SMALL -123, MEDIUM -197, GAUNTLET -154, TREASURE -232 points), hatching for a fight lost every bench (a death spiral: each replacement is killed again and costs 200). What stays: no level sends its last ants into a fight (two ants, three when no egg is left).
7. **If you are losing, force a team fight.** Built (`StrikeTask`: clearly behind the leader and a fight looks winnable). In v0.3.0 a strike did not make a seat that fell behind recover (2,020 without, 1,730 to 1,883 with), so it was part of the Hard aggressive style only, and it never fired in a close match. Since the contest batch the trigger is the pressure of the deficit, scaled by level, at every level ("Behind the leader and the endgame"); it is neutral in play between equals and, with Combat Ants only, costs nothing in a comeback (workers in the strike do: they kill nothing above two hit points).
8. **Contested food first (the middle of TREASURE).** Built as an option (`contest_aware`: the piles that several enemies reach first, then those that one reaches, the safe ones, the ally's last) and left off: every standard bot with the order scored 11 percent less (1,958 against 2,204), the centre was gone by tick 1,200 instead of 1,200 to 2,400, and 58 units of far piles stayed uneaten. What shipped in v0.3.0 was the **opening**: Medium sends one ant, Hard two, to the contested middle at the start and the rest harvest by value per trip (a tie in whole-match duels, 1.001 and 0.999). Since the contest batch it is the **race** ("The race for contested food"): the ants that the gate cannot use go to the contested piles first, at every level (`race=0` brings the opening back).
9. **Help the teammate.** Built as the accept rule and, in the Defensive style, the answer to a blow on an ally's ant ("Alliances").
10. **Three fire walls in front of the thief hole stop a thief.** Built at every level ("Defence").

### Power-ups

Power-ups are tile ids 62 to 66 on the map; an ant that walks onto one takes it, keeps the type for life and plays 17 ticks of the pick-up clip in which it takes no order. The tactics rest on these facts, measured in the engine (`docs/audit/B4_1_notes.md`):

- **Sides.** On TREASURE every base has one power-up of each kind on its side (`power_up_side`: the team whose hill reaches it at the least walking cost, strictly less than every other team of the match); on the other maps there are none to walk to (MEDIUM, GAUNTLET and SMALL have flower droppers whose drops have a side of their own, `drop_side`: "The flowers"; the flowers of ISLANDS lie where no hill can walk). `BotView::powerups()` lists the power-ups on the map now.
- **Standing.** An ant that stands on a power-up and is not walking is immune: an attack order on it is acknowledged and ends in "Can't go there." three or four ticks later, a Combat Ant's reflex fails the same way, no bomb or fire wall reaches it, and nobody else can take the power-up. The bot never attacks a standing ant (`BotView::standing()`) and treats a power-up that somebody stands on as no goal.
- **A click on a power-up tile is a pick-up.** The controller lets a move onto a power-up tile through only as a planned pick-up of ONE ant (`Orders::pick_up`); no group order, rally or spread of a bot may name one by accident, and no special order or attack names one.
- **The trip.** `PowerUpTask` sends the closest idle (or walking to a pile) worker with empty hands by the walking cost from its own tile (one `MapInfo::cost_field_onto` search serves every ant), when the walk takes at most 420 ticks and the clock leaves time, and not when an enemy ant stands closer (a contest is lost, not entered). Typed ants harvest like workers (3,000 ticks alone: worker 350 points, Thief and Swimmer 350, Bomber, Fire and Combat 325), so a Fire, Bomber or Thief ant returns to the economy between its jobs and a power-up costs the trip and no harvester. A failed trip learns (the ant and the tile are left alone for 900 and 300 ticks).
- **The opening** (measured on TREASURE, two standard bots against two workers, Medium: no power-up 2,665 against 1,743; Fire 2,657; Fire and Bomber 2,643; with the Thief 2,692; with the Combat Ant 2,714; with the Swimmer 2,526): Fire, Bomber and Thief of the own side at the first look. A thief ant that harvests is never idle, so `RaidTask` takes a thief from its harvest loop for a raid.
- **Stealing is Hard's.** A trip may go to another side's power-up (never the ally's) when no ant of its owner is quicker there; Medium and Easy take their own side's only (a measured loss otherwise: Medium stole its neighbours' Thief and scored less). The trip knows only the power-ups of the start, so the drop of a flower is nobody's in it; what a bot wants of a drop is in "The flowers".

### The flowers

A flower dropper is a daisy that lets a power-up fall on its drop tile (the plant's tile one row down) once in a cycle: a droplet falls for 16 or 17 ticks, its picture says which kind is coming, and the power-up lies on the tile when it lands (`docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, "Flower droppers"; the original's rules: `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 5.29 and 5.38). The interval of a flower is the same in every match of a map and the kind is drawn from the match's random numbers (the intervals and the odds of every map: `docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, "Flower droppers"). Where the flowers are, and how many drops a match sees:

| Map | Flowers (drop tiles) | Landings in a match (idle bots) |
|---|---|---|
| SMALL | 2: (2, 20), (37, 20) | 62 |
| MEDIUM | 1: (29, 29) | 66 |
| GAUNTLET | 1: (3, 6) | 19 |
| ISLANDS | 2: (30, 5), (30, 56) | 22 |

TINY and TREASURE have no flower that drops, and no hill walks to the flowers of ISLANDS. On SMALL, MEDIUM and GAUNTLET no Fire power-up lies on the ground at the start (`AI1.16`), so a Fire Ant there comes from a flower or from nowhere. Three facts of the engine decide the play: a landing replaces a power-up that nobody took; no drop is made while an ant stands on the drop tile, so the ant that took a drop keeps the flower shut until it steps off; and an ant takes a power-up only when a move order onto its tile arrives (an ant that merely stands on it takes nothing).

- **What a bot sees.** `BotView::flowers()` lists the flowers that drop: the plant, the drop tile and, while a droplet falls, its age (about 16 ticks) and its kind, which is what the screen draws. The dropper's interval and the draw that picks the kind are not in it: a person sees the flower, the droplet and what has landed, never the clock behind them. `MapInfo::flowers()` lists the same flowers for the analysis of the start, with the walking cost of every hill to the drop tile (`AI23.1`, `AI23.2`, `AI23.2b`).
- **What it remembers.** `Memory::flower` counts the droplets that the bot has seen fall, once each, per drop tile: the ticks of the last two landings (the cycle is their difference, known after two landings, and the next landing is one cycle after the last) and how many of each kind landed. The chance of a kind is flat (the five kinds alike) until three landings were seen, then it is the share of what has landed. No table of the maps and no interval or percentage of a level file is in the bot (`AI23.8`). Only the watcher reads the cycle, and only Hard's bot (a look every 4 ticks) sees every droplet: at Easy's look (every 100 ticks) a droplet is seen one time in six, at Medium's (every 20) four times in five, so their cycles would be wrong, and they use none.
- **Whose drop it is** (Hard, `flower_sides`). `drop_side` is `power_up_side` for a drop tile: the team whose hill walks to it at strictly the least cost (none when two teams are equally near or no hill reaches it). A bot wants the kinds it secures for its own side (Fire, Bomber and Thief, as its style has them) from a drop on its side, and the ordinary trip of `PowerUpTask` fetches it: the nearest ant by walking cost, and a contest is lost, not entered. The trip itself knows only the power-ups of the start (`power_up_side`), so in it a drop is nobody's: whoever wants the kind fetches it; what keeps a bot from fetching another side's drop is its wants, which come from its own side's flowers and from the Fire below (`AI23.3`, `AI23.12`, `AI23.13`).
- **The Fire Ant at home** (Hard, `flower_fire`). A bot that has no Fire Ant also wants any Fire that lands within reach of its hill, whoever's side it is (on SMALL, MEDIUM and GAUNTLET Fire only comes from a flower, and the Fire Ant is what the thief hole's walls, the counters and the safe fire-in need); the contest check still decides who goes. A Fire that no hill walks to is wanted by nobody (`AI23.5`, `AI23.6`, `AI23.23`).
- **Two repairs of the trip.** The recall (`flower_recall`): a drop that changes kind under an ant on its way (the next landing replaces it) calls the ant back from any distance, where before only an ant three tiles or more away was called back and one nearer took the kind that the plan did not want (`AI23.9`). The recovery (`flower_recover`): a trip to a flower whose ant took food on the way (a bite does not hear an order) is given up at once and tried again with another ant, instead of after minutes (`AI23.15`); a trip to a power-up of the start goes on as before. The recall is an order and has to win a race: an ant within about two tiles of the power-up still takes the new kind before the order reaches it (not pinned by a test).
- **The watcher** (Hard, `flower_watch`). When the plan lacks a kind that it takes for its own side (no ant of the kind and none on its way) and the bot has seen the cycle of the flower on its side, one ant waits on a free tile beside the drop tile and arrives about 60 ticks before the next landing that the bot expects, so that it is on the spot when the drop lands and takes it by the ordinary trip. It is sent only when the walk is no longer than a trip may be (420 ticks) and at least 900 ticks of the match are left; it is the ant that walks least to its place and is ordered once, and again only when it stands idle 80 ticks after the order. It goes home when nothing is lacking, when the chance that the next landing is a kind that is lacking is under 15 percent, when the wait for the next landing is longer than a trip there and back, when its place is joined to the hill by no walk (the walking field is kept 100 ticks, so a ring of fire round the place is noticed within that time), or when it is hit (a fight never takes an ant of its rank, and it would not flee; the flower is then left for 600 ticks). The ant that took the drop stands on the drop tile, which keeps the flower shut, so it is ordered off at once with an urgent order (`AI23.10`, `AI23.11`, `AI23.16` - `AI23.21`, `AI23.24`, `AI23.25`). The watcher is a worker that does not harvest while it waits: that is the price, and it is paid only while something is lacking.
- **What stays out.** Combat and Swimmer drops have the side of the rule above and no rule of their own (the fights and the island task want those kinds as before, and a drop of them is fetched when it is wanted and not another side's); the flowers of ISLANDS (no hill walks there: a Swimmer gains nothing from a Swimmer drop, "Known limits" of the island play); no bot waits at a flower that is not on its side, and none guards a drop against a thief.
- **Switches.** `LevelPlan::flower_sides`, `flower_fire`, `flower_recover`, `flower_recall` and `flower_watch` (all five are Hard's: Medium and Easy play none, because the four rules cost Medium 20 points on SMALL, "The flower batch" in "Measurements") and `flower_watch_early` (60) and `flower_watch_chance` (150 in a thousand); `bot_arena --tune fside=0`, `ffire=0`, `frecover=0`, `frecall=0` and `fwatch=0` switch one off (1 switches one on), `flowers=0` all of them (the bot without the flower rules, match for match) and `flowers=1` all of them at every level (not the plan that ships: it gives Easy and Medium the rules and the watcher); a plan without a side switches the watcher off (`fside=0`). The plans of the levels are pinned (`AI23.7`). On TREASURE, TINY and ISLANDS no rule runs (no flower drops, or no hill walks to it), so the three maps play tick for tick as before at every level (`AI23.22`; "The flower batch" in "Measurements").
- **What the arena counts.** The landings and the kinds that landed, and what each seat's ants took and where (`FlowerTally`; the report's fields are in "Running bots"; `AI23.4`, `AI23.4b`, `AI23.14`); `bot_arena` prints the totals on one line.
- **Fair.** A bot uses what the screen shows: the flower, the droplet with its picture and its age, what has landed and when it was seen. It sends ordinary moves, pick-ups and stops, never reads the dropper's interval, its clock or the kind before its droplet falls, and keeps no table of the maps. What it takes from the analysis is where the plants stand and how far each hill walks to the tile in front of them, as for a power-up of the start (a person judges the distance by eye). The deviations that remain are in `docs/audit/bots_flowers_notes.md`.

### Defence

- **The thief hole.** A thief reaches the raid tile `(bx + 3, by + 2)` of a hill only by stepping from one of the three tiles `(bx + 4, by + 1 .. by + 3)` east of it, so a fire wall on each of them stops every raid (verified against the engine's own raid order: with the three walls the order ends in "Can't go there." and the victim keeps its points; with two walls, or three one tile further east, the raid goes through; the walls burn for exactly 3,600 ticks; the owner's deposits are not affected). `WallTask` keeps the walls with a Fire Ant (the first wall comes 67 ticks after the order from four tiles away, then 51 and 41), renews them before they burn out by the level's lead, never lights one on a tile that holds a power-up and cannot shut a hole whose tiles are mud (no wall can stand on mud). A Fire power-up is taken for it at every level. Reachable Fire power-ups on foot exist on TREASURE only (four, 185 to 211 ticks from the hills' queues); the flower droppers of MEDIUM, GAUNTLET and SMALL drop a Fire power-up in 25, 40 and 45 percent of their drops.
- **Enemy fire walls and bombs** near the hill (10 tiles) or a pile that the hill reaches (3 tiles) are put out by an own Fire Ant, or defused by an own Bomber (a special order onto the bomb: nothing is hurt), or, without one, set off by a healthy idle worker on purpose (it loses 2 of its 10 hit points and heals at the hill, instead of a carrier on its way losing them and its walk). A bot never orders an ant into a fire wall (`fire_aware`: a pile with a wall near it is asked again with the map as it is now) and never plants or lights anything on a power-up tile. **Fairness:** every bomb is drawn on every screen in the colour of its owner (the renderer's layer-2 pass, pinned by a render test), so `BotView::bombs()` lists them with their owners; fire walls are drawn alike for every owner, so `BotView::fire_walls()` lists tiles only (the owner and the remaining life that `grid()` lends are the accepted deviation of "Fairness in detail").
- **Fighting back** (`FightTask`): a blow on an own ant (`Memory`: its hit points fell and an enemy ant stands within two tiles) sends the nearest healthy ants that carry nothing (Combat Ants first), as many as the level says, to attack that ant; one attack order is one blow (the victim loses 1 hit point, a Combat Ant's blow 2, and is thrown), so a defender is ordered again each time it is idle after its blow, while the enemy stays within the leash; the fight ends when the enemy dies, leaves, stands on a power-up or no blow was seen for a while. An attack that shows "Can't go there." soon after it left means that no order reaches the target: it is left alone for 900 ticks. A hit carrier stands idle with its food (the blow cleared its walk, and nothing in the engine sends it on): `CarrierAidTask` clicks it home at once. A Combat Ant that harvests is a worker that fights: its reflex punches what comes within three tiles of its way and it goes on harvesting; there is no guard post (the parked guard lost: Hard 81.4 against 96.7 percent against Medium, Medium and Easy, and 10.9 against 25.0 in the mirror). Since the contest batch a fight is kept up through the hit clips only while a kill is in reach, and a wounded enemy is hunted without a blow first ("Fights of its own").
- **Never wiped out.** No ant is sent to a fight while the bot has two or fewer (three when no egg is left); a fight that is on ends when it is down to them.

### Raids

A Thief raids, again and again: a special order onto an enemy hill makes it walk to the raid tile, play 75 ticks of the raid clip (nothing can interrupt it) and take `min(the victim's score, 50)` points, which it walks home with. `RaidTask` picks the **leading team (by the score boxes)** that has points to take (30 at Medium, 15 at Hard), is not an ally, is not shut by three fire walls or bombs (a tile in front of its hole on which an ant stands still is no free tile, and a hole with an ant on its raid tile is left alone: the order would end in "Can't go there.", see "The can't-go loop"), can be reached on foot from where the thief stands and leaves time for the round trip; the cheap checks (the hole's state, the score) come before the search of the map, because a thief that looks every four ticks must not search the map for nothing (a full search per look and thief was the most expensive thing the bot did: the overhead of a whole Hard match on TREASURE fell from 2.2 to 0.9 s). A hill that could not be reached is left alone for 600 ticks (300 for the Raider style). No level keeps away from a hill that an enemy Combat Ant stands near: an enemy Combat Ant is a worker that fights, not a guard (measured: keeping away lost raids and gained nothing). Raids pay: without them Hard won 74.5 instead of 96.7 percent against Medium, Medium and Easy (margin +155 instead of +515).

### Aggression

"Hard bots should be really aggressive" (the owner). Every number below is TREASURE, the map that is played most, judged by the **win rate** (the seat's score is strictly above the best of all the others) and the **margin** (its score minus the best other score; in 2 + 2 the best of the pair against the best of the other pair), the score being secondary, against opponents that fight: mirrors of the bot itself, the bench aggressor and the centre rusher (bots that exist only in `tools/`), and the other styles. The economy tournaments (A1 to A5 below) are a sanity check, not the measure. What the tournaments found:

- **What pays.** The opening's power-ups (Fire first), raids once the thief is taken from its harvest loop, the double thief and the theft at Hard (+46 margin: +515 against +469 with one thief), the gate at Hard (the biggest single gain), the Combat Ant as a harvesting worker with its reflex (against the bench aggressor Hard keeps 2,251 points with it and 1,191 without, win rate 95.1 against 85.4 percent; against a mix of aggressor, rusher and plain bots 60.4 against 48.8 percent), low raid thresholds, and no keeping away from guarded hills.
- **What costs, and so is off in every plan** (Hard against Medium, Medium and Easy, 288 matches each; the mirror is Hard against three plain Hard bots, where 25.0 percent is the symmetric result): the raw harassment squad of Combat Ants (76.4 percent, margin +268 against 96.7, +515; in the mirror 7.3 percent), the parked guard (81.4, +224; mirror 10.9), hatching for the squad (37.7, -146; mirror 0.0, -1,246), the ambush at a thief hole (inert against the Medium bots, 18.8 in the mirror), the strict contest order (-198 and -287 of margin at Medium and Hard when it is added to the shipped plan, in a mirror of four standard bots), strikes and the wipe-out focus (inert in symmetric play), hatching for fights (-28 and -41), no raids at all (74.5, +155). A harvesting Combat Ant is neutral against Medium and Easy bots (96.2, +522 without it) and costs about eleven points of win rate in the Hard mirror (35.9 against 25.0 percent without it), where its reflex starts fights that nobody gains; it is kept because it halves what an aggressor takes and it is the fist of the aggressive styles.
- **The squad that survives.** The Hard aggressive style's harassment squad (Combat Ants that harvest and go for the carriers of the best opponent that are within eight tiles of them, a Combat Ant counting 8 and an ant that is drawn attacking 4 against the enemy's strength near the carrier) lost the cross table against the raider style 16.9 to 83.1 percent. It learns now: a member that is hurt (below 7 hit points) leaves and the team of the ant next to it is left alone for 3,000 ticks, twice as long every time (up to 16 times as long); so is a team that answers with three ants at once (a Hard bot's defenders); the reach is 8 tiles (14 tiles lost 35 points of the group wins in that table). The cross table is 40.0 to 60.0 percent now (margin -71 and +71), against Medium, Medium and Easy it wins 88.4 percent (margin +416) and against the aggressor 95.8.
- **Left out and why** (flags kept, `bot_arena --tune`, `docs/audit/B4_1_notes.md`): the parked guard, the interception of thieves (the Defensive style won 13.9 percent against the mix with it and 29.2 without), hatching, the strict contest order, strikes and the wipe-out focus (they never trigger in symmetric play and cost in the benches; the strike is part of Hard aggressive), the ambush, the skirmish of the bot's own and workers in the strike (the contest batch: "Fights of its own", "Behind the leader and the endgame"), the sabotage with a single Fire Ant (the Hard aggressive style steals a second one: its walls round the best opponent's gate, `SabotageTask`: eight tiles, the queue row sealed, until the victim puts one out or they burn out, 3,600 ticks).

### The race for contested food (contest batch)

"They eat their food in a weird order ... the center food in the treasure map is contested on all four sides, so that is usually the starting food" (the owner, 2026-10-05). A pile is **contested** when two enemy teams reach it at 70 to 130 percent of the bot's own walking cost (`PileClass::Multi` in `HarvestTask::tier_for`; one enemy: `One`; none: `Safe`; an ally as near: `Shared`; an enemy far nearer: `Hopeless`).

*What was wrong (v0.5.0).* `HarvestTask::step` ordered the piles by points per trip, and only the capped opening (`contest_opening_ants`: Medium one ant, Hard two, Easy none, until tick 1,200) put the middle first, so for most ants, and at Easy always, the near Cheerios came first. The strict answer, every contested pile before every safe one (`contest_aware`), loses: it won 6.2 percent of 96 matches on TREASURE (25 percent being equal) because the far contested piles starve the near ones that are the bot's own.

*What ships is a race.* The gate banks about one deposit per 100 ticks whatever the number of ants, so only the ants that the gate cannot use are free to go. The ants at work are counted by their trips; the ants beyond what fills the gate (`race_gap_ticks` 100, `race_slack_percent` 100) take the contested piles (two enemies reach them), each by points per trip, before the plain order. The race lasts the first 1,200 ticks and needs six ants at the start (TINY has three, SMALL four). It does not run into a pile that an enemy army holds (within four tiles of the pile an enemy Combat Ant counts 8 and an ant drawn attacking 4, against the own ants within six tiles at 150 percent). A pile that two enemies reach is a race though a third enemy is far nearer (that one eats a little before the ants arrive; the pile holds 30 units). A pile with one competitor is no race in the shipped plans (`LevelPlan::race_one`: `bot_arena --tune raceone=1` makes it one, and even then not where an enemy is far nearer, which eats it first). Every level races; the Economic and Defensive styles do not (their style is no contest of the middle). `bot_arena --tune race=0` brings back the opening of v0.5.0.

### Fights of its own: health, the kill and the skirmish (contest batch)

"If they're attacking an ant and they're low health, they should continue and try to kill the ant. KDR." and "the ant was 4 HP and it just didn't kill it" (the owner).

*What was wrong (v0.5.0).* `FightTask::start_fights` began a fight only from a blow on an own (or allied) ant, so a wounded enemy that hit nobody was never attacked. A fight ended with the first blow it landed (the struck ant plays a hit clip and `attackable` is false meanwhile). And `AntView::hp` was 0 for every other team's ant, so a wounded enemy could not be told from a fresh one: the view now gives the hit points of every ant it lists ("Fairness in detail").

*What the engine allows* (`kill_plan` in `standard_tasks.hpp`, measured with the bot's own orders on a clear field). One blow lands per hit clip (about 22 ticks) however many ants strike. A worker's blow takes 1 hit point, a Combat Ant's punch 2 and throws the victim four tiles. An ant left with ONE hit point walks home at the speed of its hunters and is not caught (the engine's `low_hp_check`). So only punches finish an ant, workers kill nothing above two hit points, and the order that gets the kill is: a Combat Ant strikes alone while the hit points are even, and when they are odd (3 or more) one other ant opens first so that no ant is left standing with one. A Combat Ant and a worker kill every wounded worker of 2 and of 4 to 9 hit points (12 of 12 arrangements of side and enemy hill), two Combat Ants the even numbers 12 of 12 and the odd ones 3 to 4 of 12, three workers only 1 and 2.

*Hunting the kill* (`plan.hunt`, every level, `FightTask::start_offence`). At every look the free ants (they carry nothing, are healthy, take orders; never the ants of a pick-up, a raid, a wall or a bomb, never the last ants) go for a **wounded** visible enemy (below 10 hit points) for which the plan of the blows has a kill within two blows (`hunt_blows`: a target of at most 4 hit points with a Combat Ant in the force, of 1 or 2 without one) by a force of at most 2, 3 and 3 ants from within 6, 10 and 10 tiles of the target (Combat Ants first) that outweighs what answers there (120 percent, against the three strongest enemy ants within five tiles that carry nothing). The scope scales by level: Easy what stands within three tiles of one of its free ants, Medium also what is within the leash of its hill (10 tiles) or within four tiles of a pile that its ants work, Hard also the carriers of the leading team wherever they are. The orders are Urgent, as a defence is; the hunt is bound to its target (a fresh enemy that steps in is not attacked) and ends when the target is dead or out of reach, walks home with a lead of two tiles, when nobody is left to hunt it (the force was hurt, taken by a task of a higher rank or killed, and no free ant near it takes over), or after 400 ticks; the odds that it began with are not asked again (a wounded target is finished, as the owner asked). While only Combat Ants strike (an even number of hit points) the plain ants of the force wait at the target without orders: they open for an odd number and relieve. The command budget and the reaction delay are the level's: no extra speed.

*Fights that a blow begins* (`plan.health_aware`, every level). The attacker that dies soonest is the target. The fight is kept up through the hit clips only while a kill is in reach (within the two blows of a hunt; a duel that cannot end in a kill costs ants and time and nothing else: keeping every fight up cost 115 of 2,207 points in a mirror of four Hard bots). An ant leaves when it would die before its target (3 hit points next to an enemy Combat Ant, else 4) and another takes its place, and one stays when its target is one blow from death.

*The skirmish* (`plan.skirmish`: with the stronger force, 150 percent, three ants attack a carrier on its way or a worker at a pile without waiting for a blow) is built and **off in every plan**. Against three bots of v0.5.0, Hard won 14.1 percent with it and 28.6 without (TREASURE, 96 matches each, 25 percent being equal): the force walks away from the economy and the enemy's defenders come.

### Fire play (contest batch)

"It doesn't really make sense to send a single fire ant to their base. It only makes sense if you already killed their fire ant." and "they can extinguish fire on a tile where a fire ant is standing on top of it. They should not be able to do that." (the owner).

*The fire-in is safe* (`SabotageTask`, `plan.sabotage_safe`; the Hard aggressive style lights the ring round the best opponent's gate with a stolen Fire Ant). (The war batch below turns the safety off at Medium and Hard: the rule and its measurements stay as written.) The bot lights it only when the enemy cannot simply put it out: no Fire Ant of that team (or its ally) is alive, and no Fire power-up is on the field that the team reaches (a Fire Ant that dies drops its power-up, so its death alone is not enough: the drop has to be taken). Otherwise the Fire Ant goes with at least two Combat Ants (`sabotage_escort`) that walk to the entrance first (the first wall waits for them, at most `sabotage_escort_wait`, 2,400 ticks: then the team is left alone for 3,600 ticks) and stay until 900 ticks after the ring stands; with fewer, or with an entrance that no ant can stand on and walk to, there is no fire-in, and no Combat Ant is called to a gate without a Fire Ant to light it and a wall to light (a ring that stands already, lit by another bot, needs none; when a wall has to be renewed the escorts are called again before it is lit). A wall that is gone long before its 3,600 ticks were put out; walls put out twice end the attempt at that team for 3,600 ticks.

*The defence* (`plan.fire_defence`, every level). While fire walls stand on the ring round the own gate, the enemy Fire Ant within 14 tiles of the hill is hunted by the level's defenders and one more (a wounded one first), and the own walls are put out only when no enemy Fire Ant is within ten tiles of the wall (for 1,800 ticks at the most from the first look at lit walls, `fire_defence_hold`: a Fire Ant that no blow reaches, on a wall or a power-up, would hold them for the 3,600 ticks that they burn) and the enemy's force near it is not stronger than the own (`fired_in_wall`): the owner's report that the fired-in team "just extinguishes all of the fire. It doesn't actually try to kill the fire ant that is trying to fire them in".

*No special order onto an ant* (project rule 5: what a person's click can do). The HUD shows the target cursor of a special order (extinguish, defuse, plant, ignite, bridge, demolish, a thief's raid) only where no ant is under the pointer; a click on an ant selects it or attacks it (`evaluate_cursor` in `src/ants_app/hud_input.cpp`). `BotController::allowed` therefore refuses (`Fate::Filtered`) a special order whose tile holds an ant of any team, the seat's own included, one that dies on its clip (about 42 ticks) too (`BotView::dying_at`: it is in no list of the view, but the standard bot's tasks look there; the island tasks do not, and a bridge or bomb order onto such a tile is refused and asked again). The tasks never propose one: the keeper steps aside before it puts out or renews a wall it stands on, the sabotage and the raid pass over an occupied tile (a thief raids by any free tile of the enemy mound), the extinguish job leaves a tile with an ant to the fights. **The simulation is not changed**: the engine rejects bomb and fire placement on a tile with a living ant (`docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`, about line 2837) and documents no occupant check for putting a fire out; settling that for the engine needs the original program.

### Behind the leader and the endgame (contest batch)

"I think the losing player should be a little more aggressive" (the owner).

*What was wrong (v0.5.0).* The strike (a force of Combat Ants that hunts the leader's carriers near its hill: `StrikeTask`) began only when `Standing::behind` held: a margin of 150 points or 12 percent of the leader's box, with the leader at 300 points or more (`standing_of`). In a close match nobody is that far behind until the last minutes (four equal bots end within 100 to 250 points of each other), and the strike was off in every plan but one style.

*The pressure* replaces the fixed margin: the deficit (the leader's box above the own, by the boxes a person reads) in percent of what can still be earned, the less of the food on the field in points and the clock at 0.16 points per tick (the best bots bank about 2,200 points in 14,400 ticks). Its **tiers** begin at 40, 80 and 130 percent at Hard, 1.5 times that at Medium and 2.5 times at Easy: the weaker the level, the later it escalates. Tier 1: the strike with Combat Ants (more Combat Ants wanted) and raids for two thirds of the loot. Tier 2: raids for a third, a hill with a guard is raided, and Easy (which does not raid) raids and wants a Thief. Tier 3: all-in: raids for 10 points, odds of 100 percent, hunts at half the odds, the strike's reserve is two ants and its stop moves from the last 900 ticks to the last 240. The leader must hold 300 points. (The shipped plans reach these tiers earlier than the marks say: the war tier of the losers' rule, "The war batch", raises the tier at its lower marks and with a leader of 150 points.)

*Workers do not join.* The tiers can take workers into the strike and open the skirmish (`catchup_workers_tier`), and the shipped plans never do: a worker kills nothing above two hit points, and a Hard bot that started 2,000 ticks late on TREASURE scored 1,720 points with workers from tier 2, 1,756 from tier 3, 1,823 with Combat Ants only (as shipped) and 1,836 with no catch-up at all (96 matches each; win rate 1.0 percent with workers, 2.1 without them). The strike therefore needs two Combat Ants of the bot's own, which most maps give only through power-ups.

*The last minute* (1,200 ticks, `plan.endgame`). The bot that leads guards: no hunt, skirmish, harassment, sabotage or strike, one defender more answers a blow, and what it carries is banked (the endgame veto that already keeps trips that cannot finish from starting). The bot that is behind is at tier 3.

### The war batch

"They just don't fight, they just eat. ... I think they need to start more fights with each other, fire at their opponent, and try to kill their fire ant if they try to come for them ... The bomber doesn't place any bombs currently, but he should be bombing up the food so they can't eat it" (the owner, 2026-10-09, after a match of four bots on SMALL).

*What was wrong.* Four bots of one level on SMALL (seeds 1 to 40) made 0 to 1 attack orders, 0 kills, 0 bombs and 0.17 fire walls a match at every level: the economy took everything in minutes 0 to 5, the hunt and the fire-in wait for a wounded enemy or an enemy that cannot put the fire out, and the Bomber of the opening is taken for a defence that no thief ever called for. Four rules were measured one by one, each behind plan fields (`bot_arena --tune war=0` puts the plans of before back; `LevelPlan` made by hand has none of it; `without_war_batch(plan)` is the same for the tests).

- **The assault** (`plan.assault`, every level; `FightTask::start_offence`). Once the plan's time has come (`assault_after`: 600 ticks at Hard, 900 at Medium, 1,800 at Easy) the ants that the economy has nowhere to send (`Tactics::surplus`, at least `assault_min` of them) go for an enemy ant within `assault_reach` tiles: a force of `assault_force` (3, 2, 2) of free ants (standing idle with empty hands; a worker on its way to a pile or with food is never taken), a Combat Ant first, when it has `assault_odds_percent` of the strength near the target (Hard 60, Medium 100, Easy 200: the weaker the level, the better the odds it asks for). The target is the nearest enemy ant that the force outweighs, a Fire or Bomber Ant, a wounded ant and a carrier first; the fight is a hunt of its own (one attack order per blow, 900 ticks at the most, not followed further than `assault_chase` tiles) and ends when the force falls below 80 percent of the odds it asked for, but never above the 80 percent of the strength of the enemy that `skirmish_abort_percent` names (so at Easy, which asks for 200, the fight ends at 80, not at 160; at Hard, which asks for 60, at 48; a bot that is behind asks for less, see below). Workers kill nothing above two hit points ("Fights of its own"), so the assault is pressure, kills come from the Combat Ants a map gives; it cost nothing in score where it was measured alone (+11 points at Hard on SMALL).
- **The fire-in** (`plan.sabotage` with `sabotage_safe` off, Medium and Hard, every style; `war_fires` Fire Ants wanted). The ring round the gate of the best opponent is lit whether or not the enemy can put the fire out (from tick 600 at Hard, from 5,400 at Medium: its economy is slower and the walls cost the victim the food he would still bring in; Easy none). The safe version stays in the code (`--tune sabsafe=1`, AI20.6) and refused almost always: 0.2 fire walls a match at Hard, 7 to 13 without it, with no loss of score against bots that do not light any (+12 points at Hard).
- **The raider hunt** (`plan.raider_hunt`, every level; `FightTask::start_fire_defence`). An enemy Fire or Bomber Ant within `raider_radius` tiles of the own hill (Hard 6, Medium 5, Easy 4) is hunted by the defenders whether or not walls stand on the ring; the blows cancel what it is doing. A hunt at a pile the own ants work (`raider_piles`, off) cost 170 points at Hard: the enemy Fire and Bomber Ants harvest there too.
- **The mines** (`MineTask`, rank 4; Hard `mine_per_pile` 3 and `mine_gate` 3, Medium `mine_gate` 2, Easy none (`mine_per_pile` is 10 at Hard since "The stand batch", which also lays mines round the own hill); `war_bombers` Bomber Ants wanted: 1). A Bomber of the economy lays them on the tiles round a pile that an enemy works (the enemy is as near as 170 percent of the bot's own walking cost, or an enemy ant stands within six tiles of the pile), on a tile where the enemy's walk is no dearer than the bot's (a tile of the bot's own side is never mined, an enemy ant that idles near the bot's own pile makes no target), never within six tiles of the own hill, never on a power-up or an ant; a mine that went off is laid again after 240 ticks; with no such pile the ring round the gate of the best opponent is mined (`mine_gate`) - the tiles that the fire-in lights too: a tile with a mine is not lit, so at Hard up to three of the eight stay mines while the others burn (measured alone, the gate mines change no score, kill or win: +0.1 percent at Hard, -0.1 at Medium, 1.7 to 3.4 bombs a match on SMALL, which is what the owner asked to see). Own and allied bombs block the bot's own walks ("Fairness in detail", R7), which is why the tiles are chosen where the bot's own cost is the higher. Enemy ants walk onto them: -2 hit points and a throw of four tiles (a dud one time in five), and a hit that clears the walk of a carrier.

- **The losers fight harder** (`plan.behind_war`, every level; `Standing::war`). "Especially the players that are losing, the bots should become more aggressive to try to gain a lead because at that point you're not going to out-eat them" (the owner, 2026-10-09). The war tier is the pressure of the catch-up ("Behind the leader and the endgame": the deficit in percent of what can still be earned, the food on the field or the time left at 0.16 points a tick, the less of the two) against lower marks: Hard 15, 35 and 70 percent, Medium 25, 55 and 105, Easy 45, 100 and 180 (the weaker the level, the later); a leader below 150 points leads nothing; in the last minute a bot that is behind is at tier 3. The war tier is also a tier of the catch-up (raids for a smaller loot, the Combat Ants and the strike force, a Thief for Easy from tier 2), and it grows the war rules: from tier 1 the assault begins at tick 300 (Hard), 600 (Medium) or 900 (Easy) at the latest, asks 12 (Hard), 10 (Medium) or 8 (Easy) percent less of the odds for every tier, goes for the ants of the leader first, and from tier 2 sends one ant more; from tier 3 (`behind_free_tier`, every level) it takes the Combat Ants off the piles (a Combat Ant harvests between its blows; a worker on its way to a pile or with food is never taken, it kills nothing above two hit points, but one that stands idle with empty hands joins the assault at every tier, and from tier 3 without the surplus that the others ask for), and a Bomber lays mines although the economy needs the hands, the piles the leader works first (taken from tier 2 instead of 3 these cost Hard 1.9 percent of its food four against four on the six maps, 5.5 percent on TREASURE and 2.8 on GAUNTLET, seeds 1 to 30, for the same kills); from tier 1 the fire-in of Medium begins at tick 1,800 instead of 5,400. `bot_arena --tune behind=0` takes it all out again (`bt1`, `bt2`, `bt3`, `bmin`, `bfree`, `bease`, `bafter`, `bsab` are its marks).

*What the measurements said* ("Measurements", "The war batch"). Mines laid while the economy needs every hand cost: fetching the Bomber alone 24 points at Hard on SMALL, 15 mines a match 77, and the enemy did not lose what the bot lost (the opponents' scores rose 15 to 35 points). Mines therefore wait for an ant that has nothing to harvest (`war_free_only`: `Tactics::surplus` above zero), and a Bomber that another task holds is never taken (`MineTask` takes ants of the economy only: the Bombers of the island expedition fly its crew, and wanting two Bombers instead of one cost ISLANDS 5 percent of its food). The consequence is that mines come late and few: 2 to 5 a match at Hard on SMALL, 0.2 to 4 on the other maps, and where the Bombers belong to the expedition (ISLANDS) none.

### The stand batch

"That combat ant is blocking where the regular ant could walk, and it doesn't know that it can move it ... the fire ants need to fight each other. This fire ant is firing in his team, and he has a fire ant, and he could just go and try to kill the other fire ant to get him to stop. At least try to knock him off the fire and then get him with his other ants. They're really bad at fighting. You got to stand your ground." "It started putting down some bombs, but it waited until the last two minutes. ... Especially since they were losing, Green should have started putting a bunch of bombs down on Red's base and harassing them instead of letting Red come fire in their base." "Rushing them with all their ants is always an option. If you're down by a few hundred points, you're very unlikely to win by just continuing to eat. So why not select all your ants and take them to their base? That's why it's really important for you to put bombs around your base. ... If you put bombs down, then they can't just walk. They'd have to go one or two ants at a time and path around the bombs." "The bot should also use the bomber for offensive and defensive bombs. ... I also noticed that the bomber only places three bombs. It could bomb up a whole area. The bombers can place unlimited bombs, so it could create walls out of the bombs" (the owner, 2026-10-10, after a match of four bots on TREASURE).

*What was wrong.* (1) The hunt of a Fire Ant that fires the gate in could never succeed: the enemy Fire Ant stands ON a wall of the ring, only a Fire Ant can walk onto a fire wall (the path of every other ant ends on the tile, "Can't go there."), so the hunt skipped it (no blow of a worker or a Combat Ant reaches an ant on a wall): the bot left the other walls burning for `fire_defence_hold` (1,800) ticks, put them out and the enemy Fire Ant stayed where it was. (2) The Bomber laid its mines for an ant with nothing to harvest, or from war tier 3 (the last minutes of a lost match), and never more than three at a pile, at the gate or (when it was added) at home. (3) Nothing round the bot's own hill stopped a walk to it. (4) A bot far behind kept eating: its assault is a force of two or three. (5) An ant without food that stood on the ramp shut the way for a carrier, and the gate only waited for it. Six rules, each behind plan fields (`bot_arena --tune stand=0` puts the plans of before back, `without_stand_batch(plan)` is the same for the tests; a `LevelPlan` made by hand has none of it):

- **The fire duel** (`plan.fire_duel`, every level; `FightTask::start_fire_defence`). The Fire Ant of the walls (rank 4) may be taken for it, **except while a thief threatens** (`Tactics::wall_demand`: the Fire Ant that keeps the three walls of the thief hole stays at them; `plan.fire_duel_walls` lets it go): a first version took it and the thieves raided 100 points a match more on TREASURE. It cost food and won nothing the duel did not ("Measurements").

The gate used to wait for it up to 200 ticks; a Combat Ant that guards the ramp never moved ("it doesn't know that it can move it"). An ant that holds food, or that a task of a higher rank holds, is left.
- **The mines of a loser** (`plan.behind_mine_tier`, `behind_mine_gate`; Medium and Hard tier 2, with 3 mines (Medium) and 5 (Hard) at the gate of the best opponent). Tier 1 was built first and is the cost of mutual mining: four Hard bots that all lay mines early lose a tenth of their food on TREASURE ("Piece by piece").
- **A field of mines: at the piles and round the own hill** ("the bomber only places three bombs"). ### Styles

A standard bot draws a **style** at the start of a match from its own seat's random generator (`mix64(seat seed ^ 0x57A1E57A1E)`: the same match seed and seat give the same style and the same plan, so a replay needs no bot) or the spec pins it: `--bot 2:standard:hard:raider`, `--bot 1:medium:defensive`, the room specification's `"bot": "hard:raider"`; without one it draws. The style never changes what a level may do (the profile, and the tactics a level unlocks: theft and the gate are Hard's, raids Medium's and Hard's): it picks among them and says how early and how hard; Easy keeps its plan (only its numbers move). The numbers of a plan also move by 10 to 25 percent from the seat's generator (raid thresholds, waits, leash, strike margin), and the Thief and the Combat Ant, which a person values equally, come in an order of their own half of the time. **Hard plays Aggressive or Raider only; Easy and Medium draw from all four.** The name of a bot seat stays "Bot (Level)" (project rule 5: nothing pretends that a bot is a person, and the style is not in the name nor on the wire); the status JSON of a server's room lists the style of every bot (`style`: "random" for one that draws).

| Style | Opening and economy | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| Aggressive | one more ant for the contest of the middle (Medium 2, Hard 3), one more defender | raids for two thirds of the loot; the harassment squad (Medium: only when clearly stronger than what stands near the carrier, 150 percent; Hard: at 100 percent); at Hard also a second Combat Ant, the sabotage of the best opponent's gate with a stolen Fire Ant and the strike when behind |
| Economic | no contest of the middle | fire walls before a thief shows, raids only for three times the loot |
| Raider | Fire and Thief first (no Bomber) | raids for half the loot, a shorter wait (300 ticks) after a hill that could not be reached |
| Defensive | no contest of the middle, one more defender | fire walls before a thief shows, the ally's blows answered (no interception of thieves: it lost) |

How the styles fare (win rate, margin, attack orders per match, what each keeps against the bench aggressor, the cross table of the Hard styles and the round robin of the Medium ones) is in "Measurements". At Hard both styles are far above the floor that the coordinator set (at least 80 percent against Medium, Medium and Easy with a clearly positive margin: 88.4 and 95.8), and the aggressive one holds its own against the raider (40.0 to 60.0 in the cross table, from 16.9 to 83.1 before its squad learned); at Medium the aggressive style is the weakest against equals (19.3 percent against 25.3 to 28.5 in the round robin) and by far the strongest against Easy bots (96.9 against 71.9 to 83.3): the squad is the price and the prize of visible aggression.

**Proposal (not built):** show the style on the results screen ("Bot (Hard) - raider") after the match, never before: nothing in the lobby or on the wire would change, the name of the seat stays what rule 5 says, and a person could learn what a style does by playing it.

### The gate

"Guiding for eating" (the owner: "bypassing the queue by manually controlling which ant deposits food"). The engine's queue caps a hill at 10 to 13 deposits a minute: an ant that finds the entrance busy goes to a waiting tile on the far side of the mound `(bx - 1, by + 3)` and the hill's task dispatches it from there on a walk of 44 to 68 ticks around the mound, one deposit per 93 to 116 ticks (the original's rule, `Ants.exe` `FUN_0100ff1f` ANTHILLQ, `FUN_0101f780`, `FUN_010202e7`: the same waiting ring, the same FIFO order and about 100 ticks per queued deposit, so the economy that these tables rest on is the original's; a click on the entrance is redirected to the ring while the entrance is occupied or claimed or any own ant is queued). By hand a deposit takes 55 to 65 ticks: carriers are staged on slots of the queue row away from the exit tile (two kept filled) and a buffer four rows north, and one is clicked onto the entrance at the moment the gate will be free (predictively, a look's latency early), never while a bite runs. Measured with a Hard-limited client in the engine: +55 to +86 percent for 8 to 12 workers on a pile 8 to 20 tiles away, 70 to 91 percent of the ideal, 0.4 to 1.05 commands a second. `GateTask` plays it at Hard only (the economy's own rescue of a carrier and the carrier aid are off while it runs: the gate owns every carrier, a thief's loot included, because one thief queued at the far ring poisons the FIFO). Alone against three idle bots Hard scores +4 to +23 percent over the worker (A1); at Medium the gate measured +1.3 to +7 percent and is left off by decision (the Hard bot uses it). Two deposits can never overlap; the engine's gate is the bottleneck of a seat alone (TREASURE alone 2,962 points = rate times length), four teams exceed the pot (8,900) so a full table is limited by the pot, and a 16th or 20th worker adds nothing. A click that the controller refuses (a power-up on the tile) blocks that tile for 900 ticks, and with the entrance blocked the gate does not guide. Nor does it while a ring of fire walls cuts the doorstep off from the hill, nor does it place a carrier that no walk joins to the hill, and it waits while an own ant stands on the ramp (at most 200 ticks) or an own ant without food is on it on its way out (at most 60) ("The can't-go loop"). A run of 16 clicks onto the entrance that delivered nothing (the carriers are boxed in, as on a causeway jammed head on; a healthy gate fails up to 10 in a row on the shipped maps): the gate stops for 900 ticks, which turns the economy's rescue and the carrier aid on. Before, such a gate clicked the entrance about every 20 ticks (and ordered its slots and buffer tile again and again) for the rest of the match: 2,400 commands in a match, nothing banked.

### The stall detector

The safety net of a seat that spends its command budget and banks nothing, for the worlds that no rule foresees (a jammed causeway, a hill that the carriers cannot reach, an order that the world never carries out). The only progress is the score rising. A seat plays the **plain economy** for `fallback_ticks` (2,400, doubled at every later stall, at most 9,600) when nothing was banked for `stall_ticks` (3,600: three minutes) while it has ants and food that its hill can reach lies on the map, or when it sent the same order (type, tile, first ant; an attack is one blow and is exempt) `repeat_limit` (12) times within `repeat_window` (1,200 ticks) and nothing was banked for that whole window (a jam that clears by itself is no stall: a fallback costs the gate for minutes). The plain economy is the worker's harvest with its default parameters (no contested piles, the rescue on, the plan's can't-go check), no gate, no raids, the carrier aid on; defence and fights go on. Every level has it; the numbers are `LevelPlan` fields (`stall_ticks`, `repeat_limit`, `repeat_window`, `fallback_ticks`, `gate_user_fails`, 0 switches a trigger off) and `bot_arena` tuning keys (`stall`, `repeat`, `repwindow`, `fallback`, `gatefails`); the report counts the stalls of a seat (`stalls`). It uses nothing but the ticks of the views and of the release: no clock, no random draw. Tests AI7.29, AI10.6 and AI10.7; what it did on the community maps is in "Measurements".

### The can't-go loop

"Can't go there." (a walk finds no path) and "Can't do that..." (a special order is refused) are the engine's answers to an order that cannot be carried out: the ant plays its can't-go clip (a worker 0.36 s, a thief 0.89 s) with a sound, and the status line shows the words for the person's own ants. Phase A (84 matches of the v0.5.0 bots) found that a bot's own refused orders are rare (546 of 28,016 group orders, 9.6 percent of the reactions) and that most of what a person hears is the original's own **can't-go loop** (79 percent of the reactions): an ant that stands on a special tile of a hill (queue row, ramp, entrance, raid tile; of ANY hill) and is shut in by fire walls and ants shows the clip, and when the clip ends the engine orders it to its own waiting tile; the walk fails in the same tick and the clip starts again, every 8 ticks for a worker and 18 for a thief, until a wall burns out or an ant leaves. No order of a bot ends it (a Stop or a move at every tick for 300 ticks changed nothing). The loops come from two plays: a thief shut in on the raid tile by the walls of the hole that it raided (TREASURE), and the ring that the sabotage lights round a gate (SMALL, Aggressive Hard).

**What the bots do about it** (`LevelPlan::cantgo_aware`, on at every level). Orders that cannot work are not given, and an own ant that shuts a thief in steps aside:
- the gate's staging and entrance clicks, the economy's rescue and the carrier aid use the walking field of the hill as it is now (`MapInfo::field_now`; the gate makes it again every 200 ticks and at once when a tile of the ring round the gate or of the queue row changed): a carrier that no walk joins to the hill (`MapInfo::reaches_hill`: a pocket, a ring of fire walls) gets no order into it, and with the gate's doorstep cut off the gate does not guide. A Swimmer is always taken as joined (the field is the walkers': water does not cut it off, and a ring of fire is not checked for it), and a Fire Ant, which a fire wall does not stop, is judged by that field like a worker (AI10.8 - AI10.13, AI1.23);
- a thief does not raid a hole while an ant stands on its raid tile, and a tile in front of the hole on which an ant stands still (an own ant too; a walking ant moves on) is not a free tile, so a hole whose last free tile an ant holds is left alone: the order was given and refused (AI12.4);
- a thief that has raided and is shut in on the raid tile by an own ant that stands on the last free tile in front of the hole gets out: that ant steps aside ("Two thieves on one hole", below), and no second thief is sent to a hole while an own thief is on its raid tile (AI12.5);
- the gate clicks nobody onto the entrance while an own ant stands on the ramp, for 200 ticks at the most (a longer one is not waited for: nothing moves it) (AI10.12);
- nor while an own ant without food is on the ramp, for 60 ticks at the most (`LevelPlan::gate_leaver_ticks`, Hard's): it has banked and still leaves by the one-wide way, the view draws it as Walking while the ants ahead of it hold it up, and a carrier clicked onto the entrance meets it head on (AI10.15);
- the stall detector's plain economy keeps the check; the worker bot, the frozen yardstick, does not have it (AI7.29, AI10.14).

`bot_arena --tune cg=0` switches all of it off: the bot as it was, match for match (measured on 504 matches of the three levels: every score, state hash and command count equal; AI10.14 shows for each scene what the switch lets through).

**What they do not do** (decided by the owner, 2026-10-05): the loops themselves stay. The walls of the thief hole closing behind an enemy thief and the ring closing over an enemy ant are what those plays are for (shutting ants in is their purpose and the fire-in that the owner asked for); not changing them leaves the original's tactics as they are. Two other ways to silence the noise change the original and are the owner's to decide, not the bots': an engine change (the re-order of the clip's end stops after the n-th failure) and a limit on what a person hears (the sound for the person's own ants only, at most one a few seconds per ant: the application, never the simulation).

### Alliances

An invitation to team up is answered by one rule at every level (`StandardBot::accepts_invitation`): accept unless the alliance would unite all live teams (the match would end at once: the last two live teams that ally win), or the bot or the inviter already has an ally. A team counts as live when it plays, has not dropped out and has an ant in sight. The bot never invites, never withdraws and never breaks an alliance (the worker still says no once). With an ally the bot's score box is the sum of the two; a raid never goes to an ally's hill, an attack never to its ants (the controller refuses it) and the ally's piles come last in the contest-aware order (an option that is off). The Defensive style answers a blow on an ally's ant with the own ants within 10 tiles of it, and the counters reach the ally's hill and piles; help beyond that (following a thief to the ally's base) is not built.

**Why a bot said no.** The original's "... rejected teaming up" says that a bot declined, never why. When a bot declines the local player's invitation the game adds ONE line to the chat log (the original's text stays the status line, unchanged), asked of the function that the bot answers by (`ai::team_up_answer`, `src/ants_ai/team_up.cpp`; the bot and the line cannot drift apart): "Bots team up only while three or more teams play." (two live teams), "You already have a teammate.", "<name> already has a teammate." (the name is the seat's as the game shows it) and, for the worker bot, "This bot never teams up." A person's refusal gets no line (a person says it in words), and nothing is added when the rule would accept (the world changed since the bot decided). In a room the slot state `Bot` says that a seat is a bot; a guest cannot know a bot's kind, so a worker there is explained by the standard rule. Tests AI8.7 (the function), 7.6e (a local game), AI6.12 (a room); the engine's news carries the answering seat (`NewsEvent::subject`, presentation only, N1.9c).

**Teams at the start of a local game.** `--teams ffa|A+B` (default `ffa`, free for all): seats A and B (0 to 3, different) are a team, and the two other seats too when both play. The game makes them with the original's own commands before the first tick (the first seat of a pair invites, the second accepts, applied straight to the simulation), so the engine's own News Flash "... are a team now!" is in the chat log and no invitation dialog opens; the standard bot never breaks a team. A pair with a seat that does not play, or a team that would be the whole match (it would be over as soon as the pair's score is above 0, the original's end rule: [`GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`](GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md) section 5.47), is told ("--teams 0+3: seat 3 does not play in this match. The game starts without teams.") and the game starts without teams. A room accepts it since network protocol 13: the leader's choice for the START it leads (a room that has teams of its own, set when it was made, makes those for every start, and the leader's choice does not count), made by every engine of the room before the first tick (`docs/NETWORK_PORT.md`, "Protocol 13"; a team that the seats which play cannot make is told to everybody in the room and the match starts without). The desktop start menu's Single player panel (Teams row) chooses the same thing for a game on this computer, offered only while two or more bots play (the game page still reads `&teams=0%2BN` from a typed address, but no page makes that link). The Host panel and the front page's card choose a room's teams (four players: Green with Red, Blue or Black, and the two others are a team too; three players: any two of the three) and make them the room's own teams (a field of the create block since protocol 15; a word of the room's code, `t01` to `t23`, in protocol 13). Tests 7.6f and 7.6g, AI6.13 (a room accepts `--teams`), M2.5, A2.3, N1.23 / N1.24, N5.78.

### Islands

ISLANDS has no food that a hill can walk to (0 of 5,600 points), SMALL has 1,000 of its 4,000 on an island: both need a way across water. The analysis (`island_info.hpp`, `IslandInfo::analyse`: per team, from `MapInfo`) was measured on the real engine and every line below is pinned by `AI14.x`; what the bots do with it is further down.

**The map.** ISLANDS has 21 walker components for every team: the four hills' islands (8 x 8 tiles with the hill, no food, its own Bomber power-up at the island's corner nearest the corner of the map: the only power-up a hill reaches on foot), twelve satellites (four hold 600 points each, two piles of 10 units of 30; eight hold a Bomber power-up and no food), the two halves of the ring around the lake (each holds one pile of 80 units of 20, 1,600 points), the two strips at the top and bottom of the ring, with a flower each (the daisies at (30, 4) and (30, 55): their drops fall on (30, 5) and (30, 56), every 60 s, 70 percent of them Swimmers, 20 Thief, 5 Bomber, 5 Fire; the view shows the droplet fall, and no rule uses these flowers: no hill walks there, "The flowers"). Two of each hill's eight start ants stand on satellites that each have a Bomber (8 ants in all, none with food on its island). The twelve Swimmers are in the four corners of the map, in rows of seven power-ups along the edge (Fire, Fire, Swimmer, Swimmer, Swimmer, Fire, Fire), with an entrance at each end. SMALL: two components (the mainland with the four hills, 3,000 points, the two flowers (2, 19) and (37, 19) (drops B 45, S 10, F 45 percent, every 15 s) and the two Swimmers that lie on the banks of the lake at (13, 20) and (27, 20), which every hill reaches on foot; the island (18 .. 22, 18 .. 22) with the 1,000 points). **The island task** (`IslandTask`, `island_tasks.hpp`, every level of the standard bot; idle, with no claim, order or want, where no pile lies beyond water that a chain of at most 8 tiles reaches: TINY, MEDIUM, GAUNTLET, TREASURE). It wants the Swimmers of the level (Easy 2, Medium 3, Hard 3: the power-up task takes them from the map, the expedition from the rows) and keeps every bridge safe. It can also dig: given a builder (`island_builders`, `--tune ibuild=1`, the labs of `AI15.x`) a Swimmer digs the cheapest chain of water tiles from the hill to a pile that no walker reaches (a special order onto one tile at a time, from the shore outward, over finished tiles of earlier bridges, at most 6 new tiles), and the economy walks over it because the pile is now reachable in the map as it is. **The level plans have no builder, because a bridge loses to the ferry.** Two bots of the plan against two of today's (SMALL, seeds 1 to 16, both seat arrangements: 32 matches a level): the difference in the mean score was -94, +21 and +82 points at Easy, Medium and Hard with a builder and +163, +220 and +195 without (ahead in 29, 32 and 26 of the 32 matches). A Swimmer that carries food earns 700 to 850 points in a match; a bridge carries one ant at a time, lasts 3,600 ticks, takes a worker from the mainland piles, and its Swimmer idles between two bridges. What stays on is the guard of the bridges that others dig (a person does; bots dig none), four rules, each pinned by `AI15.x`:

- **One ant to a bridge.** Two ants that meet head-on on a one-wide bridge stop for good (the engine repaths round a standing ant and there is no way round: `AI15.5`), so the piles whose way crosses a bridge get one ant at a time and one pile per bridge (`HarvestTask::set_pile_limits`); more income needs more bridges that do not touch, and the next one is dug beside the first.
- **Life.** A finished tile lasts exactly 3,600 ticks and drowns whoever is on it, so the task notes when it first saw each tile finished and closes the piles whose way would not last a round trip (the walk, the bite, the bot's own latency and a margin that grows with the level: Easy looks every 100 ticks and reacts after 60). A bridge that is going is renewed beside itself while it still carries.
- **Guard.** An ant on a bridge whose way off would not last is sent off by the way with the most time; an ant that works a closed pile is called home; an ant that walks towards a bridge about to go is stopped at its end, and an idle one by such an end waits farther off (the engine fixes a path when an ant starts to walk and takes the cheapest way from where the ant stands, so which bridge it takes cannot be told); the ends are counted in steps over land, not across the water; an order is not repeated before it could have worked (it would queue up behind the first in the level's budget of commands); the power-up task does not plan a walk over a bridge that will not last (`Tactics::shut_tiles`; the raid, the fights and the gate read the map without it, and the guard skips the ants of tasks of rank 3 and up: see "measured limits" (8) of the island play). `AI15.7`, `AI15.8`, `AI15.10` - `AI15.12`: no ant is lost when a bridge collapses under the economy (the same match with the guard off loses one), an ant put on a bridge with 700 ticks left is off it within 400.
- **Idle stays idle.** Nothing is claimed, ordered or wanted on the four maps without such water (`AI15.1`).

**The expedition** (`ExpeditionTask`, `island_expedition.hpp`, every level; idle where a Swimmer lies within a walk of the hill: SMALL's two lie on the banks and the power-up task takes them, and the four maps without water). On ISLANDS no hill can walk to a Swimmer, so the bot flies a crew to the corner rows with bombs (the owner's play: "bomb yourself over"):

- **Route**: the cheapest way over the islands to the row whose entrance is nearest to where the last flight lands, so that two bots on one side of the map take different rows. A flight costs 2, and 1 more from an island that has no Bomber to take (a Bomber power-up lies on every hill's island and on eight of the twelve satellites, and the start ants on the satellites stand next to theirs): there the Bomber of the flight before flies on with the crew. Every hill is two flights from its row; the way of hill 3 was three, over two satellites that have a Bomber each, and goes over the satellite east of it, which has none, since the Bomber flies on. Easy does not fly the Bomber on (`LevelPlan::island_fly_on`, `--tune ifly=0`): it looks every 100 ticks, a leg that waits for the Bomber costs it more than the flight that is saved, and its routes are the old ones, with a Bomber to take on every island ("The expedition batch").
- **Crew**: the plain ants of the hill's island with the hit points for every hop (each explosion costs 2 of 10): one for every token before the Swimmers the level wants (Fire, Fire, then the Swimmers) and one spare, never fewer than `crew_min` (3: a row that starts with a Swimmer needs one ant, and the same group would be chosen and refused at every look; `AI17.11`), and one more that takes the Bomber power-up of the island (the six ants of a hill's island are five for the crew and this one, so Medium and Hard, whose row takes five tokens, have no spare). The Bomber of every island on the route is made ready while the crew is still on its way. **The Bomber flies on as the last of the crew** when no ant that can hop is left behind and the next island has no Bomber to take (there it plants for the crew again), or when the crew that is left is one ant short of the tokens (it takes the last one, see "Row"; a Bomber that has flown on already and can hop or stands on the island of the row takes it, and no second one flies): it plants a bomb on B for itself, walks onto it and is thrown like the others (the owner's report: the bots "don't know how to hit a bomb with their own bomber"). A crew that is two or more ants short keeps the Bomber at home, and the expedition ends with the Swimmers that the plain ants reach (`AI17.23`). Hard with timed clicks needs one ant for every Swimmer and the Fire hats stay where they are: the crew is the Swimmers the level wants and one spare ("Timed clicks").
- **Hop**: the Bomber plants on B from a tile beside it (the engine moves a planter that stands on a diagonal, and a ring of ants round B leaves it none), the crew ant on S is clicked onto the bomb and is thrown. One bomb in five is a dud (the ant burns on B and then lies stunned there, loses 2 hit points and is ordered on at once, as soon as its state shows it; a bomb is planted again). The flight is chosen once per leg and kept (the tile of a bomb that is planted or being planted is no bomb ground). **The landing must be free**: when an ant lands on a tile where another stands, the engine throws every ant of the tile to a free neighbour tile and water is no reason to refuse one (the pile-up of 5.36), so two of five ants drowned in the first version; a crew ant walks off the landing at once (an order ends the stun of a flight), and the next hop waits only while an own ant stands on the landing (a crew ant that walks does not count) or an ant of another team is on or beside it: a flight takes longer than a step, so an ant that walks away is gone before the next lands (the hop used to wait for every crew ant within four tiles of the landing). A second order to one ant follows after the level's latency and 20 ticks, 205 at Easy, 80 at Medium and 44 at Hard (Hard's was 60; an order ends the stun of a flight, so it can follow at once). An ant with too few hit points for another hop (under 4) that stands on S is sent to the waiting tiles: it shut S for the ants that can still go, and a leg stood still until the attempt was given up.
- **Row**: the crew ant nearest to the end of the row takes the first token (a planned pick-up: the click that names a power-up tile), walks out, and the next one goes in; the ants that wait stand three steps from the end of the row, never in the first two (one tile wide on ISLANDS: an ant that stands there shuts in the Swimmer that walks out, and in one match of four bots it did for good). The Swimmers are taken when the level has the number it wants (Easy 2, Medium 3, Hard 3) or when the row has none left. A Bomber that flew with the crew takes the last token that is wanted and no other, and only after every plain ant (`AI17.38`): its own power-up is left on a free neighbour tile, which in the one-wide row is behind it, and a token taken earlier would shut the way for the ants that follow. The plain ants drop nothing. At the last token the drop can still shut that Swimmer in (a Swimmer on the fifth token leaves the corner diagonally only if the drop landed on the one tile beside it: 5 of the 13 Bombers that took the last token in 112 expeditions at Medium and Hard, seeds 1 to 14, four seats); no rule frees it, and it counts as a Swimmer that the level has, so the expedition is not made again.
- **Patience**: an attempt that makes no progress for 2,400 ticks is given up (the crew and the Bomber go back to the economy), after 200 when no plain ant of the crew can take a token any more and two or more are left (the ants that are left are stranded with fewer than 4 hit points, and a Bomber takes only the last token; with one token left the Bomber is waited for, and at Easy, where none flies on, one counts too; `AI17.31`, `AI17.35` - `AI17.37`), and made again after 900 ticks, the next one after 1,800, then 3,600 and 7,200 (`ExpeditionTask::pause_after`; `AI17.12`, `AI17.13`): a row that nothing reaches (a stranger keeps the shore tile, no flight has the station tile that it needs) does not hold the crew for two thirds of the match, as the fixed pause of 900 ticks did.
- **Measured**: the tables "The island play" (the expedition as first built) and "The expedition batch" in "Measurements"; the first bomb is planted at tick 115 to 221 at Medium and Hard.

**Timed clicks** (Hard; `LevelPlan::island_timed_row`, `--tune itimed=0` gives the row as it was; `Orders::chain`, `CommandSink::applied_at`, `island_expedition.cpp`). A corner row is Fire, Fire, Swimmer, Swimmer, Swimmer, Fire, Fire with an entrance at each end, and a power-up is solid for the planner: a walk across a hat finds no path, and a walk that ends on one takes it. The old crew sent one ant for every token, so a row cost five ants to get three Swimmers out. A person can do better with the engine's own rules (measured on the engine): a walk that ends on a hat takes it only on arrival, and the next click, if it lands after the ant's tile has become the hat's and before the ant is there, ends that walk without taking the hat (the ant is snapped to the tile and walks on). Chained like that, one ant crosses the hats, takes the first Swimmer and walks out over the hats again; the next ant does the same.

- **The window.** From rest the next click must be APPLIED `L` to `L + 1` ticks after the one before, `L = 10 + 8 * (tiles - 1)` for the `tiles` tiles that the click before named (one tile is 8 ticks of walking). The measured safe window is 10 to 12 ticks for a walk heading west or north and 9 to 11 for east or south (3 ticks, 150 ms), so `[L, L + 1]` lies inside both. A click that comes later takes the hat (the old row, as it was: the ant is a plain crew ant that has made its token); one that comes earlier leaves the ant standing, which costs a try and nothing else. No rule of the engine and no command changed: every click is an ordinary move, and a recording replays without any bot.
- **The clock.** A bot decides what it clicks now, not when the engine applies it: the controller asks the sink (`CommandSink::applied_at(now)`, default `now`). The arena's delayed sink says the first even tick at least its latency later (the arena models a turn of 2 ticks, as a room's was before protocol 8), a room's (and the application's host bot) the number of the turn the sequencer seals next, which is the tick at which the turn is applied (a turn is one tick since protocol 8, so a room's clock is exact and the arena is the harder case). In the arena and in a local game no clock but the engine's is read and a match reproduces from its seed; in a room the release of a later click depends on the turns the sequencer has sealed, which follow the room's pace, so such a bot's clicks depend on the timing of the network (the recorded commands replay on a fresh engine without any bot, as for every bot). The controller releases the later steps of a chain at the first tick at which the sink would apply them inside their window; if that moment has passed it drops the step and the rest of the chain (`Fate::Expired`), and so does a newer order that names the ant, a sink that does not accept a click (a paused room answers Ignored) or the ant's death (`Fate::Pruned`). The first click of a chain goes before the other orders of its priority that are due on the same tick (the engine finds paths in the order in which the orders reach it, one a tick: an order that went first would start the walk a tick late and the second click would come too early). The first step is an ordinary urgent click (reaction delay, budget); the later ones skip the reaction delay and the queue but each costs a token (the bucket goes into debt, so the rate over time is still the level's: 3 a second at Hard).
- **The expedition.** One crew ant is staged at rest on the entrance tile and chained over the hats to the first Swimmer (the pick-up is a planned click, which the filter lets through). After the pick-up (about 30 ticks of the Powering-up clip) the Swimmer is chained out over the hats toward an end that it is in line with and that has no Swimmer between (the entrance first), a plain click if the row has no hats there. It stays the task's until it is out. The crew is sized for the Swimmers and one spare (four plain ants for three Swimmers, where the old row took five for its five tokens and had no spare). The row's tokens are ordered by the steps of a walk from the entrance (`walk_of`: from the west end of the south-east row, (58, 59) comes before (59, 59), which the order by distance in a straight line did not give), and the ant that is sent is the one nearest by steps, not by distance. A chain misses when the ant takes a hat (a click that came late) or stands in front of the row with nothing taken; a Swimmer taken by a chain ends the run, and after two misses in a row the task is `timed_off` and goes back to one ant for every hat (an ant that is still behind the hats comes out over them all the same). A chain whose first click never left (refused, or too old) is no miss; an ant that is left in the row without a token (another team took the Swimmer, or a chain ran out) is brought out over the hats like one that took a token. A Bomber takes nothing behind hats, so a crew with no plain ant left is given up after 200 ticks, as in the old row. A row whose hats do not lie on one line with the tile in front of it is worked the old way as well.
- **Fairness.** Hard only, as it needs the 150 ms of a person who watches the ant; Easy and Medium click the old way. The bot reads its own seat's sink and the engine's tick, never a wall clock and never another seat's commands. **Limit:** a room whose sealed-turn counter jumps by 3 or more between two ticks of the engine (a client that catches up) misses the window of 2 ticks; the step is then dropped (`Fate::Expired`) and the row tried again, at worst the old way after two misses.
- **Measured**: "The timed clicks" in "Measurements".

**The ferry** (`FerryTask`, `island_ferry.hpp`, where the island task is active): a Swimmer carries food across the water. It is sent like a worker (one move onto a cell of the pile) to the pile that pays best per tick of its own trip, the search over land and water with the Swimmer's step weights (the water weighs 21 for it and 8,000 for a walker), the piles that no walker reaches first, at most two Swimmers to a pile (`AI18.6`); the engine's harvest loop does the rest (bite, swim home, deposit, back to the pile). Open water only: a tile with a rock or a reed in the lake is no way, the engine lets no ant into it (`AI18.9`). A Swimmer that stands where it stood, empty, 160 ticks after its order left did not obey: it is let go and kept from that pile for 900 ticks (`AI18.7`); one that stands idle with its food for 900 ticks (the engine's walk home ended in "Can't go there.", e.g. a ring of fire walls round the hill) is sent to the hill's entrance, whether or not it works a pile; once it has banked the food its record ends (`AI18.8`; the record of a Swimmer that carried was never looked at before the review). No bridge is needed and none can collapse or jam. A trip to the western pile of the ring takes 464 ticks for 20 points, to the island north of hill 0 223 predicted and 251 measured for 30 points (`AI18.4`); a single Medium bot on ISLANDS (seeds 1, 3, 4, 5; 14,400 ticks, no enemy) scores 2,040 to 2,130 with three Swimmers that carry food: the more Swimmers the better, up to the three of a row, which is why the levels want 2, 3 and 3 (`AI18.3`).

### Cost

(A6) The time of one look (`BotView::build` plus `think`, mean of 400 looks of a warm bot on a match after 3,000 ticks of four standard bots, seat 0, `AI13.1`): TINY 0.8 to 1.2 microseconds (the worker's 0.6 to 0.8), SMALL 1.1 to 1.4 (0.9 to 1.0), MEDIUM 2.1 to 2.7 (1.9 to 2.1), GAUNTLET 2.3 to 2.6 (2.1 to 2.4), TREASURE 3.2 to 3.7 (2.8 to 3.3), ISLANDS 3.1 to 3.8 (2.7 to 2.9), at the three levels, over three runs on a quiet development machine (release build; these are the bot of v0.3.0 to v0.5.0: since the island play of B4a a standard bot's look costs about twice the worker's on SMALL and ISLANDS, 4 against 2 and 10 against 5 microseconds in one run of `AI13.1` on a loaded machine, because its island, expedition and ferry tasks search over water (on the other four maps they are idle); four standard bots play a whole ISLANDS match (seeds 1 to 4) in 0.7 s of CPU time at Medium and 1.2 s at Hard, 0.3 s more than the bots that stood still there). A look costs a few thousandths of the time between two looks (every 4 ticks, 200 ms, at Hard). Whole matches (TREASURE, 14,400 ticks, four bots of one kind, wall time of the arena's process: the engine, the controller, the views and the bots): Easy 131 / 250 / 256 ms (idle bots / workers / standard bots), Medium 144 / 262 / 322, Hard 209 / 320 / 596; the idle bots' time is the engine's and the controller's, so the bots' own time in a match of four is 119, 118 and 111 ms for workers and 125, 178 and 387 ms for standard bots at Easy, Medium and Hard (27 microseconds a look at Hard against the 3.5 of a warm look at a still world: a real look carries the searches of the pick-up trips, the raids and the squad). The dedicated server (`test_server` S3.71: twelve rooms, one person and three bots each, only the server's own thread timed): Hard standard bots cost 2.7 ms per second of play for the twelve rooms on TINY and 15.6 ms on TREASURE (1.3 ms a room), against 2.1 and 9.8 ms with three Hard workers in the same rooms (1.3 and 1.6 times: **within the twice that the acceptance allows**) and 1.6 and 8.1 ms with idle bots (the engine and the referee are most of it); the standard bot's own work at Hard on TREASURE is 7.5 ms a second for twelve rooms against the worker's 1.4 (5.5 times, 0.6 ms per room and second, 0.75 percent of the server's thread); the worst single pass was 14.0 ms (the test's bound is 250 ms). Medium bots on TINY: 2.1 against 1.8 ms.

## Running bots

```
ants --map Original-Ants/Maps/TINY.LVL --bot 1:medium              # you (seat 0) against one bot
ants --player 2 --bot 0:hard --bot 1:easy --bot 3:easy             # three bots
ants --host --bot 2:medium                                         # a room on the local network whose seat 2 is a bot
ants --map Original-Ants/Maps/TINY.LVL --bot 1 --bot 2 --bot 3 --teams 0+1        # you and the red bot against the blue and the black bot
bot_arena --map TINY,MEDIUM --seeds 1..8 --seat 0=standard:hard --seat 1=standard:easy --rotate --out report.json    # B2: cmake --build build --target bot_arena
bot_arena --map TREASURE --seeds 1 --seat 0=standard:hard --seat 1=standard:medium --save-replays DIR   # every match kept as a replay (docs/REPLAYS.md "Matches of computer players"): the site's list shows them
```

**On the game server.** The game server now plays its own matches of computer players, at the normal speed, so that anybody can watch them live, free for all or in teams (`--bot-match-every-min`, [`SERVER.md`](SERVER.md#matches-of-computer-players)); the arena below plays free for all only; the stack file keeps the arena as the service `ants-arena` behind the compose profile `arena`, so it is not started unless `docker compose --profile arena up` asks for it. The stack file (`docker-compose.stack.yml`) describes the arena as the service `ants-arena`: the stage `arena` of `Dockerfile.server` and the loop `docker/arena_loop.sh`. Every `ANTS_ARENA_EVERY_MIN` minutes (5 to 1440, 60 by default) it plays one match of two to four standard bots (a random shipped map and seed, a random choice of two to four of the four seats, and Medium or Hard for each seat by its own coin: any colour can be left out, and any colour can get the Hard bot) and keeps it in the game server's replays folder, so the site's list shows it as "Bot (Medium)" / "Bot (Hard)" ([`REPLAYS.md`](REPLAYS.md#matches-of-computer-players)). It has no network and no ports. Its log says the map, the seats and the seed of each match, and, at the start of the loop, the seed of its own random numbers (`ANTS_ARENA_SEED=N` plays the same run again with the same bash; the arguments of a match are the portable way); the same arguments give the same match (`bot_arena --map MAP --seeds SEED --seat 0=standard:LEVEL ...`). The loop is checked by `tests/scripts/test_arena_loop.py` (with a stand-in for the program) and by the CI, which builds the stage and plays one match of a fixed seed (`ANTS_ARENA_ONCE=1 ANTS_ARENA_SEED=1`) into a folder.

- `--bot SEAT[:SPEC]` may be repeated. SEAT is 0 to 3 (green, red, blue, black), not the local player's own seat. SPEC is `easy`, `medium` (default), `hard`, `idle`, `worker` or `standard`, or `KIND:LEVEL`, and for the standard bot `LEVEL:STYLE` or `KIND:LEVEL:STYLE` (`2:hard:raider`, `1:medium:defensive`: the styles are `aggressive`, `economic`, `raider`, `defensive` and `random`; Hard plays aggressive or raider only; a bot without a style draws its own at the start of every match from its seat's random generator, see "Styles"; the bot's name stays "Bot (Level)"). Refused at startup with a message on stderr: a spec that does not parse, a bot on your own seat, two bots on one seat, `--join`. A game started from the setup screen checks again at START (Fog of War is chosen there): the refusal appears on the setup screen's prompt line.
- **From the desktop start menu** (a native game started with no mode on its command line, [`CONTROLS.md`](CONTROLS.md#start-menu-a-native-game-started-without-a-mode), "Start Menu"): the Single player panel has a row for each of the three seats that are not the player's, **Empty / Easy bot / Medium bot / Hard bot**, and Continue starts the match with exactly the bots that `--bot SEAT:LEVEL` gives: the standard bot of the level (it draws its own style) at the seat, through the same path (`Application::menu_start_single` fills `config_.bots`, `start_game` checks the setup again at START). With two or more bots a **Teams** row appears (Free for all, or You + each bot: `teams=0+1` in the settings file; it reaches the game as `--teams` does), and the panel has the player's own **Your name** field (the one remembered name of Join and Host; the game is played under it, checked by the same rules). All Empty is the original's single-player game with no bot code at all. The panel says "Bots play without fog of war. Empty seats have no ants." when a bot is seated and "No bots: the original single-player game (the other colours stand still)." when nobody is (the other colours then keep their hills and the ants that the map starts each team with (3 to 8 on the six maps of the game, [`GAMEPLAY.md`](GAMEPLAY.md)), the original's roster `0xF`; with a bot the unseated colours have none, the roster of `--bot`) (rule 5: a bot never sees through the fog, so START with Fog of War on is refused with the reason on the setup screen, whose fog buttons are the original's and unchanged; the second sentence is the next rule). The choice is remembered (`bots=off,easy,off,hard` in the settings file, one word per seat), a bot never sits at the player's own seat, and a bot is seated only when asked for: the menu is a person's choice, nothing seats one by itself. `--start-menu --bot 1:hard` starts the rows as the command line says. The menu's **Host** panel offers bots for an online room the same way, a row for each seat after the leader's (below, "On the dedicated server").
- **The "Get ready to play!" dialog costs nobody anything** (v0.2.0): the match clock waits for it, in a local game, in a room on the local network and on the dedicated server, so no bot moves while the dialog is up, whatever the level. In a local game the match then starts for you and the bots on the same tick. In a room the machine that runs the bots (a LAN host, the server's referee) is ahead of a person on the far side of a link by the link's delay plus the jitter buffer (measured 20, 150, 85 and 290 ms on loopback links of 20 and 150 ms, and of 60 and 250 ms with 40 ms of jitter), so a Hard bot's first order can be sealed a few ticks before a person's first click can reach the host: the head start that remains is that delay, not seconds. The first orders of a bot come at about 0.5 s (Hard), 1.3 s (Medium) and 3.1 s (Easy) into the match.
- A game with bots plays with the seats that are taken (you and the bots); empty seats have no hill. The bots are called "Bot (Medium)" etc. (`-N` / `--team-name` names win) on the HUD labels, in the chat log and on the results screen.
- `--host --bot SEAT`: the room shows the bot as a player with the good thumb, a guest that asks for its seat gets the first free one, and the host's machine runs the bot from the moment the match begins (it looks and orders only on the ticks of the match, which start 5 s after the match began, when the "Get ready to play!" dialog is gone: the opening, "Fairness in detail").
- `idle` stands still; `worker` harvests (B3, the yardstick); `standard` is the standard bot (B4-1, "The standard bot"), of three levels that differ as described under "Difficulty levels" and four styles.
- Bots run only on the machine that owns them: a local game, a LAN host, or the dedicated server. A guest never runs bots. If a LAN host leaves, its bot seat leaves with it.
- **On the dedicated server (B6, built in network protocol 11)** the bots of a room are virtual clients that the **referee** runs: one `BotController` per room over the referee's own engine, called from the runner's tick hook after every tick, with a sink per bot that leads into the room's sequencer (`HostSession::submit_bot`: the issuer is the bot's seat, the controller's filter and the level's budget decide what may leave: reaction delay and commands per second, exactly the machinery of the bot seats of a LAN host). Two ways to seat them: (1) a room's specification names them, `"bots": [{"seat": 2, "bot": "medium"}]` (`bot` is what `--bot` takes after the seat: `easy`, `medium`, `hard`, `idle`, `worker`, `KIND:LEVEL`, and a pinned style: `LEVEL:STYLE`, `KIND:LEVEL:STYLE`); the bots sit in the room from the start and count towards the room's players, at least one seat must be left for a person, the seats are distinct, Fog of War is refused (a 400 for each); (2) the room's **leader's START** carries a fill level for each seat (`--fill-bots none|easy|medium|hard`, or four words for the seats 0 - 3: `none,none,easy,hard`; `StartRequest.fill`, protocol 13; **the desktop start menu's Host panel rows "Red / Blue / Black at START" and the web page's `?fill=`**) and, since protocol 13, the **teams** the match starts with (`--teams` for a START; a room that has teams of its own makes those, the Host panel's and the web card's Teams, for every start): the server seats a bot of the seat's own level, named "Bot (Easy)" and so on, in **each empty seat that has a level** (up to the room's players; a seat that a person took is skipped) and starts, one person being enough; a room that fills up and starts by itself, or whose waiting time ends, adds none; with Fog of War the fill is refused and the leader is told why (a notice from the room); a cancelled start takes the fill away again. The status JSON lists the bots (`bots`: seat, bot (with a pinned style), kind, level, style ("random" for a bot that draws its own), name, fill). A bot seat is **never absent, never votes and is not counted as connected** under reconnect (a vote is won by more than half of the **people** who are there; while a person is away the bots' orders wait). A LAN host's own START takes the same options (`NetGame::fill_bots`, `set_start_teams`; its machine runs the bots). Server cost: twelve rooms of one person and three bots each cost the server's thread a few milliseconds a second (`test_server` S3.71; the CHANGELOG has the table), and each room analyses its map once when the match starts.
- (B6, built) The browser build runs bots in a game on this computer (`ants_ai` has no sockets), from the old address `/play.html?map=<key>&bots=<levels>`; rooms with bots need the server, and since v0.8.0 the front page seats them there (the lobby's plan buttons since v0.12.0).
- **Reconnect (protocol 10, a server's room that holds seats: `docs/NETWORK_PORT.md`; tested for a server's bots too since protocol 11, `test_server` S3.67):** a bot's seat is never absent, never votes and is not counted as connected: it has no connection to lose and no state at the table (`Attendance` knows the seats of persons only), so the vote is won by more than half of the **people** who are there; while the match is paused (a person's connection is lost) a bot's orders wait (`HostSession::submit_bot` is refused), and it plays on when the match resumes. Test: `test_lockstep` N2.62.

`--no-wall-time` makes it bit-reproducible; the file is opened BEFORE the first match and judged after the last, and it is written match by match, so memory does not grow with the number of matches: a match keeps its numbers, not its command log), `--selftest`, `--write-baselines` (B3: prints the table of `tests/test_ai/baselines.inc` for the three levels on the six shipped maps, the seeds and the seat rotation of `ants_ai/baselines.hpp`; `--threads` spreads the 18 rows over threads). Exit code 0, 1 (a match that was not played or a check that failed) or 2 (usage, an empty `--out`, a report that could not be written completely, more than 200,000 matches asked for: that count is checked before a single match is built). The arena refuses a map that `LevelData::validate(roster)` says the seated teams cannot play (a start marker outside the grid), as the server and the application do. Every match is independent, so the report is the same for any number of threads; a full-length idle four-team match takes 23 ms (TINY) to 160 ms (TREASURE, ISLANDS) on one core of a release build; four worker bots take about 0.1 s (TINY) to 0.35 s (TREASURE). The report (format 5) has per seat the engine's score, shown score, living ants, eggs, hatched, kills and losses, and what the arena counts itself: `banked` (points credited at the hill: deposits of food and of loot a thief brought home, counted as the rises of the seat's score between two ticks; the price of an egg, min(score, 200), is neither a deposit nor a raid, whether a hatch command or the engine itself starts the egg for a team that lost its last ant, and a deposit in the same tick still counts) and `raided` (points taken by raids). Format 3 adds the can't-go counts of the seat (see "The can't-go loop"): `orders` (the group orders that name ants, as the engine was given them), `refused_orders` (of them, the ones that were followed within 10 ticks by the first "Can't go there." or "Can't do that..." of an ant they named: an indicator of the bot's own refused orders, not a proof of cause), `cantgo` (the news items of those two texts that the seat heard) and `cantgo_began` (of them, the ones in which the ant was not already in the can't-go state a tick before: the rest are the repeats of the original's can't-go loop); a run prints their totals when it ends. Format 4 adds the flowers (see "The flowers"): per match `landings` (the droplets that landed) and `landed` (them by the kind of ant they make: bomber, fire, thief, combat, swimmer), per seat `took` (the power-ups that its ants took, by the kind they became) and `took_at_flowers` (of them, those taken within one tile of a drop tile); a run prints the totals. Format 5 adds the expedition over water (see "Islands"): per seat `expedition` with `planned`, `given_up`, `planted`, `hops`, `landings`, `duds`, `taken`, `swimmers_taken` and the ticks `first_plant`, `first_landing` and `first_swimmer` (0 for what never happened, and everything 0 for a seat that has no standard bot or no water to cross); a run prints, for each seat that made one, the means of the three ticks and of the Swimmers taken and the totals of the attempts given up and of the duds. The engine's own `food_deposited`, `food_stolen` and `food_lost` counters are never incremented by the simulation (they are 0 in every match, and the state hash holds them as 0, so they cannot be fed without changing every golden hash), which is why the report does not carry them.

### The standard bot (B4-1; release build, v0.3.0)

Every number is a `bot_arena` tournament on the real engine: the arena's sink latency of 3 ticks, the opening of the product (`start_hold` 1: one token, the first look on tick 1 + seat), every distinct arrangement of the seats ("rotated": seats are not symmetric on a map), seeds 1 to 12 unless it says more. The tools are `tools/bot_arena.cpp` and the bench bots of `tools/bench_aggressor.hpp` (the scripts that aggregate the reports are not part of the repository; `docs/audit/B4_1_notes.md` has the tuning keys that reproduce every row). The seed of a match is also the seed of the bots' draw of their styles, so the numbers include the styles as they come.

**The metric.** A tactic is judged by the **win rate** (the seat's score is strictly above the best of all the other seats; a tie counts one half; in 2 + 2 the best of the pair against the best of the other pair) and the **margin** (its score minus the best other score), per seed and then as a mean with its standard error over the seeds; the score itself is secondary, and the rank is reported. The decision of the project owner and the coordinator (2026-10-03): the economy tournaments against passive bots reward a bot that never fights, so every conflict tactic is measured against opponents that DO conflict: mirrors of the standard bot with every conflict tactic switched on, the bench aggressor (a bot that takes a Thief and raids the richest hill again and again, and attacks carriers with Combat Ants), the double-thief aggressor, the centre rusher (it sends ants to the contested middle first, like strong players) and the plain standard bot, in free-for-alls and in 2 + 2 alliances, **TREASURE first** (it is the map that is played most) and in the most detail. The economy tournaments of the original acceptance (A1 to A5) stay as a sanity check: a bot that fights must not forget to eat.

### The contest batch (release build)

How it was measured, as in B4-1: one bot with the rules of the contest batch (`standard+styled=1`: its style drawn as the product's bot draws it) against three bots of the strategy of v0.5.0 (`standard+styled=1,prev=1`, below), all at one level, the four rotations of the seats, 16 seeds, so 64 matches a cell. Win rate: the seat's score strictly above the best of the others (a tie counts a half; 25 percent is equal); margin: its score minus the best other score; the numbers after the signs are standard errors over the seeds. ISLANDS has no food that a hill can walk to: nobody scores and every cell is a tie (50 percent). Wherever nothing is contested, wounded or lit (TINY, and Easy everywhere but TREASURE) the new bot plays the game of the old ones and sits at exactly 25 percent. **The reserve Bomber.** A crew that is one ant short of the tokens (a dud, a blow, an ant stranded with 2 hit points) gets the Bomber as its last member, which takes the last token and no other; 13 of 112 expeditions of one bot (Medium and Hard, every seat, seeds 1 to 14) needed it, and without it each would have ended with two Swimmers. In 5 of the 13 the Bomber's own power-up, left on a free neighbour tile, shut that Swimmer in at the corner of the row; it counts as a Swimmer that the level has, so no second expedition goes. Two or more ants short: the Bomber stays at home, the expedition ends with the Swimmers that the plain ants reach and is made again after the pause, with the ants that the hill has by then.

**Where the expedition has nothing to plan, nothing changes.** `bot_arena` of the build before the batch and of this one play the same matches on TINY, SMALL, MEDIUM, GAUNTLET and TREASURE: four standard bots of one level (seeds 1 to 8, three levels) and four worker bots (seeds 1 to 4) a map, 140 matches, and every score, state hash and command count is equal.

## Known limits

- Host migration drops a LAN host's bot: the bot lives in the old host's process and has no endpoint, so the new host drops its seat in its first turn (the survivors continue identically); a dedicated server never migrates.
- Fog of War with bots is refused. A per-team view would need the engine to keep a reveal mask per seat.
- A room's teams are chosen before the start (protocol 13): one pair, and the two other seats too when both play, as in a local game. They are in the room's create block (protocol 15; a word of its code in protocol 13: the Host panel and the front page put them there), so the room makes them for every start, when it fills with people and starts by itself as well as when its leader's START starts it early; a room whose block names none has the leader's START choice (`--teams`) or none, and the players team up in the match with the team-up button as ever. The seats' levels stay the leader's START choice (a level for each seat, as in a local game).
- The worker bot declines every invitation (one Deny); the idle bot says nothing (an invitation stays pending until the human withdraws it); an alliance of all live teams with a positive combined score ends the match within a fifth of a second, so the rules for accepting are deliberate: the standard bot accepts by the rule of "Alliances", never invites and never breaks an alliance.
- Two things the engine does that a worker bot has to live with: a carrier can be left idle with its food when its way home is refused ("can't go" right after the bite or on the way to the gate; the original does exactly this and nothing retries it, `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 5.40: the worker sends the carrier home by hand after 900 ticks, or after 40 when it is far from a gate that nobody queues at, and not at all while a queue of 8 or more carriers is idle: a deep queue is the gate's bottleneck and a rescue only stirs it); and two hills can bite the same last unit of a pile in the same moment, both ants get the points (the sum of four scores can exceed the pot by a few units; this adds score, it costs nobody any).
- The worker has no answer to a hill with many more workers than the gate can serve (12 to 24 ants on one hill, which the shipped maps never start with): the queue at the gate gets deep, every ant beyond the first 6 to 8 adds nothing, and 24 workers score 1310 where 8 score 1650 in the hand-made test world (the economy never orders fewer ants out; "using all of them" beat "limiting them" on every shipped map).
- When two ants that are shut in fail on the same pile together, the pile is blacklisted once (900 ticks) before the ants are blamed; a lone shut-in ant never blacklists a pile that the seat works elsewhere.
- Many community maps give walking ants no food at all; a bot idles gracefully there.
- **The standard bot on the community maps** (user-made maps, not in the repository; `bot_arena --maps-dir DIR` plays any library read-only): the independent review played 533 of the 587 files, alone and with four Hard bots, two seeds each, and found no crash, no hang and no breach of the budget, the view, the determinism or the filters; its economy findings are fixed (H1, M1, M2 in `docs/audit/B4_1_notes.md`). Alone the bot banks 1.165 times the worker on the maps where the worker scores at least 400 and under 75 percent of it on 7 of 225 (choices of the Hard opening's power-up trips, one stranded ant, noise: the notes say which). Not played on the library: Easy and Medium, and the styles against each other. It was also played in hand-made worlds of every rule and in sixteen random worlds with lakes, rocks, unreachable piles and power-ups anywhere (`AI13.3`: nothing refused, bit-reproducible), and its tests ran under AddressSanitizer and UBSan clean.
- **The standard bot (B4-1), measured limits** (numbers in "Measurements"): (1) on TINY and SMALL a Medium standard bot has nothing to do that the worker does not (no power-up to walk to, though Medium takes no flower drop; nothing to fight) and ties with it in two against two; the gate that would break the tie loses there (it is Hard's). (2) A Hard bot of the aggressive style loses to the raider style 40 to 60 percent in the cross table and wins only 16 percent against three plain Hard bots (30 percent for the raider): visible aggression costs against equals and pays against everybody else. At Medium the aggressive style is the weakest (19.3 percent against 25.3 to 28.5 for the others, against each other). (3) The Easy bot keeps about 2.5 points less of its score than the worker against a Hard aggressor on TINY and SMALL (one defender is fed to the aggressor's Combat Ants) and more on MEDIUM and GAUNTLET. (4) The contest of the middle at Hard (two ants) costs about 5 percent of the margin in whole-match duels on TREASURE (K = 0 won 56.5 percent of the duels against K = 2); it shipped by decision, because the owner expects to see it (the contest batch replaced the opening by the race). (5) A bot cannot see another team's eggs, so the wipe-out focus (hunting a team that shows very few ants) never triggers in the benches and is off; the strike never triggered in symmetric play (the contest batch: the pressure triggers it, 0.1 to 0.4 times a match at Hard). (6) The standard bot never invites and never follows a thief to the ally's base. (7) A Combat Ant that harvests costs about ten points of win rate in the Hard mirror against bots that never fight and halves what an aggressor takes: it stays.
- **The standard bot (contest batch), measured limits** (numbers in "The contest batch"): (1) the catch-up strike needs two Combat Ants of the bot's own, which most maps give only through power-ups; it begins 0.1 to 0.4 times a match at Hard and changes no score, and where the map has none (SMALL, TINY) nothing is left to escalate with; workers are no substitute (they kill nothing above two hit points). (2) The safe fire-in refuses while a Fire power-up lies on the field that the victim reaches, and on a map with a flower dropper one nearly always did (measured before the flower batch, whose Hard bots take the Fire that lands): SMALL at Hard gives 4 points of win rate to the bots of v0.5.0, whose lone Fire Ant pays there. The rule is the owner's as built; `sabsafe=0` is the old one, and a cost field from the dropped power-up (who gets there first) would be a refinement. (3) A hunt converts a quarter to a third of what it begins (Hard on TREASURE: 1.84 begun, 0.52 dead a match); a wounded ant that walks on cannot be caught by ants that walk at its speed. (4) The race needs six ants and the first 1,200 ticks: nothing changes on TINY (3 ants) and SMALL (4); ISLANDS has no food on foot. (5) The controller refuses a special order onto any ant, a dying one included, as the HUD's pick does; the simulation accepts some of them (putting out a fire under an ant) and is not changed.
- **The standard bot (can't-go batch), measured limits** (numbers in "The can't-go loop"): (1) the loops stay where the original makes them: a thief shut in on the raid tile of the hole it raided (TREASURE: 6.5 and 8 reactions per 1,000 ticks and seat at Medium and Hard), the ring that the sabotage lights over an enemy gate (SMALL: 0.67 at Hard); the can't-go sound of every ant reaches the person, not only that of his own ants, and a limit on it is the owner's to decide. (2) The quiet raid rule (`raid_min_free` 2) cuts the TREASURE loops by two thirds and costs the bot 5 percent (Medium) and 1.7 percent (Hard) of the score against bots that raid at one free tile. (3) The view cannot tell a waiting walker from a moving one: the gate waits up to 60 ticks for an ant without food on the ramp, and a click decided before that ant steps onto it is still refused, and an ant that stands on the ramp for good and holds food, or that a task of a higher rank holds, is not moved (the ramp unjam of the stand batch takes the others after 40 ticks; the bot's own refused orders are 0.3 to 3.7 a match and seat at Hard, TREASURE the most).
- **The standard bot (flower batch), measured limits** (numbers in "The flower batch"): (1) the gain is on MEDIUM (+90, +85 and +80 points a match in three samples), and the watcher is the rule that carries it; GAUNTLET and SMALL are inside the noise (Hard, the three maps together: +32.8 +- 5.5 over 3,240 matches); Medium and Easy play no flower rule, and one that wins for Medium on SMALL is open (the four rules lost 20 points there: a trip costs income and SMALL has no raid to win back). (2) A watcher is a worker off the economy while it waits, and it cannot tell that another bot will take the landing first (a contest is lost, not entered); no bot guards its drop against a thief. (3) The recall is an order and loses the race for an ant within about two tiles of the power-up: it takes the kind that has just replaced the one the plan wanted. (4) Combat and Swimmer drops have no rule of their own (the flowers of ISLANDS: limit (3) of the island play). (5) A watcher that dies between two looks leaves no hold on the flower (a hit one does: 600 ticks), so the next goes to the same place a cycle later while an enemy camps there; it needs a blast or a burst. (6) A kind that was not among the first three landings has chance 0 until it is seen (on MEDIUM a given kind is missing from the first three 42 percent of the time), so the watcher stays at home when only that kind is lacking, until it lands. (7) Two flowers a tile apart (no shipped map) could make the watcher's place the other flower's drop tile, which keeps that flower shut.
- **The island play (B4a), measured limits** (numbers in "Measurements"): (1) the crew that is flown over stays there (a bomb flight is one way) and is all the workers of the hill's island, so a team whose crew is lost has no Swimmer and scores nothing (as first built, 3 of 30 matches at Hard for seat 3, whose way was three flights, none for the other seats; the expedition batch has seat 3 fly two, "The expedition batch"). (2) No bridge is dug by the level plans: a bridge lost to the ferry (see "The island task"); the guard and the closing are there for the bridges of others, and the labs dig. (3) The flower droppers of ISLANDS (the daisies at the top and bottom of the ring) are not served: nobody can walk there and a Swimmer gains nothing from a Swimmer power-up. (4) The Fire Ants and the Bombers of the expedition stay on the islands: they are spent. (5) A shore is where a punch flings an ant four tiles: an ant of the plan next to an enemy at the water can drown (2 in 96 matches); the plan does not avoid it. (6) The Swimmers of SMALL: only the two on the banks exist at the start, so two seats of four have one early and the others none (Medium: first Swimmer at the median tick 272 and 753 for two seats, 3,655 and 4,000 for the other two). (7) The ferry sees an order that did nothing (the Swimmer stands where it stood) and a carrier that stands with its food, not a Swimmer that the engine stops on the way (a step into a tile that is refused when the Swimmer gets there): it stands idle and empty and keeps its record until its pile is gone. (8) A bridge of a person that is about to go is guarded for the economy's ants: the power-up task alone plans no walk over it (`Tactics::shut_tiles`) and the guard skips the ants of tasks of rank 3 and up, so a raiding Thief, a fighter or a gate carrier on it is nobody's; bots dig no bridge, so this shows only in a room with a person who digs. (9) The analysis counts the hills of teams that dropped out among the special base tiles, as it is made once at the first look (the engine skips them): after a drop-out a few tiles are refused that would be free.
- **The standard bot (expedition batch), measured limits** (numbers in "The expedition batch"): (1) Easy does not fly the Bomber on (`island_fly_on`): its look is every 100 ticks and a leg that waits for the Bomber costs more than the flight saves; its gain is the quicker hops. (2) The Bomber of every island on the route stays where the crew left it, with the Fire Ants and the others that were spent (limit 4 of the island play): at tick 3,200 of a match of one bot about two Bombers of a Medium or Hard seat (one at Easy) are not on the hill's island. (3) A crew that is two or more ants short of the tokens keeps its Bomber at home, and the expedition ends with the Swimmers that the plain ants reach (given up 200 ticks after the last of them can go no further); it is made again after the pause. (4) The Bomber that takes the last token leaves its own power-up on a free neighbour tile, and that shut the Swimmer in at the corner in 5 of the 13 expeditions that needed it; no rule frees it. (5) A hop is a draw (a bomb in five is a dud, and the first dud moves every later one): single matches differ by hundreds of ticks for a rule that changes nothing else, so a rule of the expedition is judged on means over many seeds. (6) The crew counts as one ant short from the ants that can still reach a token: an ant with fewer than 4 hit points, a Bomber that has flown on included, is counted only when it stands on the island of the row. (7) A row more than two flights away is not measured, for no shipped map has one: 10 hit points are four hops with no dud among them (the crew is taken for routes of four flights at the most), and the one Bomber that flies over islands with no Bomber to take spends 2 on each. (8) A dud is told by the state of the ant (burning or stunned on B): an Easy bot, which looks every 100 ticks, finds the ant already idle on B in about 6 percent of its duds (5 of 84 in matches of one bot) and frees the leg only after the wait of a hop (345 ticks). (9) A leg's Bomber that a blast throws to another island is taken for one that flew on, and the leg fetches no other; a second Bomber to take on that island would be needed to gain anything, and none was seen.
- **The standard bot (timed clicks), measured limits** (numbers in "The timed clicks"): (1) Hard only: Easy and Medium click the old way. (2) The window of a click is 2 ticks (`[L, L + 1]`) inside a safe one of 3, and a click applied late takes the hat (a miss; two in a row end the timed row, and the ant that took the hat is Fire for life); the clock of a room is the number of the turn that the sequencer seals, which advances one turn per tick in a match that keeps up and can jump by 3 or more when a room catches up (a step that misses its window is dropped with its chain, the ant stops on the hat before and takes it). A sink that is wrong about its own clock by 3 ticks costs ants: with a lying clock the bot has two Swimmers where it had three. (3) Measured in the arena at latencies of 0 to 4 ticks and in tests of a net game's and a room's sinks, not on a slow link with a real browser. (4) A Swimmer's exit chain is now and then refused ("Can't go there.") when an older order of the same tick reached the engine's path queue first; the first click of a chain goes before the older orders of its tick since the review, and one that is refused is ordered again.
- **The standard bot (war batch), measured limits** (numbers in "The war batch"): (1) the mines are few: they wait for an ant with nothing to harvest (a bot far behind lays them anyway), never take the Bombers of the island expedition and are laid at a pile only on tiles where the enemy is no further away than the bot, so a Hard bot plants 2 to 5 a match on SMALL, 0.2 to 4 on the other maps and none (but the expedition's own) on ISLANDS; a mine hurts a walking ant (-2 hit points, a throw) and costs the enemy no food that was measured. (2) Everyone fighting costs food: four against four the banked food falls 3.6 percent at Hard, 2.6 at Medium and 0.2 at Easy over the six maps (7.6 percent at Medium on SMALL, 7 at Hard on TREASURE); against bots that do not fight the shipped plans bank the same (-0.1, +0.8 and +0.4 percent). (3) Workers kill nothing above two hit points: the kills (0.5 to 2.9 a seat and match) come from the Combat Ants that a map gives and from the bombs. (4) The raider hunt answers an enemy Fire or Bomber Ant within six tiles of the hill only; one that works a pile of the bot far from the hill is left alone (hunts there cost 170 points).
- The reference numbers depend on how the hill banks deposits; if that part of the simulation (or the bot) changes on purpose, the baselines are regenerated with `bot_arena --write-baselines > tests/test_ai/baselines.inc`.
- `MapInfo` is the map as it was at the start of the match. Bombs, fire walls, bridges and eaten power-ups change the real map; a bot re-checks in the next view (`approach_now` asks again, with the engine's dynamic rules: own and allied bombs, a dropped team's queue row; a fire wall or a bridge is read from the grid it is given). On the six shipped maps the flood fill is exact (0 disagreements with the engine's path finder in 18,000 random pairs); on the 586 community maps it was measured at about 93 percent, so it is a hint and failure learning (an order that came back `Applied` and changed nothing) stays mandatory.
- The engine's path finder gives up when a path costs 8,000 or more (a long maze, mud all the way): `MapInfo` offers no approach to such a pile although a flood fill reaches it. It is also not exact: about 3 to 6 percent of the paths it finds cost a little more than the best, so costs are estimates. How much more is a tail, not a number: the worst was +11.8 percent in the run of 3,000 pairs per map that these figures come from, and +22.4 percent over 60 other samples of 300 pairs on TINY alone (5 of the 60 samples were above +15 percent); the test AI1.9 therefore asserts only a sanity bound of +50 percent on that ratio, and that the engine's path is never cheaper than the analysis' optimum.
- Two objects of the table on one anchor (two on TREASURE, neither reachable on foot): the last one owns the cells and sets the points of a bite, the first one loses the units. `PileView::remaining` lists both; `MapInfo` (`cells` empty, `bite_index`) says which can be clicked and which is bitten.
- The trip model is the cycle of ONE worker (there and back at 0.4 tick per unit of cost, plus 70 ticks). Measured against the solo cycle of a lone worker (one run, the numbers of the model after the cost fields start from the whole queue row): for the NEAREST pile of every hill of the five maps with food on foot (the 20 cases that test AI1.17 pins at 25 percent) the model is -0.5 to +12.0 percent off, mean +4.8; over ALL 144 reachable (hill, pile) pairs it was -7.6 to +15.6 percent, mean +2.7 (a one-off run: the suite does not reproduce the 144-pair range). A constant of 45 instead of 70 would be -14.9 to +6.1 percent over the 144 pairs and off by more than 8 percent in 29 of them, so the 70 stays. The 0.4 tick per unit of cost is exact for orthogonal walking and about 10 percent too much for a run of diagonal steps (test AI1.35: 40 diagonal tiles of grass, 448 predicted, about 401 measured), so a diagonal-heavy route is overestimated. With several workers the hill gate takes over (about one deposit per 100 ticks), and the per-ant cycle is the number of ants times that.
