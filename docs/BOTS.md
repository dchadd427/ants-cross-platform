## What a bot is

A bot is a **virtual client**: a seat in a match whose commands are produced by a program instead of a person. It sits next to the simulation, never inside it. It reads the world through a read-only view, decides, and sends `ants::sim::Command`s through exactly the door a human uses.

## The rule

Project rule 8 ([`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md)) allows bots as virtual clients, in the `ants_ai` library only:

- A bot uses the public command interface of a human: a `Command` (group move, special, attack, stop, hatch, the alliance commands) through the `CommandSink` of a local game or the sequencer of a room. `SimulationEngine::apply_command` validates every command, so a bot has a human's powers and cannot break a rule.
- A bot never changes the simulation, the original screens, the lock-step rules of the network protocol or any golden state hash. A normal game runs no bot code.
- **Off by default, always visible.** A bot exists only when asked for (command line, room specification). A bot seat is shown as a bot: its name is `Bot (Level)`, the room marks the seat as a bot, and a person cannot take a name that looks like a bot's (the lobby renames them: spaces and case do not matter), while a bot's name always carries the marker. (In a local game `--team-name` / `-N` may rename a seat; a room never does.)
- **Fair.** A bot knows nothing that a human of its team could not know, has a command budget per difficulty level (see below) and does not move before the people it plays against can: the simulation does not run while the "Get ready to play!" dialog that opens every match is up (v0.2.0: the match clock waits for it), so no bot looks or orders while it is up, and it opens with one token, not a burst. The machine that runs the bots (a LAN host, the server's referee) starts the match before a remote person's screen does, by the link's delay plus the jitter buffer (see "Fairness in detail"). Fog of War is rarely used; with fog off every player sees all ants, so a bot reads the whole world. **A bot together with fog is refused** until a per-team view exists.
- The only computer behaviour inside the simulation stays the original's own: an idle Combat Ant punches the first enemy within three tiles, then returns to where it stood (the auto-engage reflex).

## Fairness in detail

**What a bot sees (`BotView`).** Its own ants (type, tile, hit points, state, whether it carries food), every other team's ants (type, tile, whether they carry food, and their state **as the screen draws it**), the numbers of the score boxes (a team's score plus its ally's, never below 0, not the individual scores; 0 for a team that is not in the match or has dropped out, because its box is covered), its own egg count and incubation, the food piles (remaining units, points per unit; a lunchbox is always reported as worth 30 points, because the picture of a lunchbox does not tell what the ant that died carried), the map, the clock, and an alliance invitation waiting for it. A bot does **not** see other teams' eggs or incubation, other teams' hit points or carried points, or any ant's order. It never reads the news or audio queues (they belong to the screen). (Complete since B2.)

**The state of another team's ant** is read off the clip that is drawn, not off the engine's label. The engine labels an ant "walking" the moment it is ordered, but its sprite keeps the idle clip until the path manager has delivered the path (one path per tick and team: the last of 24 ants waits more than a second) and while it waits behind a blocker; a person sees a standing ant, so the view says idle (the idle label of the ant's type: guard for a Combat Ant, swimming for a swimmer on water). Own ants keep the engine's label. `has_pending_path` answers only for the seat's own ants and `predict_ack` only counts them, so neither says what another team's ants were told; the test AI1.25 compares the views of two engines that differ only in what other teams hide (hit points, carried points, eggs, an egg incubating, orders in flight) and requires them to be identical, for every seat.

**Accepted deviations** (a bot sees a little more than a person, and nothing that decides a game; each is listed so that nobody is surprised): the exact egg stock (the tray draws at most nine eggs), the exact units of a pile (a person counts the bites), and **the owner and the remaining life of fire walls and bridges** on the grid that `BotView::grid()` lends (the screen draws every fire wall and every bridge alike). Decision on the last one: it is accepted, not hidden. A bot that watches continuously would know the same by noting the moment it first saw a wall (a wall burns for a fixed 3,600 ticks) and who lit it is the fire ant standing next to it; hiding it would mean a copy of the grid at every look (144 kilobytes on a 60 x 60 map, 2.6 megabytes on a 256 x 256 one, against a look that costs 1.6 to 3 microseconds now) or a different type for the grid that `MapInfo`, the tests and the bots share. `BotView::grid()` is the one place to change if it ever matters.

**`predict_ack` is the prediction a person's click gets, not a copy of `apply_command`.** It is the engine's own `predict_order_ack` (the lock-step client's immediate feedback), and the differential test AI1.5b holds it against `apply_command` over typed ants, a lake, rocks, bombs, a fire wall, a team that drops out and tiles off the map (about 4,200 random orders from random seats in six worlds: 91 percent agree). It differs in exactly two ways, which the test pins: (1) a special order (bomb, fire wall, bridge) of a Bomber, Fire ant or Swimmer onto a tile where a walking ant stands is predicted 0 (the cursor's rule refuses any occupant) but acknowledged by `apply_command` (the order's rule refuses only an ant that stands still); (2) a goal that the engine's goal adjustment then refuses, such as a water tile with nothing enterable within four tiles, is predicted as acknowledged but gets 0 from `apply_command` (status `Applied`). So 0 is not a promise of a refusal and non-zero is not a promise that the order will be carried out: a bot checks the next view.

**The opening (v0.2.0): a bot does not move while the dialog is up, and starts with one token.** Every match opens with the original's "Get ready to play!" dialog (string 105). It takes every click and every key for at least 5 s: the original's task KWFO closes it 5000 ms after it was made (`docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`, section 21: Ants.exe `0x10254b0`). In the original the match clock and the ants run behind it, so a person loses those seconds; **the remake deviates on purpose, at the owner's request: the simulation does not run while the dialog is up** (a local game counts it in real time, a match of the network starts its first turn 5 s after the match began: network protocol 12), tick 0 runs when it closes, and a person can select and order from the first tick. A bot is a virtual client of the simulation: `BotController::on_tick` is called after every simulation tick and nowhere else, so while the dialog is up no bot looks and no bot orders (the tests assert it in a local game, in a LAN host's room and in a server's room: AI6.9, AI6.10, S3.76, N5.75) and nothing needs to be held back, which is why v0.1.1's hold of 100 ticks is gone. What the opening still gives is the human-like start: a bot looks first on tick 1 + seat (the stagger of the seats is kept, so not every seat looks on the same tick), its first order leaves one reaction time later, and its token bucket starts with ONE token (and refills from the first tick at the level's rate, up to its depth), so its first orders come one by one, as a person's first clicks do, instead of in a burst of up to ten on one tick (the bucket of v0.1.0 started full). History: v0.1.0 started the bucket full and looked on tick 1 + seat; v0.1.1 held the bots for the dialog's 100 ticks while the simulation ran behind it (first look on tick 100 + seat, one token, no refill before tick 101); v0.2.0 has the first look of v0.1.0 and the one token of v0.1.1. **The first orders of a bot come at about 0.5 s into the match at Hard** (a look on tick 1 + seat, a reaction of 6 to 10 ticks: tick 7 to 14), **1.3 s at Medium** (a reaction of 18 to 30 ticks: tick 19 to 34) **and 3.1 s at Easy** (a reaction of 45 to 75 ticks: tick 46 to 79); the match begins for everybody after the 5.0 s of the dialog, which no longer count against it. The opening is the same in every product path: a local game (`--bot`, the desktop start menu), the bot seats of a LAN host, the rooms of a dedicated server and the arena, because `BotController::set_start_hold` has the one constant `ai::kStartHoldTicks` = 1 as its default (the first tick: the first on which a seat looks, releases and refills; a hold of N ticks still works, the tests of the mechanism use 100). Only the tests that measure something else from tick 0 switch it off (`set_start_hold(0)`, `ArenaSpec::start_hold = 0`, each with a comment): with it off the controller is exactly the controller of v0.1.0 (a full bucket). The dialog's own length is another number for another thing, `sim::kMatchStartDialogMs` = 5000 (the HUD's dialog and the hosts' start delay: `net::kMatchStartDelayMs`). There is no wire change for the bots (they run on one machine and their commands travel as data); a match without bots is as every match is. On a dedicated server the referee, which runs the bots, and on a game on the local network the host, whose machine runs them, are ahead of a remote person's screen by the delay of the link plus the buffer of the lock-step runner (measured by the independent review of v0.2.0 with loopback links: 20 and 150 ms on links of 20 and 150 ms one way, 85 and 290 ms on links of 60 and 250 ms with 40 ms of jitter), so a Hard bot's first order can be applied a few ticks before a person's first click can reach the host: the head start that remains is that delay, not seconds.

**How fast a bot acts.** After the opening above, three limits, all in simulation ticks (50 ms): it looks at the world only every `decision_interval` ticks; every command of one look is released after a `reaction_delay` (plus or minus 25 percent, drawn ONCE per look from the seat's own random generator, so the commands of a look leave in the order they were proposed and a later look never overtakes an earlier one); among commands that are due on the same tick the newer one wins for an ant they both name (like the later click of a person); and a token bucket limits commands per second (one token per command, whatever the number of ants). A command that cannot be paid waits and is dropped after a time-to-live because the world has moved on.

**What a bot may command.** Nothing a human could not click: at most 24 ants per command (a chosen conservative cap: the remake's HUD lets a person send up to 32, and the original's limit has not been established), special orders (bomb, defuse, fire, extinguish, bridge) and thief raids name exactly one ant, no `Quit`, no `Drop`, never an attack on an ally (a break of the alliance comes first and a bot never does that to attack), no command for another seat's ants.

**The human's zoom (v0.1.0) is no part of any of this.** The mouse-wheel zoom changes what a person's screen shows, never what the simulation holds: a bot reads the world through `BotView`, which has no camera and no view, and no state hash depends on the zoom. A match that the leader's START filled with bots is a match of the network, which **offers no zoom-out** (a person could not then see more of the map than the others); a local game against `--bot` seats does offer it, as every local game does (`docs/NETWORK_PORT.md`, "The view's zoom and the network").

**Honesty.** Bots are deterministic given the match seed and the seat (integer arithmetic, a private random generator, no clock), so a match can be reproduced and a recorded match replays from its commands alone, without any bot.

## What the game looks like to a bot (measured)

Numbers measured on the headless engine (release build) with the worker bot of B3 (the scores per map are in "Measurements" below and pinned by a test), see `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` for the rules themselves.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| What counts | The score: food deposited, plus loot stolen, minus loot lost, minus 200 per hatch. Kills, losses and ant counts are shown on the results screen but never ranked. |
| Food | Finite, shared, never respawns (the reachable points are pinned by test AI1.15). Points per map: TINY 4800, SMALL 4000 (3000 reachable on foot), MEDIUM 4900, GAUNTLET 1800 (1500 reachable), ISLANDS 5600 (none reachable on foot), TREASURE 10950 (8850 reachable). |
| Harvesting | One group move onto a pile starts a loop that runs by itself until the pile is empty: bite one unit, walk home, enter (22 ticks), score, walk back. The economy needs well under one command per second. |
| The hill gate | A hill banks about one deposit per 100 ticks (5 s) once three ants queue. Income is therefore capped near 11 deposits a minute and extra workers beyond 4 to 6 add nothing (more can lower it). |
| Hatching | Always an ant of the level's default type (a plain worker on the six shipped maps; see "Typed ants"), costs 200 points and needs 200, 160 ticks of incubation and 11 more (the emerge clip) before the worker takes an order, one egg at a time, eggs per team TINY 3, SMALL 2, MEDIUM 6, GAUNTLET 6, ISLANDS 4, TREASURE 9. A lone worker that hatches once helps a little on TINY (seat 0 gains 190 to 220 points, the mean of the four seats 35) and loses on the other maps (SMALL -6, MEDIUM -10, GAUNTLET -19, TREASURE -7 percent); one that hatches whenever it can loses 9 to 86 percent on four maps and 21 percent on TINY (see "Measurements"). |
| Typed ants | Only power-ups make Bomber, Fire, Thief, Combat and Swimmer ants: an ant walks onto the power-up and keeps the type for life. **The exception is a level that names a default ant type** (block 3 of the file: 99 of the 540 community maps that load; none of the six shipped maps): every ant of every team, the starting ones and every one that hatches, is of that type from the first second (Combat: the punch and the reflex; Bomber: mines; Fire: fire walls; Swimmer: water and bridges; Thief: the look, the voices, but no raids until it takes the power-up). `AntView::type` shows what the ant is (it is what the sprite and the panel show) and `BotView::default_ant_type()` is the level's default, so the worker bot's pool is "the ants of the default type"; the own type field, which a pick-up and a death look at, is not visible to a person and not to a bot (`docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 5.62). |
| Fighting | Every ant has 10 hit points; one blow per order (1 hp, a Combat Ant 2 hp); an ant at 1 hp walks home and heals. Fights are decided by who strikes first. |
| Thieves | A raid takes up to 50 points from a hill and cannot be interrupted at the raid tile; three fire walls (or bombs) on the tiles east of the hill stop it. |
| ISLANDS | No hill can walk to any food. A bomber plants a bomb inland of a shore tile and an ant that walks onto it is thrown over the channel. |
| Contested play | With four players the whole reachable pot is exhausted on every shipped map, so scores are shares of a pot. |

## Architecture

```
SimulationEngine --(const)--> BotView (+ MapInfo, built once) --> Bot::think(view, orders)
                                                   |
                          BotController: schedule, reaction delay, budget
                                                   |
        CommandSink, issuer = the bot's seat --> apply_command / the sequencer of a room
```

`ants_ai` (namespace `ants::ai`) depends only on `ants_sim`; it has no SDL, no sockets, no threads and no clock, so it builds for the web too. Adapters that talk to the network live in the application (`NetBotSink`) and the server.

**What B1 built** (include/ants_ai, src/ants_ai, tests/test_ai): `rng.hpp` (the splitmix64 generator of a seat and its seed), `bot.hpp` (levels and profiles, `BotSpec`, `parse_bot_spec`, `bot_display_name`, `check_setup`, `Priority` / `Intent` / `Orders`, `Bot`, `BotContext`, `make_bot`), `bot_view.hpp` (the minimal view: the clock, the score and alliance state of every team, the ants), `bot_controller.hpp` and `idle_bot.hpp`. `worker` and `standard` are accepted names that run the idle bot until B3 and B4.

**What B2 added**: the complete `bot_view.hpp` (own egg stock and incubator, the food piles, the borrowed grid, `predict_ack`, `has_pending_path`, `map()`), `map_info.hpp` / `map_info.cpp` (hills, walking-cost fields on the engine's own step weights, pile approach and click tile, walker components, power-ups, the trip model), `arena.hpp` / `arena.cpp` (one match with the real engine and the controller: pure, no threads, no clock, no files; the sink latency, recording, the replay without a bot), `tools/bot_arena.cpp` (the command line, the threads, the report; CMake target `bot_arena`, not part of the normal build), and `BotController::map()` / `BotContext::map` (the controller owns one `MapInfo`, built when it is created). Tests: `tests/test_ai` AI1.x and AI4.x, and `bot_arena --selftest` as suite 2.21.

- **`BotView`** is a copy of what the seat can see: the clock, the scores of the boxes, the alliance state, its own egg stock and incubator, its own ants (everything), the other teams' ants (what is on the screen), the food piles. Everything it keeps is a COPY (the engine's `get_world_state()` reference is a cache that is rewritten after any tick or command). Three things are BORROWED from the engine and valid only while the bot thinks: `grid()` (a const reference to the map: terrain, objects, food, power-ups, bombs, fire, bridges), `predict_ack(command, &needed)` (the engine's own prediction of which ant would acknowledge an order: the prediction a person's click gets, which agrees with `apply_command` except in two documented ways, see above) and `has_pending_path(ant)` (an order given a moment ago has not been answered by the path manager yet). A COPY of a view has none of them (an empty grid, helpers that answer "nothing"): the type cannot be used to read the engine later.
- **`MapInfo`** is the analysis of one match, built once from the grid at the start: hill geometry (ramp, hole, queue row, raid tile, waiting spot; the FIRST hill of a team, as `Grid::find_anthill` picks it), one walking-cost field per hill on the engine's own step weights (Dijkstra from EVERY walkable tile of the hill's queue row at once, because an ant on the ramp steps onto any of the three: a rock on the middle one does not cut a hill off), per pile and team the approach cost and the click tile, walker components (which ants can reach which piles: an ant stranded on another island is never sent to a pile of the hill's island), the power-up list (the engine's own cells: power-ups are tile ids 62 .. 66, so a community dictionary that calls them `.` changes nothing, test AI1.22) and the trip model (`trip_ticks`). The rules it takes from the engine (`MapInfo::walkable`, `step_cost`, `can_step_onto`): terrain, the 4 x 4 mound of every hill, solid objects, the queue row of another LIVE team (R5), a bomb of the team itself or of its ally (R7), the path finder's refusal of tile row 90 on a map taller than 90 rows (the original's path finder never generates a tile of row 90: `path_planner.cpp`, `Ants.exe` `FUN_01019a66`, verified with Capstone and the decompilation, see `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 18; a map of 91 rows or more is cut in two there; the shipped maps have at most 60), and for the LAST step onto a pile or a power-up: not water (a step into water costs the engine's 8,000), not inside a mound, not on another live team's queue row. It is a HINT, never a promise: it describes the map as it was at the start, and a bot still checks in the next view that an order did what it meant. **`approach_now`** asks the same question of the map as it is now (a pile's footprint shrinks as it is eaten, and the click tile of the start may no longer be food; a wall, a bridge or a bomb may have changed the way): it takes the engine's dynamic rules as a `WalkContext` (the team's ally, so that allied bombs block, and the dropped teams, whose queue rows are open ground; `BotView::walk_context()` gives both), works for every object of the engine's table, also a lunchbox dropped after the start (`MapInfo::pile()` is null for it), and costs one walkable mask and one Dijkstra per call (27 to 254 microseconds on the shipped maps, 1.9 ms on an open 256 x 256 map, release build): a bot that re-targets several piles in one look makes ONE `field_now()` and asks for each pile with the overload that takes it.
- **`Bot`** has `think(view, orders)` and `on_command(command, fate, tick)`. A bot never sees the engine's answer to a command (a lock-step room does not give one), so it checks the result in the next view.
- **`Orders`** is the only way out of a bot. Its named methods cannot express `Quit` or `Drop` (`push_unchecked` exists for the tests of the controller's filter, which refuses Quit, Drop and None whatever way they come).
- **`BotController`** owns the bots of one game and is called after every simulation tick: it runs each bot when due, applies the reaction delay and the budget, stamps the issuer, drops ants that died, filters forbidden commands and counts everything.
- **Tasks** (`tasks.hpp`, B3): a bot's wishes become tasks that claim ants in an `AntLedger` (an ant belongs to at most one task), emit orders through `Orders`, check progress from the next view and end with success or failure. Everything a task knows is SOFT: an ant that was hit, healed, born or stranded is simply idle and empty-handed and re-enters the pool, a pile that is empty is no candidate, so a bot may be restarted at any moment (tested: a new worker in the middle of a match finishes within 8 percent of one that was never restarted). What a task does remember (which ant was told what, when the order LEFT, which pile did not start) is a cache and a piece of learning. A bot never sees the answer of the simulation to a command, so a task counts the time of an order from the tick it was RELEASED (`Bot::on_command`, `Fate::Sent`), not from the tick it was decided (the reaction delay and the budget hide the order from the bot for a while; a timer from the decision blacklisted every pile before its first order had left in the first prototype).

**What B3 added**: `tasks.hpp` / `tasks.cpp` (`AntLedger`, `TaskContext`, `Task`, `HarvestTask`), `worker_bot.hpp` / `worker_bot.cpp` (`WorkerBot`), `baselines.hpp` / `baselines.cpp` (the procedure of the pinned numbers, shared by the test and by `bot_arena --write-baselines`), the registry (`make_bot`: `idle`, `worker`, and `standard` as an alias of `worker`), `tests/test_ai/test_ai_worker.cpp` + `baselines.inc` (suite 2.22). **`HarvestTask`** is the economy: the pool is the own Workers that stand idle with nothing in their hands and no order of the task that is pending or younger than 24 ticks; the candidates are the piles with units left that the hill can walk to and that the ant can walk to as well (walker components, so an ant stranded on another island is never sent; an ant that stands where a big pile used to be, on tiles that the start took for solid, is not stranded either), that did not fail lately and whose trip can still be finished (`trip + 10 + the level's longest reaction delay` ticks must be left on the clock: points carried at 0:00 are lost); piles are ranked by points per tick of the trip (`value * 100000 / trip`, Medium and Hard) or by walking cost (Easy: the nearest first); an ant goes to the first pile with room (at most `max_ants_per_pile` and `trip / 90 + 2` ants per pile, because the hill banks about one deposit per 90 to 110 ticks), else to the best; one group move per pile onto the pile's click tile (of the pile as it is now when it has been eaten into: `approach_now`), **and at most 8 ants newly ordered at one look** (the rest of the pool at the next looks). After that the ants loop by themselves.

- **Packed ants.** The limit of 8 is the engine's doing: the path manager (the original's PATHMGR: one request per 50 ms, four search grids) never recovers from ONE order of 24 workers that stand on adjacent tiles: they block each other's paths (a team-mate that stands idle or paused costs 8000), 15 requests stay pending for the rest of the match, and the seat scores 0 (no bot is needed for it: `test_ai_worker` AI3.18 does it with a plain group move). Groups of 8 sent at once still box each other in for hundreds of ticks; 8 at a look do not. Shipped maps start with at most 8 ants per team, so nothing changes there. A **watchdog** is the safety net: an ant on its way to a pile that walks, or waits for its path, on the same tile for 300 ticks (not one that queues at the pile, not a carrier) gets a Stop and is ordered again from the pool, and the number of ants ordered at one look is halved until a quiet time has passed (a bot that sends all 24 at once is repaired after about 300 ticks; 0 points for the whole match without the watchdog). On the community maps the throttle and the watchdog are within the noise of the totals (this bot against itself without them: +4.7 and -1.7 percent, +0.7 and -0.3 percent without 4cornersn3, a map on which no policy wins); they are there for the jam of packed starts, which the shipped maps do not have.
- **Failure learning, ant first.** An order that came back `Applied` and did nothing is a failure: the ant stands where it stood, empty, 24 ticks after the order LEFT, or it shows the "can't go" reaction within one look of those ticks while it is still within a tile of where it stood (a "can't go" later is a jam on the way, no failure of the pile). Whose fault it is: **the ant's** when another ant of the task walks to the pile, bites at it or carries from it, or when the only ant that failed there is one that failed on another pile lately (an ant that is shut in, or whose nook has a blocked door, fails on every pile it is sent to); the ant is then kept away from that pile for 900 ticks and the pile stays as it was. **The pile's** otherwise, when two ants that fail nowhere else failed on it, or when no ant of the task makes progress anywhere (a seat that gets nowhere cannot tell, and a wall that came up after the analysis of the map is learned this way, in one look): it is blacklisted for 900 ticks for every ant. Measured in a hand-made world with a shut-in ant (one or two ants in a walled nook whose door a stranger blocks, two healthy workers, four piles of 14 units): 900 to 950 points and 22 to 23 piles blacklisted before, 1050 points and at most one pile blacklisted now, which is what the two healthy workers make alone. On the community maps the new attribution against the old one (the pile is blacklisted on the first failure) is within the noise of the totals: +2.3 and -0.3 percent, +1.0 and -0.3 percent without 4cornersn3.
- **The map as it is now.** `MapInfo` is the map of the start, and the bot asks again every 200 ticks (10 s) when it matters: a pile that was shut off at the start (a wall that opens, food that plugs a corridor and is eaten) is asked for through `approach_now`, and an ant that the start analysis took for shut in is sent when the hill's walking field of now reaches it. (In a hand-made world a pile behind a plug of 8 units had 60 units left after 6,000 ticks, 350 points; now it is worked.)
- **A stuck carrier is sent home, by the clock.** After a bite the engine's walk home can end in "Can't go there." and the ant stands with its food for the rest of the match: nothing in the original retries it (the cause is written down in `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md`, 5.40: a team-mate that stands paused or idle on the doorway in front of the hill's entrance makes the hill unreachable for the moment of the search, the failed search is answered in the same tick, and the count-0 branch only stops the ant). It happens far from the hill (TINY seat 3: seeds 2, 5 and 8, the ant at its pile 7 tiles from the hill, a fifth of the seat's score) and on the tile in front of the gate (TREASURE seat 0: seeds 1, 2, 3 alone and 1, 2, 5 with four workers, one of six workers for 94 percent of the match, at Medium in v0.0.88; since the walking costs of a hill start from its whole queue row the first piles of that seat are other ones and the jam comes later and more rarely: of seeds 1 to 12 alone only seed 5 at Hard and seed 7 at Easy, with four Medium workers seeds 11 and 12), and a blast or a blow on the way home leaves a carrier standing anywhere. The ants that queue for the gate are idle with food too, so the distance from the hill tells nothing (the first version said "more than 4 tiles"; with 12 or more workers the queue reaches past 4 tiles, and that rescue stirred the queue): **time tells.** A carrier that has stood idle with its food for 900 ticks (the longest legitimate wait for the gate: 772 ticks in 100,000 waits on the five shipped maps with food, 12 seeds, three levels, alone and with four workers; the median is 130 to 136) is sent to the hill entrance by hand, as a person would click it (alone, and not again for 200 ticks); it queues for the gate and delivers. A carrier more than 4 tiles from the mound with nobody queueing within 4 tiles is helped after 40 ticks (nobody queues there: this is the fast path of TINY seat 3). Nobody is helped while 8 or more carriers stand idle at once: with 12 to 24 workers on one hill the queue is deep (waits of 870 / 1,439 / 1,800 / 2,384 ticks for 12 / 16 / 20 / 24 workers), the gate is the bottleneck, one idle carrier more or less costs nothing, and a rescue sends an ant through the crowd (those hills scored up to 10 percent less with rescues). What frees an ant on the waiting tile in the engine: the order to the entrance sets the ant's `home_state` to 1, the path of length 1 completes at once, the arrival queues the ant (priority) and ANTHILLQ dispatches it about 100 ticks later (probed on TREASURE seed 1: delivered 180 ticks after the order). TREASURE seat 0 alone: 2795 to 2980 (Medium), four workers 2265 to 2545 (v0.0.88; now, at Hard seed 5, 2845 without the rescue and 2940 with it, played without the start hold: that jam belongs to the opening of v0.1.0 and AI3.5 keeps it). On the community maps, whole matches with four Medium workers on 492 maps, the rescue is worth 4 to 5 percent: this bot without any rescue scores 5.1 and 3.8 percent less than with it (seeds 1 and 2).

**`WorkerBot`** answers an invitation with one Deny (an invitation waits for ever and an alliance of all live teams ends the match within a fifth of a second; the controller lets an answer through when it answers an invitation that is there), runs the `HarvestTask`, and nothing else: it never hatches (see "Measurements"), fights, raids, uses a power-up or builds.

Bot kinds: `idle` (stands still, for tests), `worker` (harvest only, a fixed yardstick for measuring other bots: frozen once the standard bot exists), `standard` (the bot of the three levels; until B4 it is an alias of the worker, `kind()` says "worker").

`SeatStats` counts commands after splitting: `intents` = queued, `released` = handed to the sink, `expired` = dropped after the time to live, `pruned` = commands that lost ants that died (one with no ant left is never released), `filtered` = refused when proposed, `superseded` = commands that were due together with a newer one and lost every ant to it, `rejected` = released and refused by the sink (0 when the sink is the engine or the sequencer and the bot keeps to what the filter lets through; AI6.7 and AI6.8 check it with a bot that acts, through both of the application's sinks). The controller refuses (counts `filtered`) a command that a person could not click: `Quit`, `Drop`, `None`, an empty list or more than 32 ants, a special order of other than one ant, a group order off the map, an attack on the tile of an ally, an alliance command that names no other seat.

Network additions, all additive, no protocol change (`SlotState::Bot` is in protocol 6; **protocol 11 is the one change: the leader's `StartRequest` carries a fill level and the room's notices travel as `Chat` from sender 255**, `docs/NETWORK_PORT.md` "Protocol 11"): `HostLobby::add_bot` / `remove_bot` / `has_bot`, `HostSession::add_bot_seat` / `is_bot_seat` / `submit_bot`, `NetGame::add_bot` / `remove_bot` / `submit_bot`. A bot seat is a room slot in state `Bot`: it has no connection, never reports a hash, is never dropped for silence and counts as a player. A person whose name starts with "Bot (" is renamed by the lobby, so a name alone never makes a bot, and a bot is never taken for a person.

## Difficulty levels

The economy is nearly insensitive to reaction time and command rate (a harvesting bot needs a few hundredths of a command per second), so levels are defined by the **tactics they use**; delay and rate are fairness limits that bite when fighting.

| Level | Thinks every | Reaction | Commands per second (burst) | Economy | Tactics (from B4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 5 s | 3 s | 0.4 (2) | nearest pile first, at most 4 ants per pile | one island hop on ISLANDS |
| Medium | 1 s | 1.2 s | 1.5 (6) | piles by points per trip | thief and combat power-ups, raids on the leader, guards, swimmer ferry, island hops |
| Hard | 0.2 s | 0.4 s | 3.0 (10) | as Medium | adds fire-wall and bomb defence of the hill, combat squads with a new order for every blow, shore kills, late raids, alliance invitations |

The numbers are proposals, tuned by tournaments in B5. The burst is the depth of the bucket, what a bot that has been quiet can send at once; every bot STARTS with one token (the opening, since v0.1.1), so its first orders come one by one.

## Running bots

```
ants --map Original-Ants/Maps/TINY.LVL --bot 1:medium              # you (seat 0) against one bot
ants --player 2 --bot 0:hard --bot 1:easy --bot 3:easy             # three bots
ants --host --bot 2:medium                                         # a room on the local network whose seat 2 is a bot
bot_arena --map TINY,MEDIUM --seeds 1..8 --seat 0=standard:hard --seat 1=standard:easy --rotate --out report.json    # B2: cmake --build build --target bot_arena
```

- `--bot SEAT[:SPEC]` may be repeated. SEAT is 0 to 3 (green, red, blue, black), not the local player's own seat. SPEC is `easy`, `medium` (default), `hard`, `idle`, `worker` or `standard`, or `KIND:LEVEL`. Refused at startup with a message on stderr: a spec that does not parse, a bot on your own seat, two bots on one seat, `--join`. A game started from the setup screen checks again at START (Fog of War is chosen there): the refusal appears on the setup screen's prompt line.
- **From the desktop start menu** (a native game started with no mode on its command line, README "Start Menu"): the Single player panel has a row for each of the three seats that are not the player's, **Empty / Easy bot / Medium bot / Hard bot**, and Continue starts the match with exactly the bots that `--bot SEAT:LEVEL` gives: the standard bot of the level (until B4 the worker) at the seat, through the same path (`Application::menu_start_single` fills `config_.bots`, `start_game` checks the setup again at START). All Empty is the original's single-player game with no bot code at all. The panel says "Bots play without fog of war. Empty seats have no ants." when a bot is seated and "No bots: the original single-player game (the other colours stand still)." when nobody is (the other colours then keep their hills and six ants each, the original's roster `0xF`; with a bot the unseated colours have none, the roster of `--bot`) (rule 8: a bot never sees through the fog, so START with Fog of War on is refused with the reason on the setup screen, whose fog buttons are the original's and unchanged; the second sentence is the next rule). The choice is remembered (`bots=off,easy,off,hard` in the settings file, one word per seat), a bot never sits at the player's own seat, and a bot is seated only when asked for: the menu is a person's choice, nothing seats one by itself. `--start-menu --bot 1:hard` starts the rows as the command line says. Bots in online rooms are not offered by the menu (B6).
- **The "Get ready to play!" dialog costs nobody anything** (v0.2.0): the match clock waits for it, in a local game, in a room on the local network and on the dedicated server, so no bot moves while the dialog is up, whatever the level. In a local game the match then starts for you and the bots on the same tick. In a room the machine that runs the bots (a LAN host, the server's referee) is ahead of a person on the far side of a link by the link's delay plus the jitter buffer (measured 20, 150, 85 and 290 ms on loopback links of 20 and 150 ms, and of 60 and 250 ms with 40 ms of jitter), so a Hard bot's first order can be sealed a few ticks before a person's first click can reach the host: the head start that remains is that delay, not seconds. The first orders of a bot come at about 0.5 s (Hard), 1.3 s (Medium) and 3.1 s (Easy) into the match.
- A game with bots plays with the seats that are taken (you and the bots); empty seats have no hill. The bots are called "Bot (Medium)" etc. (`-N` / `--team-name` names win) on the HUD labels, in the chat log and on the results screen.
- `--host --bot SEAT`: the room shows the bot as a player with the good thumb, a guest that asks for its seat gets the first free one, and the host's machine runs the bot from the moment the match begins (it looks and orders only on the ticks of the match, which start 5 s after the match began, when the "Get ready to play!" dialog is gone: the opening, "Fairness in detail").
- `idle` stands still; `worker` harvests (B3); `standard` is an alias of `worker` until the standard bot with its tactics exists (B4). The three levels differ in the economy only as described under "Difficulty levels".
- Bots run only on the machine that owns them: a local game, a LAN host, or the dedicated server. A guest never runs bots. If a LAN host leaves, its bot seat leaves with it.
- **On the dedicated server (B6, built in network protocol 11)** the bots of a room are virtual clients that the **referee** runs: one `BotController` per room over the referee's own engine, called from the runner's tick hook after every tick, with a sink per bot that leads into the room's sequencer (`HostSession::submit_bot`: the issuer is the bot's seat, the controller's filter and the level's budget decide what may leave: reaction delay and commands per second, exactly the machinery of the bot seats of a LAN host). Two ways to seat them: (1) a room's specification names them, `"bots": [{"seat": 2, "bot": "medium"}]` (`bot` is what `--bot` takes after the seat: `easy`, `medium`, `hard`, `idle`, `worker`, `KIND:LEVEL`); the bots sit in the room from the start and count towards the room's players, at least one seat must be left for a person, the seats are distinct, Fog of War is refused (a 400 for each); (2) the room's **leader's START** carries a fill level (`--fill-bots none|easy|medium|hard`, `StartRequest.fill`, **the desktop start menu's Host panel "Empty seats at START" and the web page's `?fill=`**): the server seats a bot of that level, named "Bot (Medium)", in **every seat that is still empty** (up to the room's players) and starts, one person being enough; a room that fills up and starts by itself, or whose waiting time ends, adds none; with Fog of War the fill is refused and the leader is told why (a notice from the room); a cancelled start takes the fill away again. The status JSON lists the bots (`bots`: seat, bot, kind, level, name, fill). A bot seat is **never absent, never votes and is not counted as connected** under reconnect (a vote is won by more than half of the **people** who are there; while a person is away the bots' orders wait). A LAN host's own START takes the same option (`NetGame::fill_bots`, its machine runs the bots). Server cost: twelve rooms of one person and three bots each cost the server's thread a few milliseconds a second (`test_server` S3.71; the CHANGELOG has the table), and each room analyses its map once when the match starts.
- (B6, design) The browser build can run bots in a local game (`ants_ai` has no sockets); rooms with bots need the server.
- **Reconnect (protocol 10, a server's room that holds seats: `docs/NETWORK_PORT.md`; tested for a server's bots too since protocol 11, `test_server` S3.67):** a bot's seat is never absent, never votes and is not counted as connected: it has no connection to lose and no state at the table (`Attendance` knows the seats of persons only), so the vote is won by more than half of the **people** who are there; while the match is paused (a person's connection is lost) a bot's orders wait (`HostSession::submit_bot` is refused), and it plays on when the match resumes. Test: `test_lockstep` N2.62.
- `bot_arena` is the headless runner (B2): many matches without a window, seats rotated, reports as JSON. Options: `--map` (names, paths or `shipped`), `--seeds A..B`, `--seat N=KIND[:LEVEL]` (kinds idle, worker, standard: **standard is an alias of worker until B4, and every report says so**), `--ticks full|N`, `--latency-ticks N` (default 3: commands reach the engine at a turn boundary of the arena's own model, every 2 ticks (100 ms), as in a room before network protocol 8; a room's turns are 50 ms now, one tick, so a room is a little quicker than this model, never slower; the pinned baselines are played with the model and it stays), `--rotate` (every distinct arrangement of the bots over the seats), `--repeat N` (every match N times, identical), `--replay-check` (the applied commands, fed into a fresh engine without any bot, give the same hash at every 20th tick and at the end), `--threads N`, `--out FILE` (the JSON has the map names only, no paths, also for a map that does not exist: a path is named by its file name; `--no-wall-time` makes it bit-reproducible; the file is opened BEFORE the first match and judged after the last, and it is written match by match, so memory does not grow with the number of matches: a match keeps its numbers, not its command log), `--selftest`, `--write-baselines` (B3: prints the table of `tests/test_ai/baselines.inc` for the three levels on the six shipped maps, the seeds and the seat rotation of `ants_ai/baselines.hpp`; `--threads` spreads the 18 rows over threads). Exit code 0, 1 (a match that was not played or a check that failed) or 2 (usage, an empty `--out`, a report that could not be written completely, more than 200,000 matches asked for: that count is checked before a single match is built). The arena refuses a map that `LevelData::validate(roster)` says the seated teams cannot play (a start marker outside the grid), as the server and the application do. Every match is independent, so the report is the same for any number of threads; a full-length idle four-team match takes 23 ms (TINY) to 160 ms (TREASURE, ISLANDS) on one core of a release build; four worker bots take about 0.1 s (TINY) to 0.35 s (TREASURE). The report (format 2) has per seat the engine's score, shown score, living ants, eggs, hatched, kills and losses, and what the arena counts itself: `banked` (points credited at the hill: deposits of food and of loot a thief brought home, counted as the rises of the seat's score between two ticks; the price of an egg, min(score, 200), is neither a deposit nor a raid, whether a hatch command or the engine itself starts the egg for a team that lost its last ant, and a deposit in the same tick still counts) and `raided` (points taken by raids). The engine's own `food_deposited`, `food_stolen` and `food_lost` counters are never incremented by the simulation (they are 0 in every match, and the state hash holds them as 0, so they cannot be fed without changing every golden hash), which is why the report does not carry them.

## Known limits

- Host migration drops a LAN host's bot: the bot lives in the old host's process and has no endpoint, so the new host drops its seat in its first turn (the survivors continue identically); a dedicated server never migrates.
- Fog of War with bots is refused. A per-team view would need the engine to keep a reveal mask per seat.
- The worker bot declines every invitation (one Deny); the idle bot says nothing (an invitation stays pending until the human withdraws it); an alliance of all live teams with a positive combined score ends the match within a fifth of a second, so the rules for accepting (B4b) are deliberate.
- Two things the engine does that a worker bot has to live with: a carrier can be left idle with its food when its way home is refused ("can't go" right after the bite or on the way to the gate; the original does exactly this and nothing retries it, `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 5.40: the worker sends the carrier home by hand after 900 ticks, or after 40 when it is far from a gate that nobody queues at, and not at all while a queue of 8 or more carriers is idle: a deep queue is the gate's bottleneck and a rescue only stirs it); and two hills can bite the same last unit of a pile in the same moment, both ants get the points (the sum of four scores can exceed the pot by a few units; this adds score, it costs nobody any).
- The worker has no answer to a hill with many more workers than the gate can serve (12 to 24 ants on one hill, which the shipped maps never start with): the queue at the gate gets deep, every ant beyond the first 6 to 8 adds nothing, and 24 workers score 1310 where 8 score 1650 in the hand-made test world (the economy never orders fewer ants out; "using all of them" beat "limiting them" on every shipped map).
- When two ants that are shut in fail on the same pile together, the pile is blacklisted once (900 ticks) before the ants are blamed; a lone shut-in ant never blacklists a pile that the seat works elsewhere.
- Many community maps give walking ants no food at all; a bot idles gracefully there.
- The reference numbers depend on how the hill banks deposits; if that part of the simulation (or the bot) changes on purpose, the baselines are regenerated with `bot_arena --write-baselines > tests/test_ai/baselines.inc`.
- `MapInfo` is the map as it was at the start of the match. Bombs, fire walls, bridges and eaten power-ups change the real map; a bot re-checks in the next view (`approach_now` asks again, with the engine's dynamic rules: own and allied bombs, a dropped team's queue row; a fire wall or a bridge is read from the grid it is given). On the six shipped maps the flood fill is exact (0 disagreements with the engine's path finder in 18,000 random pairs); on the 586 community maps it was measured at about 93 percent, so it is a hint and failure learning (an order that came back `Applied` and changed nothing) stays mandatory.
- The engine's path finder gives up when a path costs 8,000 or more (a long maze, mud all the way): `MapInfo` offers no approach to such a pile although a flood fill reaches it. It is also not exact: about 3 to 6 percent of the paths it finds cost a little more than the best, so costs are estimates. How much more is a tail, not a number: the worst was +11.8 percent in the run of 3,000 pairs per map that these figures come from, and +22.4 percent over 60 other samples of 300 pairs on TINY alone (5 of the 60 samples were above +15 percent); the test AI1.9 therefore asserts only a sanity bound of +50 percent on that ratio, and that the engine's path is never cheaper than the analysis' optimum.
- Two objects of the table on one anchor (two on TREASURE, neither reachable on foot): the last one owns the cells and sets the points of a bite, the first one loses the units. `PileView::remaining` lists both; `MapInfo` (`cells` empty, `bite_index`) says which can be clicked and which is bitten.
- The trip model is the cycle of ONE worker (there and back at 0.4 tick per unit of cost, plus 70 ticks). Measured against the solo cycle of a lone worker (one run, the numbers of the model after the cost fields start from the whole queue row): for the NEAREST pile of every hill of the five maps with food on foot (the 20 cases that test AI1.17 pins at 25 percent) the model is -0.5 to +12.0 percent off, mean +4.8; over ALL 144 reachable (hill, pile) pairs it was -7.6 to +15.6 percent, mean +2.7 (a one-off run: the suite does not reproduce the 144-pair range). A constant of 45 instead of 70 would be -14.9 to +6.1 percent over the 144 pairs and off by more than 8 percent in 29 of them, so the 70 stays. The 0.4 tick per unit of cost is exact for orthogonal walking and about 10 percent too much for a run of diagonal steps (test AI1.35: 40 diagonal tiles of grass, 448 predicted, about 401 measured), so a diagonal-heavy route is overestimated. With several workers the hill gate takes over (about one deposit per 100 ticks), and the per-ant cycle is the number of ants times that.
