# Bots: history

The measurements, the tests and the story of each batch of the computer players, in the order of the chapters of [`BOTS.md`](../BOTS.md). The rules and the current numbers are in that page; where the two disagree, that page is right.

**Status: B1 to B4-1 and the batches after it are built (see "Roadmap", at the end of the page)** (B1, the plumbing: the `ants_ai` library with the controller and the idle bot, `--bot` for local games and for the host of a room, bot seats in lobby, session and `NetGame`; B2, perception and the arena: the complete `BotView`, `MapInfo` (the static analysis of the map, with a conformance test for every rule it takes from the engine), the match runner and the headless `bot_arena`, followed by an adversarial review that found 35 points, all fixed: see [`docs/audit/B2_notes.md`](../audit/B2_notes.md), "Review"; B3, the economy: the task model (`tasks.hpp`: `Task`, `AntLedger`, `HarvestTask`), the worker bot (`worker`, harvest only; `standard` was an alias of it until B4-1) and the reference numbers of every shipped map pinned in `tests/test_ai/baselines.inc`, written by `bot_arena --write-baselines`; **B4-1, the standard bot (v0.3.0): `standard` is its own bot, of three levels and four styles, that gathers food, takes power-ups, raids, defends its thief hole and itself and, at Hard, guides its carriers at the gate and attacks; see "The standard bot", "Measurements" and `docs/audit/B4_1_notes.md`; B4a, the island hops, the bridges and the swimmer ferry, is built: see "Islands"; the contest batch, the race for contested food, health and the kill, a safe fire-in and the endgame, is built: see "The race for contested food" to "Behind the leader and the endgame" and [`docs/history/bots_contest_notes.md`](../history/bots_contest_notes.md); the two are joined in one bot (v0.9.0): see "Task ranks" and [`docs/history/bots_integration_notes.md`](../history/bots_integration_notes.md)**). **B6 is partly built (network protocol 11): the server runs the bots of a room, seated by the room's specification or by the leader's START (a fill), see "Running bots" and `docs/NETWORK_PORT.md` "Protocol 11"; the choice of the fill on the desktop Host panel and the web page is built (both say under it that "Bots gather food, raid and fight back."); results and the match API are still design; replays are built ([`REPLAYS.md`](../REPLAYS.md)).** B5 below is design (update this line with every milestone). **v0.2.0 (network protocol 12): the match clock waits for the "Get ready to play!" dialog that every match opens with, so no bot (and no person) acts while it is up (the machine that runs the bots, a LAN host or the server's referee, starts the match a little before a remote person's screen does: the link's delay plus the jitter buffer, 20 - 290 ms in the measurements, see "Fairness in detail"); a bot looks from tick 1 and starts with one token, so its first orders come one at a time at the speed of its level (the opening, see "Fairness in detail"). v0.1.1 had held the bots for the dialog's 100 ticks while the simulation ran behind it.** The original 1998 game has no computer players: it was an internet game and every ant command came from a person. This document describes how computer players are added without touching the 1:1 core.

## The standard bot

### Aggression

- **What pays.** The opening's power-ups (Fire first), raids once the thief is taken from its harvest loop, the double thief and the theft at Hard (+46 margin: +515 against +469 with one thief), the gate at Hard (the biggest single gain), the Combat Ant as a harvesting worker with its reflex (against the bench aggressor Hard keeps 2,251 points with it and 1,191 without, win rate 95.1 against 85.4 percent; against a mix of aggressor, rusher and plain bots 60.4 against 48.8 percent), low raid thresholds, and no keeping away from guarded hills.

- **What costs, and so is off in every plan** (Hard against Medium, Medium and Easy, 288 matches each; the mirror is Hard against three plain Hard bots, where 25.0 percent is the symmetric result): the raw harassment squad of Combat Ants (76.4 percent, margin +268 against 96.7, +515; in the mirror 7.3 percent), the parked guard (81.4, +224; mirror 10.9), hatching for the squad (37.7, -146; mirror 0.0, -1,246), the ambush at a thief hole (inert against the Medium bots, 18.8 in the mirror), the strict contest order (-198 and -287 of margin at Medium and Hard when it is added to the shipped plan, in a mirror of four standard bots), strikes and the wipe-out focus (inert in symmetric play), hatching for fights (-28 and -41), no raids at all (74.5, +155). A harvesting Combat Ant is neutral against Medium and Easy bots (96.2, +522 without it) and costs about eleven points of win rate in the Hard mirror (35.9 against 25.0 percent without it), where its reflex starts fights that nobody gains; it is kept because it halves what an aggressor takes and it is the fist of the aggressive styles.

### The race for contested food (contest batch)

*What was wrong (v0.5.0).* `HarvestTask::step` ordered the piles by points per trip, and only the capped opening (`contest_opening_ants`: Medium one ant, Hard two, Easy none, until tick 1,200) put the middle first, so for most ants, and at Easy always, the near Cheerios came first. The strict answer, every contested pile before every safe one (`contest_aware`), loses: it won 6.2 percent of 96 matches on TREASURE (25 percent being equal) because the far contested piles starve the near ones that are the bot's own.

### Fights of its own: health, the kill and the skirmish (contest batch)

A report after play: a bot that attacks an ant that is low on health should continue and try to kill it (KDR: kills over losses), and an ant of 4 HP was not killed.

*What was wrong (v0.5.0).* `FightTask::start_fights` began a fight only from a blow on an own (or allied) ant, so a wounded enemy that hit nobody was never attacked. A fight ended with the first blow it landed (the struck ant plays a hit clip and `attackable` is false meanwhile). And `AntView::hp` was 0 for every other team's ant, so a wounded enemy could not be told from a fresh one: the view now gives the hit points of every ant it lists ("Fairness in detail").

### Fire play (contest batch)

Two reports after play: it makes no sense to send a single Fire Ant to an enemy base unless the enemy's Fire Ant has already been killed; and a bot could put out a fire on a tile where a Fire Ant stands on top of it, which it should not be able to do.

### Behind the leader and the endgame (contest batch)

A request after play: the losing player should be a little more aggressive.

*What was wrong (v0.5.0).* The strike (a force of Combat Ants that hunts the leader's carriers near its hill: `StrikeTask`) began only when `Standing::behind` held: a margin of 150 points or 12 percent of the leader's box, with the leader at 300 points or more (`standing_of`). In a close match nobody is that far behind until the last minutes (four equal bots end within 100 to 250 points of each other), and the strike was off in every plan but one style.

### The war batch

A report after a match of four bots on SMALL, 2026-10-09: the bots did not fight, they only ate; they should start more fights with each other, fire at their opponent, and try to kill an enemy Fire Ant that comes for them; and the Bomber placed no bombs, though it should bomb up the food so that the others cannot eat it.

*What was wrong.* Four bots of one level on SMALL (seeds 1 to 40) made 0 to 1 attack orders, 0 kills, 0 bombs and 0.17 fire walls a match at every level: the economy took everything in minutes 0 to 5, the hunt and the fire-in wait for a wounded enemy or an enemy that cannot put the fire out, and the Bomber of the opening is taken for a defence that no thief ever called for. Four rules were measured one by one, each behind plan fields (`bot_arena --tune war=0` puts the plans of before back; `LevelPlan` made by hand has none of it; `without_war_batch(plan)` is the same for the tests).

### The stand batch

A report after a match of four bots on TREASURE, 2026-10-10: the bots were really bad at fighting and did not stand their ground. A Combat Ant blocked the way of a regular ant and was not moved; a Fire Ant that fired a team in was left alone, though the team had a Fire Ant that could have tried to kill it, or at least to knock it off the fire and then get it with its other ants; the bombs began only in the last two minutes, although a team that was losing should have put a bunch of bombs down on the enemy base and harassed it instead of letting the enemy fire it in; a team that is down by a few hundred points is very unlikely to win by just continuing to eat, so rushing the enemy with all its ants is always an option, and bombs round its own base make an attacker come one or two ants at a time and path around them; the Bomber should be used for offensive and defensive bombs, but placed only three bombs, when it could bomb up a whole area and make walls out of its unlimited bombs.

*What was wrong.* (1) The hunt of a Fire Ant that fires the gate in could never succeed: the enemy Fire Ant stands ON a wall of the ring, only a Fire Ant can walk onto a fire wall (the path of every other ant ends on the tile, "Can't go there."), so the hunt skipped it (no blow of a worker or a Combat Ant reaches an ant on a wall): the bot left the other walls burning for `fire_defence_hold` (1,800) ticks, put them out and the enemy Fire Ant stayed where it was. (2) The Bomber laid its mines for an ant with nothing to harvest, or from war tier 3 (the last minutes of a lost match), and never more than three at a pile, at the gate or (when it was added) at home. (3) Nothing round the bot's own hill stopped a walk to it. (4) A bot far behind kept eating: its assault is a force of two or three. (5) An ant without food that stood on the ramp shut the way for a carrier, and the gate only waited for it. Six rules, each behind plan fields (`bot_arena --tune stand=0` puts the plans of before back, `without_stand_batch(plan)` is the same for the tests; a `LevelPlan` made by hand has none of it):

### Styles

How the styles fare (win rate, margin, attack orders per match, what each keeps against the bench aggressor, the cross table of the Hard styles and the round robin of the Medium ones) is in "Measurements". At Hard both styles are far above the floor that was set (at least 80 percent against Medium, Medium and Easy with a clearly positive margin: 88.4 and 95.8), and the aggressive one holds its own against the raider (40.0 to 60.0 in the cross table, from 16.9 to 83.1 before its squad learned); at Medium the aggressive style is the weakest against equals (19.3 percent against 25.3 to 28.5 in the round robin) and by far the strongest against Easy bots (96.9 against 71.9 to 83.3): the squad is the price and the prize of visible aggression.

### The can't-go loop

**The quiet raid rule, one value away** (`LevelPlan::raid_min_free`, 1 in every plan; `bot_arena --tune raidfree=2`). Most of the loops on TREASURE are thieves shut in on the raid tile of the hole that they raided (86 percent in phase A), and the thieves that went to a hole with one free tile in front of it are shut in the most easily: one wall lit behind the thief, or one ant, and the hole is closed on it. Asking for two free tiles cuts the loops by two thirds, and it costs strength where the other side raids at one tile. Four bots of one level on TREASURE, seeds 1 to 48, each variant against the bot as it was (`cg=0`) in the same matches (per seat and match; the differences are paired by match, with their standard errors):

| | can't-go reactions per 1,000 ticks | refused orders | score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium, shipped (`raid_min_free` 1) | 5.70 -> 6.48 (+0.78 +- 0.46) | -1.34 +- 0.22 | +0.2 +- 7.9 |
| Medium, quiet (2) | 5.70 -> 2.27 (-3.42 +- 0.46) | -2.22 +- 0.41 | +15.9 +- 7.3 |
| Hard, shipped | 8.09 -> 8.04 (-0.05 +- 0.53) | -1.87 +- 0.28 | -6.3 +- 5.3 |
| Hard, quiet | 8.09 -> 2.00 (-6.09 +- 0.50) | -4.24 +- 0.28 | +14.7 +- 3.6 |

Among bots that all play the quiet rule nothing is lost (a raid at one free tile costs the thief about what it takes), but a person raids at one free tile, and two bots with the rule against two without it (`styled=1` against `styled=1,cg=0`, the six arrangements of the seats, TREASURE) score:

| | points a match and bot | share of matches won | reactions per 1,000 ticks and seat (with / without the rule) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium (144 matches), shipped | +3.6 +- 25.7 | 51 % | 6.02 / 6.16 |
| Medium, quiet | -112.0 +- 27.1 | 36 % | 2.38 / 5.53 |
| Hard (288 matches), shipped | -12.3 +- 20.6 | 47 % | 8.03 / 7.87 |
| Hard, quiet | -37.3 +- 18.9 | 45 % | 2.58 / 7.65 |

That is 5 percent of the score at Medium and 1.7 at Hard. Of the three parts of the quiet rule only the free tiles cost (Medium: the free tiles alone -104.3 +- 33.8; an ant standing on a tile in front of the hole alone +26.8 +- 27.6; an ant on the raid tile alone +1.4 +- 25.2), so the shipped plans keep the two checks and not the free tiles. Whether the bots should be quiet at that price is left open, next to the two ways below.

**The ant that leaves** (`gatehold`: `bot_arena --tune gatehold=0` switches the wait off, `cg=0` too). A report after play: the gate sent a carrier into the hole, but the other ant had not fully exited. The engine's own trace of 240 matches of four Hard bots (TINY, SMALL, MEDIUM, GAUNTLET and TREASURE, seeds 1 to 48) shows that most of the gate's clicks onto the entrance that the ramp refuses meet an own ant without food on its way out, held up behind another ant of the gate (170 of 196 clicks without the wait); the view draws that ant as Walking, so the ramp check above does not see it. The click now waits for it, 60 ticks at the most (`LevelPlan::gate_leaver_ticks`, Hard's, as the gate is). The gain is small and not sure: 170 such clicks in 113 matches without the wait and 140 in 100 with it (0.71 and 0.58 a match, -0.125 +- 0.069, paired by match), 94 to 62 on the 24 seeds that the rule was chosen on and 76 to 78 on the 24 played afterwards. The rest were decided up to 13 ticks before the engine gets a click, before the leaver stepped onto the ramp, or while it was held up at the hill, where the view does not show it. Strength is neither lost nor gained that the measurement can see (Hard with the wait against Hard without it, 864 matches: +3.3 +- 10.1 points a match and bot; the arena's counter of refused orders, a count by timing that takes in every order, reads 1.19 against 1.21 a seat and match, within its noise). Rules that hold the carriers back more refuse fewer clicks and, together, cost strength (-13 to -24 points a match and bot); they are not built. An ant that stands on the ramp for good was not helped (2 of the 480 matches played with and without the wait: in one the bot's own carriers shut its way on, in the other an enemy's ring of fire walls had shut it in and it was left idle there); since the stand batch an own ant without food that stands idle on the ramp or the entrance for 40 ticks while a carrier waits is sent aside ("The stand batch": the Combat Ant of the reported replay, 2026-10-10). The study, the table of the rules tried and the mutants are in `docs/history/bots_cantgo_notes.md`.

*Measured* (release build; Hard against Hard, `standard:hard`, the shipped bot with its style draw, against `standard+styled=1,unjam=0:hard`, the same style draw with the rule off; both seat orders, paired by match, with standard errors): the jam shows in two-bot matches (seats 0 and 2 of TREASURE: 13 steps aside in 16 matches, one jam left, the one that an enemy ant holds) and hardly in four-bot matches (none in 64 bot seats of seeds 1 to 16). Two-bot matches on TREASURE, seeds 1 to 480 (960 matches): +32.0 +- 43.2 points a match and bot, 53.3 percent of the matches won, can't-go reactions per match and bot -20.6 +- 6.7 (that is where the rule works: the thief gets out and banks, the loops are shorter). All six maps, seeds 1 to 96 and two seat pairs (seats 0 and 2, seats 0 and 1; 2,304 matches): +12 +- 17 points a match and bot, and on the five maps other than TREASURE between -3 and +6 points on every map, with errors of 6 to 93; no change at all on TINY, SMALL and ISLANDS, where no thief is ever shut in. ### Cost

(A6) The time of one look (`BotView::build` plus `think`, mean of 400 looks of a warm bot on a match after 3,000 ticks of four standard bots, seat 0, `AI13.1`): TINY 0.8 to 1.2 microseconds (the worker's 0.6 to 0.8), SMALL 1.1 to 1.4 (0.9 to 1.0), MEDIUM 2.1 to 2.7 (1.9 to 2.1), GAUNTLET 2.3 to 2.6 (2.1 to 2.4), TREASURE 3.2 to 3.7 (2.8 to 3.3), ISLANDS 3.1 to 3.8 (2.7 to 2.9), at the three levels, over three runs on a quiet development machine (release build; these are the bot of v0.3.0 to v0.5.0: since the island play of B4a a standard bot's look costs about twice the worker's on SMALL and ISLANDS, 4 against 2 and 10 against 5 microseconds in one run of `AI13.1` on a loaded machine, because its island, expedition and ferry tasks search over water (on the other four maps they are idle); four standard bots play a whole ISLANDS match (seeds 1 to 4) in 0.7 s of CPU time at Medium and 1.2 s at Hard, 0.3 s more than the bots that stood still there). A look costs a few thousandths of the time between two looks (every 4 ticks, 200 ms, at Hard). Whole matches (TREASURE, 14,400 ticks, four bots of one kind, wall time of the arena's process: the engine, the controller, the views and the bots): Easy 131 / 250 / 256 ms (idle bots / workers / standard bots), Medium 144 / 262 / 322, Hard 209 / 320 / 596; the idle bots' time is the engine's and the controller's, so the bots' own time in a match of four is 119, 118 and 111 ms for workers and 125, 178 and 387 ms for standard bots at Easy, Medium and Hard (27 microseconds a look at Hard against the 3.5 of a warm look at a still world: a real look carries the searches of the pick-up trips, the raids and the squad). Medium bots on TINY: 2.1 against 1.8 ms.

### The standard bot (B4-1; release build, v0.3.0)

Every number is a `bot_arena` tournament on the real engine: the arena's sink latency of 3 ticks, the opening of the product (`start_hold` 1: one token, the first look on tick 1 + seat), every distinct arrangement of the seats ("rotated": seats are not symmetric on a map), seeds 1 to 12 unless it says more. The tools are `tools/bot_arena.cpp` and the bench bots of `tools/bench_aggressor.hpp` (the scripts that aggregate the reports are not part of the repository; `docs/audit/B4_1_notes.md` has the tuning keys that reproduce every row). The seed of a match is also the seed of the bots' draw of their styles, so the numbers include the styles as they come.

**The metric.** A tactic is judged by the **win rate** (the seat's score is strictly above the best of all the other seats; a tie counts one half; in 2 + 2 the best of the pair against the best of the other pair) and the **margin** (its score minus the best other score), per seed and then as a mean with its standard error over the seeds; the score itself is secondary, and the rank is reported. The decision (2026-10-03): the economy tournaments against passive bots reward a bot that never fights, so every conflict tactic is measured against opponents that DO conflict: mirrors of the standard bot with every conflict tactic switched on, the bench aggressor (a bot that takes a Thief and raids the richest hill again and again, and attacks carriers with Combat Ants), the double-thief aggressor, the centre rusher (it sends ants to the contested middle first, like strong players) and the plain standard bot, in free-for-alls and in 2 + 2 alliances, **TREASURE first** (it is the map that is played most) and in the most detail. The reported replay is a match of four, so three arrangements are played: **two bots** (one of each; seeds 1 to 150 in both seat arrangements, 300 matches a cell), **two against two** (two shipped, two old; seeds 1 to 100 in both arrangements, 200 matches a cell) and **four against four** (a match of four shipped bots against a match of four old ones, seeds 1 to 100; the figure is a seat's mean banked food, percent of the old bots'). ## Roadmap

Each step is a release with the full test suite.

- **B1 plumbing (done)**: `ants_ai` skeleton, controller, idle bot, `--bot`, bot seats in rooms.

- **B2 perception and the arena (done)**: `BotView` complete, `MapInfo`, the match runner and `bot_arena`; see [`docs/audit/B2_notes.md`](../audit/B2_notes.md) for what was measured and where it differs from the design.

- **B3 economy (done)**: the task model, the worker bot, pinned baselines; see [`docs/audit/B3_notes.md`](../audit/B3_notes.md) for what was measured, the mutation check and where it differs from the design.

- **B4a island hops and the swimmer ferry (done)**: the expedition by bomb flights, the bridges and the ferry of the standard bot on ISLANDS and SMALL; see "Islands".

- **B4-1 the standard bot (done, v0.3.0)**: the standard bot with its three levels and four styles: the opening's power-ups, the thief hole's fire walls, the counters of enemy fire and bombs, raids, fights, the gate, the accept rule for alliances, the aggressive styles of Hard; see [`docs/audit/B4_1_notes.md`](../audit/B4_1_notes.md) for what was measured, what was left out and where it differs from the design.

- **The contest (done)**: the race for contested food, health and the kill, a safe fire-in and the defence against it, no special order onto an ant, the escalation of a bot that is behind and the last minute; see [`docs/history/bots_contest_notes.md`](../history/bots_contest_notes.md) for the evidence, the mutants and what was left out. Next, in the same plan: bombers and the other specials (stage 2; the power-ups came first, as the flowers).

- **The can't-go batch (built)**: a counter of the can't-go reactions and refused orders in `bot_arena`, no order into a hill that no walk joins, no raid at a hole that an ant holds, the ramp and the ant that leaves over it; the loops that the original makes when ants are shut in by fire stay, and the quiet raid rule is one plan value away; see "The can't-go loop" and [`docs/history/bots_cantgo_notes.md`](../history/bots_cantgo_notes.md) for the evidence, the mutants and what was left open.

- **The flowers (built)**: the view and the memory of the flower droppers, the side of a drop, the Fire Ant at home, the recall and the recovery of a pick-up trip and Hard's watcher; see "The flowers" and [`docs/history/bots_flowers_notes.md`](../history/bots_flowers_notes.md) for the evidence, the mutants and what was left out.

- **The expedition batch (built)**: the Bomber of the expedition flies on with the crew, the route is the cheapest by flights, a crew that is one ant short gets the Bomber for the last token, the hops are quicker and a crew that can go no further is given up after 200 ticks; see "The expedition" and "The expedition batch" and [`docs/history/bots_islands_notes.md`](../history/bots_islands_notes.md) for the evidence, the mutants and what was left out.

- **The war batch (built)**: the assault of the ants with nothing to harvest, the fire-in of Medium and Hard, the hunt of the Fire and Bomber Ants that come near the hill and the mines round the piles an enemy works and the gate of the best opponent, and the losers fight harder (the war tier); see "The war batch" and "Measurements".

- **B5 tournaments**: tuning by self-play, regression ladder, community-map sweep.

- **B6 integration**: server room specification (**built**: `bots`, protocol 11), the leader's fill (**built**) with its choices on the desktop Host panel and the web page (**built**; the status line says what START will do), results, replays (**built**, [`REPLAYS.md`](../REPLAYS.md)), the match API, and, built in v0.1.0, the chat box of the 16:9 setup screen with the leader's fill footer (the classic screen keeps T / a click on the status line).
