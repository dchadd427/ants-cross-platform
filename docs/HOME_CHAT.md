# Home's chat: the chat dock, emoji and voice, and the login

Part of [Home and the Room](HOME_SCREEN.md), the front page, with the room inside it. The Games list and the public Room are in [Home's games](HOME_GAMES.md).

## The chat dock

Home carries the site's global chat in a panel along the bottom of the body, under the games and the players, as wide as they are together: 160 to 260 px high (28% of the window; 240 to 300 px, 34%, in a window that is wider than 700 px and at least 501 px high, where the composer has two rows), with the composer under the log (who the visitor is, and Log out, are in the chip's dropdown in the bar, not here). The minus button folds it to its header, and it opens folded in a window under 601 px high or 700 px wide or less. On a phone (a window up to 700 px wide or up to 500 px high) it is the **Chat view** of the strip: open, filling the page between the bar and the strip, with no fold (`home.js` opens it with its view and folds it with the others). It is a part of Home's column, so nothing lies over it and it lies over nothing, and there is no "no room" state: the games take what the chat leaves. `chat.js` and `chat.css` load once, after the first paint, when Home is first shown (a link that opens the Room loads nothing until Home shows), and no other page loads them or opens a hub socket. The socket stays open while the Room shows (and [the players list](HOME_SCREEN.md#the-players-list) speaks through it: the dock hands it the welcome, the frames of the list and the loss of the link, and writes what the list sends), so the **unread count** on the strip and the header goes on: the lines that came while the dock was folded, on the Room or in a hidden tab, not the visitor's own and not the history.

- **The log** shows `name: text`, the names of a moderator (yellow) and of an administrator (red), the hub's own lines ("<name> was kicked.", "<name> was banned.") drawn quietly with no author, 100 lines at most (`del` takes one away). The name is the one that the author was shown by when it wrote the line (its Discord display name or its username, as it picked: [`HUB.md`](HUB.md#names)); a name is not unique, so a line is the visitor's own (the white name, and not unread; a staff member's own lines keep their role color) by the key that the hub sends with it and with the welcome's `you`, and by its name only for a hub that sends no keys. A moderator's copy of a line also carries the author's username (nobody else is sent it); the page draws it, in brackets after the name, for a person who holds a role and for nobody else (see Roles and commands, below), and every other line shows the name and the text; a moderator's or an administrator's name is in color (below). All of it is text from strangers: made with `textContent` only, never markup (the one thing that becomes a link is an address, see **Links** below), cut to 32 and 1,000 characters, the invisible characters that the hub removes taken out here too (a test keeps the two lists in step; a name loses its variation selectors as well), each name and line in a `<bdi>`; a frame that is too big, not JSON or of a kind that is not known is ignored. The log is `role="log"`, polite, and a flood is not read out line by line: two lines in 3 s are, then one summary ("8 more messages"), which goes when the next window of 3 s starts.
- **Lines never overlap.** The log is a column that scrolls, and no line is squeezed when it is full (`.gc-log > * { flex-shrink: 0; }`): a line is always as high as its wrapped text. The account menu's Admin page line has its own class (`gc-adminpage`), never `gc-admin`, which marks an administrator's chat line (a rule of the button once gave every administrator's line 46 px, and a full log squeezed them to it, the words over the next line). The opt-in check `tests/scripts/web_home_chat_lines_browser.py` measures it in Chromium ([`TESTING_PYTHON.md`](TESTING_PYTHON.md)).
- **Time and days.** Every line starts with its time, at the left, in a column of one width so that the messages line up: the hub's own time of the line (`at`, seconds since 1970; a line that has none shows none, the page never makes one up), written by `Intl.DateTimeFormat(undefined, {hour: 'numeric', minute: '2-digit'})`, so it is in the device's time zone and language and its 12 or 24 hour setting, dim, read before the name; its `title` is the full date and time (and, for a moderator or an administrator, the line's number first). A small centered divider ("Today", "Yesterday" or a short date, with the year when it is not this one) stands before the first line and wherever two neighbouring lines are on different local days; it is a separator and not a line (the hub's own quiet lines have a time too; a private answer has none).
- **Links.** In a line, an address that starts with `http://` or `https://`, or a bare `www.` host (made `https`), is a link: an `<a>` made with `createElement` and `setAttribute`, with `target="_blank"`, `rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow ugc"` and `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"`. Nothing else is: every other scheme (javascript, data, vbscript, file, about, blob, mailto, intent, ...) stays text, and so does an address with a user name or password, with no host or with a host that has no dot. The address is read with the `URL` constructor; the end of a sentence (`. , ! ? : ; ' "` and a closing bracket that nothing in the address opened) is not part of it. The link shows what was typed; past 48 characters it shows the host whole, then `/…` and the last 17 characters, so that a cut never hides the host; its `title` is the whole address (for a host in other letters, the punycode host first). A line makes at most five links; the rest is text. A link is underlined, has a visible focus ring, and keeps both in forced-colors mode. The hub's own lines and the in-game chat are unchanged, and the hub does nothing about links.
- **The composer** (the same in every window; `chat.css`, `talk.css`) is one box with the site's corners, as in a chat app (8px, the radius of the site's buttons; the field 4px; the emoji and microphone buttons, Send, the picker and the words bubble 8px; no pills and no circles, in every window), and it starts as **one row** in every window: the field one line high (44 px), then the emoji and microphone buttons (quiet, 44 px), then Send (the buttons stand at the right, near Send, for a right hand; the field keeps the rest of the row: 93 px at 320 px wide, 151 px at 390). When the words need a second line, `talk.js` (asked by `grow()` of `chat.js`) sets the class `gt-tall` on the form and the box has **two rows**: the field on top over the whole width, as tall as its words up to four lines (110 px), then it scrolls inside itself, whatever the length of the draft (a line of 1,000 characters is four lines and a scroll; four lines on a phone held upright, two lines in a wider window), and under it, inside the box, at the right, the buttons and Send (the room at the left is empty); the class goes away when the words fit on one line again (the field is cleared by Send, or the words are deleted). The rule is measured at the width of the one-row field, the narrow one, every time, so a text that fits on one line only in the wide field of two rows stays at two rows and the box never flips on a key; an empty field and a field that is off are always one row. A low window (500 px high or less, such as a phone held sideways) is one row always, one line high, so that the log keeps its room. On a phone held upright the page around it is a step smaller (chat text 15 px, the heading row 36 px, less padding), so the log keeps its room: with the keyboard closed, rows of chat text that fit in a page area of 448 x 842, 412 x 791, 390 x 720 and 360 x 616: 22, 20, 17 and 12 (16, 13, 7 and 6 before). The field's text stays 16 px, so that an iPhone does not zoom into it. The field is a `<textarea rows="1" dir="auto">` (right-to-left text starts at the right; the buttons do not swap sides), and it acts as the one-line field it replaced: **Enter sends** (so do Shift+Enter and Ctrl, Alt or Meta + Enter; an Enter that ends an IME composition does not, in Safari's order too, where it arrives with `keyCode` 229 and no `isComposing`; a phone keyboard whose Enter arrives as a line break, `beforeinput`, sends too), Enter goes through the form's own submit (`requestSubmit`, or where a browser has none, iOS 15, a submit event that can be cancelled), so the emoji and voice buttons hear it as they hear Send; no line break is ever kept (a run of pasted breaks becomes one space, and the caret stays after the same letter); a press on Send while the field has the focus leaves it there, so the phone's keyboard stays up (on a desktop too); the field is read to a screen reader with the hint "Enter sends the message." (visually hidden); while the box grows or shrinks a log that is read at its end stays at its end, and the box is measured again when its view is shown. `dir="auto"` holds in every window. The script (`chat.js`) sets the height of the words (one line to four) in every window, and the style sheet's `max-height` holds it to two lines in a wide window and to one in a low one; the script reads no media query. The box has a gold edge while the field has the focus. For a visitor who is not logged in the field is off and Log in stands where Send does, in one row. A page where `talk.js` or `talk.css` did not load keeps the plain field with Send beside it. A phone upright keeps the page's viewport as it was (no `interactive-widget`): the keyboard slides the page or squeezes it as the browser decides.
- **Who may write** is the hub's welcome (`docs/HUB.md`). Without `you` and with `login:false` the dock is read-only: "Chat is read-only for now", no field and no Send. With `login:true` and no `you` the field is off and says "Log in with Discord to chat", and a Log in button is where Send would be (as drawn). With `you` the field and Send are there, and the dock's login place shows who it is ([the login](#the-login)). A line goes out only on an open, welcomed socket, at the page's own pace (eight at once, then one every 0.6 s; the hub's is ten and half a second), and every `err` code has its plain words (a line with `<` or `>` is not sent: "Lines cannot hold < or >"; a text that the hub accepted in the last 31 s is not sent again, as the hub remembers every one; a text that it refused is forgotten here, so it may be sent again: the hub answers in order, so the refused line is the oldest that has no answer yet, whatever was said since; a line is answered by its echo, which the hub composes (NFC) and may take characters out of, so an echo that is the line composed, or the line with characters taken out, answers the oldest line that it is part of; a moderator's command (`/mute ...`) waits in the same list as the lines and is answered `ok` when it was done (nothing from a hub that does not say it): the hub says `bad` to a wrong one, so a `bad` is the oldest command's and any other refusal the oldest line's, and `power`, `role` and `who` are only ever a command's (a wrong command and a line sent together are told apart; when nothing of that kind waits, the oldest send of the other kind; a refusal of a command with another code, with a line that the hub took in flight, is told to that line), and a command is never put back in the field, as Enter would issue it again; a send that nobody answers is forgotten after 8 seconds). "7 here now" says "500+" over 500 (the page's own clamp: a hub keeps 300 sockets at most, so it shows only for a count that no hub makes).
- **Roles and commands.** The welcome's `you.role` (`none`, `mod`, `admin`; a hub that says only `mod` has a moderator) and the `you` frame that changes it while the page is open are the only things that say what the visitor may do. The role is the color of the NAME, never of the message: an administrator's name is red (#ff5f66, a lighter red of the Leave buttons' family, 6.0 to 1 or better on every dark panel it sits on) and a moderator's yellow (the page's gold, about 12 to 1 or better), on the account chip (on a dark plate, since the chip's face is teal, and on its circle), in its dropdown, in the Players list (the own row and the others: the Staff group, administrators first) and beside chat lines; a person with no role has the plain color. Color is the only visible sign, so the role is also in the accessible name (a visually hidden word before the name, the `title` of the name, the chip's label: "administrator", "moderator") and the dropdown says it in words. The other people's roles are the hub's `role` on a line and on a row of the list (docs/HUB.md). A person's own text never makes a role, a color or the hub's own line: only the flags `mod`, `role` and `sys` of the hub's frames do. A moderator or an administrator (nobody else, whatever the hub sends) is shown the username that the hub sends, in brackets after the name where it differs from it. No line shows a number: the line's id (the one that `/del` takes) is kept as `data-n` on the line, for everybody, and a moderator or an administrator sees it as `#n` in the title of the line's time (hold or point at the time). A line that starts with ONE slash is a command whoever types it: it is sent as typed and the hub decides (docs/HUB_COMMANDS.md); the page keeps no table of commands, so no word is offered, refused or answered here by its name. `//x` is sent as typed too, and the hub posts `/x` as an ordinary line. `/help` is never sent (in any case, with or without words after it): it leaves a slash in the field, which opens the helper. **The helper** (`cmds.js`, `cmds.css`; the dock asks for them through the loader that `home.js` gives it, once, when the hub's `cmds` frame holds a row: a guest, a reader and an account with no row download nothing) lists the account's own commands, the rows of that frame (the word, how it is typed, what it does, and the group: Everyone, Moderators, Administrators), in a list over the field while its text is a slash and letters (on a phone the same list, full width, above the keyboard). Typing narrows it to the words that start with the letters; Up and Down choose (the chosen row also shows how the command is typed); Tab or Enter write the chosen word into the field, with a space where names follow (Enter on a whole word that takes nothing more sends it); Escape closes it and the panel stays; a tap chooses. A `cmds` frame again, after a change of role, changes the open list at once, so nothing of a higher role is ever drawn. Choosing `/bots` opens **the builder**, a box over the page (a sheet from the bottom on a phone): how many (1 to 10,000, in a box, with the buttons 1, 10, 100 and 1,000), one every X minutes (0 to 120, in halves), the map (the admin page's own list of maps, `admin_map_pick.js` and `admin.css`, loaded on the first press of Map; the catalog and the number that run at once are GET /admin/api/bot-options and GET /admin/api/bot-matches/summary, asked as the admin page asks; a map that cannot be had leaves the rotation), the rules, the bots, the level and the teams (a split needs three or four bots), a plain line of what will happen with these numbers, and the exact command. It only writes the command into the field: Enter sends it, and the hub runs it under its own rules ([`/bots` is the admin page's Bot matches](MAP_SUBMISSIONS.md#bot-matches-on-the-admin-page)). The answers to a command are private lines of this screen, tagged "Only you", under a quiet echo of what was typed, never sent and never posted, and gone with the next welcome: `ok` ("Done: ..."), `bans` (the names and the day of each ban, or "Nobody is banned."), the codes `power` ("You are not allowed to do that"), `role` ("Could not confirm your role, try again in a moment"), `who` ("Nobody by that name is here"), `rate` ("You are going too fast, try again in a moment") and `bad` ("That command is not right. Type /help to see the commands."), and the hub's `reply` frame, a code and numbers whose words are the page's: a word that is no command, or a command of a higher role, is answered "That command is not one that you have. Type /help to see the commands." whoever asks; `/ignore` and `/unignore` say that it is done or why not; `/bots` says what started (how many go now and how many wait), the status of a batch (a card with a bar and its numbers), what was stopped, and what was refused (a bad count or spacing, a map that is not there with up to three close names, too fast with the seconds to wait). Names and arguments are text only: never markup and never a selector.
- **The moderation sheet** (`mod.js`, `mod.css`; the page asks for them after the dock, once, and only when the welcome or a `you` frame names a visitor with a role, or the page holds a room, for the invitation in [Invitations and mentions](#invitations-and-mentions): a person with no role who holds no room never downloads them and sees no change, and a right click and a long press on a line are the browser's own). Staff act on a person's line without typing. A **right click** on a line that has an action opens the sheet (the browser's menu is held back on those lines only, and a link in a line keeps its own menu). A **long press** on a touch screen does too: 500 ms with the finger still (lifting it, moving it more than 10 px, a scroll, a second finger and a mouse button all end it), the line shows a gold bar from 150 ms on as the feedback, and the phone buzzes lightly where it can; the phone's own callout and text selection are held back on those lines only, and only on a touch screen (a desktop keeps its text selection). The finger that opened the sheet is still on the screen, and the browser may send its click to what stands under it when it lifts: the sheet takes no press while that finger is down and for 400 ms after it lifts. The **keys** work too: the log is still one Tab stop, Up and Down choose a line among those that have an action (a hidden status line says which, and a screen reader's description of the log says how), Enter, the menu key or Shift and F10 open it, Escape lets it go (it is then not also the dock's own Escape, which folds the chat); a press of a mouse or a finger on the log lets the line that the keys had chosen go, so that one line is marked at a time. Each line that has an action carries a description for a screen reader. What a viewer is offered is the page's guess from the viewer's role and the line's (the hub decides all the same): Delete this line on every line that has an action, and on a line of one's own that is all; Mute (10 min, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week, or a number of minutes from 1 to 10,080, the hub's own longest, with its own Mute button that stays off until the number is good), Kick, and for administrators Ban and "Ban and delete their lines from the last hour" (the hour counts back from the ban); nothing on a line of an administrator, nor on one of a moderator for a moderator, nor on a line of the hub's own. Under the quoted line of another person's line the sheet has a first row, **Recent messages from Clover (6)**: the lines of that person that are in the page's log now (the welcome's history and what has come since, at most the log's 100 lines), found by the hub's key of the account that wrote them and never by a name (a changed name is the same person, the same name with another key is not, a line with no key has no row), never the hub's own lines; the row stays with one line, a line of one's own has none, and a person with no role never sees it. It opens a second step on the same sheet: the lines newest first, each with its time in the log's own words (the full date as its title; a day heading, in the log's words for the day, only when the lines are on more than one day), as text only (an address is not a link), the line that the sheet was opened on marked "this line", and Back. A line of the list opens the sheet of that line (Delete works on it; a line that the viewer may not act on is shown as text only). A Delete that was done from there goes back to the list with the focus on the next line, else the one before it, else Back. The list is one Tab stop, Up and Down, Home and End walk it, and Escape or Back return to the sheet with the focus on the row. The count and the list follow the log at once: a new line joins at the top, a deleted line goes (the focus moves on if it was there), a line that leaves the log's 100 leaves the count, and a new welcome after a reconnect gives both the new history; if the line that the sheet was opened on went while the list was open, Back closes the sheet and says so. The sheet is a card in the middle of the window, a sheet at the bottom of a phone held upright, and a card with the actions two to a row held sideways. Delete, Kick and Ban ask once more in a card whose safe answer, Cancel, has the focus and whose red buttons take no press for 400 ms; a mute asks nothing (picking a time is already a second tap, it ends by itself and `/unmute` lifts it). The sheet types the hub's own commands through the dock (`/del 148`, `/mute #148 60`, `/kick #148`, `/ban #148`, `/ban #148 purge`): a person is named by the NUMBER of one of their lines, never by a name that was typed or guessed, so there is no new power (docs/HUB.md "Moderation"). One command at a time: the controls take no press until the hub has answered, at most 8 seconds (then "Nothing came back: check the chat before trying again."). The answer is a toast under the bar, a polite status that takes no focus and no press (a press goes through it to the line below; only its OK button takes one; 6 seconds, a refusal 12 seconds with an OK button; the next sheet takes it away), and the same words are added as a private line of the chat: "Done: Clover is muted for 1 hour.", "You cannot do that to Sage (a moderator).", "Chat is offline: nothing was done." The sheet is a modal dialog labelled by its title and described by the quoted line; the focus goes to the first action (Cancel on a card), Tab stays inside, Escape is Back (from the mute step and from the list) and then Close, what stands behind is inert, a press on the dark part is Cancel (not in the first 400 ms), and on close the focus goes back to the log. A `you` that takes the role away closes the sheet and takes the marks off at once, and so does a new welcome (a reconnect) after which the viewer is offered something else on that line than the sheet shows (the line is not in the new history, the role is another); a line that goes while its sheet is open closes it (not while the list of recent messages is open: see above). The same sheet opens from a person in the players list (the list's own script tells it of its list once both are there, whichever comes second), in the same ways: a right click, a long press of 500 ms (the row shows a gold bar inside its left edge from 150 ms on) and the keys (the list is still one Tab stop; Up and Down choose a row that has an action, Down from nothing the first and Up from nothing the last; Enter, the menu key or Shift and F10 open it; the words that say which row is chosen are in a box of the list's own, since the chat dock is not shown on a phone's Players view). A press or a key that starts on a button of the row, its Join, is the button's (the arrows scroll the list) and never opens the sheet; the list shows nothing new (no button, no tag), and a person with no role sees no change. The offer follows the same rules (nothing on an administrator's row, on a moderator's row for a moderator, or on the visitor's own place, or on a person who is Away, since the hub takes a row only for an open socket, and where nothing is offered nothing opens and the browser's own menu stays), minus Delete. The person's sheet has their circle, name and where they are in the list's own words ("Waiting · Treasure · 2 of 4") and no quote, no username and no recent messages (a row has no key of an account, and a name is not one person). Mute is not offered before the hub has said whether the person is muted: the sheet asks `/mutes` when it opens (the dock's `mutes(done)`: the end of each mute and the number of the person's row, never a name), and until the answer comes (8 seconds at most) the Mute row is off and says "Checking whether Clover is muted"; a person who is muted has Unmute in its place ("Muted until 2:51 PM today", in the log's own words for the time), which asks nothing more; a check that fails is a row, "Check again", and so is a list of 50 mutes (the most that the hub lists, the ones that end soonest) that does not name the person, since it tells nothing about them. The check is as old as the sheet: a mute that another staff member makes while the sheet is open is not seen. The person is named to the hub by the NUMBER of the row (`/mute row:4 60`, `/unmute row:4`, `/kick row:4`, `/ban row:4`, `/ban row:4 purge`), never by a name (two people may share one). The numbers are the hub's own for the length of one run: the sheet of a person closes without a word when the socket goes, and with "That person is not here any more." when the list is drawn again without them. The answer is the same toast and private line ("Done: Clover can write again."). The dock offers the sheet `log`, `you()`, `line(id)`, `by(id)` (the lines of the author of line `id`, newest first, for staff only, as `{ id, text, at }`: the key stays in the dock), `when(at)` (the log's own words for a time), `arrived(fn)` (told when a line comes), `command(text, done)`, `mutes(done)`, `note(text)` and `rows(fn)` (`chat.js`), and the players list offers it `list` (its scrolling box), `person(i)` (a row as `{ i, name, role, at, where }`) and `drawn(fn)` (told each time the list is drawn again; `players.js`); nothing of it exists for a person with no role.
- **Removed.** On `bye` the hub is about to close the socket, and the page shows a plain notice that stays: after a kick "A moderator removed you from the chat. You can log in again." (the login is over, so the next welcome names nobody: the field is off and the Log in button is there), after a ban "You are banned from the chat. You can still read it." (the visitor reads on; the field is off and says why, and Send is gone). The page connects again once, as after any close, with its usual wait and backoff (a hub that sends `bye` to every socket is not met with a storm); a refusal `banned` to a line leaves the same notice, for a page that was loaded after the ban. A welcome that names a visitor again ends the notice of a kick. A person whose ban was lifted reloads the page to write again: the hub does not tell a page that a ban is over, and the page does not ask (it refuses the line itself while the notice stands). A role that changes keeps the place of a reader who is up in the log; Log out and Forget me redraw the log at once, without the usernames, the numbers and the private answers.
- **Images** (`pics.js`, `pics.css`, `post.js`, `post.css`: the page loads the reader's part with the first line that has an image and the poster's part for a person whom the hub says may post; the dock itself only reads `img` of a line and `you.img`, sends the line with the upload's id and answers a paste of a file for a person who may not post): [`HUB_IMAGES.md`](HUB_IMAGES.md#the-page).
- **Offline.** A hub that is not there, slow or garbage is "Chat is offline" and "reconnecting…": a try after 1 s, doubling to 60 s, each wait spread from half to one and a half times (never under 1 s or over 60 s), so that pages that lost one hub do not come back in waves; a link must hold 30 s to start again at 1 s; a tab or a network that comes back tries at once. A ping every 25 s, and a hub that does not answer for 30 s is let go. A hidden tab keeps the socket and paints nothing. Nothing is kept in the browser, and nothing the chat does can block Home, the Room or START.

## Invitations and mentions

The people part of the chat ([Alerts, invitations and mentions](HUB_PEOPLE.md) is the hub's side; [the bell](HOME_SCREEN.md#alerts) is the list of what comes of it). Every name and line is DOM text, never markup.

- **Invite to my room.** A person who is in a room (public or invite-only, leader or guest) can invite a person to it from the Players list and from a name in the chat. A **right click** (a **long press** on a phone, the menu key or Shift and F10) on a row of the Players list, or on the **name** of a chat line (the text of a line keeps the browser's own menu and Copy; moderators keep the whole line, as before), opens the person's sheet ([the moderation sheet](#the-chat-dock) is the same sheet): the person's circle, name and where they are, one row **Invite to my room** with the room under it ("Treasure · 2 of 4 · 2 seats free"; a guest in another's room: "Wren's room · Treasure · 2 of 4"; with no room: dim, "Open a room first"), then **Message**, **Follow** and **Ignore** and **Report** ([below](#private-messages-and-follows)), and Cancel; for staff the moderation comes after them as before. A visitor who is not logged in has the sheet only in a room, with the one row (the browser's own menu stays elsewhere). The row is dim, with the reason, when the room is full ("Your room is full (4 of 4)."), when the person is in the room ("In your room") or in a match ("Playing a match"), when the person was invited less than a minute ago ("Invited 15 s ago. Again in 45 s."), when the person is not here now (a name of the chat), and for a visitor who is not logged in ("Log in to invite people. Share sends your link."). A press asks the hub with its own command by number, never by a name: `/invite row:5` for a row of the list, `/invite #148` for the author of a line, after the page has told the hub the room as it is now (`here`'s `inv`: the code, map and seats; the code of an invite-only room goes to the hub for this alone). The answer is a toast over the page ("Invitation sent to juniper.", or the refusal in gold: the same words as the command's answers) and a private line of the chat ("You invited juniper."). Arrows keep scrolling the list for a person with no role.
- **The invitation.** The invited person gets an alert of kind invite ([the bell](HOME_SCREEN.md#alerts)) and, where they are looking: the chat is on the screen, a block **only that person sees** ("Only you": "Clover invited you to their room." and "Treasure · 3 of 4 in", with **Join**); it is dim, with no button, when the hub says that the room has closed or filled ("The room has closed."). Otherwise a toast with Join (10 seconds). Join is the Games list's own Join, with its question when the page holds a room. A person who ignored the inviter ([`/ignore`](HUB_COMMANDS.md#ignore-and-unignore)) is sent nothing and the inviter is not told. The line holds only the inviter's name, the map and the seats, never typed words, so it is no way to put text into someone's chat.
- **`/invite NAME`** is a command of everybody who is logged in ([HUB_COMMANDS.md](HUB_COMMANDS.md)) with the helper: `/inv` narrows to the row, and after `/invite ` and a space the helper lists the people who are here (the visitor's own row and a person who is away left out), the ones who can be invited first, then the dim ones with the reason as under the person's sheet; letters narrow it (a name is matched from its start, any case, the letters marked), Tab chooses (the field then holds `/invite maple.leaf`) and a whole name is sent by Enter. The answer is a private block under the typed command ("Invitation sent to juniper.", "You are not in a room. Use Quick host to open one, then invite.", "Your room is full (4 of 4).", "You invited juniper a moment ago. Try again in 45 seconds.", "Too many invitations. Wait a minute, then try again.", "Nobody called “zed” is here. Type /invite and a space to see who is." and the rest of the hub's codes), with the red edge when it is a refusal.
- **@-mentions.** An `@` at the start of a word, anywhere in the line, opens the helper's list of **people to mention**: "Here now" (the people of the Players list, with where they are) and "Wrote lately" (people who wrote in the chat and are not here, newest first, "Wrote 20 min ago · not here"); the letters after the `@` narrow it (names with a space work), the first row is chosen and shows what it writes, Tab or Enter puts the exact name and a space in place of the `@` and the letters (Enter does not send while the list is open), Escape closes it, and an `@` inside a word, a name that matches nobody and a line that starts with a slash open nothing. Only the hub says who was mentioned: it marks the names in the line it sends, and every screen draws them the same way, each as a quiet green pill; the **visitor's own** is a gold pill, and the line that names the visitor has a soft gold edge. A person who was named gets an alert ([the bell](HOME_SCREEN.md#alerts)); there is no `@here` and no `@everyone`.

## Emoji and voice

Two small buttons stand at the right of the chat's field, before Send: an emoji button and, where the browser can listen, a microphone button. `talk.js` and `talk.css` load after the chat dock, once, when the dock is mounted (`home.js` asks for them with the part loader; a page whose dock never comes has neither button), and mount into the dock's own form. They are hidden with the field when nobody may write, and when the field is off for a ban (a ban that the page learns from the hub's refusal of a line too: the chat tells them when it paints the field off). The only thing kept in the browser is the list of recently used emoji (below), and neither button sends anything: the person presses Send. In every window they are the two small buttons at the right of the field inside the chat's box ([the composer](#the-chat-dock)): in one row beside the field while the words fit on one line, and in the row under the field, at the right, with Send, once the box has two rows. While the box has two rows and the dock is open in a window wider than 700 px and at least 501 px high, the dock is 240 to 300 px high (`:has` on the class `gt-tall`), so that the log keeps its room beside the row of buttons; with one row the dock keeps its own height, and a folded dock is as low as it was.

- **Emoji.** The button opens a picker with every emoji that the hub keeps as it is: the single code points of Unicode 9 or older that draw as a picture by themselves (`Emoji_Presentation`: 879), written with the page's own code (no library, no address). The hub's text rules ([`HUB.md`](HUB.md#text-rules)) decide what the picker may send: a joiner is removed by the hub (a family would come out as three people), so there are no joined sequences, no skin tones (a lone swatch is a colored square) and no flags of countries (they need two code points); the tag characters of a flag of a country's part are removed too; a variation selector is kept but is a second code point, so the older symbols that need one to draw as a picture (the red heart among them; the other hearts are offered) are left out. A line is 1,000 code points (and 4,000 bytes), while the field's own `maxlength` counts UTF-16 units (2,000): an emoji outside the basic plane is two units and one code point, so the picker counts code points, adds nothing past 1,000 ("The message is full.") and 1,000 of them are exactly the field's 2,000 units.
  - **The panel.** Nine tabs (the recently used emoji, then Smileys, People, Animals and nature, Food and drink, Activities and games, Travel and places, Objects, Symbols; each a line picture, 44 px high), a search field (16 px, so that an iPhone does not zoom into it), the emoji of one group at a time in a grid that makes as many columns as fit (44 px cells; 46 on a sheet), the recently used row over the group, and under it a line with the large emoji, its name and its search words while the pointer or the focus is on one ("Pick an emoji" otherwise). Every emoji button carries its name as its label. A pick goes in at the caret of the field or in place of the selection and the picker stays open, so several can be added; a field that has no focus (a sheet took the keyboard away, or the search field has it) keeps the caret it had when it lost the focus, because a press on an emoji moves the browser's own caret to the start. The button, Escape (which does not fold the chat), a press outside or a send closes it, and Escape puts the focus back in the field. Each name is the Unicode name made readable, and each emoji has up to three English search words that its name does not carry (`lol`, `bunny`, `poop`), kept in the table at the top of `talk.js`: an entry is its name, then `~` and its words, and a `@` entry sets the code point of the one after it.
  - **The search.** Every word of the query must answer an emoji: a word of its name is the word or starts with it, or one of its search words is the word (or starts with it, from four letters: "cat" is not a catch); a plural counts as its singular. A whole word of the name comes first, then a search word, then the start of a word, the shorter name first (so "cat" is the cat, then the cats' faces). At most 160 results are drawn; the line over them says how many there are (or that none were found). A tab pressed while searching ends the search.
  - **Recently used.** The last 16 picks, newest first, are kept in this browser only (`ants.emoji.recent` in `localStorage`: the code points in hex, separated by blanks; every read and write is guarded, so a browser that refuses keeps them for the visit). What is read back is only what the picker's own table has, each once, so nothing else in that text is ever used. The row over a group is one row of the grid (the page says how many columns there are; 8 where it cannot), the first tab shows all 16, and the row is made anew each time the picker opens (an emoji does not slide away under a finger). The picker opens on the group that was shown last, with nothing searched.
  - **Where it stands.** The place is chosen when the picker opens, from the size of the window, and kept while it is open (a keyboard that takes the window's height must not turn a sheet into a box) until the width changes. In a wide window (over 700 px wide, over 500 px high) it is a box 380 px wide and up to 400 px high (120 at least) over the log, level with the right end of the form, so it never covers the field or Send (it can reach over the bottom of the Games panel until it is closed). In a low window (500 px high or less: a phone held sideways) it is a row as wide as the form, from 6 px under the top of the window to 12 px above the form, with the tabs and the search field in one row; where the room is under 220 px the lines over the groups and the name line are left out. On a phone held upright (700 px wide or less, over 500 px high) it is a sheet at the bottom of the screen, 52 percent of the window's height, in the place of the keyboard: the strip of views is hidden, the page keeps the room above the sheet (`html.gt-sheet-open` and `--gt-sheet`: the log gets shorter and stays at its end, the field and Send stay in sight), a press on the emoji button takes the keyboard away, and a tap on the field closes the sheet. Where the browser reports its keyboard (`visualViewport`) the sheet stands above it and is lower (55 percent of what is left), and follows it. A box in the form's grid is told where its edge is (`align-self`, `justify-self`), as Chrome aligns it by the grid's own alignment otherwise.
  - **The keyboard.** Opened by the keyboard (Enter or Space on the button) the focus goes to the search field and the down arrow goes into the emoji; opened by a press, the focus stays where it is (a box: the keyboard stays up) or the keyboard goes away (a sheet). The tabs are a tab list (left and right, Home and End; a tab shows its group at once). One emoji is in the tab order; left and right move one by one over the recently used row and the group, up and down move by row (by the places on the page, so over the two grids), Home and End go to the ends, Page Up and Page Down move three rows, and up from the top row goes back to the search field. Enter in the search field puts the first result in (and never sends the line: it is the search's key); the other keys of the field are the field's own.
- **Voice.** The microphone button uses the browser's own speech recognition (`SpeechRecognition`, or `webkitSpeechRecognition` in Chrome, Edge and Safari). It is not made at all where the browser has none (Firefox) or where the page is not on a secure address. A press asks the browser to listen in the page's language (`navigator.language`): the words go into the field at the caret as they are heard, the interim words replaced by the final ones, with the text around the caret kept, and the person presses Send. A second press stops it (the last words still come in), and so do Escape, typing in the field, a send, a page that is hidden or goes away, and a field that is switched off. The state is said in a polite status line, which is a small bubble over the last lines of the log (it takes no room, so nothing moves and the field stays free; it is still announced) that goes by itself after 6 seconds (in every window), and at once on the next action (typing, the emoji button, the microphone, Send), except the two lines that stay while the microphone asks and listens (the first press: "Your browser's speech service hears you. Allow the microphone if it asks.", then "Listening. Press the microphone again to stop. Then press Send.") and the button is a toggle (`aria-pressed`, a red look that does not move for people who ask for less motion). A refused microphone, no speech, no microphone, a service that cannot be reached or any other failure is a short plain sentence in the same status line (visible text, on a phone too), never a dialog: a refusal says "The microphone is blocked for this site. Allow it in your browser's site settings, then try again.", and the line is shown as soon as the browser reports the error. The front page is sent `Permissions-Policy: microphone=(self)` (every other page keeps `microphone=()`, see [`BUILD_AND_RUN.md`](BUILD_AND_RUN.md)), so the browser can ask; and if the page's own policy (`document.permissionsPolicy.allowsFeature('microphone')`, where the browser has it) says the microphone is not allowed, the line is "This page does not allow the microphone." and nothing is started. Words from the speech service are text from outside: they go into the field as a value, the markup characters that the hub refuses are left out, and the line is cut at 1,000 code points ("The message is full.").
- **Privacy.** The browser may send the audio to its maker's speech service (Chrome, Edge and Safari do); this page receives no audio, records nothing and uploads nothing (the script has no `MediaRecorder` and no `getUserMedia`: the browser asks for the microphone itself), and the site keeps nothing of it. The button's tooltip and label say so ("Voice to text: your browser's speech service hears you"), and so does `web/legal.html`.

The Room's chat box (`web/front/lobby_chat.js`) uses the same composer and the same two files: its form, field and Send carry the dock's classes (`gc-form`, `gc-input`, `gc-send`), and it asks `talk.js` to mount into them with no emoji button (the room's line is printable ASCII) and a limit of 100 characters; it asks for the files itself, once, after the first paint, and the dock does not ask again when they are there.

## The login

The login is the hub's ([`HUB_LOGIN.md`](HUB_LOGIN.md#login): Discord, the one scope `identify`); the page only shows it, and never sees a token or a cookie (the cookie is `HttpOnly`). It is there only where the welcome says `login:true`, so a hub without a Discord application shows none of it.

- **Logged out** (`login:true`, no `you`): the chip in the bar (the mark of Discord and "Log in"; the mark alone on a phone, held sideways or in a window under 1280 px wide, 46 px) and the dock's button (the field is off, "Log in with Discord to chat"). Either one is a full navigation to `/hub/login?next=home` (`next=room` from the Room): the tab goes to Discord and comes back to the same screen, as drawn. The two ways back are `/` and `/#room`, chosen by the hub from that one word.
- **Logged in** (`you`): the chip is a circle with the first letter and the name (on a phone held upright, up to 720 px wide, the name stands beside the circle in the bar's first row: it takes the room that is left of Games and the Menu and is cut with an ellipsis only where it does not fit (never for a name of ten letters from 360 px), and Games, the chip and the Menu stay one row; the circle alone in a window under 1280 px wide and on a phone held sideways), and the chip is a button with a dropdown (`aria-haspopup`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`): a press opens `#gchat-login-slot`, a box over the page under the chip, to its right edge, no taller than what is left of the window (it scrolls inside itself, so Log out is reachable on a phone held sideways), and puts the focus on its first control that can be used (below, in the picture and its switch); a second press, a press anywhere else, Escape (wherever the focus is; the focus goes back to the chip) and the Room close it, and when it goes under the focus (Log out, Forget me, a name that is picked) the focus goes to the chip. In it: "Chatting as <name>", the choice of the name (below), for an administrator or the owner only (the hub's `role` is `admin`) the link **Admin page** to `/admin` ([`MAP_SUBMISSIONS.md`](MAP_SUBMISSIONS.md#the-admin-page)), **Log out**, and **Forget me** (a quiet underlined control, not a button, with a sentence of what it does: the first press only asks, "Are you sure? Both are a `fetch` POST of this page to the hub (`/hub/logout`, `/hub/forget`: same origin, with its cookie, no redirect, one at a time); after either the dock drops its socket and connects again at once, as a reader. A POST that does not work says "That did not work, try again" and changes nothing. The name is text (cleaned and cut like a line's), and nothing of this is kept in the browser.
- **The two names** (an account that has a Discord display name as well as its username; [`HUB.md`](HUB.md#names)). The chat shows the server nickname where the person has one in the project's Discord server, else the display name, unless the person picks the username (a new nickname then changes only the other name, and reaches an open page as a live `you` frame). The chip's dropdown then has, above Log out, a sentence that says which one the chat shows ("Chat shows your display name.") and one button that offers the other, with its name ("Show my username: maple.leaf"). It appears only where the hub's welcome says that the account can choose (`pick` is one of the two words and `other` is a name), so a hub that says nothing of it, an account with no display name, a reader and a hub with no login have no such button. A press sends the one message `{"t":"pick","pick":...}` over the socket that is open (nothing else: no request, no address) and changes nothing on the screen by itself: the hub stores the choice and closes the sockets of the account, the dock says "Chat is offline" for a moment and connects again by its usual wait, and the new welcome names the account by the other name; the dropdown stays open, now offering the first. Lines that were written keep the name they were written with, and are still the visitor's own, by the key. The words are in `TEXT` (`shows`, `names`, `use`).
- **The picture and its switch.** Where the hub's `you` says `pics` is `on` and carries `pic` (the hub sends `pic` only then, and the page shows a picture only then, a second guard), the chip's circle (28 px) shows that picture over the letter, and the Players list shows the same for each row that carries `pic` (32 px, the visitor's own card too): an `img` made with `createElement` and attributes, `alt=""`, its address `/hub/pic/<name>.png` made only from the hub's name after the page has checked that it is exactly 32 lower-case hex characters (nothing else in a frame ever becomes an address; chat lines have no picture). It sits over the circle inside the ring (position absolute, round, `object-fit: cover`), so nothing moves when it loads, and the staff ring stays red or yellow around it. The letter is hidden only once the picture has loaded; a picture that fails to load is removed and the circle is exactly as it is without one (a name that failed is kept in one small table of the page, the last 200 names, and is not asked for again by the chip or by any row of the list, however often the list is made again, for about ten minutes (one constant); after that the name is asked for once more, only when a circle is next drawn for it, so that a passing failure does not cost the picture for the life of an open page, and no timer asks by itself; a new name is asked for as before). A `you` frame replaces the whole `you` and can come at any time, unasked and repeated (the picture arriving a second after the welcome, the switch, an operator, the end of a picture's time, a role change); one without `pic` takes the picture away at once, and a row that loses `pic` is drawn again with its letter. When the dropdown opens the focus goes to its first control that can be used: the name choice where there is one, else the switch's box, else Log out (so that a Tab forward from the opening focus meets the switch and then Log out); after a wait that ends in `blocked` the box is disabled and the focus goes to Log out. The dropdown has, between the name choice and Log out, a checkbox **Show my Discord picture** with the line "Everyone on the front page sees it next to your name." under it (the line is tied to the box with `aria-describedby`; the row is 46 px tall like the other controls). It is there only where `you.pics` is `on`, `off` or `blocked` (a guest and a hub without the Discord login get none). States: `on` with a picture (checked); `on` with none yet (checked, "Your picture is loaded when you log in." and a **Log in again** button, which is the login navigation of the page); `off` (unchecked); `blocked` (unchecked and disabled, "Your picture is hidden on this site."). A press sends `{"t":"pics","on":true|false}` and the box waits until a `you` whose `pics` is what was asked for comes (it is not `disabled` while it waits: it says `aria-disabled` and turns presses away, so that the keyboard focus stays on it; only an `err` with code `pics` or `login`, 8 seconds of silence or that `you` end the wait, an `err` for a chat line does not) (the hub answers a request for the state the account is already in too; a `you` that says `blocked` also ends the wait). The answer to a done switch is a `you` with the new `pics` and no `pic`: switching on shows nothing until the next login, except that a `you` with `pic` may follow by itself if that login's picture was still being fetched, and the page takes it. `err` with code `pics` (not done: blocked, too often, a busy database) or 8 seconds without an answer puts the box back to what the hub last said, with "That did not work, try again"; a `you` that follows a refusal wins over it. The page never sends the request again by itself. `err` with code `login` says that the socket is a guest: the switch goes. After the last logout, a kick, a ban from the chat, Forget me or a name switch the hub closes the socket and sends no `you`: the page connects again and reads the welcome, so the chip and the Players list drop the picture on that path too. The hub's side is in [`HUB_PICTURES.md`](HUB_PICTURES.md).
- **The round trip and a room.** The tab leaves for Discord and comes back: the session storage that holds the seat of the room (`ants.lobby`) belongs to the tab and survives it, so the page opens, takes the seat back in the background as after any reload, and shows Home (or the Room, for `next=room`; a link `?room=CODE` also works). ## Your name

The sheet "Your name", the guest's chip and the welcome card ([`web/home/names.js`](../web/home/names.js), `names.css`, loaded after the dock like the other parts; the rules, what the hub keeps and the frames are in [`HUB_NICKNAMES.md`](HUB_NICKNAMES.md)). The page says only what the hub said: every sentence under a field is the page's own words for a word of the hub, and a name or a tag is always text (`AntsNameLetters.paint` draws a tag and "(Guest)" dimmer than the name).

- **Logged in.** The chip's dropdown has a line that says how the others see the person ("You show as maple.leaf, your Discord name." until a nickname is chosen, then "Nickname Zoe", and "Tag ANT" or "No tag") and one button ("Choose a nickname", then "Nickname and tag"). The button closes the menu and taps the dock (`names`); the sheet opens with the account's nickname and tag in two fields, each with its placeholder ("Pick a nickname", "ABC"), its counter and a line of help under it (the letters that a nickname may hold; what a tag is) until the hub has something to say about it, a preview ("How others see you": `[TAG] Nickname`, with the letter of the name in the circle) and the rules. A moment (350 ms) after the last key the page sends one `name` frame for both fields; the answer is said under each field (the name is free, taken, a lookalike without saying whose, too short or long, letters of another script, two alphabets, reserved, rude, a staff badge, "you can change it again on 4 Dec"), and Save is on only when the hub said that both are fine and that something changes. Save sends the same frame with `save: true`; the hub's `saved` closes the sheet, and the `you` frame that follows changes the chip, the chat's lines to come, the players list and the Games list. A person who logs out while the sheet is open loses it.
- **Guest.** Where the hub has a login and the visitor is not logged in, a chip with a dashed ring (the first letter of the name and the name as the others see it) stands before the Log in button; under 375 px it takes the place of the Discord mark. The chip opens the same sheet with one field (no tag; the placeholder "Pick a name" and the line of letters as above): the name is checked by the hub's `name` frame with `guest: true` (nothing is kept there) and kept by this browser (`ants.guestname`, front/player_name.js) with the sheet's Save. A guest's name is shown as "Name (Guest)".
- **The welcome card.** On a guest's first visit to Home with no name set, a card opens over the dimmed page: "Welcome to Ants & Uncles", "Chatting needs a Discord sign-in. You can play and watch without one.", and three answers. **Sign in with Discord to chat** presses the page's own Log in button. **Set a display name** ("if you don't want to chat") closes the card and opens the guest's sheet with the field in focus. **Not now** and Escape close it. The card is a dialog (named by its title, described by its line): the focus starts on the card itself, Tab stays inside it, the page behind it is `inert`, and the focus goes back to the chip when it closes. It shows only where Home is the screen and nothing else holds the page (the Room, a dialog and the hand-off to a game do), and only where the hub has a login. It never comes back in this browser (`localStorage` key `ants.guestwelcome`) once a name is set, the guest sheet was opened (from the card or the chip), Not now or Escape was pressed, or the person logged in; Sign in with Discord to chat answers nothing by itself, so a guest who comes back from Discord without logging in sees it once more. A browser that keeps nothing shows it with each new page, never twice in one.
- **The chat's lines** carry the tag that the author had when the line was written, drawn before the name in its own dimmer node; a presence row carries the author's current tag.
- **No nickname is made up here.** A person who has chosen none shows as the login says (the display name, then the username), as before.
