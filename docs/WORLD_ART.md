# World art

The new pictures of what lies on the map: the ground, the edges between two kinds of ground, the bridge the Swimmer digs, the fire wall, the bombs and the explosion, and the grass and the plants. A later batch adds the four hills. They are this project's own drawings, made by [`tools/world_art/`](../tools/world_art/README.md) (the drawings, the rebuild command, the limits of the pictures), and the original's sprites stay: the same setting that chooses the [HUD art](VIEW_AND_HUD.md#hud-art) chooses these, New (the default) or Classic, which draws the original's sprites exactly as before. Look only: nothing here reaches the simulation, the rules, a replay or a state hash.

## What it does

- **A picture replaces a sprite by its number.** Wherever the game would draw sprite N of the original (in a tile's animation, in a bridge's) it draws the pack's picture of N when there is one, at the place the animation gives and in the same size, so every animation, its frame times and its footprint are the original's. A picture that is not the original sprite's size times the scale is not used: that sprite is drawn as the original has it (`Renderer::draw_world_art_part`).
- **Which sheet.** Like the HUD art, the pack has one PNG sheet for each of the scales 1x, 2x and 3x, and it has them for each of its **pages**: page 0 holds the ground, the bridge and the effects, page 1 the grass and the plants (a sheet may not be above 4096 pixels, so what a batch adds has a page of its own, and a page is a texture only when something of it is drawn: a view without a plant never makes the plants' textures). The picture of the world at the zoom 1 is drawn straight into the output, from the sheet that the output's scale needs (at most 1 pixel for each canvas pixel: 1x, up to 2: 2x, more: 3x: [`world_art.hpp`](../include/ants_app/world_art.hpp), the same rule as the HUD's). A zoomed pass (the mouse wheel) draws the world at its own pixels and enlarges it afterwards, so it uses the 1x sheet. A sheet that cannot be read, or that the device cannot make a texture of, gives the next smaller one; none gives the original's sprites (said once on stderr).
- **The setting.** **Art** in the [settings menu](GAME_MENU.md) (the key is still `hud_art`, and `--hud-art`), `hud_art=new|classic` and `--hud-art new|classic`: New draws the world art as well. `assets/world_art/` is read at start (a folder that is missing leaves the original's sprites, with a line on stderr). The picture of a whole map (the preview of the setup screen, the thumbnails of the game server) is always the original's look.
- **Sixteen pictures for a plain tile.** The plain gravel, dirt and mud tiles and the bubbles of the mud have sixteen pictures each, so that a field does not repeat. Which one a cell shows is a function of the map as loaded and the cell's own place, in whole-number arithmetic (`world_art::ground_variants`), so every game draws the same: calm pictures (0 to 5) in the open, damp (9 to 11) or stony (6 to 8) beside another kind of ground, worn (12, 13) and cracked (14, 15) in a corridor or a pocket two cells across, stony round the objects, and broad patches of stony, pale and cracked ground. Nothing reads it but the picture.
- **Edges.** Every edge tile of the original's ground has a picture (249 sprites: water and mud both ways, gravel and mud, gravel and dirt, dirt and mud, and slate against the others), drawn so that each piece meets its neighbors in every combination that the maps use.

## The files

`assets/world_art/` holds `world_art.txt` (the manifest) and the sheets of its pages: `sheet_1x.png`, `sheet_2x.png`, `sheet_3x.png` (page 0) and `plants_1x.png`, `plants_2x.png`, `plants_3x.png` (page 1). The format is at the top of [`include/ants_app/world_art.hpp`](../include/ants_app/world_art.hpp): the sheets' sizes, and for each picture the original sprite's number, its variant, its page, the original sprite's size and its place in each of its page's sheets; every picture has a ring of one pixel round it that repeats its edge, so that a filtered copy (a window that is not a whole multiple of the canvas) never takes a pixel of its neighbor. The web build packs the folder into its data package with `assets/hud_art/` (`src/ants_app/CMakeLists.txt`, the `Dockerfile`).

## Dense mud

A big mud field looks different in its deep middle ([`world_art_mud.hpp`](../include/ants_app/world_art_mud.hpp)). Depth is the distance in tiles from a point to the nearest cell that is not plain mud (an edge tile, another kind of ground; the map's border and the objects that stand on mud are no bank), and four looks grow with it, each exactly nothing until its first depth, so a bank and a small field keep the approved pictures (a strip of mud two cells wide has no look at all; ISLANDS, where no mud is deeper than a tile, is the same picture with and without; TREASURE's crossing, at most 3 tiles deep, changes only in its deepest part):

| Look | From depth | Full at | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wetter, deeper middle | 1.8 | 5 | the mud's colors up to 20 to 23% darker (a little less on red than on blue), varying 10% across the middle; from depth 3 a dull sheen of broad light bands |
| Ripples and swirls | 1.8 | 5 | soft ridges about 6 pixels across (lit flank upper left, shaded flank lower right, 4.6 levels at most), one about every 30 pixels, fading in and out along their length, closing into swirls; on the plain mud's pixels only, never on a bubble |
| Bigger bubbles | 2.2 | 5 | the cell's own bubble grows to a medium (1.6x or 1.07x) or a large (2.2x or 1.15x) one, more large ones deeper; 45% of the deepest plain cells bubble by themselves on the original's own time (1,600 ms: 1,200 idle, then five frames) from a start of their own; the original's biggest bursts stay |
| Broad color patches | 1.5 | 5 | brown and gray patches about ten tiles across, a few levels each |

Every look is a function of the world pixel and the depth: value noise on a hashed lattice, each octave on a turned lattice, so it crosses a tile's border without a seam, repeats nowhere and is the same on every machine (the depth lines wander by 0.45 tile at most, so that no line of equal depth follows the cell grid). The depth is made once for a map (the nearest bank, on a grid of 4 pixels). The looks of a cell are made when it comes into view (about 3 ms, kept in 6.3 KB: the smooth looks sampled once for two world pixels, the ripples twice for each, read back at the scale of the output), within 8 ms of work in a frame (a set of looks or a baked picture is one piece of work, and every frame may do one), and a cell that is not ready is drawn as the world art has it; the picture of the cell as it is now (a frame of a bubble) is baked from the looks and the mud's picture and kept. **The memory is bounded:** the baked pictures and the looks together never take more than 16 MB (8 MB in the browser, where a phone runs it); what was used least recently goes first, never what the frame in hand uses, and a cell that does not fit shows the approved picture (no room at all is the approved picture exactly); the mud is baked at 2x at most (1x in the browser) and the graphics card enlarges it at a larger output, and the mud's own pictures, cut from the sheet, are 7.7 MB at 2x and 1.9 MB at 1x. A full view of deep mud at 2x needs about 7 MB of the 16. The pictures of the bigger bubbles are 320 more pictures of the pack (the numbers 10641 to 10650, medium, and 20641 to 20650, large, each with sixteen variants), drawn by `tools/world_art/big_bubbles.py` in the approved bubble's own drawing. The approved generator's math is `tools/world_art/mud_fields.py`, and `tests/data/world_art_mud_golden.txt` holds values of it that the C++ is compared with. One difference from the approved pictures, on purpose: the lines of the ripples are placed from a gradient taken on the cell's own samples with the median length that the reference measured, not from the picture's supersampled array.

## Transitions

The original has edge tiles for most pairs of ground (water and mud, gravel and mud, gravel and dirt, dirt and mud, slate against the others), but a map maker can put any two plain tiles side by side, and then they meet in a hard line: in the community maps that is about 74,000 joins, 30,000 of them water against gravel. With New art every such join gets a transition ([`world_art_edges.hpp`](../include/ants_app/world_art_edges.hpp)); Classic keeps the hard line. It is drawing only: a water cell stays water, so movement and the simulation are the original's, and the transition is ground, drawn in the ground's own pass under every object, ant and Fog of War's stipple.

- **The rule.** The kinds are ranked water < mud < gravel < dirt < slate. At a join the higher kind reaches a few pixels into the lower kind's cell (there is no transition on the higher cell). Against water it is a **bank**: steps of the land's own color, darker and wetter outward, a lit lip where the bank faces the light in the upper left, a shaded face on the other side, and the bank's shadow on the water. Between mud, gravel and dirt it is **soft contour steps** in the original's own band colors for the pair; **slate** against anything is **rock** (lumps with the original's grey rim and dark outline).
- **Natural shapes.** The line is not a fixed tile: it is made from the Gaussian-blurred shape of the cells round it (the eight neighbors), so corners are rounded unevenly and a one-cell channel, a pond or a diagonal touch all work, and its width changes with the place in the world (bumps, wide and narrow stretches, a spit here and an inlet there; noise that depends on the place only, so neighboring cells agree). Nothing is drawn at the map's border, and an edge tile of the original is not a kind: a plain tile beside one meets it as before.
- **Thin on grids.** A cell that is boxed in (three or four of its sides are joins) or on a checkerboard's rim gets a short edge, 2 to 3 pixels, straight along each joined side and nothing across a diagonal, so boards stay crisp grids of whole cells.
- **Memory and work.** The transition of a cell is a picture made when the cell comes into view (about 4 ms) and kept: 4, 16 or 37 KB at 1x, 2x or 3x, only for cells that have a join. At most 8 MB are kept (4 MB in the browser, where they are made at 2x at most): the least recently used go first, never what the frame in hand uses, and a cell that does not fit shows the ground alone (no room at all is exactly the ground). The work of a frame is the dense mud's (8 ms for both together, one piece of work every frame): a cell that is not ready shows the ground alone.

## Fire, bombs and the explosion

- **The fire wall is light.** The approved fire (the original's shape and five frames, no outline, see-through by heat: the white-yellow core nearly opaque, the red rim and tips mostly transparent) is drawn twice, once blended at 0.85 of its own opacity and once added to what is under it at 0.25, over a warm orange glow on the ground (an ellipse of 64 x 40 world pixels round (16, 25) of the cell that falls smoothly to nothing, at most 64 of 255 added). The original's wall is drawn in the cell pass, under every ant; the light wall lies in the **y-sorted queue at the bottom of its cell**, so an ant in the cell or north of it is seen through the flames and warmed by the glow, and an ant south of it covers them. Fog of War's gate is the original's: a wall in an unexplored cell is not drawn. The Fire ant's own animations show the original's sprites 316 to 319, which stay the original's: the wall's pictures are the numbers 50316 to 50320.
- **The explosion is light.** The bomb's explosion (the original's ten frames, sizes, offsets and times) is drawn smooth, with no hard step in it: the fire is one continuous heat field from a dark red rim through orange and yellow to a white core, with a bloom round its hot parts, and the smoke is a volume of billowing puffs with a lit and a shaded side, lit warm by the fire near it. It is drawn through the effect queue as: a warm glow on the ground (the fire's light blurred by 7 world pixels, times 2.2, at most 0.34 added; made once from the 1x picture and enlarged), the whole picture by its own opacity (a see-through smoke and a fire whose opacity goes by heat), and the fire's light (the numbers 30306 to 30315) added once more at 0.25. The last four frames are smoke and have no light.
- **The bombs.** The four teams' bombs, the approved design (seen from above like the original's, a cylinder head, a team-colored plate), are new pictures of the original's sprites 298 to 305, in the original's place and size, drawn in the cell pass as before. Fog of War hides them as before.
- With Classic every one of them is the original's.

## Grass and plants

The grass and the plants of the maps are 46 new pictures on page 1: the nine grass clumps (sprites 2779 to 2787), the clovers (573 to 577), the daisy, the lilies and the googly flower (579 to 586, with the foot 580 that they share), the animated flower's pieces (590 to 601: its seven frames of 100 ms are made of them, as the original's are), the leaves, the sticks and the mushroom (479 to 486), the plant (578) and the ivy (587 to 589). They replace the original's sprites by number, like every other picture of the pack (`Renderer::draw_world_art_part`): the same size, the same place, the same frames and times, so a plant stands where it stood and the stems of a flower stay behind its head. A plant is drawn by whatever draws it (the map's object list and the second layer) and a picture's clear pixels show the ground and what lies under the plant.

The grass is drawn again blade by blade, the flowers and the clovers painted in the same matte look with a crease and fine veins in the petals, the leaves, the ivy, the plant, the sticks and the mushroom rendered in 3D at the game's camera; the 1x of the clovers and the flowers is the original's own pixels, the 1x of the grass and the rest is new. The pictures were made outside this repository (by hand and in Blender) and are packed as they came by [`tools/world_art/pack_pictures.py`](../tools/world_art/README.md): the sheets are their only copy. A map that has no plant in view never makes the page's textures; any other makes the one of the output's scale, and a device that cannot take the 3x sheet (3072 pixels wide) gets the page's 2x one, a cut 2x sheet the 1x one, each page falling back on its own.

## Size

Batches 1 to 4: 1,066 pictures on two pages; the files are 9.7 MB in all (page 0: the 1x sheet 299 KB, 2x 1.14 MB, 3x 1.96 MB; page 1, the grass and the plants: 398 KB, 2.05 MB and 3.83 MB; the manifest 52 KB), which the web build's data package grows by. In memory a sheet is a texture of its own scale and page, made when its first picture is drawn: page 0 5.4 MB at 1x, 20.6 MB at 2x and 46.0 MB at 3x; page 1 2.8 MB, 10.8 MB and 24.2 MB, for a view with a plant in it (nearly every map). Only the scales that a window has used are made (a zoomed pass needs the 1x ones). No side of a sheet is above 4096. A map with a big mud field adds, while its deep cells are in view: at most 16 MB (8 MB in the browser) of baked pictures and looks, and the mud's own pictures cut from the sheet (7.7 MB at 2x, 1.9 MB at 1x; the mud is never baked at 3x); every other map adds nothing.

## What is not in it yet

The four hills. A zoomed pass uses the 1x pictures. The Swimmer's and the other ants' own sprites, and the original's objects (stones, food, power-ups) are the original's.
