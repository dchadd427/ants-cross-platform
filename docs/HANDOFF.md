## The review of `rollback` (tip 4fbd1f6), not yet in its notes

- No flaw in the confirmed match: the engine copy copies everything by value (ants cloned, the path manager rebound, no mutable statics, the PRNGs copied); orders match by sequence; host bots read the confirmed engine; the server is untouched.
- HIGH: the budget fallback times operations on the wall clock and gives up for the whole match: on a loaded machine (30 matches on 10 cores) every match gave up within 4 - 14 s (descheduling made single operations 5 - 66 ms). Fix: thread CPU time where available, ignore a strike inside a frame that was itself long, a cool-down with back-off instead of a permanent give-up.
- MEDIUM, bias 0: nothing breaks; visible jumps are equal or fewer than with bias 1 in every model measured; a guest's lag is always a two-valued mixture (the order's phase against the 50 ms seal), so "chases" happen on ordinary links, but in real runs at most 1 of 113 orders was chased. The docs and tests that pin bias 1 must change (prediction.hpp's config comment, README's row, NETWORK_PORT.md "Why one tick of bias" and its felt-delay table, which are bias-1 numbers; RP8.1, RP8.5, RP8.6, N3.31).
- DEFAULT OFF: with it off the screen and the commands are the confirmed engine's exactly, but a `Prediction` object, its runner hooks and the cue router still run: create the prediction lazily and run the router only while predicting or while it holds cues; `web/shell.html` must accept `?prediction=on`; the tests that assume it is on must switch it on themselves (N3.25 - N3.31, PA1 - PA6, N5.10, test_web_prediction.sh).
- LOW: a jitter buffer deeper than the lead makes the picture jump forward and then stand still (submit() advances to next_receive); a one-tick jump or hold of the whole picture at every lead change (35 + 35 in ten minutes with a mixed lag). INFO: a predicted cue and its confirmed copy more than 12 ticks apart would both play; the HUD's sim_query_ can dangle after the prediction is destroyed (no reader reaches it today).
- SMOOTHING (the next feature), presentation only: `Prediction::rebuild` already computes every ant's shown position before and after (its measurement block): make that an output (ant id, dx, dy); a presentation class in ants_app keeps an offset per ant that decays over ~150 - 250 ms (at least lead x 50 ms) and snaps above ~48 px; apply it in `Renderer::draw_single_ant`, the y-sort key and the HUD's picking, selection marks and minimap.

One pull request per batch (1 - 3 a day). For example: (1) bots + web-home + view-fixes part 1 (+ persist phase 1, server only) as v0.3.0; (2) rollback (off by default) and the zoom; (3) persist phase 2. ## How releases go (beyond AGENTS.md)

- A pull request per batch, title "vX.Y.Z - title", body: the changelog entry, what to try, the Claude Code line; merged with a merge commit after the five required checks pass; `main` is protected (admins too).
- Every merge to `main` redeploys beta at once (the owner's repository webhook, which he does not want changed) and ends running matches: right before merging, `curl -s https://beta.playants.org/busy` must show `"matches":0`.
- Release files: the `## Next` draft in CHANGELOG.md, then `tools/release.py X.Y.Z "title"`; STATUS.md with the Pacific time.
- A visual change gets mock-ups first; the owner approves them on his phone. The owner delegates decisions: decide with a recommendation; ask only about licences, history rewrites, real devices, credentials.
- Never on GitHub: the original program and its decompilation, community maps, anything read on the owner's servers (AGENTS.md rules 4 and 10).

## The owner's Treasure playbook (what the bots follow)

Treasure is the main map. Strong players take power-ups first (Fire, then Bomber, then Thief, Combat and Swimmer alike), steal other players' power-ups, and some open with a stolen Thief for two thefts at once; a theft is a 100-point swing (50 won, 50 lost by the leader). Every base has one of each power-up on its side: a stolen Fire burns your basin, a stolen Bomber bombs your base. Three fire walls in front of the thief hole stop a thief. Contested food first, by value (Treasure's centre first, then the side shared with an enemy; the side shared with the teammate last). Help a teammate who is attacked or lost a power-up. Guiding for eating (choosing by hand which ant deposits) and mud humping (cancelling a mud step by clicking the next tile) are expert skills. Hatching pays for a big fight; killing all of a team's ants forces it to hatch (200 points). Force a fight when losing. Hard bots should be really aggressive; bots should differ from each other.
