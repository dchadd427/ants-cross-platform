# The chat hub: roles, the moderators' log and the admin page's door

Part of [The chat hub](HUB.md). The commands that the roles allow are in [HUB_COMMANDS.md](HUB_COMMANDS.md); the data and the login are in [HUB_LOGIN.md](HUB_LOGIN.md).

## Roles

Who may moderate is decided by the roles that a person holds in the project's Discord server and by nothing that a page says. There are two, the administrator's and the moderator's: an account that holds either one may type the commands of [Moderation](HUB_COMMANDS.md#moderation), a person who holds both is an administrator, and the account in `ANTS_HUB_OWNER` is always one, with no call to Discord (it needs no answer about itself for an action; the person it acts on does: [Moderation](HUB_COMMANDS.md#moderation)). The welcome's `you` carries `role` (`none`, `mod`, `admin`; `mod` is true for both), and a live `{"t":"you","you":{...}}` follows when it changes (the lines that a moderator writes are marked `mod` and the list of players shows them first, for both roles).

**The lookup** (`hub/roles.py`, `hub/discord.py`). With the bot's token the hub asks `GET /api/v10/guilds/<server>/members/<account>` (the bot is a member of the server; the call needs no privileged intent and no gateway connection) and reads the `roles` of the answer. The answer must be a member: `roles` a list of at most 250 ids and, if there is a `user`, its `id` the one asked about; anything else is a failed check. A person who is not in the server (404 with Discord's code 10007) holds no role, and that is an answer. The token goes in one header, to `discord.com` only (https, the certificate checked, 3 seconds to connect and 6 for a call, 32 KB at most, no redirect followed), as a local of the call; an error carries a kind and a status and nothing else.

**When.** Before an action of a moderator (the fresh check of [Moderation](HUB_COMMANDS.md#moderation)) the roles of the two people are asked at once, before anything else. At the handshake of a login the person goes to the front of the queue and the welcome says the last answer that is known (`none` for a person who is new); the answer follows as a `you` frame. Then everybody who has a socket open is asked again every `ANTS_HUB_ROLE_RECHECK_SECONDS` (180, 60 to 3600; plus or minus a tenth, so that a crowd that came together is spread out), so a promotion or a removal arrives within about that long, with no new login. A person with no socket is not asked about. The queue: role holders who are more than 30 seconds overdue; sockets that were just let in (a page that reconnects while its checks fail keeps the delay of the retries); people who hold a role or lost one in the last hour; everybody else, who wait longer (up to about nine minutes) when the hub is busy, never the role holders. One request is made at a time, two a second (a burst of 5) and by Discord's rate-limit headers (an empty bucket waits for its reset; a 429 waits for its `retry_after`, at most a minute, for every lookup; a wait that cannot be read is the longest one): far below the 50 a second that Discord gives a bot, which the bot's other uses share.

**Fail closed.** A role is believed for the interval, a tenth more (the latest that a check can be due) and 60 seconds more (258 by default); after that the person is `none` until Discord answers, and the pages are told. A Discord that does not answer, is busy or sends what is not a member is a failed check: never a role and never "no role" (the person keeps what they had, until the grace runs out); it is tried again after 15, 30 and 60 seconds, then at the normal pace, with one `roles_down` line in the log (at most one in 10 minutes) and `roles_up` when it answers again. A person whose Discord account was deleted (404, code 10013) is not in any server: it holds no role, and that is an answer. A token, a server or an access that Discord refuses (401, 403, or 404 with the code for an unknown server) stops every call for 10 minutes, takes everybody's role away but the owner account's, and writes one `warning`; then one call tries again. Chat is never held up by any of it: the chat asks `role_of`, a dictionary look with no I/O, and the lookups run in a task of their own with a time limit on every request; if that task ever ends, the clock says so in the log (with the other `error` lines of the roles, at most one in 10 minutes) and starts it again, and the roles run out meanwhile.

**What is kept.** Memory only: one small entry for each person with a socket open, and for a few minutes after the last one closes (a page that reloads keeps its role), at most 2,000; nothing in the database. The token, the server's id and the roles' ids are read from the settings once, and a person's Discord id is like a password: it is used to log the person in and to ask Discord for the roles, and is in no log line, frame, error text, representation of the state or answer (an error of a call to Discord is a kind and a status and has no cause that could hold the request's address; the log replaces any number of 15 digits or more).

**Setting it up.** (1) In Discord's developer portal, on the Bot page of the application, copy the token (the bot must be a member of the server; the token may be the one that the bot's other uses have: **resetting it in the portal stops those uses**, so look for the stored one first); (2) in Discord with Developer Mode on, copy the server's id and the ids of the two roles; (3) put `ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN`, `ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_GUILD`, `ANTS_HUB_ROLE_ADMIN` and `ANTS_HUB_ROLE_MOD` in the stack's environment (the token **only** there, never in a file of this repository, a chat or a pull request) and, for the account that must keep its powers when Discord does not answer, `ANTS_HUB_OWNER`; (4) redeploy: the start line says `roles_configured=1`, and a person who holds a role gets a `you` frame with it within seconds of connecting (the welcome itself says `none` for a hub that was just started). If the first lookup is refused with 403 (`kind=access` in the log), turn on the Server Members intent for the bot in the developer portal and try again. The hand list `ANTS_HUB_MODS` is not read any more (a leftover value only logs one warning): the people who were on it have powers when they hold a role in Discord.

## The moderators' log

Every action that a moderator or an administrator does in the chat and that is **done** (a mute, an unmute, a deleted line that is not their own, a kick, a ban, an unban), and every one that `hub.admin` does (a mute, an unmute, a ban, an unban, a deleted line; the one who did it is `terminal`), leaves one row in the table `modlog` of `hub.db`, in the same step as the action (and a start or a stop that `/bots` did leaves one too, with its numbers: [HUB_COMMANDS.md](HUB_COMMANDS.md#the-log-of-bots)): when (`at`), who did it (`actor`), what (`action`), to which account (`target`, empty where there is none) and a detail (the minutes of a mute, the number of a line). A refused command leaves no row. Where the row has an account it has its **reference**, **never the Discord id**: the first 16 hex characters of HMAC-SHA256 of the id, keyed with a secret that belongs to the hub (32 random bytes, made by the store the first time that a database is opened and kept in `hub.db`, table `secrets`; it is never printed, logged or sent). `hub/pseudo.py` is the one function that makes a reference, so that the tables that still hold an id can be moved to it by a later change. The same account has the same reference for as long as the database is the same, so the log can say that two actions were done to one person, and it says nothing of the id to anybody who reads its rows without the secret (a copy of the whole of `hub.db` holds the secret and the ids both: the references protect the rows when they are shown or copied alone, not the file). The store refuses a row whose actor or target is not a reference (or `terminal`) and whose action or detail is not one of the plain forms, so that an id cannot get in by a mistake of a caller. The `by` that a ban, a mute and a deleted line keep, for an action in the chat, is the reference as well.

It is kept **30 days and at most 10,000 rows** (the newest): the rows past the cap go in the step that adds the next, and the hub's hourly purge deletes the rows past 30 days and past the cap, with the lines and the mutes that are over. It is in no frame and no chat page can ask for it. It is read from the terminal, `docker exec ants-hub python -m hub.admin log [--days N] [--lines N]`, which prints it newest first, the actor and the target as references and, where the account still has a profile, its username, and by [the admin page's door](#the-admin-pages-door), which gives the newest 500 rows of the 30 days the same way (an administrator's change through the door is a row whose actor is that administrator's reference). **Forget me does not remove it**: its rows hold no name and no id and are what makes an action reviewable; the profile goes, so the log names nobody afterwards, and the rows go with their 30 days. The Legal page ([web/legal.html](../web/legal.html)) says so.

## The admin page's door

The admin page (`/admin`) has one way in: the visitor's own hub login. The hub decides who may, in one function (`Door.decide` in `hub/admindoor.py`), and every request of the door goes through it. The account of the login may when it is `ANTS_HUB_OWNER` (no call to Discord) or when its role is `admin` on an answer of Discord that is **a minute old at the most** (the freshness that an action of [Moderation](HUB_COMMANDS.md#moderation) needs, not the longer belief of the chat's [Roles](#roles)); an answer that is older, or none, is asked for once, urgently, from the door's own small share of the lookups (the moderators' commands live on another share, which the door never spends; each account and the door as a whole are limited), and a lookup that does not answer means no. A role that was taken away therefore closes the gate and the lists within a minute; a pass that was made just before lives its own minute more. A moderator may not. Not logged in is a 401; logged in and not allowed is a 403; a door that is shut (no good key) is a 503 for everybody, which is not a "no". Nothing else counts: no header, no query, no body.

**The gate.** `GET /hub/admin/gate` is for the site's nginx alone (an `auth_request` before it passes a request of the admin page on; the site's nginx must keep it an internal location, pass the visitor's cookie and `X-Client-Address` (a gate asked without a usable one is a `400`, said once in the log, so that nobody shares one bucket with everybody), send no body and `Content-Length: 0` (`proxy_pass_request_body off; proxy_set_header Content-Length "";`), and never answer it to a browser). It takes a GET with no body and no query and answers with no body: `204` with the header `X-Ants-Admin-Pass: v1.<payload>.<mac>`, `401`, `403` or, when the door is shut, `503`; any other method is a 405, and nothing it sends is a CORS header. The payload is base64url, with no padding, of the JSON `{"exp","name","nonce","ref","role"}`: `exp` seconds since 1970, a minute after the pass was made; `name` the Discord username; `nonce` 16 hex characters, new for each pass; `ref` the account's reference ([The moderators' log](#the-moderators-log)); `role` `admin` or `owner`. The mac is the hex (64 characters) HMAC-SHA-256 of the ASCII text `v1.<payload>` under the pass key. **The pass key is the raw 32 bytes of the digest, not its hex text**: HMAC-SHA-256 with the secret (the ASCII bytes of the setting, stripped) as the KEY and the label `ants-admin-pass-v1` as the MESSAGE (the page's token has another key, made the same way with `ants-admin-csrf-v1`). A verifier that feeds the 64-character hex text as the key, or swaps key and message, gets every pass wrong. Known answer: for the made-up secret `canary-gate-key-5d8f0c1e7a9b4263-q` the pass key is, as hex of the 32 bytes, `13be5dab7dcbeb06e979f0fa5e5f817d178fa6b40da7c92bc02588e80ae3dd25` and the token key `03a2b6b94fabcf8ae018852e8e6c0b1ec691375286a0263168238796556e3018`. The secret is `ANTS_ADMIN_GATE_SECRET`, one setting that the stack hands to the hub and to the game server alike (the hub reads its old name, `ANTS_HUB_ADMIN_GATE_SECRET`, only when that one is not set; the release after this one drops it), cut of surrounding whitespace as the hub cuts it (what Python's `str.strip()` removes from both ends; the secret is printable ASCII, so in practice a space, a tab, CR or LF). A receiver compares the mac in constant time (never `==`), then checks `exp` against its own clock, then that `role` is `admin` or `owner` and no other word, and that the payload has exactly the five fields. **A pass is not single-use by itself**: whoever sees one may use it for its minute unless the receiver keeps the nonces of the last minute, which the game server does (a pass is used once there, and nginx asks the gate for every request), so it is for a request between the two services and never for a page. A role that is fresh costs the gate no call to Discord, and requests of one account that arrive together while its answer is being asked for wait for that one lookup and share its result (one call, one share of the budget); an account that was never seen with a role has a small share of lookups of its own, so that many plain accounts do not use up the share of the staff, and one line in the log (no account) says when a share is used up, and each address has a limit (a burst of 60, then one a second; `429` beyond).

**The likes' gate.** `GET /hub/likes/gate` (`hub/likedoor.py`) is the same kind of door for the site's nginx alone, for the likes of replays: a `204` and `X-Ants-Like-Pass: v1.<payload>.<mac>` for any logged-in account (payload `{"exp","nonce","ref"}`, keys of its own made from the same secret with the labels `ants-like-pass-v1` and `ants-like-csrf-v1`), `401` for nobody, `503` when the key is not good, `400` with no `X-Client-Address`, `429` beyond a burst of 60 and then one a second for each address; the shape of the pass, the keys and the known answers are in [REPLAY_COUNTS.md](REPLAY_COUNTS.md).

**The key.** 32 to 256 printable characters with no blank and at least 8 different ones (`openssl rand -hex 32` makes one). With none, or one that is too short or not of that shape, the hub still starts, says so once in its log (`admin_page_door_shut_...`, never the value), and the door is shut for everybody: the gate answers 503 and the API 503 `door_off` (and the page's token cannot be made); nginx answers `door_off` for the gate's 503, as for a hub that is not running, so that a shut door is never told as "not an admin". The made-up key of the known answer above is published, so it is refused too (the game server does the same), and a setting copied from this page leaves the door shut. A wrong value is never a reason for the hub not to start.

**The API.** `/hub/admin/api/*`, for the admin page, same origin only: `GET me` (the username, the role `admin` or `owner` and `csrf`, the token of the page's changes), `bans` and `mutes` (with the time each ends; each row says who did it: `by` is the reference of the account, or `command line`, and `by_name` its username, empty where the account has no profile), `hidden` (the accounts whose Discord picture is blocked), `modlog`, `status` (numbers, no lines: `store`, what `hub.admin status` prints as numbers; `health`, whether the roles are asked of Discord well, `roles` `ok`, `down`, `refused` or `off`, with the number of accounts known and, while refused, `kind` (`guild`, `token` or `access`), the HTTP `status` that Discord gave and `retry`, the seconds until the hub asks again; `last_picture`, the kind of the last fetch of a picture (`ok`, `none`, `down`, `type`, `big`, `shape`, `busy` or `bug`, the kinds of the log's `picture` line) and its age in seconds, null before the first; `version`, eight hex characters made from the hub's own files (`code`) and when it started (`started`), because the image has no version of its own: [the stack](HUB.md#the-stack-and-the-sites-nginx)) and `POST` of one JSON object to `ban`, `unban`, `mute` (`account`, `minutes` 1 to 10080), `unmute`, `nopic`, `pic` (`account`) and `del` (`line`). An answer is JSON; names in it are typed text, so each answer that has some says `"untrusted": true` and lists the fields in `untrusted_fields`, as the maps API does. A change needs the checks of the hub's other POSTs (`Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`, the site's `Origin`, a JSON type, a body of at most 512 bytes) and the token in `X-Ants-Csrf`, which is the hex HMAC-SHA-256, under the key, of the login's key and the account's reference: it is another one for every login and is never in a URL. An account is a username (`@name` for one of digits) or a reference, never an id. The site's nginx passes these calls through one door, `^~ /hub/admin/api/`, for a GET of one of the six reads and a POST of one of the seven changes only (a map of the method and the decoded path; every other name, method or path is a 404 there, and an empty query is dropped), when the visitor's request was made by the site's own page (`Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`), with a body of 512 bytes at the most, and passes on nothing of the request but the login's cookie, `Origin`, `Sec-Fetch-Site`, the type of the body, the page's token and the address; the calls spend the allowance of the admin API (one a second for an address, a burst of 50; the hub's bucket is 60 and one a second), and none is written to the access log (`tests/scripts/test_nginx_hub_admin_api.py` runs it, in front of a stand-in and in front of the real hub). An empty query (`?`), a key that comes twice in the body and a body that was promised and not yet sent by somebody who is not let in are all refused before anything is read (the connection's place is kept until the answer). The moderator's checks of [Moderation](HUB_COMMANDS.md#moderation) hold: the roles of the person who acts and of the person acted on must have been answered by Discord within a minute (else 503 `role`), the table of the powers holds (an administrator has none over an administrator), and the operator's account is above every other and has nobody above it. Every change that is done leaves its row in the moderators' log with the actor's reference (a ban also ends the account's sockets). An administrator may delete a line of the hub's own (a notice of a kick or a ban), as the terminal can and the chat's commands cannot; the row has no target.

No Discord id is in a pass, an answer, a log line or an error: the account is its reference, and a name is its username.
