# Heatmaps

The two pictures that the replay pass draws from a match: where its ants died and where its action was. Part of [Replays](REPLAYS.md).

## The map of deaths

**Where ants died** is a card of the match page (and a smaller one in the Stats panel of the replay page): the match's own map with a soft glow where many ants died, or a mark on the tile of each ant, for the match, or added up over every match on that map. Nothing in the game changes: the server only watches while it replays a finished match, the same pass that counts the stats.

- **What the pass reads.** `MatchStats::Kill` (`ants_replay/match_stats.hpp`) has `tile_x` and `tile_y` besides the turn, the team that lost the ant, its type, whether it drowned and the team of the last damage: the tile that the world snapshot showed the ant on when it was last seen (the collector already kept it for every ant), and `MatchStats::map_width` and `map_height` are the grid's size. No engine code is touched; `test_history_deaths` runs an independent observer on real recordings and finds the same ants, turns and tiles, and `test_history_details` HD1.1 holds that the hashes are the same with and without the collector.
- **The field** (`ants_server/history_deaths.hpp`: its header comment is the layout; `history_deaths.cpp`). A packed text of its own with a cap of its own, `kDeathsMaxBytes` (3,000 characters; the cap of `details`, `kDetailsMaxBytes`, is untouched, and the map is kept even when the details drop their list of kills). The JSON object `{"v": 1, "w", "h", "n", "k", "d", ...}`: base64url letters, **nine for each ant** (x and y of the tile, the second, the team, the type, drowned, and the team of the last damage, or none), so the busiest real match so far (34 deaths) is about 300 characters and one match keeps about 320 ants one by one (`"k": "x"`). A match with more deaths than that, which only a very big map allows, keeps **squares** (`"k": "b"`, `"s"` tiles a side: 2, 4, 8 ... the smallest that makes the text fit) with eight letters for each square, team and cause (drowned or not) and a count, and the first and the last kill as single ants: every death is still counted (`n`), the same text comes out every time, and the cap is never passed whatever the match (`test_history_deaths` DD2.x, with ants spread over every tile of a map of 2,048 a side). A match with no lost ant, one that the pass could not count and one on a map too large to name a tile in 12 bits have no field.
- **Kept where.** Table `history_deaths` (migration 7: one row for each match whose pass found positions, with the map's file name and hash, the size, the count and the text, `CHECK (length(deaths) <= 3000)`), written in the same transaction as the record by the feeder (`HistoryFeeder::work`; a maker that throws, a text that is not a field or is over its cap costs the match nothing but its map) and taken out with its match. `GET /history/<id>` gives it under `deaths` next to `details`; the list leaves it out. **No backfill:** a match counted before this version has no map of deaths, and the backfill of the details does not make one either; the page says "No map of deaths for this match." (one quiet line, and none when the match page already has the box "More stats" for an older version or a missing recording: one box, not two).
- **The map of all the matches.** `GET /history/deaths?map=NAME` (both doors; [`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays) has the limits) adds up the fields of the matches on one game map: those of the **newest file of that name** (its hash and size: another file under the same name is another map) and at most the newest 2,000 of them, as one field in squares (`"k": "b"`, `"s"` as small as lets it fit 24,000 bytes: 1 tile for a map of 60 with a few hundred squares) with `"map"` and `"matches"` besides. The name is looked up among the names that have a field (ASCII letters in any case; the stored spelling comes back) and is never used as a path or a pattern; the query is `map=` and nothing else; the answer repeats nothing that the request said. It is made once for each state of the history and cached by the server and, for 30 seconds, by the site's nginx, which also has a low allowance for it.
- **The card** (`web/death_map.js`, `web/death_map.css`; `AntsDeathMap`). **Whose ants**: All teams or one team's chip (its color and its shape: circle, square, triangle, diamond, as in the charts; the number is that team's lost ants). **Show as**: **Heat** (the glow: dark red where one ant died, orange, gold and cream where many did; one team's own color for one team) first, or **Dots** (one mark on the tile of each ant, `×2` when two died on one tile; the squares with their counts when the field is in squares, and for the sum of a map). A map over 256 tiles, and a match kept in squares, **start as Dots**, colored squares (a mark is never smaller than 4 pixels), because a glow on such a map says little; Heat is one tap away. The choice is kept on the device (`localStorage`, one key, never needed). **Mark on the map**: First blood and Last kill (rings with their time; the first and last ant that a team is to blame for). **Ants that drowned**: one switch that hides them (they show by default: the scoreboard counts them as kills when an enemy hit the ant last); hidden ants are neither drawn nor counted, in the title or in a chip. + and -, the mouse wheel, two fingers and a drag zoom and move the map (the stage lets the page scroll with one finger until it is zoomed); zoomed in far enough the grid of tiles shows and each ant sits on its own tile; a small map of the whole shows where you are. A tap or a hover on a mark says the tile, the time, the kind of ant and who killed it ("Drowned; last hit by Gory"), and how many more died on that tile; on a phone the tooltip opens under the map. The facts: the busiest place ("5 ants died within 2 tiles of tile 29, 37"), first blood and last kill with their tiles. **This match / All matches on this map** is a switch of the card (the sum is asked for once, when it is first wanted; a map with no sum says so in one line and the card stays as it was).
- **What the page trusts.** Nothing: a field with a letter outside the 64, a length that is not whole ants or squares, a type above 5, a count of 0 or a size or version it does not know makes no map (the quiet line), every position is put on the map (x at most `w - 1`), every number is checked, the lists are capped (400 ants, 4,000 squares), a name (a player's, a map's) is cleaned of control, zero-width and bidirectional characters, cut and put in as text, and no `innerHTML` exists in the file.
- **The replay page.** The Stats panel gets the same card, smaller (about 236 px wide; the chips, Heat / Dots, a small legend, the zoom buttons and the tooltip; no marks, no facts, no sum), under the table and above "Open the match page"; a match without a map of deaths gets nothing there. A card in a closed panel has no size, so `AntsHistory.replayStats` asks the card to be drawn when the panel is opened.
- **Tests.** `test_history_deaths` (the packing, the cap in the worst case, the collector against an independent observer, the store, the sum, the route), `test_db` SV1.7 (the migration), `tests/scripts/web_death_map_check.js` run by `test_web_death_map.py` (the field written by the C++ side, damaged fields, the glow, every control and the sum on a strict fake of the DOM, and again on broken copies), `test_nginx_history.py` (the location, its check, allowance and cache), and `tests/scripts/test_web_death_map.sh` (opt-in: the pages in a real browser at 1280 and 390 wide, with screenshots). The broken-copy lists for `tools/mutate.py` are `tests/data/death_map_mutants.json`.

## The activity curve

**Where the action is** is a soft curve just above the replay page's timeline: the higher it is at a moment, the more happened then. It is for finding the big fights; it counts deaths most of all, and bombs that went off, thefts (a Thief diving into a hill), fires lit and hits, and never food, eating or walking. Nothing in the game changes: the server only watches while it replays a finished match, the same pass that counts the stats, and the page only reads.

- **What the viewer sees** (`web/replay_activity.js`, `web/replay_activity.css`). On a computer the curve shows only while the pointer is over the timeline, with a line, a dot on the curve and a small label such as "2:41 · 3 deaths, 1 bomb" (the label says what the 5 seconds around the pointer hold, or "quiet" or "fighting"), and the REPLAY tag gives way while it does. On a touch screen, which has no hover, the curve is always there, faint, and solid while a finger is on the timeline. The part of the match already played is in color. On a phone the curve has a band of its own above the timeline. A recording's bar on a computer puts the timeline on a row of its own, as wide as the picture, with the buttons under it (beside the buttons it had little room). A live match, a file of your own and the frame of the match page have no record, so no curve and the bar as it was; so has a match with nothing to draw.
- **What is counted, and how much it weighs.** The weights are the Auto camera's own ("The camera"; `include/ants_app/auto_camera.hpp`): a death 14, a bomb that goes off 10, a theft 6, a fire 5, a hit (an ant that lost hit points) 2. The page makes the weights and the curve from the counts (so a weight can be tuned without counting a match again): one combined curve, blurred a little so that a peak is a hill, as high as the match's busiest moment.
- **The field `action`** (`ants_server/history_action.hpp`: its header comment is the layout; `history_action.cpp`). `MatchStats::action` (`ants_replay/match_stats.hpp`) counts, for each of the five kinds, the events in every 5 seconds of play (a death is a lost ant, a theft a raid begun, a bomb one that an ant walked onto, a fire one that the seat's `fires_lit` counted, a hit an ant that lost hit points in a tick); it reads what the collector already sees, so a match plays out in the same hashes with or without it (`test_history_deaths` DD7.1 plays real recordings with an independent observer). The stored field is `{"v": 1, "s", "n", "death", "bomb", "theft", "fire", "hit"}`: one base64url letter for each count in each bucket (A none, up to _ for 63 and more), so a match of 8 minutes is about 560 characters; `s` is the bucket (5 seconds, then 10, 20, 40 ... for a match of more than 20 minutes) and `n` the number of buckets, at most 240, so the text is at most `kActionMaxBytes` (1,500) whatever the match (`test_history_deaths` DD7.5).
- **Kept where.** Table `history_action` (migration 11; the history service's own file: its migration 5): one row for each match whose pass counted any of the five, `CHECK (length(action) <= 1500)`, written in the same transaction as the record by the feeder (`HistoryFeeder::work`; a maker that throws, or a text that is not a field or is over its cap, costs the match nothing but its curve) and taken out with its match. `GET /history/<id>` gives it under `action` next to `details` and `deaths`; the list leaves it out. The history service copies the table with the rest of the history at its first start. The `details` have a key `activity` of their own (the orders given): it is another thing. **No backfill:** a match counted before this version has no action. The page then draws the **deaths alone** from the record's `deaths` (one ant at a time: "The map of deaths"; a match that kept squares has no second for its ants, so none) and the label says "no deaths" for a quiet bucket; a match with neither gets no curve.
- **What the page trusts.** Nothing: a version, a bucket (a multiple of 5 seconds, at most 5 x 2^20) or a count of buckets out of range, a text that is not as long as the buckets, a letter outside the 64, or a kind that is not text makes no curve (the timeline stays as it was); no `innerHTML` exists in the file; the one request (`GET /history/<id>`) is the Stats panel's: `AntsHistory.replayStats` gives the record to `onRecord`, and a handler that throws costs the panel nothing.
- **Tests.** `test_history_deaths` DD7.1 - DD7.8 (the collector's buckets against an independent observer, the letters, the buckets that double, the cap in the worst case, the table, the record's JSON and the migration), `test_history_details` HD1.6 (a bomb that goes off in its bucket), `test_db` SV1.1 and SV1.8, `test_history_service` HV1.2 (the copy), and `tests/scripts/web_activity_check.js` run by `test_web_activity.py` (the field as the server writes it, damaged fields, the deaths alone, the weights, the curve and its label, the computer and the touch screen, the played part, the record handler, on a strict fake of the DOM, and on broken copies of the scripts). The C++ mutants for `tools/mutate.py` are `tests/data/replay_activity_mutants.json`.
