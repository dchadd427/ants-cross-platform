# The map maker: the pull requests

The list of the pull requests that made the map maker's page ([`MAP_EDITOR.md`](MAP_EDITOR.md)): what each one does and which are done. The rules of the editor and how the page is made stay on that page.

| | What | State |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | The page and its shell (top bar, tools column, canvas, side panel, dialogs, the three layouts: desktop, phone upright, phone sideways); New map (the two kinds, size boxes with the live limits and file size, the core's refusals); Open a map (either a `.lvl` file from the device or any existing game map from `GET /maps/all`, with preview thumbnails, `Original` and `Extended` badges, search and sort, opened under a free numbered copy name so it can be edited and saved right away; kind and check shown); the canvas with pan and zoom (mouse drag and wheel, one finger and two fingers, the keyboard), a small map of the whole map while only a part is on screen, and drawing of the visible tiles only (measured in headless Chromium on a development machine: about 15 ms for a wheel step and its frame over the whole 512 x 512 map); the checks card for each kind; Download. The page refuses a map core of another interface version. | done |
| E2 | The drawing tools: **Ground** and **Objects** (a palette for each: the plain kinds of ground, the objects that stand on one cell, which are small rocks, the five power-ups, the fire wall, the Bridge (the game's finished bridge, which is not solid) and the four bombs, and the 14 top-layer clovers and flowers that ants walk under, with an optional power-up drop interval; while the Bridge chip is chosen the line under the palette says in plain words, never as a refusal, how many pieces of the map stand on ground that is not water: "Bridge · 2 pieces are on dry ground. A bridge needs water under it." (the tools column scrolls just far enough to show all four lines of it), and a phone has no such line), **Erase** (an object goes whole: a bigger one, such as a hill that an opened map has, is never left in halves) and **Pan** (a drag looks around and changes nothing; it was called Move when it also dragged a placed item, which Pick up does now); a tap or a click paints, a drag paints a line of cells (mouse and one finger; two fingers still pan and zoom; the Pan tool, the middle button and the space key held down pan); a brush slider of 1 to 9 cells across; **Fill**; undo and redo kept as small patches (the cells that changed, never a copy of the map) with a bounded history; the keys (Control or Command with Z, with Shift or Y to redo, a letter for each tool); "Row r, column c" under the map while the mouse or a finger is on it; the check of the map as it is now (after a change the side line says "Checking" and is run again when the hand has rested for 0.6 seconds; the Check button runs it at once and shows the card); Download writes an edited map through the core. Hills, Ants and Food were dimmed until E3a. | done |
| E3a | **Hills, Ants, Food and Plants**: a tap puts a whole hill (4 x 4 cells with its door) for the team that is chosen (Green, Red, Blue or Black, the game's seats); a team has one hill, so putting it again moves it; the starting ants of a team (one record of block 1 for each ant) stand together where the tap is, 1 to 64 of them, and putting them again moves them; a food pile (a record of block 2) of 2 to 6 stages; a top-layer plant (chosen in Objects: a record of block 1 and a waypoint or dropper record of block 4, one of the 14 clovers and flowers that ants walk under, with an optional power-up drop interval). The eraser takes a hill, a team's ants, a pile and a plant away whole, and the Pick up tool (M1) takes a placed object, hill, starting ant, pile or plant up and puts it down in one step. They are bytes of the open file like the cells, spliced exactly, in the same history as the strokes, and the core checks the map after each ("Blue has no hill" is gone once the hill is there). The letters are H, A and F. | done |
| E3b | **Bigger objects**: the **Bigger** tool (letter K) puts one of 29 objects that cover several cells (grass, leaves, rocks, toys: the ones that most of the 540 library maps use, named as the game names them) whole with a tap, the eraser takes it away whole, and the check of the map says what it said before; a hill, a team's starting ants and a food pile are never taken away by it. Rocks and power-ups of the Objects tool now carry the solid bit that the game reads. The anchors of objects follow the rules of version 9 when a map grows. | done |
| E3c | **The properties of the map** (the **Properties** button of the top bar and **Properties...** in a phone's Menu, a short summary of them with an **Edit** link in the side panel and under the map of a phone): the description, the length of a match (1 to 120 minutes), the eggs that every team starts with (0 to 255) and the default ant type (none, combat, thief, bomber, swimmer, fire), set in one dialog with **Apply** (one step of the history) and **Cancel** (nothing changes). The core makes the new file (`lvl_set_properties`, interface version 3) and the page puts the few bytes that changed into the history. The mode and the rules word are not shown. | done |
| E4 | **Copy, convert and crop**: the **Copy...** button of the top bar (and in a phone's Menu) and the link under the meter of the side panel (**Start an Extended map from this one...** on a Classic map, **Make a Classic map from this one...** on an Extended one). Every way makes a **new** map under a free name (the name of the open map, of a library map and of a draft is taken), the open map is never written over, and the new map takes its place on the screen after the question about unsaved drawing. A copy of the same kind is the same bytes under the new name, except an Extended map that the rule below ("The rules word of a new Extended map") gives the word 2: the core's copy with that word (`convertToLongOrders`); the other kind goes through the core (`convertToExtended`, or `convertToLongOrders` up to 90 rows, `convertToClassic`) and says what the core says to tell; an Extended map that does not fit shows the converter's own reasons (rows first) with **Crop it to fit...** (when a size is a reason) and **Shorten the description...** (when the description is: it opens the Map properties box on the copy). **Crop to fit** is a screen of its own: a movable frame over a picture of the whole map, never bigger than a new Classic map (the core's 90 rows, 130 columns and 10,000 cells); it starts in the center as big as allowed, is dragged (mouse, finger, arrow keys) and pulled at a corner or an edge (corners only on a touch screen), a wider frame gets shorter by itself (a taller one stops where the cells run out), and **Use this part** gives the frame to the core's `crop` (interface version 4) whose part goes through the same conversion again, so it fits or gets its reasons. The side panel says "Rules 2" for the word 2. | done |
| E5 | **Save as a new map and drafts**: the **Save as new...** button of the top bar (and in a phone's Menu) keeps the map on the screen in the browser's own database (IndexedDB) as a **new** draft under a free name and shows the saved map; there is no way to write over a map (the database call is `add`, which refuses a name that is there), and a name that the open map, a draft or a library map has is refused with **Use “Name 3”** (the next free number) in one click. The one slot of unsaved work (not a map, replaced as the person draws, emptied by a save, a download or another map) is asked about on the next visit (**Pick it up** or **Throw it away**); the question before a map is left offers **Save as a new map first**; a browser that keeps nothing says so, offers Download and brings back the browser's own question before the page is closed. The same name check serves Copy and Start an Extended map. | done |
| E6 | **My maps**: the **My maps** button of the top bar (and in a phone's Menu) opens a screen over the editor with the maps saved in this browser, newest first: the picture, the name, the kind tags ("Extended map" with "New version only"), the size, whether it works, and the filters All, Classic and Extended with their counts. **Open** puts a saved map on the screen as the working map (a new history, after the question about unsaved drawing); saving it again is always **Save as a new map**, so no saved map is ever written over. **Copy...** starts the Copy dialog from the saved map. **Delete...** asks first (the first choice is **Keep it**) and then removes that one saved map from the drafts store of the browser's database: the player's own copy only, never a library map or a downloaded file. A browser that keeps nothing says so on the screen. The saved map's kind, size and check are read from its bytes, one row at a time. | done |
| E7 | **Submit for approval and My submissions** (S2): the **Submit for approval...** button of the top bar (and in a phone's Menu, with **My submissions**) opens a dialog with the map's picture and kind, the name (the map's own, changeable), the checker's verdict, an optional note for the operator and the player name shown as the maker (asked once, kept in the browser). **The button works only when the checker says the map works**; a map that does not is listed with its reasons and a **Show me** button. A name that the game's list has is told in a yellow note (the operator may change it or add a number), never refused; an Extended map gets the line that only players on the newest version will see it. **Submit** sends the open file's bytes (as Download hands them over) in one call and then says **Sent. Waiting for approval.**; nothing is published and the player keeps the own copy. **My submissions** is the second tab of the My maps screen: Waiting (with **Withdraw**, after a question), Approved, Approved as a new name (renamed by the owner), Declined with the operator's reason (shown as text), **Open my copy** and **Change and send again** (a changed copy is always a new submission; an approved map is never replaced), and **Copy my status link** (the token is in the address's fragment, so a second device can see the list). The page works when the door is not switched on: a plain "Sending maps is not switched on right now" line, nothing else hidden. | done |
| P1 | **The game's own pictures, part 1**: the editor draws the 1998 game's sprites instead of flat colors (a cell is 32 pixels; a tile id's picture is its animation's first sub-item with all its parts put together as the game draws them, put at the cell's corner plus the offset of their box), from an atlas that the web image's build makes (see "Sprites"); the Ground tool has the five kinds of ground (Gravel, Dirt, Slate, Mud, Water) and a sheet of every tile of a kind, so any single tile can be painted; the Objects, Bigger, Hills, Food and Ants palettes and the canvas show the real pictures, the Bigger tool and a plant of Objects show the picture under the pointer, the starting ants are the game's ant in the team's color centered in its team ring. The Ground names were wrong (grass, sand) and are fixed. Part 2 is P2. | done |
| P2 | **The game's own pictures, part 2: Fix edges and Auto edges**: a **Fix edges...** button next to Fill (a phone has it in the same row of buttons under the map) and an **Auto edges: On / Off** switch beside it (on a phone held upright, under the tool buttons while Ground is the active tool, and in the phone's Menu under "Settings for this browser"). Its box offers **the part I can see** or **the whole map**, shows a before and an after drawn from the pictures with the number of cells that change, and **Fix edges** makes the change as ONE step of the history (one Undo brings everything back, the bytes exactly as they were; Redo makes it again). The game does not blend the ground, so the edge and corner pieces must be in the file: the button chooses them from the kinds of the four neighbors (see "Fix edges"). It only adds or corrects pieces (never takes one away) and nothing but layer 1's tile words and the dictionary's names changes: not a cell's kind, its solid bit, its layer 2 or the walking. When **Auto edges: On** is chosen (remembered in `localStorage` under `ants_mapmaker_auto_edges`, Off by default for a new visitor), painting plain ground automatically sets the edge and corner pieces around the stroke as part of the same stroke (one Undo step), while leaving hand-placed custom tiles next to the stroke alone until painted over directly or until **Fix edges...** is run. | done |
| T1 | **Play this map**: a gold **Play this map** button, the first of the top bar (and the last entry of a phone's Menu, with a sentence under it), starts a one-player test game on the map that is open, against bots, in a tab of its own. Its box has a row for each color (Black, Blue, Green, Red) with its ants and hills and five answers (You, Bot Easy, Bot Medium, Bot Hard, Empty), quick buttons (1, 2, 3 bots) and **Start the game**; you take exactly one color and at least one bot is needed. A map that cannot be played gets a plain refusal instead (a Classic map of more than 90 rows or an Extended map larger than 512 x 512 / 262,144 cells / 1,024 a side: "This map is bigger than the game can play yet"; a map just under that size plays, but a diagonal order of 286 tiles or more, or a straight one of 400 or more, fails and big group orders are slow: [`MAP_FORMAT.md`](MAP_FORMAT.md), "Orders on a big map"; no two colors with a hill and starting ants; a stop of the check). In the game a strip on top has **Back to editor** (one question, then the tab closes) and says "Not saved. Not counted."; the map maker's own tab was never touched, so the map is there exactly as it was, with its history. See "Play this map" below. | Built |
| A1 | **Ants: one at a time, group, mix**: choosing the Ants tool opens a **Starting ants** box (the right-hand column on a desktop, under the tool buttons on a phone). A switch picks **One at a time** or **Group**. One at a time: four team buttons (Black, Blue, Green, Red) with a live count; a tap on a free cell puts ONE ant of the picked team there, a tap on an ant of ANY team takes it away (or **Take one away** makes every tap a removal); a drag looks around, as in the other tools that put. A cell must be free as the Clump's best cells are: inside the map, no object, no hill, no food pile, not solid ground, no ant; otherwise a plain sentence says why. Group has **Clump** (today's tap: 1 to 64 ants of the team round the cell, put again to move, byte for byte as before) and **Mix**: pick the teams, the ants for each team (default 25) and the spacing (**Every cell**, or **Every other cell**, a chessboard from the box's corner so no two ants touch side by side), and drag a box: dots in the team colors preview the ants (the ants are spread evenly over the free cells of the box, the teams are dealt to them in an order shuffled by a generator seeded from the box, with a repair so that no row or column of two or more ants is one team, so every team has exactly the same number and the pattern is the same every time); **Put 100 ants** is one change and one Undo, and a box that is too small is refused with the numbers ("100 ants need 200 cells; your box has 150"). The box says what the file has one value of (the ant type, the eggs) with a link to Properties, warns in plain words (a team with none: amber, the Check wants a start for each; more than 65,535 records in block 1, ants and plants together: red, and the Put buttons stop; ants shoulder to shoulder that cannot get out: a soft amber hint) a note when the map has more than 90 rows (the game cannot play it yet) or no default ant type (the ants will not fight), and has **Clear <team> ants...** with a question that says how many. Every action is a change of the open file's bytes: a record put in or taken out of block 1 and the count word, as small splices. The Erase tool is as before (it takes a whole team's ants). | done |
| M1 | **Pick up**: the old Move tool is called **Pan** now (a drag looks around, nothing else) and a new tool, **Pick up** (letter P; M is still Pan), takes a thing up: a tap on a hill (its 4 x 4 square and its door), a starting ant, a food pile (with its stages), a plant (with its dropper) or any object (all its cells) gives it a gold ring and a bar (at the foot of the screen on a phone, a row of its own under the map on a desktop) with **Move**, **Copy here**, **Delete**, **Select an area...** and **Cancel**. Move shows a see-through ghost where the thing lands (green; red, with the reason in plain words, when it cannot go there), a tap puts the middle of it on that cell (on a desktop the ghost follows the mouse, green or red with its reason, a click puts it there, and it goes back to where it was put when the mouse leaves the map or a key is pressed), a drag of the picked thing or of the ghost moves it and **letting go of the mouse or the finger puts it down where the ghost is** (one Undo, an area or a group as a whole; if the place is red, the thing goes back to where it was picked up (nothing is written, no Undo step) and the bar says "Put back:" and the reason until the next thing you do; a lift off the map (off the canvas, past the map's edge, over the side panel or over the bar) takes it back too, and so do Escape, a cancelled pointer and a window that lost the focus; Done and Enter wait for the lift while the button is down; a plain click or tap only places the ghost, red or green, and Done puts it down: on a red place Done is off and the thing stays in hand with the reason), **Nudge arrows** (48 pixels) and the arrow keys move it one cell (Shift: 8), **Done** (or Enter) puts it down, Escape lets go, Delete takes it away. A tap on a cell with several things picks the next one each time. Several ants that stand together ask "this ant or its group (the ants of its team that touch)" for Move, for Copy here and for Delete, and **Select an area...** picks everything whole inside a dragged box. A move, a copy, a delete and the move of a whole area are each ONE Undo; the ring and the ghost are drawn on a canvas that lies over the map, so the tool never asks for the map to be painted for them (the map's own canvas still paints a frame of 1 to 7 ms as the pointer moves, as it does for a pan). A cell of an old map that names another place of the map as its anchor keeps that tie exactly (a tie to a cell that moves along moves along, a tie to a place that stays stays, and a place off the map stays off it); a move or a copy that could not keep it is refused in plain words. No ground refuses a thing: a rock, a plant and the other objects, and also a hill, a starting ant and a food pile may stand on water (the library's maps have them there). A hill, an ant or a pile that lands on water gets a green ghost and a one-line hint in the bar, and the same line after it is put down: what the test matches showed (an ant that cannot swim moves to land beside its marker, or drowns when it has none; ants that cannot swim reach a pile on water only from the shore, Swimmers swim to it or build a bridge; a hill whose way out is water may keep its ants in). The map checker and Test play stay the judge. On a short screen (a phone held sideways, a window under 700 pixels high) the bar flows in one or two rows of 44 pixel buttons, with the Hide button on the line of the words, and Hide folds it to one small button over the foot of the map, so that the map and the ghost stay in view; Escape on a button of the bar gives the focus back to the map. **The map ends above the bar**: the bar is a row of its own, so the map is as much lower as the bar is high while something is picked (`mapmaker_canvas.js` `settle`: the map keeps its top row and its zoom, so nothing under a pointer moves, and the foot of it is for panning, the last row can be brought above the bar; the picked thing, or the ghost when no pointer drives it, is brought into view when the bar would cover it; a map that was whole is cut, not shrunk, and whole again when the bar goes); a phone's bar is fixed to the screen, and the page can be scrolled until its last line is above it (`--pk-h`, the bar's height, is the room below the page, made again when the bar's size changes: a phone that is turned, a window that is resized; a `ResizeObserver` whose call only asks for the next frame, where the bar is measured and settled, so the browser reports no error at a resize; let go of when the tool is left). While a thing is on its way the bar never gets lower than it has been in that move at that width (a place where it cannot go says more than one where it can, and the map would grow and shrink under the pointer). A map under 300 pixels high shows the small map of the whole map (120 pixels, no caption), so that it never reaches into the bar. The tool is a lazily loaded script and style (`mapmaker_pickup.js`, `mapmaker_pickup.css`). | done |

The lobby's map list and the match page's "Download this map" show the kind of a map too; that is the work of the changes that load Extended maps in the game, not of the editor.
