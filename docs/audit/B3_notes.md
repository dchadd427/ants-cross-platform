Update for section 50 of `implementation_plan.md` (that file is not part of the repository; the owner copies this text into it). Milestone B3 of the computer players, as designed in [`docs/BOTS.md`](../BOTS.md): the task model, the worker bot (harvest only), the pinned baselines. Status: built, then reviewed by an adversarial review (21 findings, every one fixed or written down: see "The review of B3" below), every suite passes; the release is v0.0.88 (`include/ants_app/version.hpp` and test 12.108 say so, the CHANGELOG entry is titled "v0.0.88"). The fixes of the review of B2, which came later, changed the walking costs of the start (from the whole queue row of a hill) and with them the TREASURE rows of the pinned table and the seed of AI3.5's whole TREASURE match: [`B2_notes.md`](B2_notes.md), "On top of B3", has what moved and why, and `docs/BOTS.md`, "Measurements", the current numbers.

## Where this differs from the design, and why

- **Learning counts a pile once, not an ant.** The ants of one order fail together; the first version counted three failures for one wall. `HarvestTask::failures()` counts piles learned.
- **Two failure signals, both measured from the tick the order LEFT.** The design had the idle-at-origin rule (an ant that stands within one tile of where it stood, empty, 24 ticks after the release). B3 adds the "can't go" reaction (state `CantGo` 8 or more ticks after the release), which a Hard bot (a look every 4 ticks) sees long before the 24 ticks are over. Neither starts from the decision tick: AI3.4 runs the bot with a reaction time of 60 ticks and a look every 4, where a timer from the decision would blacklist every pile 36 ticks before its first order has left; AI3.13 pins the 24-tick clock to the tick of `on_command(Sent)`.
- **The component check needs a third answer: "unknown".** `MapInfo::ant_component` is -1 for an ant that stands on a tile the analysis of the start took for solid and that has no walkable neighbour: the middle of a big pile that was eaten (TREASURE's biggest pile; the idle ants of a finished pile stand there). The first version treated -1 as "stranded" and left three of six ants of TREASURE seat 3 idle for thousands of ticks (2400 points where 2875 are possible). `standing_component` looks at the tiles within six of the ant for the nearest known component; an ant whose component is unknown even then is not told "no" (failure learning catches it). Stranded ants (a ring of obstacles, ISLANDS' islands) are still never sent: AI3.13.
- **The endgame veto leaves room for the reaction time.** The design's `trip + 10` ticks; the order leaves up to the level's longest reaction delay (delay plus its jitter) after the look, so the bot asks for `trip + 10 + that`. AI3.8 pins the line at Medium (no order at `trip + 9`, one with `trip + 10 + delay + a look + 10`), the margin ITSELF with a profile that has no jitter and a reaction time of 1 tick (Medium's 24 ticks of delay hid every margin below 24: the margins 0 and 30 both passed the first version of the suite), and in whole matches no pile is ordered with less time left than its trip takes. Switching the margin off changes no number of the table (the veto binds rarely and late).
- **A stuck carrier is sent home, by the clock (not in the design).** Found by measuring: TINY seat 3, alone over the whole match, scored 1380 instead of 1770 on seed 2, and three of eight seeds with four workers. The prototype's 1410 for that seat was the same thing (run with a detector: for 5,727 of the match's 7,200 ticks a carrier stood idle with its food more than seven tiles from its hill). The cause was found by the review and is written down in `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` 5.40: the original does exactly this (a team-mate that stands paused or idle on the doorway of the hill makes the hill unreachable for the moment of the path search; the failed search only stops the ant; nothing retries). The first rescue (an idle worker with food more than four tiles from the mound for 40 ticks, one group move to the entrance) had two holes the first census could not see: the same refusal strands a carrier ON the ring in front of the gate (TREASURE seat 0, seeds 1, 2, 3 alone and 1, 2, 5 with four workers: one of six workers for 94 percent of the match, 1 tile from the mound), and with 12 or more workers the queue for the gate reaches past 4 tiles, so queued carriers were rescued (28 rescues, 12.7 percent lost with 20 workers). What tells a stuck carrier from a queued one is TIME: the clock is 900 ticks of standing idle with food (the longest wait that ended by itself on the shipped maps was 772 ticks in 100,000 waits: Easy 733, Medium 772, Hard 650, 12 seeds, alone and four workers; median 130, p99 428), a carrier far from the hill with nobody queueing within 4 tiles of the mound is helped after 40 (TINY seat 3 keeps its fast path: its numbers did not move), and nobody is helped while 8 or more carriers stand idle at once (waits of 616 / 870 / 1,439 / 1,800 / 2,384 ticks with 8 / 12 / 16 / 20 / 24 workers on one hill measured with no rescue at all; there the gate is the bottleneck, and 12 to 24 workers scored 0.8 / 3 / 6 / 10 percent less with an unconditional rescue). Ants that queue for the gate stand 1 to 3 tiles from the mound with up to 8 ants per hill (3 in all 100,000 shipped-map waits). A rescued ant's wait starts again.
- **`approach_now` and the bite object.** B2 left open whether the ranking wants the cost of a pile as it is now: the bot re-asks `approach_now` for a pile that has been eaten into (its click tile of the start may no longer be food; AI3.3 pins that the click is a cell of the footprint of NOW), and a pile whose bite object (`PileInfo::bite_index`, two objects on one anchor) is empty is no candidate, so an idle ant is not sent again and again to an object that never runs out.
- **No hatch policy.** The design's `HatchPolicy::Never` default is simply the absence of a hatch rule; AI3.6 pins it (rich, with eggs, in a hand-made world, and in all fifteen full solo matches: no `Hatch`, eggs unchanged, nothing hatched). The worker is a fixed yardstick that does not hatch by design; hatching helps only a little on TINY seat 0 and loses on the other maps. Measured with the arena (a wrapper bot of the test bench that calls `Orders::hatch()` when it has 200 points, an egg and none incubating; alone, Medium, the whole match, mean of the four seats and seeds 1 and 2, against the plain worker): one hatch TINY 1824 (1789), SMALL 1891 (2019), MEDIUM 1614 (1799), GAUNTLET 681 (846), TREASURE 2831 (3028); a hatch whenever it can TINY 1406, SMALL 1844, MEDIUM 648, GAUNTLET 116, TREASURE 1196. On TINY seat 0 one hatch gains 190 (seed 1) and 220 (seed 2), seats 2 and 3 lose. (The first text said "never pays on any shipped map": wrong for TINY seat 0. A worker takes orders 171 ticks after the `Hatch` command: born at tick 160, the emerge clip of 11 ticks, measured.)
- **One Deny per invitation.** The look of a Hard bot is 4 ticks and its Deny leaves 8 (6 to 10) ticks later: without a guard the invitation, still pending, is answered at every look. The bot keeps a "not before" tick (the longest the Deny can take to leave plus the time to live, shortened to 12 ticks after the release when it left). AI3.7 counts exactly one Deny per invitation at the three levels, and the controller's filter lets it through (`filtered` and `rejected` stay 0).
- **"The sum equals the reachable pot" is a band, not an equality.** Over 600 four-worker matches (40 seeds, three levels, five maps) the sum was 100.00 to 102.50 percent of the pot (180 matches, 12 seeds: 100.00 to 101.67; the first text said 99.2 to 101.7 and was under-sampled: SMALL Hard seeds 15, 20, 59 give 102.5), and at the end of every match not a unit was left on a pile that a hill can walk to (`ArenaResult::reachable_units_left` is 0): two hills can bite the same last unit in the same moment (a few units more). Before the stuck carrier of the gate was fixed the low end was 99.2 (the ant stood there with the food at 0:00). AI3.11 asserts that not a unit is left and 98 to 104 percent. ISLANDS is exactly 0 = 0.
- **Baselines.** The procedure lives in `ants_ai` (`baselines.hpp`) instead of the tool, so that the test and `--write-baselines` run the same code; two seeds (1 and 2) and the arena's latency of 3 ticks instead of the prototype's one seed and latency 0; per-seat numbers (seats are not symmetric) with a band of 8 percent, never less than 15 points (one bite), ISLANDS exactly 0. The write-baselines output of a GCC 12 build equals the macOS table byte for byte (checked again after the review). The review moved 23 numbers: MEDIUM Easy seat 2 alone over the whole match 1710 to 1700; TREASURE Easy (alone 2 minutes seat 3 440 to 448, alone seat 0 2948 to 2998 and seat 3 2775 to 2765, four workers 2350 / 2395 / 2003 / 2148 to 2338 / 2390 / 2028 / 2133, sum 8895 to 8888), Medium (alone 2 minutes seat 0 595 to 625, alone seat 0 2795 to 2980, four workers 2265 / 2178 / 2370 / 2008 to 2545 / 2188 / 2235 / 1905, sum 8820 to 8873) and Hard (alone 2 minutes seat 0 593 to 580, alone seat 0 2910 to 3010, four workers 2393 / 2168 / 2320 / 1975 to 2533 / 2150 / 2288 / 1920, sum 8855 to 8890); no other row moved.
- **A separate test program.** The suite plays whole matches (about 52 s of CPU time in a release build, 21 s on eight cores: the pinned procedure runs on up to six threads, every match is independent), so it is `test_ai_worker`, suite 2.22, and not part of the quick suite 2.20. `W_ONLY=AI3.4,AI3.7 ./build/tests/test_ai/test_ai_worker` runs the tests with exactly those numbers (the first version matched substrings: `AI3.1` ran AI3.10 to AI3.15 too, and a misspelt filter ran no test and exited 0: now the program fails when nothing ran), `W_SKIP=AI3.9,AI3.12` leaves tests out; `run_tests.sh` clears both variables.
- **Existing tests that had to change, three assertions, each because its premise was "worker and standard are the idle bot until B3 / B4"** (nothing weakened): `test_ai_setup.cpp` (the registry: `worker` and `standard` give a bot whose `kind()` is "worker", `idle` still "idle"), `test_ai_arena.cpp` (AI4.3: the seats named `worker` and `standard` report that "worker" played; the decisions counts are unchanged), `test_app/test_network_app.cpp` (AI6.3: the standard bot of seat 1 now sends its ants to the food, so `released >= 1` where it was `== 0`; the idle seat still releases 0).
- The approach cost is the exact one of `MapInfo`, as B2 made it (the design added a constant of 20): see the open points.

## Mutation check

Each deliberate fault was put into a scratch copy of the sources (the library objects and the test binary rebuilt from nothing every time), the suite 2.22 was run without the pinned table (AI3.9, AI3.12) and, when nothing failed, with it, and the failing tests are listed. **Every one of the 68 faults is caught by at least one test.** The first 25 are the faults of the first check of B3 (as they apply to the code now); the others are the guards that the review added. The check found real holes while it was being made, and the tests were strengthened until every fault was caught: `ignore_blacklist` (the ants that fail are kept away from a pile by their own exclusion, so the blacklist is only tested by an ant that has not failed: AI3.4 sends one in), `cantgo_no_window` (a white-box case with the clip of another hand's refusal after the window), `idle_at_origin_needs_no_unit_taken`, `pile_works_*`, `witnesses_*` (AI3.16: first failure of a shut-in ant on a pile that nobody works, two shut-in ants, two late ants at an unreachable pile, units taken by a stranger), `throttle_16` (it found a real defect: the constructor without parameters ignored the default of `Params::max_ants_per_look`), `no_throttle_halving`, `watchdog_stops_carriers`, `rescue_wait_not_restarted`, `rescue_cooldown_0`. (The scratch builds of two earlier runs of this check were stale after a failed build and reported false survivors; the check now removes the objects and the library before every build.)

## The match clock waits for the dialog (v0.2.0): what became of the start hold

The owner, after v0.1.1: "I believe on the original game when the get ready box appears the timer is already running, but can we make a change for this remake that the timer doesn't start until the ants actually are able to move? It wastes like five or six seconds." A deliberate deviation from the original (`docs/AUDIT_ONE_TO_ONE.md` 3b; `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` section 21, re-read in the binary with Capstone: the GO handler `0x1022432` that releases the dialog also starts the clock), network protocol 12 (`docs/NETWORK_PORT.md` "Protocol 12"). Deep tier (the network protocol and who can act when).

**What changed.** The dialog is as it was (picture, texts, 5 s, every click and key taken) but **the simulation does not run while it is up**. A local game counts it in real time (the HUD's 100 steps of 50 ms, stepped by `Application::update_simulation`; the simulation starts on the step after the last one). In a match of the network the host seals the first turn `net::kMatchStartDelayMs` = 5000 ms after the match began (`HostSession::Config::start_delay_ms`, set by `NetGame::begin_match` and `Room::begin_match`) and every machine closes its dialog when its first turn executes (`Application::post_tick`). Before the first turn nothing is sealed: no stall, no lag, no pause. **The idle rule of the lag policy counted those seconds (a defect that this change would have introduced, found on the way and fixed in `police_laggards`).** `kProtocolVersion` 12 (no message changed; a client of 11 would count its dialog in simulation ticks and be blocked for 100 ticks of the running match). The delay is a constant of the protocol, not a field of a message (no machine needs the number).

**The bots.** The v0.1.1 hold of 100 ticks is gone: the controller acts on simulation ticks only, so no bot looks or orders while the dialog is up (asserted in a local game, a LAN host's room and a server's room). The mechanism stays (`set_start_hold(N)`: first look on tick N + seat, releases and refill from tick N, one token), with the product's value `ai::kStartHoldTicks` = 1 (a look on tick 1 + seat as in v0.1.0, ONE token and a refill from the first tick; the refill used to start on tick N + 1, now on tick N, so that the product's hold of 1 refills from the first tick). `set_start_hold(0)` is still the opening of v0.1.0 (a full bucket) for the tests that measure something else.
