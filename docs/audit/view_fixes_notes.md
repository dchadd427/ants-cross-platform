# View fixes after v0.2.0: notes and handover

Two changes to the map view. Part 1 is finished. Part 2 is built, proven by mutation and sanitizers and measured for speed ("Part 2: proof"); the independent review and the browser check are what is left (end of the file).

## Part 1: the ants before the match starts (done: 8b33463, ba64703)

- **Cause.** Since v0.2.0 the simulation waits behind the "Get ready to play!" dialog. The renderer draws an ant from the clip that the simulation plays on it, and the first movement tick (`loco_sync`) is what starts the idle clip, so until then `AntSnapshot::loco_clip` was 0x7FFE (no clip, no sprite) while the hit point digits (default on) were drawn: numbers and no ants.
- **Original.** There is no ant before the GO message: GO (0x1022432) calls 0x100ec18, which posts the type-5 messages whose handler (0x10228a3) calls CreateAnt (0x100ef18) and SetAction(idle, direction rand%7+1) at 0x100efd3 .. 0x100efef. So every ant shows frame 0 of its idle clip from its creation. Noted in `docs/GAME_REVERSE_ENGINEERING.md` (section 21) and `docs/AUDIT_ONE_TO_ONE.md`.
- **Fix** (presentation only: no simulation state, state hash, replay or protocol change). `get_world_state` shows, while `current_tick_ == 0`, the clip that the first `loco_sync` will start (`pending_idle_clip`, sharing `idle_motion_clip` with `set_action`'s idle case), frame 0, held. After the first tick the snapshot is the locomotion's own.
- **Tests.** `test_movement_golden` suite 6 (every shipped map, three seeds; the hash with and without; swimmer, load, default type, other states, nothing after the first tick); new suite 3.21 `test_prestart_view` (local game on three maps in both picture shapes: every team's ants drawn and the same pixels after the first tick; nothing moves behind the dialog; a network guest). The 19 + 8 pinned frames taken at tick 0 changed by the ants' sprites only (checked image by image) and were regenerated. 10 of 10 mutants caught.

## Part 2: many zoom levels (done: 2e34032, 96569c4; proof below)

**Rules (decided by the owner and the coordinator).**
- Levels are the series 2^(k/4) from 2 down to the map's limit: 2, 1.68, 1.41, 1.19, 1, 0.84, 0.71, 0.59, 0.5, 0.42 ... One wheel notch is one level, towards the pointer. 2, 1, 0.5, 0.25 are exact.
- The limit is `max(view_w / map_w, view_h / map_h)` (the map's width or height just fills the view; the view never shows outside the map), never above 1 (a map that the view covers at 1 has no zoom-out). It is the last level; a series level closer than a quarter step above it is left out. TREASURE: 0.397 (16:9) and 0.230 (4:3); TINY 16:9: 0.768; SMALL 16:9: 0.595.
- Network matches offer the zoom-out too (no limit of the kind of match). `--zoom` and the settings key `zoom` take any number from 0.05 to 2; a match starts at the nearest level of its map and the remembered number is kept.
- Pictures: linear filter between 1 and 2 (the nearest filter gives uneven pixel widths) and below 1; 2 stays crisp. Below 1 the world is drawn at one texel per world pixel into an offscreen target, halved by exact 2 x 2 averages while the zoom is below 0.5 (so the last scale is in [0.5, 1)), and the last level is scaled into a lattice of whole screen pixels. The camera origin is on a lattice of one screen pixel at the zoom, so a scroll only moves the picture (no shimmer); the target starts at a multiple of a power-of-two block so the halvings group the same world pixels at every origin.
- A real renderer places the last copy at its fractional position (`SDL_RenderCopyF`); SDL's software renderer (the tests, and a fallback) truncates a float rectangle, so there the picture is up to a pixel off between the exact levels.

**Where.** `include/ants_app/view_zoom.hpp` (the model: levels, limit, nearest, step, camera, plan of the pass, wheel, level text), `renderer.hpp/.cpp` (the pass: textures, pyramid, lattice), `application.hpp/.cpp` (levels, `set_zoom` snaps, no network limit, `--zoom` text), `hud.cpp` (edge scroll and minimap use whole screen pixels of the map), README, `docs/NETWORK_PORT.md`, `web/shell.html` text.

**Tests (all passing).** `test_zoom_model` (1.5 million checks: levels per shipped map and view, limit, nearest, step, anchoring, camera clamps at the limit, scroll, wheel, settings text, the plan); `test_zoom_view` (16557 checks; new groups `levels` and `place`: every level of GAUNTLET and TINY in both shapes against the picture the design specifies, exact levels pixel for pixel, the others within a pixel and smooth, no black row or column, scroll invariance at the exact levels, frames in any order; the HUD, wheel, API, settings and network groups at every level); `test_zoom_fingerprint` (136 fingerprints; the two "TINY 16:9 at 0.5" application frames are gone because 0.5 is no level there any more: the row is skipped and the start at the nearest level is checked); `test_network_app` N5.69, N5.70, N5.71 rewritten (same levels as a local game, wheel works in the match). `./run_tests.sh --fast`: 49 suites, 0 failed. Build with `-DANTS_WERROR=ON`: zero warnings. Note: a stale test binary once looked like 26 failures; rebuild the test targets before reading a failure.

## Part 2: proof (deep tier)

- **Mutants** (`tools/mutate.py`, 96): the model (series, `floor_zoom`, `levels`, `nearest`, `step`, `level_for_match`, `clamp_origin`, snapping, `map_screen`, `zoomed`, `plan`, `smooth_at`, the wheel, the settings text), the pass (filters, halving, lattice crop and place, the software branch, margins, the camera), the HUD (`edge_step`, the minimap step, the radar's frame) and the application (the limits of a network match, `set_zoom`, the remembered level, `--zoom` and the settings key, the camera's clamp, the anchor, the wheel). 83 were caught at once by the suite of their rule (50 by `test_zoom_model` alone; the picture, HUD and application ones by `test_zoom_view`, 14 also by the fingerprints, and N5.69 - N5.71 for the network limit, `set_zoom`, the remembered level and `--zoom`). 12 survived and now have tests (nearest's tie, step's tolerance, `snap_down`'s epsilon, a plan over a map that is not a multiple of the alignment; the new group `place`: the software renderer's whole-pixel rectangle and the black margins around a small map; the device's texture size in `api`, which needed the hook `Renderer::set_texture_side_for_test`: the software renderer reports no limit). 1 is equivalent: `map_screen`'s `+ 1e-9` never changes a floor (an integer times a float is a multiple of 2^-28). The specs were kept out of the repository.
- **AddressSanitizer + UBSan** (halting on the first report, leak check on): `test_zoom_model`, `test_zoom_view`, `test_zoom_fingerprint`, `test_prestart_view` and N5.69 - N5.72: clean. The build used `-DANTS_WERROR=OFF`: GCC's `-Wsign-conversion` in `match_stats.hpp`, `bot_controller.cpp`, `session.cpp` and two shifts in `application.cpp` / `hud.cpp` stop an unoptimised `-Werror` build at the base (not changed here).
- **Speed** (TREASURE, 60 ants: four colonies at their full size, the three bots of the application plus a fourth controller on the person's seat, hatching forced; native Release, 60 Hz frames of `run_frame_with_delta`, 900 frames a level; **SDL's software renderer, no GPU: an upper bound of the CPU cost**).   The same with the colonies as the Hard bots leave them (24 ants): 9.25 and 12.47 ms at the two limits. By the rule of the plan (a floor only if the smallest level costs more than twice zoom 1) the software renderer asks for one, but **no floor is proposed**: (1) callgrind shows that 90% (16:9) and 94% (4:3) of the instructions of a frame at the limit are `end_world_target`, SDL's software copies of the offscreen target (the halving, the scaling, the cut: 87 and 128 million instructions a frame); drawing the world into the target is 2%, and everything else is 9.5 million instructions a frame at the 16:9 limit against 14.4 million at zoom 1 (a frame that is mostly the software window's present), so the game's own work does not grow with the zoom-out; a GPU renderer (the web, a native window) does the copies as three textured quads; (2) the first level out, 0.84, already costs 2.5 times zoom 1 (every level but 1 pays the offscreen pass), so the only floor that meets the rule is 1, no zoom-out at all; (3) the worst software figure, the 4:3 limit, is 13 ms of a 16.7 ms frame (p95 18.8 ms). The web's own cost was not measured (no GPU and no browser here). If the owner wants a limit for the software fallback anyway, the table shows what each floor costs (0.5: 6.1 ms at 16:9, 3.7 ms at 4:3).
