# The chat hub: the data it keeps and the login

Part of [The chat hub](HUB.md). The roles that a login leads to, the moderators' log and the admin page's door are in [HUB_ROLES.md](HUB_ROLES.md).

## Data

The hub, the login and the command line reach the data only through the interface of `hub/store/base.py`: lines, deletions (a counter that only goes up), purge, mutes and bans (with a counter of their changes that only goes up), logins, forget and size. A store that cannot do one now (locked, full, out of reach) raises `StoreError`, which is the only error a caller catches. `hub/store/sqlite.py` is the one backend and the default; what only it has (the log file's cut, `secure_delete`) stays on its class. `tests/scripts/test_hub_store_contract.py` runs the same checks against any backend. The rest of this section describes the SQLite one.

`/data/hub.db` (SQLite, WAL, file mode 600; the volume `ants-hub-data`): `lines` (id, time, account id, the name that was shown, text, the author's role when it wrote the line, a deletion mark), `mutes`, `bans`, `sessions` (the logins, below), `profiles` (below), `pictures` ([HUB_PICTURES.md](HUB_PICTURES.md)), `secrets` (the key of the references), `alerts` (an account's bell), `ignores` (the people that an account ignores) and `follows` (the people that it follows), all by references and all made by the first one ([HUB_PEOPLE.md](HUB_PEOPLE.md#alerts-the-accounts-list)), `dms` and `reports` (private messages and the reports of them, by references, made by the first one: [HUB_MESSAGES.md](HUB_MESSAGES.md)), and `modlog` ([the moderators' log](HUB_ROLES.md#the-moderators-log)). The schema is version 4 (`PRAGMA user_version`): a file of an earlier version is migrated at its first open (the two tables are added and the secret is made; nothing else is touched), and a hub of an earlier version refuses a file of version 4 ("from a newer hub"), so keep a copy of `hub.db` before an upgrade that may be rolled back. **The role on a line** needs no step of the schema: the column that held the moderator's flag (`mod`) holds the role that the author had when it wrote the line, 0 (none), 1 (a moderator) or 2 (an administrator). A line of before is a 1 or a 0 and is shown as it was (an administrator's line of before is a moderator's); a hub of an earlier version reads the 2 as the flag that it knows (any value that is not 0 is true, in its pages and in the purge's own condition), so a rollback shows an administrator's line as a moderator's and loses nothing. A line shows the role that its author had when it wrote it (a person who loses the role keeps the mark on the lines that they wrote while they held it, until the lines go in 7 days); the role of the moment is never taken from a line, because the powers are decided only by the fresh check. A rollback that keeps the data is one line, run in the container before the older hub starts: `python -c "import sqlite3; c=sqlite3.connect('/data/hub.db'); c.execute('PRAGMA user_version=3'); c.commit()"` (the two new tables are additive; the older hub does not trim `modlog`, and an upgrade again picks it up). A line is kept only while it is among the newest 200 lines (every line counts: a moderator's line and a line that a moderator deleted too) and for up to 7 days, and then deleted for good; whichever limit is smaller deletes first. The store takes the lines past the newest 200 out in the step that keeps a new line (at most 8 at a time, so one chat line never waits for a long delete), so the table holds 200 lines; a file that holds more (one written by an earlier version) is cut down by the hub's purge, which runs at the first tick after a start and then every hour, 2,000 lines at a time and again at once while a chunk was full, together with the lines past their 7 days (a long backlog goes over several seconds, not in one wait), the freed pages are zeroed (`secure_delete`) and the log file is cut; when a reader (for example `hub.admin list`) holds the file at that moment, the cut is tried again every 10 seconds, and once at every start. **The store makes room by itself.** From 90% of the 64 MB (`db_max_bytes`: the pages of `hub.db` that hold something, plus its log file) the hub's clock deletes the oldest lines, 2,000 a second, until the pages that hold something are down to 80%: the moderators' lines and the lines that a moderator deleted last, and the newest 1,000 never (a page shows 100, the hub keeps 50; with the 200 lines that the store keeps, this is the last resort that it was meant to be, and the 64 MB are not reached); the log file is cut after each chunk. A program that holds `hub.db` open for reading (a backup tool, a shell that was left open) keeps the log file from being cut, and a chunk of 2,000 lines would add some megabytes of pages to it: while the log file cannot be cut and is over 16 MB the hub deletes no more lines, asks again every 10 seconds, and says `database_full_a_reader_holds_the_log_file` in its log (at most once in 10 seconds) when the limit is reached meanwhile. Mutes, bans, the deletion counter, the logins, the profiles and the moderators' log are not touched. The lines go in the order of their numbers, which is also the order of their time and of their pages, so the pages they leave are whole and free, the lines that follow use them, and the file stops growing (a deletion from the middle, such as a forget, leaves pages that still hold lines, which count as in use until the oldest lines have gone past them). At the 64 MB itself no new line is kept (`rate`): that guard is the last resort, so the hub does not fill the volume by itself, and the log says `database_full_and_no_line_to_delete` once a minute if nothing may go; `python -m hub.admin status` and `purge` are the same from a terminal ([Moderation](HUB_COMMANDS.md#moderation)); when the disk refuses a write, or another process holds the database's write lock for longer than the hub waits (0.3 seconds), the line or the moderator's command is not done and the sender is told: `rate` for a line (not counted as a mistake, the line did not cost a token, and the same text may be tried again at once) and `bad` for a command, the socket stays, and one `error` line (the class of the error, never the text) is logged every 10 seconds at the most; the command line says "the database refused it" in one line. Any other database error closes the socket that caused it (1011). Deletions are numbered by a counter in `meta` that a purge cannot lower, so a mod's `/del` still reaches every screen after old lines were purged. Every ban, unban and mute (the command line's too) raises another counter in `meta`: that is how the hub knows that the two lists changed without reading them at every look of the front page's list. A purge that fails (another process holds the write lock, the disk refuses) is tried again after a minute, and the hub's writes wait at most 0.3 seconds for another process, so the one thread is never held for seconds. A line that a moderator deleted stays, marked, until it is past the newest 200 lines or its 7 days are up: a deletion is a mark on a line that the pages show, and the pages show 100 at most, so the line is among the newest 200 for as long as a page can show it; a moderator's `/del` and the command line's `del` work on any line that is still there, and the counter in `meta` that numbers the deletions never goes down when the line is taken out. The last 50 lines that are not deleted, not past their time and among the newest 200 are the history after a restart. A login (`sessions`) holds the SHA-256 of its cookie's value, the account id, the username as it was when the visitor logged in, and the time it ends; the hub deletes the ones that ran out with the lines past their time. **A profile** (`profiles`: account id, username, display name or none, which of the two is picked, and the time of the login that last refreshed it) is made or refreshed at every login (the pick stays, and is the display name for a new profile and for one that had no display name and now has one); it is what the hub reads at a handshake to name the socket (a login from before profiles were kept has none, and is shown by its username until its next login), and what a moderator's `/mute` and the command line look a username up in (an account is found only when exactly one profile has the username). A profile is deleted by Forget me, and by the hub's hourly purge when no login has refreshed it for 35 days (a login lasts 30), and the table keeps at most 100,000 (the ones refreshed longest ago go, as the logins do). Lines keep the name that they were written with. Nothing else about a person is stored. **Forget and logout leave nothing in the log file:** a forget cuts the log file (`hub.db-wal`) before it answers, so that the lines, the name and the hashes of the logins are in neither file (the freed pages are zeroed, and when a reader holds the file the cut is tried again every 10 seconds); the end of a login (a logout, a login that replaces the old one of a browser, one that the caps push out) has the cut done within 10 seconds, one cut for any number of them. **The schema is 4** (`PRAGMA user_version`; the `sessions` table came with version 3). A hub of an earlier version does not start on such a database (`RuntimeError: hub.db is from a newer hub (schema 3)`), so a rollback of the hub to a version before the login needs the volume of before it (or a new volume): the file is never changed back. **The `profiles` table is additive and came with no new version**: it is made when a hub opens a file that has none, and a hub of the version before it opens the same file, ignores the table, and works as it did (the chat shows usernames; a name is addressed as it was). What that hub leaves behind: its Forget me does not know the table, so the profile of an account that forgot itself there stays until the purge of a hub that has profiles deletes it, 35 days after the account's last login. The nicknames are three more additive tables, `nicknames`, `nick_hold` and `login_claim` ([HUB_NICKNAMES.md](HUB_NICKNAMES.md)); Forget me deletes the account's rows in all three.

## Login

The hub core asks a `Sessions` object (`hub/session.py`) who is on the other end of a handshake: `identify(headers)` returns an `Identity(account_id, name, young, session, expires, username, pick, other)` or `None`. An account id is digits (1 to 20: a Discord id), `name` is the name that is shown (`username` when there is no display name to choose), `young` says the account is too new to write, `pick` and `other` are set only for an account that has both names. The hub believes an identity only when the username is a username and the name that is shown is that username or a display name that `valid_shown` leaves as it is. `identify` is a coroutine (a real login looks something up); the hub waits 5 seconds for it. Without the settings of a Discord application the hub has `NoLogin`: nobody is identified, the welcome says `login:false`, and the hub is read-only for everyone. With them it has `DiscordLogin` (`hub/login.py`, `hub/discord.py`); the core and its tests do not change.

**What is asked of Discord, and what is kept.** The one scope `identify` (the visitor's id, username, display name and the short code of the profile picture: `GET /users/@me` carries `global_name` and `avatar` with that scope, null for a person who set none), through the authorization code grant: the visitor is sent to `https://discord.com/oauth2/authorize` with `client_id`, `response_type=code`, `scope=identify`, `redirect_uri=<ANTS_HUB_PUBLIC_URL>/hub/callback`, a `state` and `prompt=none` (a visitor who agreed before does not see the consent page again). The code that comes back is exchanged by the hub, never by the page (`POST /api/v10/oauth2/token`, a form body with the client secret), and the access token is used once, for `GET /api/v10/users/@me`. **Only the id, the username and the display name are kept.** The access token and the refresh token are local variables of one function and are gone when it returns; the email and everything else of the answer are never looked at, and the picture's short code is checked for its shape, used for the fetch of [Pictures](HUB_PICTURES.md) and never kept (what that page remembers, in memory only, to tell a changed picture is a keyed mark of it). Every answer of Discord is data: https only, the certificate checked, 3 seconds to connect, 6 for a call and 10 for the two, no redirect followed, at most 32 KB read, JSON of the shape that was asked for or no login (a username that is not a username, an id that is not an id, a token that is not a plain word; a display name of any kind is accepted as data and cleaned, so it is never a reason for no login; the answer as a whole is still held to its 32 KB). PKCE is not used: Discord's reference for the authorization code grant mentions it once, in passing (a warning at the implicit grant), and describes no parameter for it; its guide for web logins says a server-side client authenticates with its client secret and that PKCE is for public clients such as native and mobile apps. The login is therefore held by the `state` (below), and the client secret that only the hub has.

**The pages** (nginx passes these five and the socket; `hub/login.py`):

| Page | What it does |
|---|---|
| `GET /hub/login?next=home\|room\|admin\|mapmaker\|matches\|match` | a random 128-bit `state` is kept in the hub (one use, 10 minutes, at most 10,000 at a time) and in a cookie `__Host-ants_oauth` (`Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax`, because Discord's way back is a navigation from another site), and the visitor is sent to Discord. Only a request of the front page's own button (or the address typed: `Sec-Fetch-Site` `same-origin` or `none`, or none at all) starts a login; a link or an image of another site (`cross-site`, `same-site`) gets a small page, and nginx turns it away before it spends the visitor's allowance, as does the hub (a hostile page cannot use up a visitor's tries). `next` is one of a fixed list of words (`home`, `room`, `admin`, `mapmaker`, `matches`, `match`), and the way back is the page of the word (`/`, `/#room`, `/admin`, `/mapmaker`, `/matches`, `/match`): never an address that was sent. The word `match` takes the second parameter `id`, which is used only when it has exactly the shape of a match id (`ants-<name>-<yyyymmdd>-<hhmmss>Z[-<n>]`) and then makes the way back `/match?id=<id>`; any other `id` makes it `/matches` |
| `GET /hub/callback?code&state` | the state of the cookie, of the address and of the hub must be one, the hub forgets it at once (one callback for a state, whatever it ends in), the code is exchanged, a login is kept and the visitor goes back to the front page with the cookie `__Host-ants_hub`. A replayed callback, a code that Discord has seen, a state that ran out, a missing or doubled cookie, a visitor who said no at Discord, a Discord that is down: a small page with a link back (`Login unavailable, try again later` for a Discord that does not answer or answers wrongly), never a login, and the sockets of everybody else are never held up |
| `GET /hub/me` | `{"login":true,"you":{"name","mod","role","k"[,"pick","other"]}\|null[,"play":{"name"[,"pass"]}]}` (the welcome's `you`, and for a person who is logged in `play`: the name that the games show, with the hub's pass for it when the hub has the key that the game server shares: [HUB_NICKNAMES.md](HUB_NICKNAMES.md#the-pass)), `no-store`; only for the front page's own request. The front page and the game page ask it for the name to play under ([`PLAY_IN_BROWSER.md`](PLAY_IN_BROWSER.md#your-name)) |
| `POST /hub/logout` | ends this login (the row is deleted, the cookie cleared, the sockets that it let in are closed with 1000) |
| `POST /hub/forget` | ends every login of the account and deletes every chat line that it wrote (not the hub's own line that said it was kicked or banned: that goes with the other lines, within 7 days) (the screens drop them at once) and its profile, its nickname and tag, the nicknames held for it and the claim on its login name ([HUB_NICKNAMES.md](HUB_NICKNAMES.md)), and its Discord picture ([HUB_PICTURES.md](HUB_PICTURES.md): the copy and its name go, the sockets are closed and no `you` is sent), and wipes the name from its mute and its ban (the screens are told of the newest 100 of the lines, one `del` frame each: a page keeps 100 lines at most, and a forget of 300,000 lines must not be 300,000 frames for every socket on the hub's one thread; the database work for the largest backlog that the limits allow holds the hub for under a second). A ban or a mute stays, by id, and so does the operator's block of the picture, by reference (one row: the reference, a flag and the time, no id and no name; its log rows go in 30 days): **neither a logout nor forgetting lifts one**. First, when `ANTS_HUB_COUNTS_URL` is set, it asks the game server's counts door to remove the person's likes of replays ([REPLAY_COUNTS.md](REPLAY_COUNTS.md#forget-me)); the door never stops Forget me: one that does not answer is asked again by the hub's clock for a day (the reference waits in a queue in memory) and the answer is `{"ok":true,"likes":"later"}`; with no good key nothing can be asked |

`/hub/me`, `/hub/logout` and `/hub/forget` answer only what the front page itself asked: the `Origin` must be the site's (a POST always has one) **and** `Sec-Fetch-Site` must be `same-origin` (403 otherwise, nothing done); a body is refused (400), a query is 404. The cookie is also `SameSite=Strict`, so a request of another site does not carry it at all.

**The cookie.** `__Host-ants_hub=<32 hex digits>; Path=/; Max-Age=2592000; Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Strict`: 128 random bits, 30 days, of which only the SHA-256 is kept (a copy of the database logs nobody in), a new value for every login (a value that a visitor already had is never taken over, and the old row is deleted), at most 10 for an account. The `__Host-` prefix makes a browser refuse the cookie unless it is `Secure`, has `Path=/` and no `Domain`, and stops a sibling subdomain from setting or replacing it ("cookie tossing"); that is why the path is `/` and not `/hub`, and why nginx narrows what it passes on: a cookie of the site goes to every request of the site, so [nginx](HUB.md#the-sites-nginx) passes the login's cookie to the five pages and the socket only, passes no other cookie to the hub, and drops the Cookie header at the game server's doors (`/ws`, `/busy`; the admin page's API gets its own cookie and no other). `HttpOnly`: the page never reads it. A cookie that comes twice, or has the wrong shape, is nobody's.

**A socket's identity** is fixed at the handshake from the cookie and holds for its life: it ends with the login (logout and forget close the sockets of the login or of the account; so does the end of the 30 days, within a second), and a socket never changes who it is. The welcome's `you` is `{name: <the name that is shown>, mod, role, k}`, and for an account that has both names `pick` and `other`; the page reads nothing else. A switch between the two names ends every login's sockets, because a socket's name is fixed at its handshake as its identity is. A banned account can log in and read; it cannot write (`banned`), and a mute stands too. An account younger than `ANTS_HUB_MIN_ACCOUNT_DAYS` (7; the age is in the id's first 42 bits, so no call is needed) gets `young` and cannot write.

A visitor's username is untrusted text: it must be a Discord username (`[a-z0-9_.]{2,32}`) or the login is refused with a page that does not show it; a username that could pass for the hub's own voice, or a lookalike of one, is refused with a page of its own that says why ([Names](HUB.md#names)). The display name is untrusted text too, and is cleaned by its own rule; it is never a reason for a page that refuses. Both are only ever written into a JSON body, never into a header, a page or the log.

**What the site's operator does once.** (0) **Before the login goes live:** name the reverse proxy in front of the site in `docker/trusted-proxies.conf` (see [Behind a reverse proxy](HUB.md#behind-a-reverse-proxy)), so that the limits per address count each visitor's own address. (1) Create a Discord application in Discord's developer portal (a name, a picture) and read its client id; (2) under OAuth2, add the redirect `https://<the site>/hub/callback`, and one for each other copy of the site that you run; (3) put the client id and the client secret in the stack's environment (`ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_CLIENT_ID`, `ANTS_HUB_DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET`), the secret **only** there, never in a file of this repository, a chat or a pull request; (4) optionally the privacy and terms links of the application, which can be [`/legal`](../web/legal.html); who may moderate is set up in [Roles](HUB_ROLES.md#roles). The hub calls `discord.com` over https, so its container needs outbound network access. Then redeploy the stack, log in once on the real site, and look at the hub's log for a `login` line. Real Discord is never asked by the tests; this step is the first time it is. **Try it with a fresh Discord account** that never authorized the application: the hub asks with `prompt=none`, which is meant to show a first-time visitor the consent page, and that is not verified against the real Discord; if Discord answers such a visitor with an error instead, they see "Login cancelled", and the `prompt` parameter (`hub/discord.py`) is what to change.

**The page** (`web/home/chat.js`, [HOME_SCREEN.md](HOME_CHAT.md#the-chat-dock)): the Log in button shows only when the welcome says `login:true` and has no `you`, and it is a full navigation to `/hub/login?next=home` (the Room's way is `next=room`), so the tab leaves for Discord and comes back to the same screen; the page's own session storage (`ants.lobby`) is untouched, so the seat of a room is taken back as after any reload. Log out and Forget me are `fetch` POSTs of the page itself; after either the page connects again, as a reader. The dropdown that the account's chip opens (the name, Log out, Forget me) has, for an account that has a display name as well, one more button, which says which name the chat shows and offers the other; it is the message `pick` over the socket that is open, and the hub's answer is to close the sockets of the account, after which the page connects again and reads the new name.
