The chat and logging in with Discord

Anyone can read the chat on the front page. To write in it you log in with Discord, which gives the site four things only: your Discord id, username and display name and, if you have set one, your profile picture. Where you are a member of the project’s Discord server, the site also reads your nickname in that server, if you have one, in the answer that tells it your roles (below), and shows it as your name; without one it shows your display name, then your username. They are kept so that the site knows who you are, shows the name that you pick and shows your picture, and for nothing else. The site asks for no email address and no list of friends or servers, and it keeps no Discord access token.

When you log in, the site fetches one small copy of your Discord profile picture from Discord and keeps it on its own server. While you are logged in it may ask Discord again, every few minutes, whether your picture changed (through the project’s bot; for an account that is not in the project’s Discord server, at most once a day, for your public profile) and fetches a new copy when it did. It is shown to everyone who opens the front page, logged in or not, in the round image next to your name, unless you switch off Show my Discord picture in the menu of your name or the site’s operator hides it. The copy is served by this site under a random address that is new every time the site fetches your picture and holds nothing of your Discord id; other people’s browsers do not contact Discord for it. The site keeps the copy only while you have a login, and for at most 30 days after the login that fetched it. Switching it off, logging out of your last login, or Forget me deletes the copy from the server at once, and so does a kick or a ban; when your last login runs out the copy stops being served at once and is deleted within the hour. Its address can still answer for up to five minutes, and a browser that has already loaded it may keep its own copy for up to a day. Your choice to switch the picture off is remembered, by a code made from your Discord id with a secret key and not by the id, for 400 days after your last login; Forget me deletes that too. If you change your picture at Discord, the site shows the new one within a few minutes for members of the project’s Discord server and otherwise within about a day, and at your next login at the latest.

Chat lines are kept for up to 7 days and only while they are among the newest 200 lines, with the id and the name that wrote them, so that moderators can deal with abuse, and are then deleted for good. Moderators also see your username next to a display name. A ban or a mute is kept by Discord id.

Moderators, administrators and members of the project’s Discord server who hold its images role may post images in the chat, unless an administrator has taken that away from them (kept as one entry under the same kind of code, with no id and no name, which stays after Forget me). The site never keeps your image as you sent it: it writes a new file from the pixels alone (a PNG or a JPEG of at most 2,048 pixels on a side, or a GIF of at most 400 pixels that keeps moving) and a small preview, so the location, camera and other details of a photo are left out. The files are kept only as long as the chat line that shows them, and then deleted for good; they are also deleted when you or a moderator delete the image and when you use Forget me. They are served by this site under random addresses that hold nothing of your Discord id; anyone who has an address can open the picture, as with the lines of the chat; a browser that has loaded one asks the site again before it shows it again, so a picture that was deleted is not shown from the browser’s copy. The site does not look at what is in them: tell a moderator on the project’s Discord server about one that should not be there. A moderator’s deleting of an image, and taking image posting away or giving it back, are written down in the log below.

Moderators and administrators are people who hold a moderator or administrator role in the project’s Discord server. While you are logged in and the chat is open, the site asks Discord, with the project’s bot, whether you hold one of those roles, again every few minutes and when you use a power. The site also asks Discord about any account that a moderator acts on, in the chat or not. The answers are kept in memory only, except that the name the site shows for you (your nickname, if you have one, else your display name) is kept as the display name of your profile. When a moderator or an administrator mutes, kicks or bans someone, lifts a mute or a ban, or deletes a line, the site writes down who did it, to which account, what it was and when. The accounts in that log are shown by a code that is made from the Discord id with a secret key, so the log itself does not hold the id. It is kept for 90 days, at most 10,000 entries, and is read only by the operator on the server; it is not shown on the site. A kick or a ban is also shown to everyone in the chat as a line with the person’s name, without the moderator’s. Forget me does not remove these entries, nor the line that said you were kicked or banned; that goes with the other lines within 7 days. When the operator hides a picture on the server, that is written down in the same way.

The name of your login is also the name you play under. It is shown in rooms and in the Games and Players lists, and it is written into the match history, which keeps results and names for good, and into the replay files: for up to 90 days on the site, and for ever in a file that someone has downloaded. Forget me does not remove it from those. The Players list shows the people who are logged in, and whether they are looking around, waiting in a room or playing.

Nickname and tag: when you are logged in you may choose a nickname of your own and, if you like, a tag of two to five letters, from the menu of your name. The site keeps them with your Discord id and the time of each change. A nickname that you give up is held for you for 30 days, so that nobody takes it to pass for you. While you have no nickname, the site keeps for 30 days a note of the login name that it signed for the game, so that two people do not play under one name. The nickname and the tag are public: they are shown to everybody, in the chat, the Players list, the rooms, the match history and the replay files, and what is written there stays after Forget me. The game server is given a signature that is good for two minutes and holds your name and nothing of your account. A guest’s name, typed on the front page, is kept in that browser only; the site checks it once and keeps nothing of it.

A heart on a replay (a like) is kept as one entry that holds the match and a code made from your Discord id with a secret key, the same kind of code as in the log above; it holds no Discord id, no name, no address and no time. Nobody can see who liked a match: the site shows only how many hearts it has, and tells you only which hearts are yours. Forget me removes your likes. How often a replay was played is kept as a number alone: nothing about who played it.

The login sets two cookies, both for logging in and nothing else: __Host-ants_oauth while you are on your way to Discord (10 minutes at most, deleted when you come back) and __Host-ants_hub, a random number that says which login is yours (30 days, deleted when you log out).

The voice button in the chat uses your browser’s speech recognition. Your browser may send the audio to its maker’s speech service (Chrome, Edge and Safari do); this site receives no audio and keeps nothing of it.

Log out in the chat ends the login of this browser. Forget me ends every login of your account, deletes your chat lines that are still kept and removes your username, display name and the copy of your picture from the site’s records, with your nickname and tag, the nicknames held for you and the note of your login name; a ban or a mute, if there is one, stays, and so does the operator’s hiding of your picture, kept as one entry under the same kind of code, with no id and no name. Reading the chat and playing need no login. What Discord itself does with your visit is under Discord’s own privacy policy.

Private messages: a person who is logged in can write a private message to another person who is logged in. Only the two of them see it on the site: no screen and no command of the moderators or the administrators shows a message. It is kept for 7 days (at most 300 for one conversation) and then deleted for good; either of you can delete a conversation from your side at once, and Forget me deletes your messages for the other person as well. A message is not encrypted on the server, so whoever runs the server could open its database by hand. The site keeps who wrote to whom by the same kind of code as the moderators’ log (made from the Discord id with a secret key), with the names as they were shown, and never by the id.

If you report a message, the site copies that message and up to five before it in the same conversation, with both names and the reason that you chose, and shows the copy to the administrators on the server, who may then mute or ban. Nothing else of your messages is shown. The person you reported is not told that you did or who you are. A report is deleted 30 days after it is closed, or after 90 days if nobody closes it; Forget me takes your name and code out of a report that you made and the name of the person out of a report about you, but the copy of the messages stays until then, up to 90 days, even after you use Forget me or delete the conversation. That a report was made and each look at the reports are written down in the log above (a number, never a word of a message). You stop a person’s messages with Ignore (the same list that hides their lines in the chat and their invitations): they are not told, and what they write afterwards is kept for them only, not shown to you.

Follows: you can follow a person from their menu or with /follow. A follow is private: the person is not told, nobody sees who follows whom or how many people follow somebody, and the list is yours alone. It gives you the Following tab of the Players list, a star by the name, and an alert when that person opens a public room (never an invite-only one). Forget me deletes your follows and every follow of you.

Direct connections between players

During an online match the players’ browsers send each other their own orders a moment ahead of the server’s. By default this goes through a relay run by this site, so no player’s address reaches another player. If you turn on Direct connection in a room, a player who also turned it on, and who is connected to you, can see your IP address. You can turn it off again in the room at any time. The relay sees the address of every player who uses it, and its log is short-lived.
